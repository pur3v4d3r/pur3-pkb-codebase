## 4. Reflexion (5 Questions)

**Q1 — What worked well?** The 1200–1800 word target (down from Session 3's 1900–2100) was sustainable without sacrificing depth — the three notes hit all 10 gates with less prose. Tighter cases studies (one per note, decisive) replaced the longer multi-example structures of Session 3.

**Q2 — What was harder than expected?** Differentiating `feedback-loop` from `mental-model`'s Craik-loop discussion required care. The resolution: this note treats *typology* (R vs B, gain, delay, stability) and *engineering formalization*; `mental-model` treats the *cognitive instantiation*. Same topology, different framing. The intentional cross-bridge in the Bridges table acknowledges the structural overlap without producing duplicate content.

**Q3 — Cross-cutting design pattern recognized?** All three Session 4 notes share an *invisible alternative* / *invisible structure* pedagogical motif: second-order thinking surfaces invisible *future plies*; feedback-loop surfaces invisible *closed-loop topology*; opportunity-cost surfaces invisible *foregone alternatives*. This is not coincidence — they are the three classical tools for compensating for the failure modes of *attention* in consequentialist reasoning. Worth noting for the Phase-8 densification audit.

**Q4 — Latticework gain?** With Phase 2 complete (8 hub-and-spoke notes) and 27 bridges in the table, the lattice has crossed a *critical mass*. Future Phase-3 notes (cognitive science) will be able to point to existing Phase-2 notes as structural-analogs (e.g., `predictive-coding` → `feedback-loop`; `dual-process-theory` → `mental-model`), rather than always pointing back to the hub. The graph density should increase notably from here.

**Q5 — What does this reveal about the master plan?** Phase 2's 8-note count was well-calibrated. Phase 3 (6 notes on cognitive science) should be tractable in the same 2-session rhythm. The master plan's 14-session total budget remains realistic.
