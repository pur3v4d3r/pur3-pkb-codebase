# Memory Consolidation

> [!definition] **Memory Consolidation**
> The processes by which newly formed memory traces are stabilised over time. Operates at two levels: (1) Synaptic consolidation — cellular/molecular stabilisation occurring over hours, requiring protein synthesis; and (2) Systems consolidation — hippocampal-to-neocortical transfer occurring over months to years (Standard Model) or ongoing hippocampal contribution throughout the lifetime of episodic memories (Multiple Trace Theory). Consolidation is distinct from encoding: encoding determines the initial trace; consolidation determines how robustly it is stabilised. Consolidation is supported and accelerated by sleep, particularly slow-wave sleep (hippocampal-neocortical replay) and REM sleep (emotional memory consolidation).
