# Declarative, Procedural, and Conditional Metacognitive Knowledge

> [!definition] **Declarative, Procedural, and Conditional Metacognitive Knowledge**
> A tripartite taxonomy of metacognitive knowledge developed from Flavell's framework. Declarative metacognitive knowledge is knowledge *that* (e.g., knowing that spaced practice is more effective than massed practice). Procedural metacognitive knowledge is knowledge of *how* to implement strategies (e.g., knowing how to space study sessions using a retrieval practice protocol). Conditional metacognitive knowledge is knowledge of *when* and *why* to apply strategies (e.g., understanding that spaced practice is most beneficial for material requiring long-term retention rather than short-term performance). All three are necessary for effective metacognitive regulation.

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight
> Metacognitive knowledge and regulation are not fixed personality traits but learnable, developable capacities that causally influence learning outcomes. Individuals with more accurate, sophisticated metacognition learn more efficiently, adapt more readily to novel challenges, and show greater transfer of knowledge across contexts. This claim has been supported across hundreds of empirical studies spanning diverse age groups, domains, and cultures.

Each learning episode generates information — about strategy effectiveness, about personal strengths and limitations, about the characteristics of the task — that modifies the learner's metacognitive knowledge base and motivational beliefs for subsequent episodes.
