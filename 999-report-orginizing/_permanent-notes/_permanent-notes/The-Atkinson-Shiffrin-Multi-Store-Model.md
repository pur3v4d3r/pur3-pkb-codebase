# The Atkinson-Shiffrin Multi-Store Model

> [!definition] **The Atkinson-Shiffrin Multi-Store Model**
> A structural theory of memory proposing three distinct storage systems: the sensory register (which holds precise but rapidly decaying percepts for fractions of a second), the short-term store (a limited-capacity buffer holding approximately 7±2 items for roughly 15-30 seconds without rehearsal), and the long-term store (an effectively unlimited repository of knowledge and experience). Information is thought to flow sequentially through these stores, with transfer from short-term to long-term memory mediated primarily by the duration and amount of rehearsal received in the short-term store.
> 
> **Boundary:** The model does not deny that qualitative properties of processing influence memory, but these are treated as secondary to structural processing — as variables that influence rehearsal efficiency rather than as independent determinants of trace strength.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This model is the primary intellectual foil against which Craik and Lockhart define their framework. Its limitations — particularly the separation of rehearsal type from retention prediction — provide the empirical and theoretical motivation for the levels approach.
