# The Organismic Dialectical Perspective

> [!definition] **The Organismic Dialectical Perspective**
> SDT's metatheoretical foundation — the view that human beings are active organisms with inherent growth tendencies (the "organismic" part) that continually interact with, and are shaped by, social environments (the "dialectical" part). The perspective holds that neither biology alone nor environment alone determines outcomes; instead, the continuous transaction between the person's inherent tendencies and the nutritive or impoverishing qualities of social contexts is the proper unit of analysis for motivational psychology. This perspective explains why SDT insists on studying both person and environment, and why it resists both nativist and social constructivist reduction.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application**
> The empirically supported elements of an autonomy-supportive instructional style include: (1) providing meaningful, non-manipulative rationale for assignments and requirements that students may not find inherently interesting; (2) acknowledging student perspectives, including negative ones, as legitimate rather than as obstacles to be overcome; (3) using informational rather than controlling language — "you might consider" rather than "you must," "here's what I observe" rather than "you have to…

> [!warning] **Key Distinction**
> Several misconceptions pervade popular appropriations of SDT. First: **autonomy support is not permissiveness**. Providing structure, setting high standards, and maintaining accountability are fully compatible with autonomy-supportive practice; what matters is how these elements are implemented, not whether they exist.
