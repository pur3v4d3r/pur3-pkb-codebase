# Accretion, Tuning, Restructuring

> [!definition] **Accretion, Tuning, Restructuring**
> Three modes of schema change proposed by Rumelhart and Norman. *Accretion* is the addition of new information to an existing schema without structural change — the most common mode. *Tuning* is the gradual modification of schema structure through experience — narrowing, broadening, or adjusting slot constraints and default values. *Restructuring* is the radical reorganization of existing schemas or the creation of new ones to accommodate information that cannot be handled by modification — the rarest and most cognitively demanding mode.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight
> Bartlett's most radical and enduring claim is that memory is not reproductive but *reconstructive*. When we recall a past experience or a text we have read, we do not simply replay a stored recording. We reconstruct it — building a plausible account from schema-consistent fragments, filling gaps with schema-generated defaults, and unconsciously editing anomalies toward what our schemas lead us to expect. > [!analytical-insight] Key Insight
> For Rumelhart, schemas are data structures that contain *variables* — open slots that take specific values when the schema is instantiated in a particular context. The room schema, for example, has slots for walls, floor, ceiling, doors, and furniture; the values of these slots are set when the schema is applied to a particular room.
