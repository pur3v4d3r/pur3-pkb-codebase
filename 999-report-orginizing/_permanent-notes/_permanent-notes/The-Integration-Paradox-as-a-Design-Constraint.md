# The Integration Paradox as a Design Constraint

> [!definition] **The Integration Paradox as a Design Constraint**
> The Integration Paradox implies a specific constraint for motivation change design: you cannot begin with the internalization sequence. Before an autonomy-supportive environment can do its work of enabling identification and integration, it must first repair the sense of autonomy that controlled environments have eroded. This suggests a two-phase model for motivation intervention: Phase 1 is **autonomy restoration** — creating the relational and environmental conditions in which the person can re-experience choice, perspective acknowledgment, and unconditional regard, rebuilding the experiential foundation that the internalization mechanism requires. Phase 2 is **internalization facilitation** — providing rationale, structure, and challenge within the restored autonomy context. Educational, therapeutic, and organizational interventions that go directly to Phase 2 without attending to Phase 1 will typically produce introjection rather than identification, because the person does not have the experiential autonomy base from which genuine valuing can operate. The Integration Paradox is not a problem to be solved; it is a constraint to be designed around.
