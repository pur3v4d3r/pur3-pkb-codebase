# Abductive Reasoning

> [!definition] **Abductive Reasoning**
> The logical form of inference that moves from a surprising or anomalous observation to the hypothesis that would, if true, render that observation intelligible or expected. Abduction does NOT establish that the hypothesis is true — only that it is worth investigating. It is the logic of hypothesis *generation*, not hypothesis *confirmation*. It is sometimes called *retroduction* in Peirce's earlier writings, and its more recent analytic reconstruction is known as [[Inference to the Best Explanation]].

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight
> Among the three forms of inference, only abduction generates genuinely *new* content — hypotheses that were not already present in the premises. Deduction is truth-preserving but non-ampliative (it cannot tell you more than the premises already contain). Induction is ampliative but not creative (it generalizes from what has already been observed). Abduction is both ampliative and creative: it projects an explanatory structure that was not directly observed and was not logically necessitated.…
