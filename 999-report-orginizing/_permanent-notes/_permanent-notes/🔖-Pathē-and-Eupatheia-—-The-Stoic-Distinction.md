# 🔖 Pathē and Eupatheia — The Stoic Distinction

> [!definition] **🔖 Pathē and Eupatheia — The Stoic Distinction**
> **Pathē** are emotionally disordered responses constituted by assent to false evaluative judgments about external things. They are "excessive impulses" — responses that represent the agent's evaluative system as out of rational alignment. **Eupatheia** (good feelings) are the correct emotional analogues: they arise when the same evaluative faculty reaches accurate conclusions about genuinely good and bad things (i.e., virtues and vices). The three eupatheia are: joy (*chara* — rational elation over genuine goods), wish (*boulēsis* — reasonable desire for what is genuinely good), and caution (*eulabeia* — appropriate avoidance of genuine evils). Importantly, there is no eupatheia corresponding to distress — because the Stoic sage, confronting any present situation, can always find something genuinely good to pursue.
