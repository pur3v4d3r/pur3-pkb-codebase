# Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) Model

> [!definition] **Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) Model**
> Gary Klein's model of expert decision-making in naturalistic settings. When an expert encounters a situation, they rapidly match it to a prototype or schema built from prior experience. This recognition simultaneously activates an understanding of the situation's dynamics, relevant goals, cues to monitor, and a typical action script. The expert then mentally simulates the action to check for obvious obstacles. If no critical obstacle is found, the expert acts. If an obstacle is found, the expert modifies the action or searches sequentially for alternatives — not through comparative evaluation but through simulation of candidates until a workable option is found.

## Reflection Prompts

> [!reflection] **Reflect** *(from report-11-the-practical-reasoner.md)*
> Think of a situation where you knew you needed to make a decision but kept gathering more information, seeking more opinions, or running more analyses. Were you genuinely uncertain about something that more information could resolve, or were you looking for certainty that the situation structurally could not provide? What was the cost of delayed action? What would the Stoic kathekon — appropriate action given your current wisdom — have counseled?

> [!reflection] **Reflect** *(from report-11-the-practical-reasoner.md)*
> Identify a decision you are currently facing or recently faced where deliberation has not converged toward a clear answer. Are you experiencing Knightian uncertainty — where more analysis seems to generate more questions rather than resolving them? What do your emotional and somatic signals indicate? What would the kathekon — the appropriate action for someone with your character, values, and situation — look like, independent of outcome guarantees?
