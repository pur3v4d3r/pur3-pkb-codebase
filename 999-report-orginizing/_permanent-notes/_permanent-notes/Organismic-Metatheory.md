# Organismic Metatheory

> [!definition] **Organismic Metatheory**
> The philosophical framework underlying SDT, which posits that human beings are inherently active organisms with an innate propensity toward psychological growth, integration, and the elaboration of a unified self. Unlike mechanistic models that treat behavior as the product of external forces acting on a passive organism, the organismic perspective treats the human organism as a naturally proactive agent whose developmental trajectory depends on the degree to which its basic psychological needs are satisfied by the social environment.
> 
> **Boundary:** The organismic metatheory is not a claim about biological determinism — it does not specify genetic programs for particular behaviors. Rather, it describes a general developmental directionality (toward integration and growth) that can be supported or derailed by environmental conditions.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This metatheoretical commitment is what makes SDT fundamentally different from reinforcement-based approaches to motivation. It is the ground from which all of SDT's specific predictions arise.
>
