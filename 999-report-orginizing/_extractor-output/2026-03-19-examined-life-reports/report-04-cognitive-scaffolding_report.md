#### 4. [DEFINITION] 📖 Zone of Proximal Development *(Line 203)*

> [!definition] 📖 Zone of Proximal Development
> The Zone of Proximal Development (Vygotsky, 1978) is defined as "the distance between the actual developmental level as determined by independent problem solving and the level of potential development as determined through problem-solving under adult guidance or in collaboration with more capable peers." It describes not a property of the learner alone, but of the *interaction* between the learner and a supportive environment. It is the cognitive space where learning is simultaneously possible (the learner can engage) and generative (the engagement produces development).

#### 5. [PRINCIPLE-POINT] 🧭 Core Principle *(Line 212)*

> [!principle-point] 🧭 Core Principle
> The ZPD is not a property of the learner; it is a property of the *interaction* between learner and environment. This means that the same individual has different ZPDs in different domains, at different times, and with different supports. The examined life requires continuously re-locating one's ZPD across the domains one inhabits.

#### 6. [DEFINITION] 📖 Dewey's Continuity and Interaction Principles *(Line 219)*

> [!definition] 📖 Dewey's Continuity and Interaction Principles
> Dewey (1938) proposed two fundamental criteria for genuinely educative experience. *Continuity* requires that each experience both draw upon and prepare the ground for future experience — it must connect to what the learner already knows and extend toward new understanding. *Interaction* requires that experience involve a genuine transaction between the learner's internal state and the external environment — both must be modified by the encounter. Experience that lacks continuity is disconnected and fails to build; experience that lacks interaction is passive and fails to teach.

#### 7. [DEFINITION] 📖 Assimilation, Accommodation, and Equilibration *(Line 234)*

> [!definition] 📖 Assimilation, Accommodation, and Equilibration
> Piaget's three-part schema dynamics describe the continuous movement of cognitive development. *Assimilation* incorporates new information into existing schemas — the schema handles the new input, extending its application. *Accommodation* restructures the schema in response to information that existing schemas cannot adequately process — the schema is modified, differentiated, or replaced. *Equilibration* is the self-regulating process that seeks cognitive balance — a state in which the learner's schemas are adequate to handle their experience. When equilibration is disrupted (when a schema fails), the learner enters a productive state of *disequilibrium* that drives accommodation.

#### 8. [INSIGHT] 💡 The Paradox at the Core *(Line 241)*

> [!insight] 💡 The Paradox at the Core
> Schema theory reveals a fundamental paradox of learning: schemas are simultaneously the *precondition* for understanding (you must have organized structures to connect new information to) and the *obstacle* to growth (schemas that have served well in familiar territory become active misrepresentations of unfamiliar territory). Learning requires holding schemas open for revision — using them as tools while simultaneously recognizing their provisional, revisable character. This is precisely the [[Fallibilist]] epistemological posture described in [[Report 02]]: the examined thinker treats their schemas as excellent but improvable models of reality, not as transparent windows onto it.

#### 9. [DEFINITION] 📖 Working Memory and Long-Term Memory in CLT *(Line 248)*

> [!definition] 📖 Working Memory and Long-Term Memory in CLT
> Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller, 1988) is grounded in a two-system account of memory. *Working memory* (WM) is severely limited in capacity (holding approximately 2–4 novel elements simultaneously) and duration (information persists for seconds without rehearsal). *Long-term memory* (LTM) is effectively unlimited in capacity but requires effortful transfer from WM. Crucially, schemas stored in LTM can be retrieved into WM as single functional units regardless of their internal complexity — a complex skill or concept that took months to learn can be activated as a single "slot" in WM once adequately schematized. This means that expertise is not primarily the possession of more knowledge, but the *organization* of knowledge into schemas that compress complexity into manageable units.

#### 30. [DEFINITION] 📖 Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) *(Line 409)*

> [!definition] 📖 Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)
> Vygotsky's (1978) concept describing the cognitive zone between what a learner can accomplish independently (Zone of Actual Development) and what they can accomplish with appropriate support from a More Knowledgeable Other. The ZPD is not a property of the learner alone but of the interaction between learner and supporting environment. It is the site where genuine developmental growth occurs — where existing schemas are engaged but insufficient, and where scaffolding can catalyze new schema construction. The ZPD is dynamic: it shifts as schemas develop, requiring continuous recalibration of what constitutes appropriate challenge and support.

#### 31. [DEFINITION] 📖 Scaffolding *(Line 412)*

> [!definition] 📖 Scaffolding
> The temporary, graduated support provided by a More Knowledgeable Other (teacher, peer, text, or self-designed structure) that enables a learner to operate within their ZPD. The term was coined by Jerome Bruner, David Wood, and Gail Ross (1976), not Vygotsky himself. Effective scaffolding has three characteristics: it enables the learner to engage with challenges beyond their independent capability; it is calibrated to the learner's current schema state; and it fades as the learner develops new schemas, preventing dependence. Hattie (2023): effect size d=0.82.

#### 32. [DEFINITION] 📖 Intrinsic, Extraneous, and Germane Cognitive Load *(Line 415)*

> [!definition] 📖 Intrinsic, Extraneous, and Germane Cognitive Load
> Sweller's (1988, 2010) three-category taxonomy of cognitive demands on working memory. *Intrinsic load* reflects the inherent element interactivity of the material — the number of elements that must be simultaneously held in working memory to understand the content — determined by both the material's complexity and the learner's existing schema state. *Extraneous load* is generated by instructional design factors that are not necessary for schema construction (redundant information, split attention, poor organization). *Germane load* is the cognitive effort directed specifically at schema construction. Well-designed learning maximizes germane load by minimizing extraneous load and ensuring intrinsic load is challenging but not overwhelming.

#### 33. [DEFINITION] 📖 Schema (cognitive) *(Line 418)*

> [!definition] 📖 Schema (cognitive)
> A cognitive knowledge structure that organizes information into a functional unit that can be activated as a whole in working memory, regardless of its internal complexity. Schemas encode prototypical patterns, relationships between elements, and procedures for action. Schemas develop through repeated encounters with structured information and enable experts to process domain-specific information with dramatically reduced cognitive load. The key dynamics of schema change are Piaget's *assimilation* (fitting new information into existing schemas) and *accommodation* (restructuring schemas to handle information that resists assimilation).

#### 34. [DEFINITION] 📖 Expertise Reversal Effect *(Line 421)*

> [!definition] 📖 Expertise Reversal Effect
> Kalyuga's (2007) finding that instructional techniques effective for novice learners become ineffective or counterproductive for advanced learners. The primary mechanism is redundancy: for learners with well-developed schemas, explicit instruction and worked examples force them to process information their schemas already handle efficiently, consuming working memory resources unnecessarily. The expertise reversal effect is a practical expression of the need for continuous ZPD recalibration — as schemas develop, what constitutes appropriate scaffolding must shift toward productive challenge.

#### 35. [DEFINITION] 📖 Constructive Attentiveness *(Line 424)*

> [!definition] 📖 Constructive Attentiveness
> The emergent meta-skill of this report's synthesis: the cultivated metacognitive capacity to locate one's current cognitive boundary (the edge between existing schemas and what demands new construction), calibrate the challenge and support conditions appropriate to one's current schema state, recognize consequential feedback that signals genuine schema construction versus verbal familiarity, and track the dynamic movement of that boundary as schemas develop. Constructive Attentiveness integrates the ZPD's spatial location of productive challenge, Dewey's consequential verification of genuine understanding, Schema Theory's account of the cognitive architecture being built, and CLT's resource economics of the building process.
