#### 5. [DEFINITION] 📖 **Argumentative Theory of Reasoning *(Line 208)*

> [!definition] 📖 **Argumentative Theory of Reasoning
> The evolutionary hypothesis (Mercier & Sperber, 2011, 2017) that the primary function of human reasoning is not individual truth-seeking but the social production and evaluation of arguments — generating justifications for one's positions and assessing the justifications offered by others. This account explains both the systematic biases of solo reasoning (which serves the argumentative function by generating supporting arguments) and the surprising effectiveness of reasoning in dialogic contexts (where the adversarial structure triggers genuine evaluation). The theory makes reasoning a cognitive mechanism aimed at *social interaction*, grounding its evolution in communication rather than in individual cognition.

#### 6. [DEFINITION] 📖 **Distributed Cognition *(Line 221)*

> [!definition] 📖 **Distributed Cognition
> The theoretical framework (Hutchins, 1995) that cognitive processes — computation, memory, reasoning, learning — routinely extend beyond individual minds and are distributed across multiple people, tools, and representational media organized into functional systems. The central claim is not that individuals are helped by social and material resources but that cultural activity systems can have cognitive properties of their own that differ from and exceed the cognitive properties of any individual participant. Cognition is not merely *socially situated*; it is, in many of its most important forms, genuinely *socially constituted*.

#### 7. [DEFINITION] 📖 **Epistemic Injustice *(Line 232)*

> [!definition] 📖 **Epistemic Injustice
> The family of harms in which a person is wronged specifically in their capacity as a knower (Fricker, 2007). Fricker identifies two primary forms. *Testimonial injustice* occurs when identity prejudice — concerning someone's gender, race, class, age, disability, or other social characteristics — causes a hearer to assign them a deflated level of credibility, thereby robbing them of their epistemic due as a source of knowledge. *Hermeneutical injustice* occurs when a structural gap in collective interpretive resources renders some aspect of someone's social experience unintelligible — to themselves and to others — because the concepts needed to understand and communicate that experience do not yet exist or are not widely shared. The harm is done not by an individual agent but by a social structure, making hermeneutical injustice, as Fricker writes, "purely structural."

#### 8. [PRINCIPLE-POINT] 🔑 **The Two Levels of Epistemic Injustice *(Line 237)*

> [!principle-point] 🔑 **The Two Levels of Epistemic Injustice
> Testimonial injustice wrongs individuals in *epistemic encounters* through deflated credibility assignments driven by identity prejudice. Hermeneutical injustice wrongs entire social groups at the level of *shared interpretive frameworks* by excluding their experiences from the conceptual resources available to everyone. The two forms often compound: hermeneutical injustice makes certain experiences hard to communicate, and testimonial injustice makes the attempts at communication that do occur less likely to be believed.

#### 9. [METHODOLOGY-AND-SOURCES] 🔬 **On Groupthink: An Important Empirical Caveat *(Line 246)*

> [!methodology-and-sources] 🔬 **On Groupthink: An Important Empirical Caveat
> It is essential to note that while Janis's descriptive account of groupthink symptoms (concurrence-seeking, self-censorship, illusions of unanimity, mindguarding) captures genuine phenomena, his proposed causal model — linking these symptoms specifically to group cohesion as the primary antecedent — has not received consistent empirical support after fifty years of research. Studies have generally confirmed that the symptoms Janis described are real and occur across a wide range of group settings; they have generally failed to confirm that the specific causal pathway he proposed (cohesion → concurrence-seeking) is primary. The framework is most productively used as a checklist of danger signs rather than a validated causal theory. For more robust empirical grounding, [[Cass Sunstein]]'s work on *group polarization* — the well-supported finding that group deliberation tends to push members toward more extreme versions of their pre-deliberation views — provides a complementary framework with stronger experimental backing.

#### 12. [CONNECTION-IDEAS] 🔗 **The Convergent Discovery *(Line 271)*

> [!connection-ideas] 🔗 **The Convergent Discovery
> Socrates discovered, through philosophical practice and observation, exactly what Mercier and Sperber's evolutionary theory predicts: that reasoning performs best in adversarial-collaborative dialogue, that individuals exposed to genuine challenges to their views are forced to reason more carefully than individuals deliberating alone, and that the social pressure of having to defend one's positions to a critical interlocutor activates cognitive capacities that solo reflection consistently underutilizes. Two and a half millennia separate Socrates from Mercier and Sperber; centuries of philosophical and scientific development separate their methodological traditions. But they are describing the same underlying fact about human cognition: *the social environment of reasoning is not a luxury but a functional requirement for its optimal operation.*

#### 13. [INSIGHT] 💡 **Cognition at the System Level *(Line 282)*

> [!insight] 💡 **Cognition at the System Level
> Hutchins's key insight for the examined life: the unit of cognitive analysis should sometimes be the *system* rather than the individual. Just as a navigator's cognitive performance is best evaluated at the level of the navigation team — how reliably the team avoids navigational errors — the epistemic performance of an examined life is best evaluated partly at the level of the epistemic communities the person participates in and contributes to. Wisdom includes not only individual epistemic virtue but the capacity to participate well in distributed cognitive systems, contributing to their reliability and benefiting from their error-correction.

#### 14. [PRINCIPLE-POINT] 🔑 **The Resolution: Complementarity, Not Competition *(Line 291)*

> [!principle-point] 🔑 **The Resolution: Complementarity, Not Competition
> Individual reflective capacity and social reasoning are complementary, not competing, epistemic tools. The Tier 1 framework's emphasis on individual metacognition, intellectual virtue, and fallibilist epistemology is *necessary but insufficient* for the examined life. It is necessary because being a good participant in social reasoning requires individual epistemic discipline — the capacity to evaluate others' arguments rather than merely react to them, to update beliefs based on genuine evidence rather than social pressure, and to contribute genuine insights rather than conformist agreement. It is insufficient because individual reasoning consistently tends toward myside bias, and the error-correction that genuine dialogue provides cannot be replicated internally. The examined life requires both excellent individual epistemic practice *and* excellent social epistemic participation; neither alone is enough.

#### 16. [INSIGHT] 💡 **The Three Social Epistemic Virtues *(Line 311)*

> [!insight] 💡 **The Three Social Epistemic Virtues
> **[[Testimonial Sensitivity]]** is the virtue of giving appropriate credence to others' testimony, calibrated to their actual reliability rather than to their social identity. It is the antidote to testimonial injustice: the person who possesses testimonial sensitivity neither inflates credibility (epistemic sycophancy) nor deflates it (testimonial injustice) on the basis of who the speaker is rather than what they are saying and how well they are positioned to know it. Testimonial sensitivity requires ongoing critical reflexivity about one's own credibility assessments and the stereotypes that unconsciously shape them.
> 
> **[[Hermeneutical Humility]]** is the recognition that one's interpretive frameworks — the conceptual resources through which experience becomes intelligible and communicable — are shaped by one's social position, and that experiences shaped by different social positions may genuinely exceed one's current conceptual vocabulary. The person with hermeneutical humility does not assume that a failure to understand another's experience means that nothing real is being described; they assume, rather, that the gap may reside in their own interpretive resources rather than in the other's experience. Hermeneutical humility is the antidote to the complementary error of hermeneutical injustice: not the structural gap itself, which no individual virtue can eliminate, but the individual's contribution to it.
> 
> **[[Dialogical Courage]]** is the willingness to voice genuine disagreement, present unwelcome evidence, and maintain an unpopular position in social reasoning contexts where conformity is easier and more comfortable. It is the social analogue of intellectual courage (Report 05) and the individual-level antidote to groupthink dynamics: the person who possesses dialogical courage provides the independent critical voice that makes social reasoning genuinely rather than merely formally adversarial. Without dialogical courage, the social conditions for excellent reasoning that the argumentative theory predicts collapse into mutual reinforcement — the epistemic equivalent of a navigation team in which every crew member reports the same bearing as the one before them, regardless of what their instrument shows.

#### 25. [DEFINITION] 📖 **Distributed Cognition *(Line 418)*

> [!definition] 📖 **Distributed Cognition
> The theoretical framework (Hutchins, 1995) holding that cognitive processes are routinely constituted across multiple agents, tools, and representational media organized into functional systems. #### 26. [DEFINITION] 📖 **Testimonial Injustice *(Line 421)*

> [!definition] 📖 **Testimonial Injustice
> One of Fricker's (2007) two forms of epistemic injustice: the harm done to a speaker when identity-based prejudice causes a hearer to assign them a deflated level of credibility, thereby wronging them specifically in their capacity as a knower. The harm is simultaneously epistemic (their knowledge is not received) and ethical (their status as a knower is diminished).

#### 27. [DEFINITION] 📖 **Hermeneutical Injustice *(Line 424)*

> [!definition] 📖 **Hermeneutical Injustice
> The second of Fricker's (2007) two forms of epistemic injustice: a purely structural harm occurring when a gap in collective interpretive resources leaves some aspect of a person's social experience unintelligible — to themselves and to others — because the concepts needed to understand and communicate that experience are absent from the shared vocabulary. Unlike testimonial injustice, it has no individual agent and cannot be remedied by individual virtue alone.

#### 28. [DEFINITION] 📖 **Social Epistemic Virtue [Emergent Concept] *(Line 427)*

> [!definition] 📖 **Social Epistemic Virtue [Emergent Concept]
> The family of character dispositions required for excellent participation in social epistemic practices, extending the individual intellectual virtues of Report 05 to the social domain. Comprises three primary virtues: Testimonial Sensitivity (accurate credibility assignment uncorrupted by identity prejudice), Hermeneutical Humility (recognition that one's interpretive frameworks may be inadequate to experiences shaped by different social positions), and Dialogical Courage (willingness to maintain genuine epistemic independence against social pressure toward conformity).

#### 29. [DEFINITION] 📖 **Epistemic Bubble vs. Echo Chamber *(Line 430)*

> [!definition] 📖 **Epistemic Bubble vs. Echo Chamber
> Nguyen's (2020) distinction between two types of degraded social epistemic environments. An epistemic bubble is a social structure from which contrary voices are accidentally excluded — remediated by exposure to alternative views. An echo chamber is a social structure that has actively damaged members' trust in outside sources — not remediated by mere exposure because the damage is to the epistemic relationships through which outside testimony would be evaluated.

#### 30. [DEFINITION] 📖 **Cognitive Ecology *(Line 433)*

> [!definition] 📖 **Cognitive Ecology
> The study of the social and material environment as constitutive of cognitive performance — how the structure of one's epistemic community, the tools and representations available within it, and the norms governing epistemic exchange shape cognitive outcomes at both individual and system levels. The term captures the insight that cognition, like biological organisms, is always situated in an environment that shapes its possible range of expression.
