#### 2. [DEFINITION] Working Memory *(Line 176)*

> [!definition] Working Memory
> A limited-capacity cognitive system responsible for the temporary maintenance and simultaneous manipulation of information in the service of complex cognition, including comprehension, reasoning, learning, and planning. Working memory is distinguished from mere short-term storage by its emphasis on active processing and executive control.
> 
> **Boundary:** Working memory is not synonymous with [[Short-Term-Memory]], though the two constructs share historical lineage. Short-term memory, as classically conceived, refers to passive, time-limited storage; working memory adds the dimension of active processing and attentional control.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Working memory serves as the foundational construct for this entire analysis — the bottleneck through which all higher cognition must pass, and therefore the system whose architecture most directly constrains and enables learning.
>

> There is a deep irony in working memory's architectural position: the system that makes complex thought possible is also the system that most severely constrains it. #### 7. [WARNING] The Rehearsal Fallacy *(Line 233)*

> [!warning] The Rehearsal Fallacy
> The Atkinson-Shiffrin model's emphasis on maintenance rehearsal as the primary mechanism for long-term encoding proved to be one of its most significant empirical vulnerabilities. Subsequent research, particularly the work of [[Fergus-I.-M.-Craik|Craik]] and Lockhart on [[Levels-of-Processing]], demonstrated that the depth of processing — not the duration of rehearsal — is the critical determinant of memory encoding. Information that is processed semantically, in terms of meaning and association, is retained far more effectively than information that is merely repeated, regardless of how many times the repetition occurs. This finding undermined the multi-store model's account of transfer from short-term to long-term memory and created theoretical space for a more process-oriented conception of temporary memory.

#### 8. [EXAMPLE] The Dual-Task Paradigm and Its Revelations *(Line 242)*

> [!example] The Dual-Task Paradigm and Its Revelations
> Consider a person asked to repeat a sequence of six random digits aloud (engaging the phonological loop) while simultaneously navigating a complex spatial environment (engaging the visuospatial sketchpad). The multicomponent model predicts that these two tasks should interfere with each other relatively little, because they draw on separate subsystems. This prediction has been confirmed repeatedly: verbal and spatial concurrent tasks produce far less mutual interference than two verbal tasks or two spatial tasks performed simultaneously. This pattern of selective interference is one of the strongest lines of evidence for the architectural independence of the phonological loop and the visuospatial sketchpad.

#### 12. [DEFINITION] Phonological Loop *(Line 272)*

> [!definition] Phonological Loop
> A specialised subsystem of working memory comprising a temporary phonological store (with a decay period of approximately 1.5–2 seconds) and an articulatory rehearsal process (analogous to inner speech) that refreshes decaying representations. It is the primary mechanism for maintaining verbal-sequential information.
> 
> **Boundary:** The phonological loop does not process meaning. It operates on sound-based codes. Semantic processing requires the involvement of the [[Central-Executive]] and [[Long-Term-Memory]].
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The phonological loop provides the clearest illustration of how a domain-specific subsystem can be characterised through converging experimental effects, and it anchors the broader claim that working memory is multicomponent rather than unitary.
> 
> **See also:** [[Maintenance-Rehearsal]], [[Dual-Coding-Theory]], [[Modality-Effect]]

#### 13. [KEY-CLAIM] The Central Executive as Attentional Controller *(Line 295)*

> [!key-claim] The Central Executive as Attentional Controller
> The central executive is not a memory store in any conventional sense. It does not maintain representations; rather, it controls the deployment of attention and coordinates the operations of the storage subsystems. Its functions — selective attention, task switching, inhibition, and long-term memory retrieval — correspond closely to the construct of [[Executive-Function]] as it has been elaborated in the neuropsychological literature.

#### 14. [CLAUDE-INSIGHT] The Homunculus Problem and Its Resolution *(Line 300)*

> [!claude-insight] The Homunculus Problem and Its Resolution
> The history of the central executive illustrates a recurring pattern in cognitive science: a construct is introduced at a high level of abstraction to explain a broad range of phenomena, is then criticised for its explanatory opacity, and is subsequently decomposed into more specific and empirically tractable subprocesses. The homunculus charge — the complaint that the central executive merely names the problem rather than explaining it — was methodologically productive precisely because it forced researchers to fractionate executive control into components that could be independently measured and experimentally dissociated. The Miyake-Friedman framework represents one of the most successful outcomes of this decomposition programme.

#### 15. [DEFINITION] Episodic Buffer *(Line 309)*

> [!definition] Episodic Buffer
> A limited-capacity component of working memory that serves as a multimodal binding interface, integrating information from the phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, and long-term memory into unified episodic representations. It provides a mechanism by which schemas and semantic knowledge can influence working memory performance.
> 
> **Boundary:** The episodic buffer is not equivalent to [[Episodic-Memory]] in the sense of Tulving's theory, though the two constructs share the notion of integrated, contextualised representations. The buffer is a working memory component; episodic memory is a long-term memory system.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The episodic buffer resolves several theoretical gaps in the original tripartite model and provides a mechanism for the well-documented influence of long-term knowledge on working memory capacity — a phenomenon central to [[Schema-Theory]] and [[Cognitive-Load-Theory]].
> 
> **See also:** [[Episodic-Memory]], [[Schema]], [[Baddeley's-Working-Memory-Model]]

#### 18. [DEFINITION] Embedded-Processes Model (Cowan) *(Line 341)*

> [!definition] Embedded-Processes Model (Cowan)
> A theoretical framework in which working memory is not a separate structural system but rather the product of attentional processes operating on the temporarily activated subset of long-term memory. The model distinguishes between a broad activated region (unlimited in principle but subject to temporal decay) and a narrow focus of attention (limited to approximately 3–4 items).
> 
> **Boundary:** This model does not deny domain-specific processing differences but attributes them to long-term memory representations and attentional strategies rather than to dedicated architectural subsystems.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Cowan's model provides the most influential alternative to the multicomponent model and offers a lower capacity estimate (3–4 items vs. 7±2) that has reshaped debate about [[Working-Memory-Capacity]].
> 
> **See also:** [[Attention]], [[Long-Term-Memory]], [[Working-Memory-Capacity]], [[Miller's-Magical-Number-Seven]]

#### 19. [KEY-CLAIM] Working Memory Capacity as Attentional Control *(Line 356)*

> [!key-claim] Working Memory Capacity as Attentional Control
> Engle's framework redefines working memory capacity as the ability to maintain information in an active, retrievable state through controlled attention, particularly under conditions of interference or distraction. This redefinition shifts the explanatory locus from storage architecture to attentional processes and predicts that individual differences in WMC should correlate with performance on any task that requires controlled attention, not only memory tasks.

#### 20. [CLAUDE-INSIGHT] Convergence Beneath Diversity *(Line 365)*

> [!claude-insight] Convergence Beneath Diversity
> Despite their apparent differences, the major models of working memory converge on several fundamental points. All agree that working memory is severely capacity-limited. All agree that attention plays a central role in determining what is maintained and what is lost. All agree that the relationship between working memory and long-term memory is intimate and bidirectional. And all agree that individual differences in working memory function are among the most consequential predictors of higher cognitive performance. The models diverge principally on architectural questions — whether the system comprises dedicated modular stores or attentional processes operating on activated long-term memory — rather than on functional ones. This convergence suggests that the empirical phenomena are robust; the theoretical interpretations, while genuinely different, may be more complementary than contradictory.

#### 24. [KEY-CLAIM] The Revised Capacity Estimate *(Line 397)*

> [!key-claim] The Revised Capacity Estimate
> Contemporary evidence supports a core working memory capacity of approximately 3–4 items when chunking and rehearsal are controlled. Miller's classical estimate of 7±2 reflects the augmented effective capacity achieved through strategic grouping and subvocal rehearsal, not the raw capacity of the attentional workspace.

#### 25. [DEFINITION] Chunking (Cognitive) *(Line 404)*

> [!definition] Chunking (Cognitive)
> The process of organising individual items of information into larger, integrated units (chunks) that can be maintained in working memory as single entities. [[Cognitive-Chunking|Chunking]] reduces the effective number of items that must be held simultaneously, thereby circumventing — though not expanding — working memory's capacity limits.
> 
> **Boundary:** Chunking is a representational strategy, not an expansion of working memory's structural capacity. The number of chunks that can be held remains limited (~4); what changes is the informational richness of each chunk.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Chunking is the primary mechanism by which expertise reduces cognitive load and represents the most direct bridge between working memory constraints and the knowledge structures elaborated by [[Schema-Theory]].
> 
> **See also:** [[Schema]], [[Schema-Automation]], [[Expertise-Reversal-Effect]], [[Herbert-Simon]]

#### 26. [WARNING] The Training Question *(Line 421)*

> [!warning] The Training Question
> The strong correlation between working memory capacity and fluid intelligence has prompted interest in whether WMC can be trained — and, if so, whether such training transfers to improved reasoning and academic performance. The evidence on this question is mixed and often discouraging. While participants can improve their performance on trained tasks (e.g., n-back), the transfer of these gains to untrained tasks and to fluid intelligence has proven inconsistent and, when observed, often small in magnitude. Several large-scale meta-analyses have concluded that working memory training produces reliable near-transfer effects (improvement on tasks structurally similar to the training task) but limited and unreliable far-transfer effects (improvement on reasoning, academic achievement, or everyday cognitive function). The enthusiasm for "brain training" programmes must therefore be tempered by the recognition that working memory capacity, while not entirely immutable, appears to be substantially resistant to lasting expansion through training alone.

#### 27. [CLAUDE-INSIGHT] The Bandwidth-Gating Thesis *(Line 428)*

> [!claude-insight] The Bandwidth-Gating Thesis
> One possible resolution to the puzzle of working memory's limited capacity draws on computational considerations. A system that maintained all potentially relevant information in an equally active state would face a combinatorial explosion in the number of possible bindings and relationships that would need to be evaluated. By restricting the number of simultaneously active representations, working memory imposes a form of bandwidth gating that constrains the combinatorial space and makes real-time processing computationally tractable. In this view, limited capacity is not a failure of the system but a design feature that trades breadth for depth — ensuring that the few items within the workspace can be processed thoroughly rather than the many items being processed superficially. This perspective aligns with [[Selective-Attention|selective attention]] as a filtering mechanism and suggests that the constraints of working memory are intimately related to the constraints on attentional selection.

#### 30. [EXAMPLE] Neural Dissociation of Phonological Loop and Visuospatial Sketchpad *(Line 457)*

> [!example] Neural Dissociation of Phonological Loop and Visuospatial Sketchpad
> Neuroimaging studies have provided striking confirmation of the architectural distinctions proposed by the multicomponent model. Tasks that engage the phonological loop preferentially activate left-hemisphere regions, including Broca's area (associated with articulatory rehearsal) and the left supramarginal gyrus (associated with phonological storage). Tasks that engage the visuospatial sketchpad preferentially activate right-hemisphere posterior parietal and occipital regions. This neural dissociation parallels the behavioural dissociations observed in dual-task studies and provides converging evidence from an entirely different level of analysis for the claim that verbal and visuospatial working memory involve separable processing systems.

#### 31. [CLAUDE-INSIGHT] The Prefrontal Vulnerability Principle *(Line 470)*

> [!claude-insight] The Prefrontal Vulnerability Principle
> There is a notable symmetry in the developmental and ageing trajectories of working memory: the prefrontal cortex is the last region to mature and among the first to decline. This "last in, first out" pattern means that the executive components of working memory — the components most critical for complex reasoning, flexible adaptation, and self-regulation — are also the most developmentally fragile and the most vulnerable to age-related deterioration. This principle has significant implications for educational practice (the executive demands placed on young learners should be calibrated to their prefrontal maturity) and for the design of supportive environments for older adults (external scaffolding can compensate for declining executive resources).

#### 34. [DEFINITION] Element Interactivity *(Line 492)*

> [!definition] Element Interactivity
> The degree to which the elements comprising a learning task must be processed simultaneously rather than sequentially in working memory. High element interactivity characterises material in which understanding any single element depends on its relationship to multiple other elements, creating an intrinsic cognitive load that cannot be reduced without simplifying the learning objective itself.
> 
> **Boundary:** Element interactivity is a property of the material relative to the learner's expertise, not an absolute property of the material alone. As schemas develop, previously interacting elements are integrated into single chunks, reducing effective element interactivity.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Element interactivity is the mechanism by which working memory constraints translate into learning difficulty — it specifies precisely why some material overwhelms working memory while other material does not.
>

> [!original-synthesis] Working Memory as the Architectural Rationale for CLT
> The relationship between working memory research and cognitive load theory is more than historical or inspirational — it is structural. Each of CLT's major instructional effects can be derived logically from the architecture of working memory: the worked-example effect from the limited capacity of the central executive; the split-attention effect from the cost of maintaining and integrating cross-referenced information; the modality effect from the partial independence of verbal and visual subsystems; the redundancy effect from the attentional cost of processing unnecessarily duplicated information. In this sense, CLT does not merely draw upon working memory research — it is an applied theory of working memory, one that translates architectural constraints into design prescriptions. The power of this translation is precisely why CLT has become one of the most empirically productive frameworks in educational psychology.

#### 39. [KEY-CLAIM] Working Memory as the Bottleneck for Comprehension *(Line 545)*

> [[Working-Memory-in-Reading|Working memory in reading]] functions as the processing space in which textual input, prior knowledge, and inferential processes converge. #### 40. [ORIGINAL-SYNTHESIS] The Metacognitive Overhead Problem *(Line 562)*

> [!original-synthesis] The Metacognitive Overhead Problem
> There is a tension inherent in the relationship between working memory and metacognition that has not been sufficiently foregrounded in the literature. Metacognitive monitoring — assessing whether one understands, identifying what one does not know, evaluating the quality of one's reasoning — requires working memory resources. But working memory is the very resource that is often most constrained during demanding cognitive tasks. The result is a catch-22: the situations in which metacognitive monitoring is most needed (high-load, complex tasks) are precisely the situations in which working memory is least available to support it. This suggests that effective metacognitive functioning depends not only on metacognitive knowledge and skills but on the degree to which primary task processing has been automated through [[Schema-Automation|schema automation]], thereby freeing working memory resources for the overlay of self-monitoring. The development of metacognitive competence may therefore be inseparable from the development of domain expertise — not because expertise teaches metacognition directly, but because expertise frees the working memory resources that metacognition requires.

#### 41. [CLAUDE-INSIGHT] Working Memory as the Common Currency of Cognition *(Line 567)*

> [!claude-insight] Working Memory as the Common Currency of Cognition
> Across domains — reading, reasoning, metacognition, self-regulation, problem-solving — working memory emerges as the common bottleneck, the shared resource that every higher cognitive function must draw upon and compete for. This observation suggests that working memory is not merely one cognitive system among many but a central clearinghouse through which all complex mental activity must pass. The practical consequence is that any factor that frees working memory resources — schema development, strategy instruction, environmental redesign, reduction of extraneous load — has the potential to improve performance across multiple cognitive domains simultaneously, because the freed resources become available for whatever cognitive function most needs them.

#### 42. [SECTION-SUMMARY] Section 8 Summary *(Line 570)*

> [!section-summary] Section 8 Summary
> - Working memory is the processing space in which reading comprehension, reasoning, and metacognitive monitoring are executed, and individual differences in WMC predict performance across all three domains.
> - Reading comprehension is constrained by working memory because it requires simultaneous text maintenance, inference generation, and situation-model updating.
> - Reasoning — deductive, inductive, and analogical — depends on working memory for premise maintenance, structural mapping, and interference resolution.
> - Metacognitive monitoring creates a "metacognitive overhead" problem: the monitoring operations that are most needed during complex tasks are precisely those that compete for the working memory resources already consumed by the task itself.
> - The development of domain expertise, through schema construction and automation, frees working memory resources for metacognitive and self-regulatory operations.

#### 44. [FAR-TRANSFER] Artificial Intelligence: Context Windows as Working Memory Analogues *(Line 588)*

> [!far-transfer] Artificial Intelligence: Context Windows as Working Memory Analogues
> Large language models (LLMs) operate with a fixed context window — a limited-capacity processing space that determines how much text the model can attend to when generating a response. This context window is a striking structural analogue of working memory: it is severely limited relative to the model's total "knowledge" (stored in its parameters, analogous to [[Long-Term-Memory]]), it constrains the complexity of reasoning that the model can perform in a single pass, and its effective capacity can be extended through chunking-like strategies (such as summarisation and structured prompting). The parallel is not merely metaphorical; it reveals that any information-processing system — biological or artificial — that must operate in real time faces a fundamental trade-off between the breadth of information it can hold in active processing and the depth with which it can process that information. Understanding this trade-off in the human case provides design heuristics for AI systems that face the same architectural constraint.
> 
> **See also:** [[Information-Processing-Theory]], [[Cognitive-Architecture]]

#### 45. [FAR-TRANSFER] Human-Computer Interaction: Interface Design and Cognitive Bandwidth *(Line 593)*

> [!far-transfer] Human-Computer Interaction: Interface Design and Cognitive Bandwidth
> The design of user interfaces is, at its core, a problem of managing the user's cognitive load — and therefore a problem of working memory management. Interfaces that present too many options, require the user to maintain too many intermediate states, or demand the mental integration of spatially separated information sources create precisely the conditions that overwhelm working memory. The [[Split-Attention-Effect]] translates directly into interface design: labels should be physically integrated with the elements they describe, related controls should be grouped together, and the number of simultaneously visible options should respect the capacity limit of approximately four chunks. Working memory research thus provides a principled foundation for interface design that goes beyond aesthetic intuition to engage the constraints of human [[Cognitive-Architecture|cognitive architecture]].
> 
> **See also:** [[Cognitive-Load]], [[Attention]], [[Selective-Attention]]

#### 46. [FAR-TRANSFER] Clinical Assessment: Working Memory Deficits and Neurodevelopmental Conditions *(Line 598)*

> [!far-transfer] Clinical Assessment: Working Memory Deficits and Neurodevelopmental Conditions
> Working memory deficits are a transdiagnostic feature of numerous neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric conditions, including attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), specific learning disabilities, autism spectrum conditions, and schizophrenia. In each case, the deficit manifests not merely as reduced storage capacity but as impaired [[Executive-Control|executive control]] — difficulty suppressing irrelevant information, switching between task demands, or updating representations in light of new input. Recognising working memory as the common substrate of these diverse conditions opens possibilities for targeted assessment and intervention that address the shared mechanism rather than the surface-level symptoms.
> 
> **See also:** [[Executive-Function]], [[Cognitive-Control]], [[prefrontal-cortex]]

#### 47. [FAR-TRANSFER] Organisational Management: Team Cognitive Load and Information Flow *(Line 603)*

> [!far-transfer] Organisational Management: Team Cognitive Load and Information Flow
> The concept of working memory capacity can be applied, by analogy, to teams and organisations. A team working on a complex project must collectively maintain, share, and update information about the project's state — a distributed working memory function. When the information flow exceeds the team's collective processing capacity — due to too many concurrent communication channels, too many interdependent decisions, or insufficient shared mental models — the team experiences a form of cognitive overload analogous to what an individual experiences when element interactivity exceeds working memory capacity. Strategies that work at the individual level — [[Chunking|chunking]] information, [[Schema-Automation|automating routine procedures]], reducing extraneous processing demands — have organisational analogues in standardised procedures, clear role definitions, and the use of shared documentation and dashboards.
> 
> **See also:** [[Element-Interactivity]], [[Cognitive-Load-Theory]]

#### 48. [DEFINITION] Working Memory (Baddeley & Hitch, 1974) *(Line 632)*

> [!definition] Working Memory (Baddeley & Hitch, 1974)
> A limited-capacity cognitive system responsible for the temporary maintenance and simultaneous manipulation of information in the service of complex cognition. Distinguished from [[Short-Term-Memory]] by its emphasis on active processing, attentional control, and multicomponent architecture.
> 
> **Boundary:** Not synonymous with short-term memory. Working memory includes executive control processes; short-term memory, as classically conceived, does not.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The foundational construct of this entire report — the cognitive workspace whose architecture constrains and enables learning, reasoning, and self-regulation.
> 
> **See also:** [[Working-Memory]], [[Cognitive-Architecture]], [[Executive-Function]], [[Cognitive-Load-Theory]]

#### 49. [DEFINITION] Phonological Loop (Baddeley, 1986) *(Line 641)*

> [!definition] Phonological Loop (Baddeley, 1986)
> A subsystem of working memory comprising a phonological store (decay period ~1.5–2 seconds) and an articulatory rehearsal process. Specialised for the maintenance of verbal-sequential information through subvocal rehearsal.
> 
> **Boundary:** Operates on sound-based codes only. Semantic processing requires central executive involvement.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The best-specified component of the multicomponent model, providing the clearest evidence for domain-specific subsystems.
> 
> **See also:** [[Phonological-Loop]], [[Maintenance-Rehearsal]], [[Modality-Effect]]

#### 50. [DEFINITION] Visuospatial Sketchpad (Baddeley & Hitch, 1974) *(Line 650)*

> [!definition] Visuospatial Sketchpad (Baddeley & Hitch, 1974)
> The working memory subsystem responsible for maintaining and manipulating visual and spatial information. Supports mental rotation, spatial navigation, and imagery. Contains dissociable visual (object features) and spatial (location/movement) subcomponents.
> 
> **Boundary:** Does not process verbal information; verbal-spatial integration requires the episodic buffer.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Demonstrates that working memory is multimodal, underpinning the modality effect in instructional design.
> 
> **See also:** [[Visuospatial-Sketchpad]], [[Dual-Coding-Theory]], [[Cognitive-Theory-of-Multimedia-Learning]]

#### 51. [DEFINITION] Central Executive (Baddeley, 1986; fractionated by Miyake & Friedman, 2000) *(Line 659)*

> [!definition] Central Executive (Baddeley, 1986; fractionated by Miyake & Friedman, 2000)
> The attentional control component of working memory, responsible for coordinating subsidiary systems, directing attention, inhibiting irrelevant information, switching between tasks, and retrieving information from [[Long-Term-Memory]]. Decomposed into three separable but correlated functions: updating, shifting, and inhibition.
> 
> **Boundary:** Not a storage system. Functions as a controller, not a container.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The locus of the "working" in working memory — the component that transforms passive storage into active processing.
> 
> **See also:** [[Central-Executive]], [[Executive-Function]], [[Executive-Control]], [[Miyake-and-Friedman]]

#### 52. [DEFINITION] Episodic Buffer (Baddeley, 2000) *(Line 668)*

> [!definition] Episodic Buffer (Baddeley, 2000)
> A limited-capacity multimodal store that integrates information from the phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, and long-term memory into unified episodic representations. Provides a mechanism for schema-based augmentation of working memory capacity.
> 
> **Boundary:** Not equivalent to [[Episodic-Memory]] in Tulving's sense. The buffer is a working memory component; episodic memory is a long-term memory system.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Resolves theoretical gaps in the tripartite model and provides the mechanism linking working memory to [[Schema-Theory]].
> 
> **See also:** [[Episodic-Buffer]], [[Schema]], [[Long-Term-Memory]]

#### 53. [DEFINITION] Chunking (Miller, 1956; Simon, 1974) *(Line 677)*

> [!definition] Chunking (Miller, 1956; Simon, 1974)
> The process of organising individual information elements into larger, integrated units (chunks) that each occupy a single slot in working memory. The number of chunks that can be held remains ~4; what varies is the informational richness of each chunk.
> 
> **Boundary:** A representational strategy, not a capacity expansion. Does not increase the number of available slots.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The primary mechanism by which expertise reduces effective cognitive load and the bridge between working memory constraints and schema theory.
> 
> **See also:** [[Chunking]], [[Cognitive-Chunking]], [[Schema-Automation]], [[Herbert-Simon]]

#### 54. [DEFINITION] Element Interactivity (Sweller, 1994) *(Line 686)*

> [!definition] Element Interactivity (Sweller, 1994)
> The degree to which learning elements must be processed simultaneously rather than sequentially in working memory. Determines [[Intrinsic-Cognitive-Load|intrinsic cognitive load]]. Relative to the learner's expertise: as schemas develop, previously interacting elements are integrated into single chunks.
> 
> **Boundary:** A relational property between material and learner, not an absolute property of material alone.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The mechanism by which working memory constraints translate into learning difficulty in cognitive load theory.
> 
> **See also:** [[Element-Interactivity]], [[Cognitive-Load-Theory]], [[Schema-Construction]]

#### 55. [DEFINITION] Embedded-Processes Model (Cowan, 1988; 1999) *(Line 695)*

> [!definition] Embedded-Processes Model (Cowan, 1988; 1999)
> A theoretical framework in which working memory is constituted by the activated portion of [[Long-Term-Memory]] plus a limited-capacity focus of [[Attention]] (~3–4 items). Dispenses with dedicated modular stores in favour of attentional processes operating on activated memory traces.
> 
> **Boundary:** Does not deny domain-specific processing differences but attributes them to LTM representations rather than dedicated architectural modules.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The principal alternative to the multicomponent model, providing the revised capacity estimate of ~4 items that has reshaped theoretical debate.
> 
> **See also:** [[Cowan's-Embedded-Processes-Model]], [[Nelson-Cowan]], [[Attention]], [[Working-Memory-Capacity]]

#### 56. [DEFINITION] Cognitive Load (Sweller, 1988) *(Line 704)*

> [!definition] Cognitive Load (Sweller, 1988)
> The total demand placed on working memory during a cognitive task, comprising intrinsic load (material complexity), extraneous load (presentation inefficiencies), and germane load (resources devoted to schema construction). The central construct of [[Cognitive-Load-Theory]].
> 
> **Boundary:** Cognitive load is not equivalent to task difficulty in the subjective sense. It refers specifically to working memory demand.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Provides the conceptual vocabulary for translating working memory architecture into instructional design principles.
> 
> **See also:** [[Cognitive-Load]], [[Intrinsic-Cognitive-Load]], [[Extraneous-Cognitive-Load]], [[Germane-Cognitive-Load]]

#### 57. [DEFINITION] Schema Automation (Sweller & Chandler; Anderson) *(Line 713)*

> [!definition] Schema Automation (Sweller & Chandler; Anderson)
> The process by which schemas, through repeated activation and practice, become executable with minimal or no demand on working memory resources. Frees working memory capacity for higher-order operations including metacognition and self-regulation.
> 
> **Boundary:** Automation applies to schemas that have been thoroughly practised. Novel or partially formed schemas still require conscious working memory resources.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The mechanism by which expertise transforms working memory's effective capacity and enables the metacognitive overlay discussed in Section 8.
> 
> **See also:** [[Schema-Automation]], [[Automaticity]], [[Procedural-Memory]], [[Self-Regulated-Learning]]

#### 64. [TENSION] Modular Stores vs. Activated Long-Term Memory *(Line 758)*

> [!tension] Modular Stores vs. Activated Long-Term Memory
> *Position A (Baddeley):* Working memory comprises dedicated, domain-specific subsystems — the phonological loop and visuospatial sketchpad — that are architecturally distinct from long-term memory.
> *Position B (Cowan):* Working memory is not a separate system but the currently activated subset of long-term memory, with a capacity-limited attentional focus.
> *Current state of evidence:* Both positions account for the core data. Neuroimaging evidence supports domain-specific processing regions (favouring modular accounts) but also shows deep integration with long-term memory networks (consistent with embedded-processes accounts). The debate may ultimately prove to be about the most productive level of description rather than incompatible empirical claims.
> *Why it matters:* The resolution determines whether working memory training should target specific subsystems or general attentional processes.
> *This report's stance:* The convergence noted in Section 4 suggests the models are more complementary than contradictory, with each capturing different aspects of the same underlying phenomena.

#### 65. [TENSION] Capacity as Storage vs. Capacity as Attention *(Line 765)*

> [!tension] Capacity as Storage vs. Capacity as Attention
> *Position A (Miller, Baddeley):* Working memory capacity is fundamentally a measure of how many items can be stored and maintained.
> *Position B (Engle):* Working memory capacity is fundamentally a measure of how effectively attention can be controlled in the face of interference.
> *Current state of evidence:* The correlation between complex span tasks and attention-demanding tasks strongly supports the attentional view. However, domain-specific storage effects (phonological similarity, word length) demonstrate that storage properties also matter.
> *Why it matters:* The answer determines the appropriate target of intervention: storage augmentation or attentional training.

#### 81. [PROTOCOL] Protocol: Assessing Working Memory Demands Before Instruction *(Line 917)*

> [!protocol] Protocol: Assessing Working Memory Demands Before Instruction
> 1. **Identify all elements** the learner must process simultaneously (not sequentially).
> 2. **Count interacting elements** — how many elements must be held in mind at once to understand the relationship?
> 3. **Estimate learner expertise** — can any of these elements be chunked using existing schemas?
> 4. **Calculate effective element interactivity** = total interacting elements − elements chunked by prior knowledge.
> 5. **If effective interactivity > 4:** Redesign instruction to reduce simultaneous demands (worked examples, completion problems, sequencing).
> 6. **If effective interactivity ≤ 4:** Instruction can proceed; consider adding germane load activities.

#### 82. [PROTOCOL] Protocol: Dual-Channel Presentation Design *(Line 925)*

> [!protocol] Protocol: Dual-Channel Presentation Design
> 1. Identify visual and verbal information components in the learning material.
> 2. Present visual information (diagrams, animations) through the visual channel → [[Visuospatial-Sketchpad]].
> 3. Present verbal explanation through the auditory channel (narration) → [[Phonological-Loop]].
> 4. **Avoid** presenting the same information through both channels simultaneously (redundancy effect).
> 5. **Ensure** verbal and visual information are complementary, not redundant.
> 6. **Synchronise** timing so that verbal and visual information refer to the same concept simultaneously.
> 7. Validate against the [[Modality-Effect]], [[Split-Attention-Effect]], and [[Redundancy-Effect]].
