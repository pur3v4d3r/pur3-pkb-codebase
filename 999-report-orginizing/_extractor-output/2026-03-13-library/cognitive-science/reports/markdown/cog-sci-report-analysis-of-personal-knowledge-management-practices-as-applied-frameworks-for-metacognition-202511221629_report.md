#### 5. [CORE-PRINCIPLE] Untitled *(Line 52)*

> [!core-principle] Untitled
> **The Mirror Principle of PKM**
> The structure of your external knowledge graph inevitably mirrors the structure of your internal semantic network. A fragmented, disconnected vault reflects fragmented, rote memorization, whereas a highly connected, dense graph reflects deep, conceptual understanding.

#### 6. [ANALYSIS-COGNITIVE] Untitled *(Line 60)*

> [!analysis-cognitive] Untitled
> **The Decision Cost of Linking**
> When a student utilizes [[Rote-memorization|Rote Memorization]], facts are stored in isolation. In contrast, [[Associative Learning]] through linking imposes a "desirable difficulty"—a term coined by Robert Bjork—that strengthens retention. The cognitive load required to determine *where* a note belongs and *what* it connects to effectively encodes the information into long-term memory.

#### 7. [KEY-CLAIM] Untitled *(Line 71)*

> [!key-claim] Untitled
> **Structure is an Emergent Property of Understanding**
> Rigid, top-down hierarchies (folders) often stifle metacognition because they force information into pre-conceived categories. Conversely, bottom-up structuring (emergent ontologies) requires the learner to periodically survey their atomic notes and synthesize them into higher-order clusters. This synthesis is the highest form of Bloom's Taxonomy: **Creation**.

#### 8. [INSIGHT] Untitled *(Line 85)*

> [!insight] Untitled
> **The Note as a Living Organism**
> Notes in a metacognitive PKM system are never "finished." They are snapshots of understanding at a specific point in time. The act of reviewing and updating a note—changing a link, refining a definition, splitting a concept—is the act of updating one's own mental model. This is **Accommodation** in Piagetian terms: altering existing schemas in light of new information.

#### 9. [EVIDENCE] Untitled *(Line 91)*
