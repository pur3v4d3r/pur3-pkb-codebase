#### 4. [DEFINITION] Untitled *(Line 50)*

> [!definition] Untitled
> **Personal Knowledge Management (PKM)**
> PKM is a conceptual framework and set of practices enabling individuals to take responsibility for their own learning and professional development. It involves the processes of capturing, classifying, storing, searching, retrieving, and sharing knowledge in daily activities (Pauleen, 2009). In its native context, it is primarily viewed as a logistical solution to the problem of [[Information Overload]].

#### 5. [CORE-PRINCIPLE] Untitled *(Line 56)*

> [!core-principle] Untitled
> **Core Principles of PKM in Information Management**
> 
>   * **Principle 1:** **Capture and Retrieval.** The efficacy of a PKM system is often measured by the speed and accuracy of retrieval, governed by the mathematical relationship between precision and recall, denoted in Information Retrieval theory as $$F_1 = 2 \cdot \frac{\text{precision} \cdot \text{recall}}{\text{precision} + \text{recall}}$$.
>   * **Principle 2:** **Atomicity.** Information should be broken down into discrete, atomic units (Zettels) to maximize modularity and reusability across different contexts.
>   * **Principle 3:** **Interoperability.** Data must exist in formats that allow for longevity and transfer between systems (e.g., plaintext Markdown), prioritizing the preservation of the data over the features of the tool.
