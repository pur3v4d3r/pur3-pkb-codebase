#### 15. [PHASE-ONE] Untitled *(Line 154)*

> [!phase-one] Untitled
> **Building from Principle 1: Explaining Systematic Redshift**
> 
> The first question is: what causes light wavelengths to increase systematically with the distance traveled? We can immediately eliminate several possibilities:
> 
> **Not a conventional Doppler effect:** The cosmological redshift is fundamentally different from a Doppler shift because Doppler shift depends on motion through space at the moment of emission, while cosmological redshift depends on expansion of space during the photon's journey. If galaxies were simply moving through a static background space, we would expect to see some blueshifted galaxies (approaching us) mixed with redshifted ones (receding). Instead, we observe that *all* sufficiently distant galaxies are redshifted. As the universe expands, the wavelength of light traveling through it is stretched, producing the observed redshift which is directly related to how much the universe has expanded during the light's travel time.
> 
> **The scale factor interpretation:** The Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric describes an expanding universe where redshift is determined by the changing scale factor $a(t)$, with the relationship $1 + z = a(t_{\text{observed}})/a(t_{\text{emitted}})$. The scale factor $a(t)$ represents how distances between points in space change over time. If $a(t)$ doubles between emission and observation, then $z = 1$—wavelengths double too.
> 
> This leads to a revolutionary interpretation: **space itself has a scale that changes with time**. Wavelengths stretch not because photons are doing work or losing energy in the conventional sense, but because the "ruler" by which we measure wavelength—the fabric of space itself—is growing. The proper interpretation is that cosmological redshift is a stretching of light caused by the stretching of space as time passes, with wavelengths stretching along with the expansion.
> 
> Consider the [[raisin bread analogy]]: imagine a loaf of unbaked bread dough studded with raisins. As the bread rises in the oven, the dough expands, making the space between raisins increase even though the raisins themselves remain stationary within the dough. If the raisins could see, each would observe all other raisins moving away, with distant raisins receding faster than nearby ones—precisely the pattern described by Hubble's law. Crucially, *no raisin is special*—every raisin sees the same pattern. There is no center to the expansion within the dough; the expansion is uniform throughout.
> 
> Mathematically, if the distance between two points in space is $d(t) = a(t) \times d_0$, where $d_0$ is their "comoving distance" (fixed coordinate separation) and $a(t)$ is the time-dependent scale factor, then the rate of change is:
> 
> $$\frac{dd}{dt} = \frac{da}{dt} \times d_0 = \frac{da/dt}{a} \times d$$
> 
> This is precisely [[Hubble's law]] ($v = H_0 d$), where $H_0 = \dot{a}/a$ is the Hubble parameter. The "recession velocity" is not motion through space but rather the rate at which the spatial separation increases due to expansion of space itself.

The observed temperature evolution therefore provides independent, compelling evidence that space itself is expanding.
