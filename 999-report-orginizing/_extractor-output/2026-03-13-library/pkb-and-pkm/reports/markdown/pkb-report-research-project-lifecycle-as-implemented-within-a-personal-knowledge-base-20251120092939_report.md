> - **Core Principle 1: The Principle of Atomicity and Conceptual Independence**
>     - At the foundation of all successful Personal Knowledge Base systems lies the principle of atomicity: each note should contain exactly one idea, expressed completely and independently. When an idea is isolated to a single note, it becomes a reusable building block that can be incorporated into multiple contexts without modification. This transforms the researcher's relationship with past work—instead of "I read something about this somewhere," the thought becomes "I have a specific note that addresses exactly this concept." The compound effect of thousands of such atomic notes is a knowledge base where ideas exist as discrete, addressable units that can be freely recombined. Luhmann's remarkable productivity stemmed largely from this principle: his notes were modular components that could be assembled and reassembled into articles and books without requiring significant rewriting. Each note was written with sufficient context to stand alone, meaning that when he needed to write about a concept, he already possessed a polished expression of that idea waiting to be incorporated.
