# Reflective Inquiry

> [!definition] **Reflective Inquiry**
> Dewey's account of genuine thinking as a specific kind of response to a problematic situation — what he called a "forked road" situation in which the habitual flow of action is disrupted by a felt difficulty. The five stages of reflective inquiry are: (1) a **felt difficulty** (awareness of a problem or confusion); (2) **location and definition** of the difficulty (clarifying what exactly the problem is); (3) **suggestion** of possible solutions (generating hypotheses); (4) **reasoning through** the implications of each suggestion; and (5) **further observation and experiment** testing the hypothesis. Crucially, Dewey insisted that genuine inquiry requires actual doubt — not performed or ritualized reflection, but a genuine encounter with uncertainty that motivates real cognitive work. Boundary condition: reflective inquiry applies to situations of genuine uncertainty; routine situations call for habit, not inquiry.
