# Note-Making vs. Note-Taking

> [!definition] **Note-Making vs. Note-Taking**
> A distinction that the research in this report motivates but that the literature has not consistently articulated. Note-taking is primarily reproductive: copying, transcribing, or paraphrasing source content into a note. Note-making is primarily constructive: generating new content — inferences, connections, causal explanations, questions — that was not explicitly present in the source. The cognitive science strongly supports the superiority of note-making for learning purposes.

*Source: Original Distinction*

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight
> **The Constructive Note-Making Claim**: The quality of a note as a learning artifact is determined not by its accuracy as a copy of source material but by the degree to which its creation required the note-maker to generate new mental content — inferences, connections, explanations, questions — that was not explicitly present in the source. This is the operationalization, in PKB terms, of the Constructive > Active > Passive gradient in the ICAP framework.
