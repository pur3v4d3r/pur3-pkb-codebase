## 📚 Cognitive Science Integration Standards

### Tier 1: Intermediate Depth Requirements

When presenting cognitive mechanisms:

**Required Elements**:
1. **Specific Brain Regions**: Name relevant neural structures (e.g., "prefrontal cortex," "amygdala," "anterior cingulate cortex")
2. **Neurotransmitter Systems**: Identify relevant chemical systems (e.g., "dopaminergic reward pathways," "serotonergic regulation")
3. **Cognitive Models**: Reference established frameworks (e.g., "dual-process theory," "working memory model," "predictive processing")
4. **Experimental Evidence**: Cite specific studies with methodology (e.g., "fMRI studies by Kross et al. (2011) demonstrated...")
5. **Effect Sizes**: Where available, include quantitative findings

**Example**:
```markdown
> [!evidence] **Neural Basis of Stoic Reappraisal**
> 
> Cognitive reappraisal—a core stoic technique—engages the [[dorsolateral 
> prefrontal cortex]] (dlPFC) and reduces [[amygdala]] activation (Ochsner et al., 
> 2002). fMRI studies show that when participants reframe emotional stimuli, 
> dlPFC activity increases while amygdala responses decrease by approximately 
> 30-40% (Buhle et al., 2014). This top-down regulatory mechanism involves 
> [[working memory]] systems in lateral PFC modulating limbic emotional responses.
>
> The underlying mechanism operates through predictive coding: the PFC generates 
> alternative interpretations that update emotional predictions, reducing 
> prediction error signals in the amygdala (Barrett, 2017).
>
> **Key Citations**:
> - Ochsner, K. N., et al. (2002). "Rethinking feelings: An fMRI study of the 
>   cognitive regulation of emotion." *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience*, 14(8).
> - Buhle, J. T., et al. (2014). "Cognitive reappraisal of emotion: A meta-analysis." 
>   *Cerebral Cortex*, 24(11).
> - Barrett, L. F. (2017). "The theory of constructed emotion: An active inference 
>   account." *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience*, 12(1).
```

### Tier 2: Accessible Translation Requirements

Following Tier 1 presentation, provide:

**Required Elements**:
1. **Clear Analogy**: Non-technical comparison
2. **Practical Implication**: "What does this mean for you?"
3. **Actionable Insight**: How to apply this knowledge

**Example**:
```markdown
> [!analogy] **Understanding Cognitive Reappraisal**
> 
> Think of your brain as having an "emotional alarm system" (the amygdala) and 
> a "control center" (the prefrontal cortex). When you practice stoic reframing, 
> you're essentially training your control center to turn down the alarm volume 
> by changing how you interpret situations.
>
> **Practical Implication**: This isn't just philosophy—it's a trainable neural 
> skill. Each time you successfully reframe a stressful situation, you strengthen 
> the neural pathways that make reappraisal easier in the future.
>
> **Actionable Insight**: Start with low-stakes situations (minor frustrations) 
> to build the neural habit before applying it to major stressors.
```
