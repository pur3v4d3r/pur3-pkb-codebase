*The Lord of the Rings* is an epic high-fantasy novel by J.R.R. Tolkien, published in three volumes between 1954 and 1955. Set in the richly detailed world of Middle-earth, it follows a diverse company of characters drawn into a world-altering struggle against a rising darkness. Originally conceived as a sequel to Tolkien's earlier work *The Hobbit*, it grew into something far larger in scope and depth — a work that effectively defined the modern fantasy genre and has remained one of the best-selling novels ever written, with over 150 million copies sold worldwide.

John Ronald Reuel Tolkien was a professor of Anglo-Saxon and English Language and Literature at Oxford University, a philologist of exceptional breadth, and the inventor of multiple complete constructed languages — most notably Quenya and Sindarin — which form the linguistic bedrock of Middle-earth's cultures and history.

At its heart, *The Lord of the Rings* explores the corrupting nature of power and the moral weight of even the smallest, most seemingly insignificant acts. Running alongside these darker threads is an abiding celebration of friendship, loyalty, and the quiet courage of ordinary people confronted with extraordinary circumstances.
