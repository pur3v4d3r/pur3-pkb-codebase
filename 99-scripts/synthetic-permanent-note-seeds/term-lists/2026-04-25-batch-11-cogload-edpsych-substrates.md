# Batch: Cognitive Load Theory & Educational Psychology Substrates

## The Evolution Of Germane Load

- secondary_domains: [cognitive-load-theory, instructional-design]
- broader: [cognitive-load]
- related: [intrinsic-load, extraneous-load, schema-acquisition, working-memory]
- prerequisites: [cognitive-load]

**definition**: The Evolution Of Germane Load is the historical re-positioning of "germane load" inside Cognitive Load Theory, moving it from a third additive category of working-memory load (alongside intrinsic and extraneous) to a description of the *learner's allocation of working-memory resources to schema-construction processes acting on intrinsic load*, rather than a separate kind of load to be measured or budgeted.

**key_claim**: The Evolution Of Germane Load resolved a measurement contradiction in the original triple-additive model — if all three loads sum to a fixed working-memory ceiling, manipulating one should predictably trade off with the others, but germane manipulations did not behave that way; treating germane processing as *re-investment of freed-up capacity* into [[schema]] formation made the theory internally consistent.

**warning**: Citing The Evolution Of Germane Load to claim that "germane load doesn't exist anymore" overstates the move; the construct was not eliminated, it was relocated — instructional designers still need to plan for whether learners will spend freed [[working-memory]] resources on productive schema-construction, which is precisely what the revised germane construct captures.

## The Componential Structure Of Working Memory

- secondary_domains: [cognitive-psychology, memory]
- aliases: [working memory components]
- broader: [working-memory]
- related: [phonological-loop, visuospatial-sketchpad, central-executive, episodic-buffer, baddeley-and-hitch-working-memory-model]
- prerequisites: [working-memory]

**definition**: The Componential Structure Of Working Memory is the multi-component model in which short-term cognitive workspace is decomposed into specialized subsystems — a phonological loop for verbal/acoustic material, a visuospatial sketchpad for visual and spatial material, a central executive that allocates attention and coordinates the slave systems, and an episodic buffer that integrates information across modalities and links to long-term memory.

**key_claim**: The Componential Structure Of Working Memory predicts dual-task interference patterns that a unitary short-term store cannot: two verbal tasks compete heavily, two spatial tasks compete heavily, but a verbal-and-spatial pair barely interferes — direct behavioural evidence for modality-specific resources rather than one shared buffer.

**warning**: Treating The Componential Structure Of Working Memory as a literal anatomical map is a common misreading; the components are functional roles inferred from interference patterns, and current neuroscience suggests overlapping neural substrates rather than cleanly dedicated brain regions, so the model's value is explanatory and predictive, not localizational.

## Baddeley And Hitch Working Memory Model

- secondary_domains: [cognitive-psychology, memory]
- aliases: [Baddeley-Hitch model]
- broader: [working-memory, the-componential-structure-of-working-memory]
- related: [phonological-loop, visuospatial-sketchpad, central-executive, episodic-buffer, short-term-memory]
- prerequisites: [short-term-memory]

**definition**: The Baddeley And Hitch Working Memory Model is the 1974 cognitive architecture that replaced Atkinson and Shiffrin's unitary short-term store with a three-component active workspace — central executive, phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad — later extended in 2000 by Baddeley with the episodic buffer to explain cross-modal binding and chunking with long-term memory.

**key_claim**: The Baddeley And Hitch Working Memory Model reframed short-term memory from a passive temporary store into an *active processing system* whose limits are not just capacity but also the coordination overhead of the central executive, which is why working-memory limits explain reasoning and comprehension performance better than digit-span ever did.

**warning**: The Baddeley And Hitch Working Memory Model is often confused with general "[[working-memory]]" as a folk term; the model is a *specific theoretical commitment* to component decomposition and rivals exist (Cowan's embedded-process model, Engle's controlled-attention view), so citing the model implies endorsement of those structural claims, not just the broad idea that short-term workspace is limited.

## The History And Evolution Of Cognitive Load Theory

- secondary_domains: [history-of-psychology, instructional-design]
- aliases: [CLT history]
- broader: [cognitive-load]
- related: [the-evolution-of-germane-load, schema, worked-example, evolutionary-educational-psychology, biological-primary-knowledge, biological-secondary-knowledge]
- prerequisites: [cognitive-load]

**definition**: The History And Evolution Of Cognitive Load Theory traces the development of CLT from Sweller's original 1988 work on problem-solving and goal-free problems, through the introduction of intrinsic/extraneous/germane categories in the 1990s, the integration with multimedia learning principles, the alignment with evolutionary educational psychology (biological primary vs. secondary knowledge), and the late re-conceptualization of germane load as resource allocation rather than a separate load type.

**key_claim**: The History And Evolution Of Cognitive Load Theory shows that the framework's predictive successes (worked-example effect, expertise reversal, redundancy effect) drove its theoretical revisions rather than the reverse — empirical anomalies forced the construct refinement, which is why CLT survived where many cognitive-instructional theories did not.

**warning**: Treating The History And Evolution Of Cognitive Load Theory as a unified, settled theory ignores ongoing internal disputes — measurement of load, the status of germane load, and the boundary between intrinsic and extraneous load all remain contested; "CLT says X" claims should be qualified by which historical layer of the theory is being invoked.

## Scientific Communication

- domain: research-methods
- secondary_domains: [philosophy-of-science, science-of-science]
- related: [scientific-method, peer-review, replication-crisis-in-psychology, evidence-evaluation, expertise-recognition]
- prerequisites: [scientific-method]

**definition**: Scientific Communication is the family of practices by which research findings are encoded, transmitted, evaluated, and integrated into the cumulative knowledge base — encompassing manuscript writing, peer review, conference presentation, preprint posting, replication reporting, and public-facing science communication — each with its own epistemic norms about what counts as evidence, attribution, and warranted confidence.

**key_claim**: Scientific Communication is not a transparent transport layer for findings but a *generative* process: the choice of journal, framing, statistical reporting, and figure design materially shape which results get cited, replicated, and built upon, which is why methodological reform efforts target communication norms (preregistration, registered reports) rather than just researcher behavior.

**warning**: Treating Scientific Communication as synonymous with "explaining science to laypeople" misses the harder problem — peer-to-peer scientific communication itself fails routinely (incomplete methods sections, p-hacking obscured by selective reporting, terminology drift across subfields), and these intra-scientific failures are upstream of the public communication problems that get most of the attention.

## Evolutionary Educational Psychology

- secondary_domains: [evolutionary-psychology, learning-science]
- broader: [educational-psychology]
- related: [biological-primary-knowledge, biological-secondary-knowledge, cognitive-load, explicit-instruction, geary-david]
- prerequisites: [educational-psychology, evolutionary-psychology]

**definition**: Evolutionary Educational Psychology is the framework, developed primarily by David Geary, that distinguishes between *biologically primary knowledge* — abilities humans acquire effortlessly through enculturation because evolutionary pressure shaped dedicated cognitive mechanisms (basic language, social inference, folk physics) — and *biologically secondary knowledge* — culturally invented domains (literacy, algebra, formal logic) that require deliberate instruction because no evolved mechanism supports their acquisition.

**key_claim**: Evolutionary Educational Psychology supplies the theoretical justification for [[explicit-instruction]] in formal academic content: because biologically secondary knowledge is evolutionarily novel, the brain has no module designed to acquire it incidentally, so discovery-based pedagogies systematically misallocate effort by treating secondary content as if it were primary.

**warning**: Evolutionary Educational Psychology is sometimes invoked to argue that *all* discovery learning is wasteful or that *all* direct instruction is superior; the actual claim is conditional on knowledge type, and within biologically primary domains (early language, social skill development) discovery-rich environments remain appropriate — the framework targets *secondary* content specifically.

## Explicit Instruction

- secondary_domains: [instructional-design, learning-science]
- aliases: [direct instruction]
- broader: [instructional-strategy]
- related: [worked-example, evolutionary-educational-psychology, biological-secondary-knowledge, cognitive-load, scaffolding, fading]
- prerequisites: [biological-secondary-knowledge]

**definition**: Explicit Instruction is the family of teaching approaches in which the structure, vocabulary, worked steps, and underlying schema of a target skill are *directly shown and named* by the instructor — through demonstration, worked examples, guided practice with immediate feedback, and gradual fading of support — rather than left for the learner to induce from problem-solving experience.

**key_claim**: Explicit Instruction outperforms minimally guided discovery for novices acquiring biologically secondary knowledge because novices lack the [[schema]] needed to filter the problem space, and unguided exploration loads [[working-memory]] with search rather than [[schema]]-construction; the meta-analytic record on this is unusually consistent across decades of comparison studies.

**warning**: "Explicit Instruction" is often misread as "lecture-only" or "teacher talks, students passive"; the actual specification includes high-frequency student response, immediate corrective feedback, and gradual transfer of responsibility — Explicit Instruction without active practice and feedback is just lecture, and lecture without practice does *not* show the same effects.

## Biological Primary Knowledge

- secondary_domains: [evolutionary-psychology, cognitive-development]
- broader: [evolutionary-educational-psychology]
- related: [biological-secondary-knowledge, language-acquisition, folk-physics, theory-of-mind, geary-david]
- prerequisites: [evolutionary-educational-psychology]

**definition**: Biological Primary Knowledge is the class of cognitive competencies — basic spoken language, face recognition, social-attention reading, intuitive number sense, folk physics — that humans acquire effortlessly across cultures because evolutionary pressure has built dedicated processing mechanisms for them, requiring exposure rather than instruction to develop.

**key_claim**: Biological Primary Knowledge is the implicit baseline that makes much of cognitive development look "automatic"; recognizing which competencies are primary clarifies why early childhood looks like spontaneous mastery in some domains while later academic learning requires sustained deliberate effort — the difference is evolutionary ancestry, not learner motivation.

**warning**: Labeling something as Biological Primary Knowledge is not a license to assume zero environmental input is needed; primary domains still require species-typical exposure (a child without language input does not develop language), and the "primary" label refers to the *type* of cognitive support, not the absence of any developmental requirement.

## Biological Secondary Knowledge

- secondary_domains: [evolutionary-psychology, learning-science, instructional-design]
- broader: [evolutionary-educational-psychology]
- related: [biological-primary-knowledge, explicit-instruction, cognitive-load, schema, worked-example]
- prerequisites: [evolutionary-educational-psychology]

**definition**: Biological Secondary Knowledge is the class of cognitive content — literacy, formal mathematics, scientific reasoning, formal logic, second-language grammar — that is evolutionarily novel, has no dedicated cognitive mechanism, and therefore requires deliberate instruction, sustained practice, and external scaffolding to acquire, even though humans use general-purpose learning machinery to do so.

**key_claim**: Biological Secondary Knowledge is the domain in which [[cognitive-load]] effects are most pronounced; because no evolved support exists, all the work falls on [[working-memory]] and [[schema]] construction, which is why instructional design choices (worked examples, fading, explicit instruction) have measurable effects on secondary content but largely vanish in primary domains.

**warning**: Biological Secondary Knowledge does not mean "harder" or "more important" knowledge in any normative sense; it just means evolutionarily unsupported, and treating the primary/secondary boundary as a value judgement (rather than a cognitive-architecture fact) leads to misapplied pedagogical prescriptions.

## Domain Specific Knowledge

- secondary_domains: [cognitive-science, expertise-research]
- broader: [knowledge-types]
- related: [domain-general-knowledge, schema, expertise, transfer-of-learning, biological-secondary-knowledge]
- prerequisites: [schema]

**definition**: Domain Specific Knowledge is content tied to a particular subject area — the facts, procedures, conventions, and conceptual schemas of, say, chess, organic chemistry, or constitutional law — that does not transfer freely to unrelated domains because its structure, vocabulary, and inference patterns are local to that subject.

**key_claim**: Domain Specific Knowledge is the largest predictor of expert performance within a field, dwarfing general intelligence and domain-general strategies once a threshold of practice is met; this is why subject-matter [[schema]] density, not general "thinking skills" training, is the reliable lever for advancing competence in any specific domain.

**warning**: The dominance of Domain Specific Knowledge is sometimes overgeneralized into "transfer never happens"; transfer is rare and conditional rather than absent, and shared deep structure (analogical mapping, abstract principles) can produce real if modest cross-domain transfer when explicitly bridged — the point is that general-skills training without domain content is a poor bet, not that transfer is impossible.

## Research Methods In Education

- domain: research-methods
- secondary_domains: [educational-psychology, learning-science]
- related: [randomized-controlled-trial, quasi-experiment, design-based-research, replication-crisis-in-psychology, educational-assessment, learning-analytics]
- prerequisites: [scientific-method]

**definition**: Research Methods In Education is the methodological landscape covering how educational claims are tested — encompassing randomized controlled trials of interventions, quasi-experiments where randomization is infeasible, design-based research that iteratively refines instructional artifacts in classrooms, qualitative studies of teaching practice, and large-scale observational studies leveraging assessment and learning-analytics data.

**key_claim**: Research Methods In Education must contend with constraints physics and biology rarely face — non-random assignment is the norm, intervention fidelity is hard to verify, teacher and context effects rival treatment effects in size, and ethical limits forbid many comparisons — which is why methodological pluralism is principled, not lazy, in the field.

**warning**: Importing the "gold standard" rhetoric of medical RCTs into Research Methods In Education without adjustment is a known failure mode; the question of *whether* an intervention works rarely matters in education without knowing *for whom*, *under what implementation conditions*, and *at what dosage*, which RCTs alone are poorly equipped to answer.

## Replication Crisis In Psychology

- domain: research-methods
- secondary_domains: [philosophy-of-science, meta-science]
- aliases: [replication crisis]
- broader: [meta-science]
- related: [research-methods-in-education, p-hacking, preregistration, registered-reports, scientific-communication, publication-bias]
- prerequisites: [scientific-method]

**definition**: The Replication Crisis In Psychology is the empirical finding that a substantial fraction of published psychological findings — including landmark, textbook results — fail to replicate when independent teams repeat the studies with adequate power and faithful protocols, exposed most starkly by large-scale projects (Open Science Collaboration 2015, Many Labs) that replicated under 40% of high-profile effects.

**key_claim**: The Replication Crisis In Psychology was not principally caused by outright fraud but by structural features of the field — small samples, flexible analytic pipelines, publication bias against null results, and HARKing (hypothesizing after results known) — which is why methodological reforms (preregistration, registered reports, open data) target the *system* rather than individual researchers.

**warning**: The Replication Crisis In Psychology is sometimes used to dismiss psychology wholesale; the actual lesson is more discriminating — well-powered, preregistered, mechanism-rich findings replicate at much higher rates, and rejecting psychology because of the crisis is symmetrical to the original error of overweighting any single underpowered study.

## Developmental Psychology

- secondary_domains: [psychology, cognitive-development]
- broader: [psychology]
- related: [piaget, vygotsky, scaffolding, zone-of-proximal-development, language-acquisition, theory-of-mind, attachment-theory]
- prerequisites: [psychology]

**definition**: Developmental Psychology is the subfield concerned with how cognitive, emotional, social, and motor capacities change across the lifespan — from prenatal development through old age — through the interplay of biological maturation, environmental input, and the learner's own activity, drawing on Piaget's stage theory, Vygotsky's sociocultural account, information-processing approaches, and contemporary neurodevelopmental data.

**key_claim**: Developmental Psychology established that learners are not miniature adults — children's reasoning errors are not random noise but reflect coherent if non-adult cognitive structures — and this constructivist insight is what underwrites age-appropriate instructional design, scaffolding decisions, and the ZPD concept that pervades modern pedagogy.

**warning**: Treating Developmental Psychology's stage theories as strict timetables is a common misreading; modern evidence shows substantial within-age variability and domain-specificity (a child can reason at "concrete operational" level in one domain and "preoperational" in another), so age stages are coarse statistical regularities, not deterministic milestones.

## Educational Assessment

- secondary_domains: [educational-psychology, measurement, psychometrics]
- broader: [assessment]
- related: [formative-assessment, summative-assessment, validity, reliability, learning-analytics, retrieval-practice, testing-effect]
- prerequisites: [psychometrics]

**definition**: Educational Assessment is the systematic process of gathering evidence about what learners know and can do — through tests, performance tasks, portfolios, observations, and process traces — and interpreting that evidence against intended learning outcomes, encompassing both *formative* assessment that informs ongoing instruction and *summative* assessment that certifies attainment.

**key_claim**: Educational Assessment is itself an instructional intervention, not merely a measurement instrument; the act of being tested produces the [[testing-effect]] that strengthens retention, and the form of assessment shapes what learners attend to and rehearse — meaning assessment design is curriculum design by another name.

**warning**: Reducing Educational Assessment to "testing" obscures the validity question that does the real work — a perfectly reliable test of the wrong construct is worse than a noisy test of the right one — and high-stakes summative regimes routinely degrade the construct they measure by inducing teaching-to-the-test behaviors that hollow out the underlying competence.

## Learning Analytics

- secondary_domains: [educational-data-science, educational-assessment]
- broader: [educational-data-science]
- related: [educational-assessment, formative-assessment, learning-management-system, data-mining, ethics-of-educational-data]
- prerequisites: [educational-assessment]

**definition**: Learning Analytics is the measurement, collection, analysis, and reporting of data about learners and their contexts — keystrokes, click-streams, time-on-task, assessment patterns, discussion-forum behavior — for the purpose of understanding and optimizing learning and the environments in which it occurs, drawing on data-mining, statistical, and visualization techniques.

**key_claim**: Learning Analytics promises a tight feedback loop between behavioural trace data and instructional adjustment that traditional assessment cannot, but realizing that promise requires *theoretical* models of learning to interpret the data; without theory, learning-analytics dashboards surface correlations that are descriptively rich and prescriptively useless.

**warning**: Learning Analytics raises ethical questions — surveillance of learners, algorithmic profiling that shapes opportunity, false precision masking measurement noise — that are not solved by adding privacy policies; the more accurate the analytics, the more weight individual decisions carry, which is precisely when measurement error and model bias become consequential rather than academic.

## Multimedia Learning

- secondary_domains: [instructional-design, cognitive-load-theory]
- aliases: [Mayer multimedia principles]
- broader: [instructional-design]
- related: [cognitive-load, dual-coding-theory, modality-effect, redundancy-effect, signaling-principle, mayer-richard]
- prerequisites: [cognitive-load]

**definition**: Multimedia Learning is the field, developed primarily by Richard Mayer, that studies how people learn from combinations of words and pictures — text plus diagrams, narration plus animation — and identifies the design principles (multimedia, modality, redundancy, signaling, coherence, contiguity) that align presentations with the dual-channel, limited-capacity, active-processing constraints of human cognition.

**key_claim**: Multimedia Learning shows that adding more information is not neutral: redundant on-screen text alongside narration *decreases* learning compared to narration alone, because both feed into the same verbal channel and create [[cognitive-load]] — a non-obvious result that undermines the intuition that "more channels = better explanation."

**warning**: Multimedia Learning principles are population-level effects measured against novices; they reverse for experts (the [[expertise-reversal-effect]]), so a multimedia design optimized for beginners can actively hurt advanced learners, and applying Mayer's principles uniformly across a course's expertise range is a documented failure mode.

## Analytical Frameworks

- domain: epistemology
- secondary_domains: [research-methods, critical-thinking]
- aliases: [conceptual frameworks]
- broader: [thinking-tools]
- related: [theoretical-framework, mental-model, conceptual-scheme, decision-framework, systems-thinking]
- prerequisites: [conceptual-frameworks]

**definition**: Analytical Frameworks are explicit conceptual structures — sets of categories, relations, and inferential rules — that an analyst imposes on a problem to render it tractable: PESTEL for environment scanning, SWOT for strategic position, Porter's Five Forces for industry analysis, Toulmin's argument model for reasoning structure, the OODA loop for decision contexts.

**key_claim**: Analytical Frameworks earn their value through *constraint*: by deciding in advance what categories are admissible and what relations matter, a framework prevents the analyst from drifting through the problem space and surfaces gaps that ad-hoc reasoning would silently skip — which is why framework choice is itself a substantive analytic act, not a presentational one.

**warning**: Analytical Frameworks become liabilities when they ossify into checklists; the moment "did we apply the framework?" replaces "does the framework illuminate this case?" the framework is generating false confidence rather than insight, and seasoned analysts treat frameworks as scaffolds to be abandoned once the problem's actual structure becomes visible.
