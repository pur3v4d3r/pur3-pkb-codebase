# Batch: Universe 01 — Cosmology Core

## Big Bang Theory

- secondary_domains: [astrophysics, theoretical-physics]
- aliases: [Big Bang model, hot Big Bang]
- broader: [physical cosmology]
- related: [cosmic microwave background radiation, expansion of the universe, inflation, big bang nucleosynthesis]
- prerequisites: [general relativity, redshift]

**definition**: The Big Bang Theory is the prevailing cosmological model in which the observable universe began roughly 13.8 billion years ago in an extremely hot, dense state and has been expanding and cooling ever since, with this evolution governed by general relativity applied to a homogeneous and isotropic matter–energy distribution.

**key_claim**: The Big Bang Theory is supported by three independent pillars of evidence — the Hubble–Lemaître expansion, the blackbody spectrum of the cosmic microwave background, and the observed primordial abundances of light elements — which together rule out every steady-state alternative ever seriously proposed.

**warning**: The Big Bang Theory does not describe an explosion of matter into pre-existing space; it describes the expansion of space itself, and the term "explosion" is a popular-science metaphor that misleads students into looking for a centre that the model does not posit.

## Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation

- secondary_domains: [observational-cosmology, astrophysics]
- aliases: [CMB, cosmic background radiation, relic radiation]
- broader: [cosmological observations]
- related: [big bang theory, recombination epoch, last scattering surface, planck satellite]
- prerequisites: [black-body radiation, redshift]

**definition**: The Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation is the nearly-uniform thermal radiation filling the observable universe with a present-day temperature of about 2.725 K, released when the cooling primordial plasma first became transparent to photons roughly 380,000 years after the Big Bang.

**key_claim**: The Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation is the most precisely measured blackbody spectrum in nature, and its tiny anisotropies (~one part in 100,000) encode the initial density perturbations from which all subsequent large-scale structure grew.

**warning**: The Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation is not light from the Big Bang itself but light from the much later recombination epoch; treating it as a snapshot of the singularity confuses two epochs separated by hundreds of thousands of years and many orders of magnitude in temperature.

## Dark Energy

- secondary_domains: [theoretical-physics, observational-cosmology]
- aliases: [dark-energy, cosmological dark energy]
- broader: [components of the cosmic energy budget]
- related: [cosmological constant, vacuum energy, quintessence, expansion of the universe, lambda-cdm-model]
- prerequisites: [general relativity, redshift]

**definition**: Dark Energy is the unknown component, currently inferred to make up roughly 68% of the cosmic energy density, that exerts a negative pressure and drives the observed accelerating expansion of the universe at late times.

**key_claim**: Dark Energy was forced upon cosmology by the 1998 Type Ia supernova measurements of cosmic acceleration; its existence (or whatever underlying physics replaces it) is required by the data regardless of which specific microphysical model is preferred.

**warning**: Dark Energy is a placeholder name for an empirical phenomenon, not a named substance — cosmological constant, quintessence, and modifications of gravity are competing explanations of the same observations, and conflating "dark energy" with any one of them prejudges an open question.

## Dark Matter

- secondary_domains: [astrophysics, particle-physics]
- aliases: [dark-matter, cold dark matter, CDM]
- broader: [components of the cosmic energy budget]
- related: [galactic rotation curve, lambda-cdm-model, gravitational lensing, large-scale structure of the cosmos]
- prerequisites: [gravity, general relativity]

**definition**: Dark Matter is the gravitationally-active but electromagnetically inert component, currently inferred to constitute about 27% of the cosmic energy density, whose presence is required to explain galactic rotation curves, gravitational lensing, the CMB power spectrum, and the growth of large-scale structure.

**key_claim**: Dark Matter is detected through five logically independent gravitational signatures spanning galactic to cosmological scales; this convergence makes it a robust empirical fact, even though the identity of its constituent particle (if any) remains unknown after decades of direct-detection effort.

**warning**: Dark Matter is not the same as dark energy, and the two are routinely confused in popular accounts; dark matter clusters and exerts ordinary attractive gravity, whereas dark energy is smooth and effectively repulsive on cosmological scales.

## Expansion Of The Universe

- secondary_domains: [observational-cosmology, general-relativity]
- aliases: [cosmic expansion, metric expansion of space]
- broader: [cosmological dynamics]
- related: [hubble's law, redshift, dark energy, big bang theory, scale factor]
- prerequisites: [general relativity]

**definition**: Expansion Of The Universe is the increase over cosmic time of the metric distance between unbound objects, encoded in general relativity as the time-dependence of the FLRW scale factor and observed empirically as the Hubble–Lemaître relation between redshift and distance.

**key_claim**: Expansion Of The Universe is a property of the spacetime metric itself, not the motion of galaxies through a static space; this distinction explains why distant galaxies can recede at apparent superluminal rates without violating special relativity, which constrains only local motion.

**warning**: Expansion Of The Universe does not stretch gravitationally bound systems — atoms, planets, stars, and galaxies do not expand — and reasoning that imagines the cosmic expansion driving local objects apart misapplies a global metric property to local dynamics.

## Gravity

- secondary_domains: [classical-mechanics, general-relativity]
- aliases: [gravitation, gravitational interaction]
- broader: [fundamental interactions]
- related: [general relativity, newton's law of universal gravitation, quantum gravity, gravitational waves]
- prerequisites: []

**definition**: Gravity is the universal attractive interaction between mass-energy that, in the Newtonian limit, follows the inverse-square law and, in the relativistic regime, is described as the curvature of spacetime caused by the local stress-energy tensor.

**key_claim**: Gravity is unique among the four fundamental interactions in being purely attractive, in coupling to all forms of energy (not just mass), and in being geometrised — described as the shape of spacetime rather than as a force exchanged between particles.

**warning**: Gravity is well described by general relativity in the classical regime and by Newtonian mechanics in the weak-field non-relativistic limit, but no consistent quantum theory of gravity yet exists; treating quantum gravity as a settled matter overstates the current state of physics by a wide margin.

## General Relativity

- secondary_domains: [theoretical-physics, mathematical-physics]
- aliases: [GR, Einstein's general theory of relativity]
- broader: [theories of gravity]
- related: [special relativity, einstein field equations, equivalence principle, curvature of space-time, gravitational waves]
- prerequisites: [special relativity, differential geometry]

**definition**: General Relativity is Einstein's 1915 geometric theory of gravitation in which gravity is identified with the curvature of a four-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian spacetime, and the matter–energy content of that spacetime determines its curvature through the Einstein field equations.

**key_claim**: General Relativity has passed every quantitative test attempted, from perihelion precession of Mercury and gravitational lensing through gravitational redshift, frame-dragging, the Hulse–Taylor binary pulsar decay, and direct detection of gravitational waves, with no confirmed deviation from its predictions on solar-system to cosmological scales.

**warning**: General Relativity is a classical field theory and breaks down at the Planck scale and inside black-hole singularities; treating it as the final word on gravity ignores the open problem of quantum gravity that all current programmes (string theory, loop quantum gravity) are attempting to solve.

## Hubble's Law

- secondary_domains: [observational-cosmology]
- aliases: [Hubble–Lemaître law, Hubble relation]
- broader: [observational consequences of cosmic expansion]
- related: [expansion of the universe, redshift, hubble space telescope, edwin hubble]
- prerequisites: [redshift, doppler effect in astronomy]

**definition**: Hubble's Law is the empirical relation v = H₀ · d, stating that the recession velocity v of a sufficiently distant galaxy is proportional to its proper distance d, with the proportionality constant H₀ — the Hubble constant — measured today at roughly 67–73 km/s/Mpc.

**key_claim**: Hubble's Law was the first direct empirical evidence that the universe is expanding rather than static, and the Hubble constant H₀ that appears in it is one of the most-measured numbers in physics — though present-day measurements from the early universe (CMB) and the late universe (distance ladder) disagree at the 5σ level, the so-called "Hubble tension."

**warning**: Hubble's Law is a local approximation valid only when relativistic and acceleration corrections are small; the simple v = H₀ · d form breaks down at high redshift, where cosmography requires the full FLRW formalism and the running of the Hubble parameter H(z).

## Inflation

- secondary_domains: [theoretical-cosmology, particle-physics]
- aliases: [cosmic inflation, inflationary epoch, inflation theory]
- broader: [early universe physics]
- related: [cosmic inflation theory, horizon problem, flatness problem, monopole problem, big bang theory]
- prerequisites: [general relativity, quantum field theory]

**definition**: Inflation is the hypothesised epoch of nearly-exponential expansion of the very early universe — driven by the potential energy of a scalar inflaton field — that lasted on the order of 10⁻³² seconds and stretched a quantum-scale region into the seed of the entire observable universe.

**key_claim**: Inflation simultaneously resolves the horizon problem, the flatness problem, and the monopole problem of the standard hot Big Bang, and it predicts a near-scale-invariant spectrum of adiabatic Gaussian primordial perturbations whose later imprint on the CMB has been confirmed by WMAP and Planck.

**warning**: Inflation is a paradigm with many concrete model implementations rather than a single specific theory, and definitive evidence for it (such as primordial gravitational-wave B-modes in the CMB) has not yet been observed; treating "inflation happened" as established at the same level as the Big Bang itself overstates its empirical standing.

## Multiverse

- secondary_domains: [theoretical-physics, philosophy-of-physics]
- aliases: [multiverse hypothesis, parallel universes]
- broader: [cosmological hypotheses]
- related: [inflation, anthropic principle, fine-tuned universe, string theory, many-worlds interpretation]
- prerequisites: [inflation, quantum mechanics]

**definition**: The Multiverse is a family of hypotheses in which our observable universe is one of many distinct cosmological domains, regions, or branches — generated, for example, by eternal inflation, the string-theory landscape, or the branching of the quantum wavefunction.

**key_claim**: The Multiverse arises naturally as a generic prediction of several independently-motivated theories (eternal inflation, string-theory landscape, many-worlds quantum mechanics), which is why physicists take it seriously even though no direct empirical test for the existence of other universes has been devised.

**warning**: The Multiverse is frequently presented as established science in popular media; in fact it is a contested theoretical hypothesis whose testability is itself disputed, and reasoning that uses it (for example to dissolve fine-tuning) presupposes both the multiverse's existence and an appropriate measure on it — neither of which is settled.

## Redshift

- secondary_domains: [observational-astronomy, spectroscopy]
- aliases: [cosmological redshift, z]
- broader: [spectroscopic shifts]
- related: [doppler effect in astronomy, expansion of the universe, hubble's law, gravitational time dilation]
- prerequisites: [doppler effect in astronomy]

**definition**: Redshift is the increase in the observed wavelength (decrease in frequency) of light from a receding or distant source, parameterised by z = (λ_obs − λ_emit) / λ_emit and produced by three physically distinct mechanisms: relative motion (Doppler), expansion of space (cosmological), and gravitational potential difference (gravitational redshift).

**key_claim**: Redshift is the principal observable through which cosmology is done — distance, lookback time, velocity, and gravitational potential are all inferred from it — and the cosmological redshift in particular is the operational definition by which "distance" to the most remote galaxies is even meaningful.

**warning**: Redshift is often described as a Doppler shift caused by galaxies "moving away," but the cosmological component is more accurately a stretching of light by the expanding metric while the photon is in transit; treating high-z redshift as a velocity gives wrong numerical answers (apparent v > c at z > ~1.4) and conceptually misrepresents the physics.

## Singularity

- secondary_domains: [general-relativity, theoretical-physics]
- aliases: [gravitational singularity, spacetime singularity]
- broader: [pathologies of general relativity]
- related: [black hole, big bang theory, schwarzschild metric, penrose-hawking singularity theorems]
- prerequisites: [general relativity]

**definition**: A Singularity in physics is a point or region where physical quantities (curvature, density, tidal force) diverge to infinity in a classical theory, marking the breakdown of the theory's predictive power; in general relativity the term refers specifically to a place where geodesics terminate after finite proper time.

**key_claim**: A Singularity, in the technical sense formalised by the Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems, is a generic and unavoidable feature of classical general relativity under physically reasonable energy conditions — it cannot be wished away by symmetry or initial-condition fine-tuning, only by abandoning classical gravity in that regime.

**warning**: A Singularity in general relativity is widely misread as an actual physical infinity at the centre of a black hole or the start of the Big Bang; the standard interpretation among physicists is the opposite — a singularity is a signal that the classical theory has reached its domain of validity, and a quantum theory of gravity is expected to replace the divergence with finite physics.

## Space Time

- secondary_domains: [special-relativity, general-relativity]
- aliases: [space-time, spacetime]
- broader: [structures of physical theory]
- related: [minkowski space-time, curvature of space-time, special relativity, general relativity]
- prerequisites: []

**definition**: Space Time is the four-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian manifold that combines the three spatial dimensions and time into a single geometric structure, in which the spacetime interval ds² = −c²dt² + dx² + dy² + dz² (or its curved generalisation) plays the role that Euclidean distance played in Newtonian physics.

**key_claim**: Space Time, as a unified entity, is the central conceptual innovation that allows special relativity to absorb and explain the Lorentz transformations and that allows general relativity to recast gravity as geometry — without it, both theories collapse into incomprehensibility.

**warning**: Space Time is sometimes presented as merely "putting time on the same footing as space," which understates the difference: the metric signature treats time with the opposite sign, the causal structure (light cones, past/future) has no spatial analogue, and treating time as just another spatial dimension produces wrong predictions.

## Standard Model Of Particle Physics

- secondary_domains: [particle-physics, quantum-field-theory]
- aliases: [Standard Model, SM]
- broader: [theories of fundamental matter]
- related: [quantum field theory, higgs boson, quark, lepton, boson, fermion]
- prerequisites: [quantum field theory, gauge theory]

**definition**: The Standard Model Of Particle Physics is the SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) gauge quantum field theory that describes the strong, weak, and electromagnetic interactions and classifies all known elementary fermions (six quarks, six leptons), gauge bosons (photon, W, Z, gluons), and the Higgs boson into a single internally consistent framework.

**key_claim**: The Standard Model Of Particle Physics is the most precisely tested theory in the history of science — its predictions agree with collider data to 10⁻⁸ in some channels — and yet it is known to be incomplete, with no place for gravity, dark matter, neutrino masses (without extension), or the matter–antimatter asymmetry.

**warning**: The Standard Model Of Particle Physics is sometimes described as "the theory of everything"; it is not — it deliberately excludes gravity and is silent on cosmological observations like dark matter and dark energy, and treating it as a complete description of nature is a category error.

## String Theory

- secondary_domains: [theoretical-physics, quantum-gravity]
- aliases: [string theory, superstring theory]
- broader: [candidate theories of quantum gravity]
- related: [m-theory, supersymmetry, multiverse, ads-cft correspondence, quantum gravity]
- prerequisites: [quantum field theory, general relativity, supersymmetry]

**definition**: String Theory is a family of theoretical frameworks in which the fundamental degrees of freedom are one-dimensional extended objects ("strings") rather than point particles, with different vibrational modes corresponding to different particle species and gravity arising automatically from the spectrum.

**key_claim**: String Theory is the most-developed attempt to unify quantum mechanics and general relativity in a single mathematically consistent framework, and it has produced major spin-off insights (AdS/CFT correspondence, black-hole entropy counting, holography) that have reshaped theoretical physics independently of whether the theory itself is ultimately correct.

**warning**: String Theory has so far made no experimentally verified prediction that distinguishes it from the Standard Model plus general relativity; calling it "the theory of quantum gravity" overstates its empirical standing, and the existence of a vast landscape of solutions ("the string landscape") makes its predictive power genuinely contested even within the theory community.

## Supernova

- secondary_domains: [stellar-astrophysics, observational-astronomy]
- aliases: [supernovae, SN]
- broader: [stellar explosions]
- related: [type ia supernova, core collapse supernova, neutron star, black hole, stellar nucleosynthesis]
- prerequisites: [stellar evolution]

**definition**: A Supernova is the catastrophic explosion of a star, divided into two physical classes — thermonuclear (Type Ia, from runaway carbon fusion in a white dwarf) and core-collapse (Types II, Ib, Ic, from the gravitational collapse of a massive star's iron core) — releasing on the order of 10⁴⁴ J and briefly outshining its host galaxy.

**key_claim**: A Supernova is responsible for synthesising and dispersing most of the elements heavier than oxygen in the universe, making supernovae the principal source of the heavy-element abundances on which planetary chemistry — and therefore biology — depends.

**warning**: A Supernova of Type Ia is used as a "standardisable candle" in cosmology, but the assumption of standardisability has known systematics (host-galaxy metallicity, progenitor channel diversity), and treating Type Ia distances as systematics-free is a recurring source of overstated precision in cosmological parameter estimates.
