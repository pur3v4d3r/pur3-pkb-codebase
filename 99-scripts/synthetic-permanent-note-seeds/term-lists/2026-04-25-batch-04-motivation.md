# Batch: Motivation

## Regulatory Focus Theory

- secondary_domains: [self-regulation, social-psychology]
- aliases: [RFT, promotion-prevention focus]
- broader: [motivational-psychology]
- related: [approach-avoidance-motivation, goal-orientation, self-determination-theory]
- prerequisites: [motivational-psychology]

**definition**: Regulatory Focus Theory is Higgins's account of how people pursue goals through one of two distinct self-regulatory orientations — a promotion focus oriented toward gains, ideals, and the presence of positive outcomes, or a prevention focus oriented toward safety, oughts, and the absence of negative outcomes — each with characteristic strategies and emotional signatures.

**key_claim**: Regulatory Focus Theory is not a re-labeling of approach-avoidance: the two foci differ in the type of end-state pursued (ideal vs. ought), the strategic means preferred (eagerness vs. vigilance), and the felt outcomes of success and failure (cheerfulness/dejection vs. quiescence/agitation).

**warning**: Regulatory Focus Theory is often collapsed into a personality typology, but Higgins's claim is that focus is partly chronic and partly situationally induced; treating any one person as purely promotion- or prevention-focused over-simplifies the theory's central regulatory-fit prediction.

## Procrastination

- secondary_domains: [self-regulation, behavioral-psychology]
- aliases: [academic procrastination]
- broader: [self-regulation]
- related: [temporal-motivation-theory, intention-behavior-gap, volitional-control, self-handicapping]
- prerequisites: [self-regulation]

**definition**: Procrastination is the voluntary delay of an intended course of action despite expecting to be worse off for the delay, distinguished from rational delay by the actor's own anticipation that postponement will produce a worse outcome than acting now would.

**key_claim**: Procrastination is most usefully framed as a self-regulation failure of emotion management rather than of time management: the immediate emotional aversiveness of the task drives the avoidance, which is why interventions targeting affect outperform interventions targeting scheduling.

**warning**: Diagnosing all delay as Procrastination conflates a self-regulation failure with strategic prioritization; the defining feature is the actor's own expectation of net loss from the delay, and missing this criterion produces both over- and under-diagnosis.

## Need for Cognition

- secondary_domains: [personality-psychology, social-cognition]
- aliases: [NFC]
- broader: [individual-differences]
- related: [intellectual-humility, dual-process-theory, cognitive-miserliness]
- prerequisites: [dual-process-theory]

**definition**: Need for Cognition is the stable individual-difference dimension introduced by Cacioppo and Petty that captures the extent to which a person enjoys and seeks out effortful cognitive activity for its own sake, with high-NFC individuals voluntarily engaging in deliberation that low-NFC individuals avoid.

**key_claim**: Need for Cognition predicts a wide pattern of consequential behavior — from message processing under the elaboration likelihood model to academic persistence and resistance to cognitive miserliness — by capturing the chronic disposition to deploy System-2 processing when System-1 would have sufficed for immediate action.

**warning**: Need for Cognition is sometimes treated as a proxy for cognitive ability; the construct is an enjoyment-and-seeking measure orthogonal to ability, and conflating the two erases the theoretically central distinction between can and wants-to think hard.

## Self-Concordance Model

- secondary_domains: [self-determination-theory, goal-pursuit]
- aliases: [self-concordance]
- broader: [self-determination-theory]
- related: [autonomous-motivation, internalization-continuum, goal-contents-theory, well-being]
- prerequisites: [self-determination-theory]

**definition**: The Self-Concordance Model is Sheldon's extension of self-determination theory to goal pursuit, holding that goals pursued for autonomous reasons (intrinsic interest or identified value) yield more sustained effort, greater attainment, and more well-being benefit than equivalent goals pursued for controlled reasons (external pressure or guilt).

**key_claim**: The Self-Concordance Model identifies a positive feedback loop in which autonomous goals produce attainment, attainment produces need-satisfaction, and need-satisfaction reinforces autonomous goal-setting — an upward spiral that explains why two people pursuing the same nominal goal can show divergent trajectories of persistence and welfare.

**warning**: The Self-Concordance Model does not imply that controlled motivation cannot produce attainment in the short term; the model's distinctive prediction is about sustained pursuit and well-being downstream of attainment, not about peak effort in any single episode.

## Situational Interest

- secondary_domains: [educational-psychology, motivation]
- aliases: [triggered situational interest, maintained situational interest]
- broader: [interest]
- related: [individual-interest, curiosity, hidi-renninger-model, intrinsic-motivation]
- prerequisites: [motivational-psychology]

**definition**: Situational Interest is the temporary, environment-triggered form of interest in Hidi and Renninger's four-phase model — typically catalyzed by novelty, surprise, complexity, or vividness in a learning task — and is distinguished from individual interest by its dependence on features of the immediate context rather than on the learner's stable dispositions.

**key_claim**: Situational Interest is the developmental gateway to enduring individual interest: stable interests originate as triggered situational interest that, with sustained engagement and felt competence, transitions into the maintained and then well-developed phases of the Hidi-Renninger sequence.

**warning**: Designing instruction around Situational Interest at the trigger level alone produces engagement that does not survive the removal of the triggering features; without scaffolding the transition to maintained situational interest, the gains evaporate at the end of the activity.

## Individual Interest

- secondary_domains: [educational-psychology, motivation]
- aliases: [personal interest]
- broader: [interest]
- related: [situational-interest, intrinsic-motivation, hidi-renninger-model, expertise-development]
- prerequisites: [motivational-psychology]

**definition**: Individual Interest is the stable, well-developed phase of interest in the Hidi-Renninger model — a relatively enduring predisposition to re-engage with a particular content domain over time, anchored by accumulated knowledge, value, and positive affect — and contrasts with situational interest in being a property of the person rather than of the immediate situation.

**key_claim**: Individual Interest is most usefully understood as the durable end-state of an interest-development trajectory rather than as a stable trait: well-developed individual interest is built, not discovered, and the building is mediated by the situational-interest experiences that preceded it.

**warning**: Individual Interest is sometimes invoked as a justification for "letting students follow their interests," but well-developed individual interest is rare in any given domain at the entry phase, and using its absence as a reason to avoid a domain forecloses the very experiences that would have produced it.

## Volitional Control

- secondary_domains: [self-regulation, action-control]
- aliases: [volition, action control]
- broader: [self-regulation]
- related: [implementation-intentions, the-rubicon-model-of-action-phases, intention-behavior-gap, procrastination]
- prerequisites: [self-regulation]

**definition**: Volitional Control is Heckhausen and Kuhl's term for the post-decisional self-regulatory processes — distinct from motivational deliberation — that protect a chosen intention from competing impulses, distractions, and rival goals, comprising attention control, emotion control, motivation control, and environment control.

**key_claim**: Volitional Control is causally distinct from motivation: a fully motivated person can still fail to act if the volitional sub-processes that shield the intention break down, which is why interventions that boost motivation alone systematically under-perform interventions that also strengthen the post-decisional shielding mechanisms.

**warning**: Treating Volitional Control as synonymous with willpower collapses a structured set of distinct sub-processes into a single resource metaphor and forecloses the more diagnostic question — which specific volitional sub-process is failing in this particular intention-behavior gap.

## Temporal Motivation Theory

- secondary_domains: [behavioral-economics, self-regulation]
- aliases: [TMT]
- broader: [motivational-psychology]
- related: [procrastination, expectancy-value-theory, hyperbolic-discounting, intention-behavior-gap]
- prerequisites: [expectancy-value-theory]

**definition**: Temporal Motivation Theory is Steel and König's integrative model that expresses the utility of pursuing a task as expectancy multiplied by value, divided by the product of impulsiveness and delay — yielding the prediction that motivation rises sharply as deadlines approach and falls when delay-discounting is steep.

**key_claim**: Temporal Motivation Theory unifies expectancy-value theory with hyperbolic-discounting work on procrastination, deriving the empirical procrastination pattern (effort concentrated near deadlines) as a direct consequence of how delay enters the utility function rather than as a separate phenomenon to be explained.

**warning**: Temporal Motivation Theory is a useful integrative model but is sometimes treated as a complete theory of motivation; it inherits the limits of its components — especially the assumption that expectancy and value are stably accessible to introspection — and over-stretching it into territory those components do not cover produces false precision.

## Self-Worth Theory

- secondary_domains: [educational-psychology, achievement-motivation]
- aliases: [self-worth motive]
- broader: [achievement-goal-theory]
- related: [self-handicapping, performance-avoidance-goal, fixed-mindset, ego-involvement]
- prerequisites: [achievement-goal-theory]

**definition**: Self-Worth Theory is Covington's account of academic motivation, holding that students are primarily motivated to maintain a sense of personal worth, that worth is widely equated with ability, and that this equation makes effort a double-edged sword — visible effort that produces failure becomes evidence of low ability, motivating self-protective avoidance strategies.

**key_claim**: Self-Worth Theory predicts the apparently paradoxical pattern in which capable students under-prepare or self-handicap before high-stakes evaluations: when failure-with-effort threatens the worth-equals-ability identity, withdrawing effort preserves the attribution of failure to circumstance rather than to lack of ability.

**warning**: Self-Worth Theory is sometimes read as endorsing effort-avoidance as rational; the theory describes a defense mechanism, not a strategy worth recommending, and interventions that detach worth from ability — by reframing ability as malleable — undercut the threat that makes the defense feel necessary.

## Approach-Avoidance Motivation

- secondary_domains: [neuroscience, personality-psychology]
- aliases: [approach motivation, avoidance motivation, BAS-BIS]
- broader: [motivational-psychology]
- related: [regulatory-focus-theory, performance-approach-goal, performance-avoidance-goal]
- prerequisites: [motivational-psychology]

**definition**: Approach-Avoidance Motivation is the broad theoretical distinction — instantiated in Gray's BAS/BIS, Elliot's achievement-goal valences, and Higgins's regulatory foci — between motivational systems that orient action toward desired end-states (approach) and systems that orient action away from undesired end-states (avoidance), each with distinct neural, affective, and behavioral signatures.

**key_claim**: Approach-Avoidance Motivation is a more fundamental dimension than valence of outcome: a positive outcome can be pursued via approach (move toward gain) or avoidance (move away from non-attainment), and the strategies, costs, and downstream affect differ systematically between the two even when the nominal goal is identical.

**warning**: Approach-Avoidance Motivation is sometimes mapped onto good vs. bad motivation, but each system has appropriate domains; chronic dominance of either at the expense of the other produces characteristic dysfunctions, and the theoretically interesting question is not which is better but which fits the situation.

## Utility Value

- secondary_domains: [educational-psychology, expectancy-value]
- aliases: [perceived utility]
- broader: [task-value]
- related: [expectancy-value-theory, intrinsic-vs-extrinsic-goal-contents, identified-regulation]
- prerequisites: [expectancy-value-theory]

**definition**: Utility Value is one of the four task-value components in Eccles's expectancy-value model — alongside intrinsic value, attainment value, and cost — and captures the perceived usefulness of a task for the learner's own future goals, distinct from inherent enjoyment or identity-relevance.

**key_claim**: Utility Value interventions are among the most reliably replicated motivational interventions in education: brief writing exercises that prompt students to articulate the personal usefulness of course material produce measurable downstream gains, especially for students with low initial expectancy, by routing the material into the learner's pre-existing goal structure.

**warning**: Utility Value interventions backfire when delivered as direct external persuasion ("here is why this matters to you"); the effect depends on the learner generating their own utility connection, which means scripted utility-arguments imposed from outside reproduce the very pressure they were meant to dissolve.
