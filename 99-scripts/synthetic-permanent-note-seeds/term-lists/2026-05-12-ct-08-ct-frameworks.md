## Facione Critical Thinking Model

- secondary_domains: [critical-thinking-pedagogy, assessment-research]
- aliases: [Facione model, Delphi consensus model of critical thinking, APA Delphi report model]
- broader: [critical thinking frameworks]
- related: [ennis critical thinking model, critical thinking dispositions taxonomy, watson glaser model]

**definition**: The Facione Critical Thinking Model is the consensus characterisation of critical thinking developed under Peter Facione's leadership for the American Philosophical Association's Delphi project, identifying interpretation, analysis, evaluation, inference, explanation, and self-regulation as the six core cognitive skills, paired with a set of dispositional components.

**key_claim**: The Facione Critical Thinking Model provides a tested operational vocabulary for both pedagogy and measurement; its dual structure — cognitive skills plus dispositions — addresses the recurring observation that skill alone does not predict actual reasoning behaviour without the underlying motivation to deploy it.

**warning**: The Facione Critical Thinking Model is sometimes treated as the definitive account of critical thinking, but it is one consensus product of one expert panel; alternative frameworks emphasise different decompositions, and adopting Facione's six skills as the framework forecloses without argument the choices that the panel itself debated.

## Ennis Critical Thinking Model

- secondary_domains: [critical-thinking-pedagogy, philosophy-of-education]
- aliases: [Ennis model, Robert Ennis model of critical thinking]
- broader: [critical thinking frameworks]
- related: [facione critical thinking model, critical thinking dispositions taxonomy, watson glaser model]

**definition**: The Ennis Critical Thinking Model is Robert Ennis's account of critical thinking as "reasonable, reflective thinking focused on deciding what to believe or do," operationalised through a taxonomy of abilities (clarification, basis-assessment, inference, supposition-and-integration) and dispositions oriented toward truth, clarity, and care for others' dignity.

**key_claim**: The Ennis Critical Thinking Model anchors critical thinking in decision-making rather than in argument-evaluation alone; this practical framing extends the construct from "evaluating others' arguments" to "regulating one's own beliefs and actions," which broadens the educational target.

**warning**: The Ennis Critical Thinking Model overlaps substantially with the Facione model in skill content but differs in the role assigned to dispositions and in the prominence given to action; treating the two frameworks as interchangeable obscures genuinely different commitments about what critical thinking is for.

## Critical Thinking Dispositions Taxonomy

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, assessment-research]
- aliases: [dispositions component of critical thinking, CCTDI dispositions list]
- broader: [critical thinking frameworks]
- related: [facione critical thinking model, ennis critical thinking model, truth seeking disposition]

**definition**: The Critical Thinking Dispositions Taxonomy is the structured inventory of standing tendencies — typically including truth-seeking, open-mindedness, analyticity, systematicity, self-confidence in reasoning, inquisitiveness, and maturity of judgement — that consensus models hold to be necessary complements to critical-thinking skills.

**key_claim**: The Critical Thinking Dispositions Taxonomy operationalises the empirical finding that skill possession does not by itself predict skill deployment; the dispositional inventory captures the affective and motivational layer that determines whether trained reasoners actually reason carefully outside testing conditions.

**warning**: The Critical Thinking Dispositions Taxonomy is operationalised primarily through self-report instruments (notably the CCTDI), which inherits the well-known limitations of self-report — social-desirability effects, weak correspondence to behaviour — and should not be treated as a direct measure of underlying disposition.

## Watson Glaser Model

- secondary_domains: [assessment-research, occupational-psychology]
- aliases: [Watson-Glaser Critical Thinking Appraisal, WGCTA]
- broader: [critical thinking frameworks]
- related: [facione critical thinking model, ennis critical thinking model, critical thinking dispositions taxonomy]

**definition**: The Watson Glaser Model is the conceptual framework underlying the Watson-Glaser Critical Thinking Appraisal — one of the oldest standardised critical-thinking instruments — which decomposes critical thinking into inference, recognition of assumptions, deduction, interpretation, and evaluation of arguments.

**key_claim**: The Watson Glaser Model has long served as the de facto industry standard for occupational selection in roles where reasoning quality is at a premium; its longevity reflects both genuine predictive utility and the inertia of institutional adoption rather than uncontested theoretical supremacy.

**warning**: The Watson Glaser Model treats critical thinking as a primarily cognitive ability and gives less attention to the dispositional layer that more recent frameworks emphasise; high scores on the WGCTA indicate test-validated reasoning capacity but predict actual real-world reasoning behaviour only modestly.

## Argument Reconstruction

- secondary_domains: [logic, informal-logic, dialectic]
- aliases: [reconstruction of arguments, charitable reconstruction]
- broader: [argument analysis]
- related: [argument structure, argument mapping, enthymeme, steelmanning]

**definition**: Argument Reconstruction is the practice of recovering the explicit and implicit premises, the conclusion, and the inferential structure of a piece of reasoning from its original presentation — typically prose — into a form suitable for evaluation.

**key_claim**: Argument Reconstruction is the prerequisite for nearly every other critical-thinking activity; one cannot evaluate validity, soundness, or relevance until the argument has been recovered from the rhetorical packaging it usually arrives in.

**warning**: Argument Reconstruction is governed by the principle of charity, which requires reconstructing the strongest plausible version the speaker could endorse; reconstructions that maximise refutability rather than fidelity produce critiques that are dialectically empty even when they are formally accurate.
