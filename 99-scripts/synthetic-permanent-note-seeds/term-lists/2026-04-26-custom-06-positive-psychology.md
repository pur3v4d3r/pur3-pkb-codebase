# Batch: Positive Psychology

## Affective Forecasting

- domain: positive-psychology
- secondary_domains: [decision-science, emotion]
- aliases: [emotional forecasting, hedonic forecasting]
- broader: [emotion-regulation]
- related: [hedonic-adaptation, peak-end-rule, focusing-illusion]
- prerequisites: [emotion]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Affective Forecasting is the cognitive process by which people predict the intensity and duration of their future emotional reactions to anticipated events, and the accumulated empirical record on its systematic biases.

**key_claim**: Affective Forecasting research shows that people robustly overestimate both the intensity and the duration of their future affective reactions — a phenomenon called the "impact bias" — because forecasts ignore hedonic adaptation and the cognitive immune system that buffers actual experience.

**warning**: Affective Forecasting failures cannot be repaired simply by warning people about the impact bias; the bias persists even when subjects are explicitly told about it, suggesting it is grounded in the simulation mechanism itself rather than in ignorance about its outputs.

## Grit

- domain: positive-psychology
- secondary_domains: [motivation, individual-differences]
- aliases: [perseverance and passion, Duckworth Grit]
- broader: [self-regulation, conscientiousness]
- related: [growth-mindset, conscientiousness, self-efficacy, hope-theory]
- prerequisites: [self-regulation]
- confidence: medium

**definition**: Grit is a personality construct introduced by Angela Duckworth defined as the trait-level combination of perseverance of effort and consistency of interest in pursuit of long-term goals, distinguished from short-burst motivation by its emphasis on stability across years.

**key_claim**: Grit is theoretically distinct from conscientiousness in that it specifically targets the consistency of long-horizon goals, but meta-analytic work has found Grit's incremental predictive validity over conscientiousness to be small, raising questions about whether it earns its status as a separate construct.

**warning**: Grit risks becoming a moralized explanation for life outcomes that are largely structural; framing achievement gaps as Grit gaps shifts attention away from material conditions and instructional quality and onto a personal trait that the same research suggests is only modestly modifiable.

## Hedonic Adaptation

- domain: positive-psychology
- secondary_domains: [emotion, decision-science]
- aliases: [hedonic treadmill, adaptation-level theory]
- broader: [emotion-regulation]
- related: [affective-forecasting, peak-end-rule, savoring]
- prerequisites: [emotion]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Hedonic Adaptation is the empirical regularity that affective responses to recurring stimuli — both pleasant and unpleasant — diminish over time as the experience becomes a baseline against which subsequent variation is judged, returning subjective wellbeing toward a stable set point.

**key_claim**: Hedonic Adaptation explains why major life events — winning a lottery, acquiring a chronic illness — produce smaller and more transient changes in long-run wellbeing than affective forecasts predict, because the adaptation mechanism is precisely what the forecaster fails to anticipate.

**warning**: Hedonic Adaptation is sometimes used to justify resignation to bad circumstances, but the rate and ceiling of adaptation differ markedly across stimulus types; chronic interpersonal stress, noise, and financial insecurity show much weaker adaptation than discrete life events, and the literature is misused when these distinctions are flattened.

## Hope Theory

- domain: positive-psychology
- secondary_domains: [motivation, clinical-psychology]
- aliases: [Snyder Hope Theory]
- broader: [motivation-theory]
- related: [self-efficacy, grit, psychological-capital, perma-model]
- prerequisites: [motivation, self-efficacy]
- confidence: medium

**definition**: Hope Theory, developed by C. R. Snyder, defines hope as a goal-directed cognitive set comprising two components: agency thinking (the perceived capacity to initiate and sustain action toward a goal) and pathways thinking (the perceived ability to generate routes to that goal).

**key_claim**: Hope Theory distinguishes hope from both optimism and self-efficacy by treating it as a content-specific goal-pursuit construct, in which agency without pathways produces frustrated motivation and pathways without agency produce passive planning.

**warning**: Hope Theory's measurement instruments correlate strongly with self-efficacy and conscientiousness, raising the question of whether the construct's incremental validity justifies its separate theoretical status; treating Hope Theory results as discoveries beyond what efficacy and trait conscientiousness already predict is methodologically risky.

## PERMA Model

- domain: positive-psychology
- secondary_domains: [wellbeing, applied-psychology]
- aliases: [Seligman PERMA, PERMA wellbeing model]
- broader: [wellbeing-theory]
- related: [psychological-capital, savoring, hope-theory, eudaimonia]
- prerequisites: [wellbeing]
- confidence: medium

**definition**: The PERMA Model, proposed by Martin Seligman, frames human flourishing as the cultivation of five distinguishable elements — Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, and Accomplishment — each of which is independently pursued for its own sake and jointly constitutive of wellbeing.

**key_claim**: The PERMA Model's central theoretical move is to deny that wellbeing reduces to subjective happiness; by treating engagement, meaning, and accomplishment as components rather than means, it provides a normative framework for interventions that improve flourishing without necessarily increasing positive affect.

**warning**: The PERMA Model's five components are derived from theoretical commitments more than from a rigorous factor-analytic separation; empirical work has questioned whether the elements are truly distinct dimensions of wellbeing rather than correlated facets of a general flourishing factor.

## Psychological Capital

- domain: positive-psychology
- secondary_domains: [organizational-psychology, applied-psychology]
- aliases: [PsyCap]
- broader: [positive-organizational-behavior]
- related: [self-efficacy, hope-theory, optimism, resilience]
- prerequisites: [self-efficacy, hope-theory]
- confidence: medium

**definition**: Psychological Capital is a higher-order construct in positive organizational behavior comprising four state-like resources — self-efficacy, hope, optimism, and resilience — proposed to jointly predict performance, satisfaction, and wellbeing in occupational contexts.

**key_claim**: Psychological Capital is theoretically defended as state-like rather than trait-like, which is its key practical commitment: if PsyCap is malleable, then targeted interventions on its four components should produce measurable performance gains, a prediction the intervention literature has partially supported.

**warning**: Psychological Capital research is concentrated in organizational settings where outcome measures are aligned with employer interests; generalizing PsyCap as a model of human wellbeing risks importing the workplace framing — productivity, engagement, performance — into life domains where those outcomes are not the relevant criteria.

## Savoring

- domain: positive-psychology
- secondary_domains: [emotion-regulation, mindfulness]
- aliases: [hedonic savoring, positive emotion regulation]
- broader: [emotion-regulation]
- related: [hedonic-adaptation, mindfulness, perma-model, gratitude]
- prerequisites: [emotion-regulation]
- confidence: medium

**definition**: Savoring is the deliberate cognitive and behavioral process of attending to, prolonging, and intensifying positive experiences, treating positive emotion regulation as an active skill rather than as the absence of negative-emotion regulation.

**key_claim**: Savoring counteracts hedonic adaptation by interrupting the automatic baseline-shift that erodes positive feeling: deliberate attentional engagement with a positive experience appears to slow the rate at which it ceases to register as positive against the new baseline.

**warning**: Savoring can paradoxically dampen positive affect when it shades into self-conscious monitoring; meta-emotional appraisal of "am I enjoying this enough?" tends to disrupt the experience itself, so Savoring practices need to be calibrated to attentional rather than evaluative engagement.
