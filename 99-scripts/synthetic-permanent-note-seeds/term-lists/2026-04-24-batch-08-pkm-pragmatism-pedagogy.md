# Batch: PKM, Pragmatism & Pedagogy

## Obsidian

- domain: personal-knowledge-management
- secondary_domains: [tools, software]
- aliases: [Obsidian.md, Obsidian app]
- broader: [pkm-tools]
- related: [zettelkasten, wiki-links, personal-knowledge-base, [[markdown], [[plugin-ecosystem]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: Obsidian is a local-first, markdown-based personal knowledge management application built around bidirectional wiki-links and a graph view, with a substantial third-party plugin ecosystem (Dataview, Templater, Tasks, QuickAdd, Meta-Bind) that extends the core app into a programmable knowledge environment.

**key_claim**: Obsidian's design choices — plain-text markdown files in a local folder, wiki-links as the primary connection mechanism, plugin-based extensibility — make it especially well-suited to long-horizon personal knowledge bases where data portability, longevity, and customisation matter more than collaboration features or polish, which is why it has become the dominant tool for serious Zettelkasten and PKM practitioners.

**warning**: Obsidian's plugin ecosystem produces real value but also a real risk: heavy plugin reliance creates lock-in to specific plugin behaviours and frontmatter conventions, and notes whose meaning depends on Dataview queries or Templater scripts are no longer just markdown — they are markdown-plus-implicit-runtime, which limits the portability that motivated choosing Obsidian in the first place.

## Zettelkasten

- domain: personal-knowledge-management
- secondary_domains: [note-taking, scholarly-method]
- aliases: [slip-box, Zettel method]
- broader: [note-taking-systems, personal-knowledge-management]
- related: [obsidian, wiki-links, [[luhmann], [[atomic-notes], [[evergreen-notes]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: The Zettelkasten ("slip-box") is the note-taking method developed by sociologist Niklas Luhmann in which each idea is captured as an atomic note, given a stable identifier, linked richly to related notes, and accumulated over time into an associatively-traversable knowledge base — a method credited with enabling Luhmann's exceptional scholarly output across decades.

**key_claim**: The Zettelkasten's productivity dividend comes not from any individual note but from the *network* of links: the same atomic note becomes more valuable as it acquires more links into more contexts, and the slip-box becomes a *thinking partner* whose unexpected juxtapositions surface ideas the author would not have generated by linear thinking — which is why Zettelkasten enthusiasts emphasise linking discipline over capture volume.

**warning**: The Zettelkasten can degenerate into "collector's fallacy" — accumulating notes whose mere existence is mistaken for knowledge — if practitioners do not actually *work* with their notes through writing, restructuring, and explicit linking; a Zettelkasten of ten thousand isolated atomic notes is less useful than one of two hundred well-linked notes the author actively reasons through.

## Wiki Links

- domain: personal-knowledge-management
- secondary_domains: [note-taking, hypertext]
- aliases: [wikilinks, internal links, double-bracket links]
- broader: [hypertext]
- related: [obsidian, zettelkasten, [[bidirectional-links], [[backlinks]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: Wiki Links are the double-bracket reference syntax (`[[target]]`) originating in the WikiWiki tradition and adopted by modern PKM tools (Obsidian, Logseq, Roam, Foam) to create bidirectional links between notes — where the target is created on first reference and accumulates *backlinks* from every note that points to it.

**key_claim**: Wiki Links, unlike conventional one-way hyperlinks, create *bidirectional* connections that make a note discoverable from every other note that references it; this property is what enables the emergent associative network of a PKM system, since the network is built incrementally as a side effect of writing without requiring an upfront table of contents or explicit topic taxonomy.

**warning**: Wiki Links are sometimes treated as a substitute for genuine conceptual organisation — a note dense with `[[term]]` mentions is mistaken for a note that has thought through how the linked concepts actually relate — but a list of bracketed terms is not an argument; the productive use of Wiki Links is to mark connections that the surrounding prose actually develops, and link-bombing without conceptual work degrades the network.

## Personal Knowledge Base

- domain: personal-knowledge-management
- secondary_domains: [knowledge-management, note-taking]
- aliases: [PKB, personal knowledge management system]
- broader: [knowledge-management]
- related: [obsidian, zettelkasten, [[second-brain], [[evergreen-notes], [[building-a-second-brain]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: A Personal Knowledge Base is an individually-owned, long-horizon repository of one's working knowledge — capturing ideas, source material, syntheses, and project-specific notes in an interlinked structure — and is the working medium of Personal Knowledge Management (PKM) practice as developed in the Zettelkasten, Building-a-Second-Brain, and evergreen-notes traditions.

**key_claim**: A Personal Knowledge Base differs from a notebook or document folder in being *interlinked, evolving, and reusable*: the same atomic concepts feed multiple projects, syntheses written for one purpose seed others years later, and the cumulative network produces leverage that linear file-and-folder organisation does not, which is why long-horizon scholars and writers increasingly treat PKB construction as a primary professional investment.

**warning**: A Personal Knowledge Base requires sustained investment in writing discipline, link maintenance, and periodic restructuring; PKB construction has a long-tail payoff curve in which the early months produce mostly costs (capture, formatting, linking) and the later years produce most of the benefits (reuse, synthesis, unexpected connections), and practitioners who abandon a PKB during the cost phase rarely realise its value.

## Note Making vs Note Taking

- domain: personal-knowledge-management
- secondary_domains: [study-skills, learning-science]
- aliases: [note-making vs. note-taking distinction]
- broader: [note-taking]
- related: [active-note-making, zettelkasten, [[generative-processing], [[encoding]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: The Note Making vs Note Taking distinction, popularised in PKM discourse by Sönke Ahrens and others, contrasts *note-taking* (transcription or summary of source material, often verbatim, primarily for later retrieval) with *note-making* (active reformulation of ideas in one's own words, reconnected to prior knowledge, primarily for thinking) — and treats note-making as the cognitively productive form.

**key_claim**: The Note Making vs Note Taking distinction maps onto the empirically established difference between shallow and generative encoding: notes that paraphrase, restructure, and connect new material to existing knowledge produce the encoding strength and retrieval cues that support durable learning, while notes that transcribe the source verbatim produce weaker memory traces and rarely support later reuse.

**warning**: The Note Making vs Note Taking distinction is sometimes preached as an absolute dichotomy in which note-taking is bad, but capturing source material accurately is sometimes necessary (for citation, for fidelity to nuance, for source-checkable extracts), and the productive use of the distinction is to distinguish *capture* (which can be verbatim) from *processing* (which must be generative), with both serving distinct functions.

## Active Note Making

- domain: personal-knowledge-management
- secondary_domains: [learning-science, study-skills]
- aliases: [active note-making, generative note-making]
- broader: [note-making-vs-note-taking]
- related: [note-making-vs-note-taking, zettelkasten, [[elaboration], [[self-explanation]]]
- prerequisites: [note-making-vs-note-taking]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Active Note Making is the disciplined practice of producing notes through generative cognitive operations — reformulating in one's own words, connecting to prior knowledge, identifying open questions, recording disagreements with the source — rather than through transcription or passive summary, and is the cognitive engine that makes a Personal Knowledge Base durable rather than archival.

**key_claim**: Active Note Making is the practical implementation of the elaboration and self-explanation interventions that the empirical learning-science literature has shown to substantially improve retention and transfer; the cognitive operations that make those interventions work — paraphrase, comparison, integration, question-generation — are precisely what Active Note Making routinely demands.

**warning**: Active Note Making is more cognitively demanding than transcription and is therefore reliably skipped under time pressure in favour of capture-only practices that *feel* productive but produce little durable understanding; sustaining Active Note Making requires explicit time allocation rather than fitting it into the residual after capture, and PKM systems whose design encourages capture without processing reliably starve Active Note Making.

## Pedagogy

- domain: education
- secondary_domains: [learning-science, philosophy-of-education]
- aliases: [theory of teaching]
- broader: [education]
- related: [hetogogy, [[andragogy], [[didactics], [[instructional-design]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: Pedagogy is the theory and practice of teaching — encompassing the principles, methods, and decisions by which an instructor structures learning activities, sequences material, scaffolds practice, and assesses progress — and traditionally refers to the teaching of children but is now used broadly across educational levels.

**key_claim**: Pedagogy as a field has accumulated substantial evidence about what reliably improves learning (spaced practice, retrieval, worked examples for novices, gradual fading of guidance, formative feedback, productive failure with appropriate scaffolding) — and a defensible Pedagogy is one that systematically deploys these evidence-based moves rather than relying on instructor preference or learner enthusiasm.

**warning**: Pedagogy is highly susceptible to fashion cycles (discovery learning, learning styles, "flipping the classroom," various technology-driven enthusiasms) whose adoption regularly outruns the supporting evidence; a serious Pedagogy treats pedagogical innovations as hypotheses to be tested in the local context rather than as commitments to be defended, which requires institutional discipline that is rarer than the rhetoric suggests.

## Hetogogy

- domain: education
- secondary_domains: [learning-science, adult-learning]
- aliases: [heutagogy, self-determined learning]
- broader: [adult-learning]
- related: [pedagogy, [[andragogy], [[self-directed-learning], [[hase-and-kenyon]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: Hetogogy (heutagogy), proposed by Hase and Kenyon (2000), is the theory of self-determined learning in which the learner takes responsibility not only for the *path* of learning (as in self-directed learning) but for *what is learned* — choosing the goals, the content, the methods, and the criteria of success — and is positioned as the developmental successor to pedagogy and andragogy along an autonomy continuum.

**key_claim**: Hetogogy describes the learning posture appropriate to mature, self-aware learners operating in domains of high uncertainty where the instructor cannot prescribe what should be learned because the relevant knowledge is emergent or context-specific; this includes much of contemporary professional development, advanced research training, and self-led PKM-supported study, which is why Hetogogy has gained traction as adult-learning contexts have shifted.

**warning**: Hetogogy presupposes substantial *capability for* self-determined learning — metacognitive skill, domain awareness sufficient to identify gaps, motivational regulation, evaluative discipline — and learners pushed into Hetogogy without these capacities reliably under-perform learners under more directive pedagogy; Hetogogy is a developmental endpoint, not a starting condition, and treating it as universally applicable harms the learners least equipped for it.

## Reflective Thinking

- domain: critical-thinking
- secondary_domains: [philosophy-of-education, pragmatism]
- aliases: [reflective inquiry, reflection]
- broader: [critical-thinking]
- related: [deweys-reflective-thinking, [[dewey], [[double-loop-learning], [[reflective-practice]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: Reflective Thinking, as theorised by John Dewey in *How We Think* (1910/1933), is the active, persistent, and careful consideration of any belief or supposed form of knowledge in light of the grounds that support it and the further conclusions to which it tends — distinguished from impulsive belief-formation and from routine habitual cognition by its disciplined, evidence-engaging character.

**key_claim**: Reflective Thinking, in Dewey's analysis, proceeds through identifiable phases — encountering a felt difficulty, locating and defining the problem, generating possible explanations or solutions, developing those by reasoning, and testing through observation or experiment — which provides a working model of inquiry that anticipates much later work in problem-solving, scientific reasoning, and reflective practice.

**warning**: Reflective Thinking is widely invoked in education and professional development as a goal but is rarely operationalised with the rigour Dewey specified; "being reflective" in practice often means writing personal-experience narratives without the disciplined evidence-engaging structure Dewey described, and the resulting reflective-practice literature has been criticised for confusing self-narration with genuine Reflective Thinking.

## Pragmatism

- domain: philosophy
- secondary_domains: [epistemology, ethics]
- aliases: [pragmatist tradition, classical pragmatism]
- broader: [philosophy]
- related: [pragmatic-maxim, fallibilism, deweys-reflective-thinking, [[peirce], [[james], [[dewey]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: Pragmatism is the philosophical tradition originating with Charles Sanders Peirce, William James, and John Dewey in late-19th and early-20th-century America that grounds the meaning and warrant of beliefs in their practical consequences for inquiry and action, treating knowledge as fallibilistic, inquiry as community-situated, and theory as instrumentally continuous with practice.

**key_claim**: Pragmatism's central insight is that philosophical disputes about meaning and truth are productively reframed as questions about *what difference would the dispute make in practice*; if no practical difference can be specified, the dispute is empty, and if practical differences can be specified, those become the criterion by which the dispute is settled — the move that motivates the pragmatic maxim and underlies pragmatist treatments of truth, meaning, and value.

**warning**: Pragmatism is regularly conflated with the colloquial "pragmatic" sense of "what works" or "what's expedient," but classical Pragmatism in Peirce's strict version is a *theory of inquiry* with substantive epistemological commitments to long-run convergence, community-based correction, and fallibilism — not an endorsement of doing whatever is locally convenient — and the colloquial reading produces persistent misreadings of what pragmatist philosophers actually argued.

## Pragmatic Maxim

- domain: philosophy
- secondary_domains: [epistemology, semantics]
- aliases: [Peirce's pragmatic maxim]
- broader: [pragmatism]
- related: [pragmatism, [[peirce], [[meaning], [[verificationism]]]
- prerequisites: [pragmatism]
- confidence: high

**definition**: The Pragmatic Maxim, formulated by Peirce in "How to Make Our Ideas Clear" (1878), holds that the meaning of a concept is exhausted by the practical consequences one would expect a thing falling under that concept to have — "consider what effects, that might conceivably have practical bearings, we conceive the object of our conception to have" — and is the methodological core of classical pragmatism.

**key_claim**: The Pragmatic Maxim is a *clarification rule* rather than a verification criterion: its function is to render abstract concepts cognitively manageable by tying them to expected experience, not to dismiss as meaningless any concept lacking direct empirical content; this distinction is what differentiates Peirce's Pragmatic Maxim from the later logical-positivist verification principle that it is sometimes wrongly assimilated to.

**warning**: The Pragmatic Maxim is famously susceptible to multiple non-equivalent formulations even within Peirce's own corpus, and James's, Dewey's, and contemporary neo-pragmatist readings differ further; treating "the" Pragmatic Maxim as a single doctrine misrepresents the family of related principles that pragmatist philosophers have endorsed, and substantive engagement requires specifying which formulation is being deployed.

## Fallibilism

- domain: epistemology
- secondary_domains: [philosophy, philosophy-of-science]
- aliases: [epistemic fallibilism]
- broader: [epistemology]
- related: [pragmatism, intellectual-humility, [[scepticism], [[peirce], [[popper]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: Fallibilism is the epistemological position — central to Peirce's pragmatism and prominent in Popperian and contemporary epistemology — that no empirical belief is immune to revision in light of further evidence or argument, even beliefs we are presently most warranted in holding, and that this in-principle revisability does not undermine warrant but is constitutive of responsible inquiry.

**key_claim**: Fallibilism is the position that resolves the false dichotomy between dogmatic certainty and corrosive scepticism: one can rationally hold a belief with high confidence while acknowledging that confidence could in principle be defeated by evidence, and the openness to defeat is what distinguishes Fallibilism from dogmatism, while the high-confidence holding is what distinguishes it from scepticism.

**warning**: Fallibilism is often equated with "I might be wrong about anything," but the productive form of Fallibilism is *calibrated*: one is more fallible about some beliefs than others, and the discipline is in correctly tracking which beliefs are well-warranted and which are weakly warranted — a discipline that requires substantive evidential engagement rather than blanket professions of fallibility that make no distinctions.

## Double Loop Learning

- domain: organisational-learning
- secondary_domains: [systems-thinking, management]
- aliases: [double-loop learning, governing-variable revision]
- broader: [organisational-learning]
- related: [reflective-thinking, [[argyris-and-schon], [[single-loop-learning], [[mental-models]]]
- prerequisites: []
- confidence: high

**definition**: Double Loop Learning, formulated by Chris Argyris and Donald Schön (1974, 1978), is the form of learning in which not only the *strategies* used to achieve goals but also the *governing variables* (goals, assumptions, framings) themselves are examined and revised — distinguished from *single-loop learning* in which strategies are corrected within unexamined goal frames.

**key_claim**: Double Loop Learning is the learning capacity that distinguishes adaptive from merely efficient organisations: single-loop learning improves execution within an existing model but cannot detect or correct a model that has become inappropriate to the environment, while Double Loop Learning is what allows the model itself to evolve — making Double Loop Learning the core competency for organisations operating in non-stationary domains.

**warning**: Double Loop Learning is hard to enact even when individuals are capable of it, because it threatens existing power structures, identity commitments, and sunk-cost investments in current models; Argyris's later work extensively documented "defensive routines" that systematically suppress Double Loop Learning at the organisational level, which is why Double Loop Learning capacity does not naturally propagate from individuals to institutions without explicit structural support.

## Deweys Reflective Thinking

- domain: philosophy-of-education
- secondary_domains: [pragmatism, critical-thinking]
- aliases: [Dewey's reflective thinking, Dewey's model of inquiry]
- broader: [reflective-thinking, pragmatism]
- related: [reflective-thinking, pragmatism, [[dewey], [[how-we-think], [[experience-and-education]]]
- prerequisites: [reflective-thinking]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Deweys Reflective Thinking refers specifically to the model of disciplined inquiry developed in John Dewey's *How We Think* (1910, revised 1933), in which thinking is the active resolution of a felt difficulty through phases of problem-definition, hypothesis-generation, reasoning-out of consequences, and empirical or experiential testing — the prototype for subsequent models of scientific reasoning, problem-based learning, and reflective practice.

**key_claim**: Deweys Reflective Thinking is the philosophical-educational source from which much subsequent work on critical thinking, scientific inquiry pedagogy, problem-based learning, and reflective practice descends; tracing those traditions back to Dewey reveals that they share a common ancestor and a common diagnosis of unreflective thinking as habitual or impulsive, and a common prescription of disciplined inquiry as the developmental remedy.

**warning**: Deweys Reflective Thinking is regularly invoked in education without engagement with what Dewey actually argued — his insistence on the *phasic* structure of inquiry, his rejection of dualisms between thought and action, his demand that reflection terminate in tested experience rather than in unverified narrative — and contemporary "reflective practice" literature is criticised for retaining Dewey's vocabulary while abandoning the disciplined-inquiry substance that gave it cognitive value.
