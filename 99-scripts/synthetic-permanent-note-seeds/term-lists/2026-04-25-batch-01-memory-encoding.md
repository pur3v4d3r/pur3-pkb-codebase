# Batch: Memory & Encoding

## Source Monitoring

- secondary_domains: [memory, metacognition]
- aliases: [reality monitoring]
- broader: [reconstructive-memory]
- related: [false-memory, episodic-memory, hindsight-bias]
- prerequisites: [episodic-memory]

**definition**: Source Monitoring is the cognitive process of attributing a remembered piece of information to its origin — whether it was perceived, imagined, told by another person, or self-generated — and is the mechanism by which the mind decides "where did I get this from?"

**key_claim**: Source Monitoring is not a passive lookup of a stored tag but an inferential reconstruction made at retrieval, which is why confidently-held memories can systematically misattribute origin while preserving content.

**warning**: Treating Source Monitoring as a binary "true vs. false memory" classifier obscures its real failure mode: high-confidence misattribution of origin without any felt subjective uncertainty, which means strong belief is not a reliable signal of correct sourcing.

## Transactive Memory Systems

- secondary_domains: [social-cognition, organizational-psychology]
- aliases: [TMS]
- broader: [distributed-cognition]
- related: [communities-of-practice, collective-efficacy, externalized-cognitive-architecture]
- prerequisites: [semantic-memory]

**definition**: Transactive Memory Systems are the shared cognitive division of labor by which members of a group encode, store, and retrieve information by relying on each other as specialized memory stores, with each person remembering who knows what rather than duplicating the knowledge themselves.

**key_claim**: Transactive Memory Systems shift the unit of analysis for memory from the individual to the dyad or group, and predict that team performance depends as much on accurate metaknowledge of "who knows what" as on the sum of individual knowledge.

**warning**: Transactive Memory Systems break down catastrophically when key members exit, because the remaining members have not stored the offloaded content — only the index; under-investing in shared documentation in the name of TMS efficiency creates fragile group expertise.

## Reconsolidation

- secondary_domains: [neuroscience, memory]
- broader: [memory-consolidation]
- related: [reconstructive-memory, sleep-and-memory-consolidation]
- prerequisites: [long-term-memory]

**definition**: Reconsolidation is the post-retrieval process by which a previously consolidated memory trace re-enters a labile, modifiable state and must be re-stabilized, providing a window in which the trace can be updated, strengthened, or weakened.

**key_claim**: Reconsolidation reframes long-term memory as periodically rewritable rather than permanently fixed once consolidated, which means every act of remembering is also an act of editing the trace being remembered.

**warning**: The Reconsolidation window is a real but fragile mechanism, not a license for the popular claim that "all memories can be rewritten on demand"; the conditions that open the window are narrow and overgeneralizing the finding distorts both the science and the therapeutic promises built on it.

## Context-Dependent Memory

- secondary_domains: [cognitive-psychology, memory]
- aliases: [context-dependent retrieval]
- broader: [encoding-specificity-principle]
- related: [state-dependent-memory, encoding-variability, cue-utilization-framework]
- prerequisites: [encoding-specificity-principle]

**definition**: Context-Dependent Memory is the empirical phenomenon that retrieval is more successful when the external environmental context at recall matches the context that was present at encoding, because contextual features become retrieval cues bound to the trace.

**key_claim**: Context-Dependent Memory implies that the boundary between "what is memorized" and "where it was memorized" is not crisp; the surrounding context is encoded as part of the trace, so manipulating context is a lever on retrieval, not a confound to ignore.

**warning**: Context-Dependent Memory effects are typically modest and easily masked when the to-be-remembered material is intrinsically meaningful or deeply elaborated; treating context-matching as a primary study tactic underweights the much larger gains from elaborative and retrieval-based encoding.

## Semantic Priming

- secondary_domains: [cognitive-psychology, psycholinguistics]
- broader: [priming]
- related: [spreading-activation, semantic-memory, processing-fluency]
- prerequisites: [semantic-memory]

**definition**: Semantic Priming is the empirical effect that processing a word or concept is faster and more accurate when it has been preceded by a semantically related word or concept, taken as evidence that activation spreads through a network of meaning-related representations.

**key_claim**: Semantic Priming provides the cleanest behavioral evidence for the network organization of semantic memory, demonstrating that meaning is stored as a set of typed relations between concepts rather than as isolated lexical entries.

**warning**: Semantic Priming effects are real but small and easily inflated by experimenter degrees of freedom; treating priming magnitude as a direct readout of "how connected" two concepts are in the mind ignores task-specific strategic processes that ride on top of automatic activation.

## Interference Theory

- secondary_domains: [cognitive-psychology, memory]
- aliases: [interference theory of forgetting]
- broader: [forgetting-curve]
- related: [proactive-interference, retroactive-interference, encoding-variability]
- prerequisites: [long-term-memory]

**definition**: Interference Theory is the account of forgetting which holds that loss of access to a memory is caused primarily by competition from other similar memories — earlier learning blocking later (proactive) or later learning blocking earlier (retroactive) — rather than by passive decay of the trace.

**key_claim**: Interference Theory is the strongest explanation for why time-since-learning predicts forgetting only weakly once the amount of intervening similar learning is controlled, displacing pure decay as the default model of long-term forgetting.

**warning**: Interference Theory does not entail that all forgetting is reducible to competition; cue overload, retrieval-induced forgetting, and consolidation failure each contribute, and reading any single forgetting event as "interference proved" overfits the framework.

## Sleep and Memory Consolidation

- secondary_domains: [neuroscience, memory]
- aliases: [sleep-dependent memory consolidation]
- broader: [memory-consolidation]
- related: [reconsolidation, slow-wave-sleep, rem-sleep]
- prerequisites: [long-term-memory]

**definition**: Sleep and Memory Consolidation refers to the body of evidence that sleep — particularly slow-wave sleep for declarative material and REM sleep for procedural and emotional material — is an active processing window during which recently encoded traces are stabilized, integrated with prior knowledge, and selectively strengthened.

**key_claim**: Sleep and Memory Consolidation reframes sleep from a passive recovery state into an obligatory cognitive operation, implying that the schedule of sleep around learning is a first-class variable of learning outcomes, not a hygiene factor.

**warning**: Sleep and Memory Consolidation effects are robust at the population level but are routinely overstated as "sleep makes you smarter"; the gains are specific, modest, and depend on adequate prior encoding — sleep cannot consolidate what was never properly learned.

## Encoding Variability

- secondary_domains: [cognitive-psychology, learning-science]
- broader: [encoding-specificity-principle]
- related: [context-dependent-memory, interleaving, desirable-difficulties]
- prerequisites: [encoding-specificity-principle]

**definition**: Encoding Variability is the principle that varying the contexts, modalities, and cue structures under which a single piece of material is studied produces a richer set of retrieval routes to that material, improving long-term flexibility of access at the cost of slower initial gains.

**key_claim**: Encoding Variability supplies the theoretical underpinning of interleaving and varied-practice schedules: the cost of changing context during study is paid in encoding effort but recovered as a gain in transfer-appropriate retrieval.

**warning**: Encoding Variability is sometimes invoked to justify any kind of variation, but the variability must remain meaningfully tied to the target concept; introducing irrelevant surface variation increases extraneous load without producing the multiple-route encoding that the principle predicts.

## Knowledge Compilation

- secondary_domains: [cognitive-architecture, expertise]
- aliases: [proceduralization]
- broader: [schema-automation]
- related: [automaticity, deliberate-practice, the-power-law-of-practice]
- prerequisites: [declarative-memory, procedural-memory]

**definition**: Knowledge Compilation is the process — central to ACT-R-style cognitive architectures — by which slow, interpreted declarative knowledge is gradually converted into fast, domain-specific procedural rules through repeated application, producing the speed and effortlessness characteristic of expertise.

**key_claim**: Knowledge Compilation explains why expertise development is non-substitutable by mere reading: the procedural-rule layer can only be constructed by actual rule-application, which is why "knowing about" a skill is causally distinct from "being able to" perform it.

**warning**: Knowledge Compilation produces speed and accuracy at the cost of flexibility; compiled procedures resist re-decomposition, which is why expert performance can be brittle when the task surface changes in ways the compiled rules cannot inspect.
