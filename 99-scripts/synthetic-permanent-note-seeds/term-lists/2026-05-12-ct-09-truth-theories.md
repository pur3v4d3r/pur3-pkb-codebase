## Coherence Theory Of Truth

- secondary_domains: [metaphysics-of-truth, idealism, philosophy-of-science]
- aliases: [coherentism about truth, coherence theory]
- broader: [theories of truth]
- related: [pragmatic theory of truth, deflationary theory of truth, epistemic theory of truth, reflective equilibrium]

**definition**: The Coherence Theory Of Truth holds that the truth of a proposition consists in its membership in a maximally coherent system of propositions — its logical compatibility with, and mutual support among, the other propositions in that system.

**key_claim**: The Coherence Theory Of Truth dissolves the worry about how truth-bearers (propositions) connect to a mind-independent world by relocating truth entirely within the relations among propositions; this explains how truth could be epistemically accessible but at the cost of severing it from correspondence.

**warning**: The Coherence Theory Of Truth faces the well-known plurality objection: incompatible total systems can each be internally coherent, so coherence by itself fails to single out a unique set of truths, and supplementary criteria smuggled in to fix this typically reintroduce the very correspondence intuition coherentism set out to avoid.

## Pragmatic Theory Of Truth

- secondary_domains: [American-pragmatism, philosophy-of-science]
- aliases: [pragmatism about truth, pragmatist theory of truth]
- broader: [theories of truth]
- related: [coherence theory of truth, deflationary theory of truth, epistemic theory of truth]

**definition**: The Pragmatic Theory Of Truth, in its various forms (Peirce, James, Dewey), holds that truth is to be understood through its connection to inquiry, action, or practical consequences — for example, as the ideal limit of inquiry, or as that which works in practice over the long run.

**key_claim**: The Pragmatic Theory Of Truth ties truth to the concrete practices of inquiry and action rather than to an abstract correspondence relation; this makes truth methodologically tractable and scientifically respectable but at the cost of conflating truth with success-in-inquiry, which are not obviously the same thing.

**warning**: The Pragmatic Theory Of Truth has highly disparate formulations across its founders; James's "what works" formulation is much more vulnerable to relativist readings than Peirce's "ideal limit of inquiry," and treating "the pragmatic theory" as a single unified position misrepresents an internally contested tradition.

## Deflationary Theory Of Truth

- secondary_domains: [philosophy-of-language, formal-semantics]
- aliases: [deflationism, redundancy theory of truth, disquotational theory]
- broader: [theories of truth]
- related: [coherence theory of truth, pragmatic theory of truth, epistemic theory of truth, truth aptness]

**definition**: The Deflationary Theory Of Truth holds that the predicate "is true" does not denote any substantive property; its function is logical and expressive — to assert content, to generalise over propositions, or to disquote a sentence — rather than to ascribe a metaphysically loaded relation.

**key_claim**: The Deflationary Theory Of Truth treats the apparent depth of the truth predicate as a grammatical illusion; once the logical role of "true" is properly understood, the traditional metaphysical question "what is truth?" largely dissolves, leaving only the question of how the predicate functions.

**warning**: The Deflationary Theory Of Truth is sometimes characterised as denying that anything is really true, but the deflationist position is the opposite — true sentences are exactly those described by the disquotation schema; what deflationism denies is not the application of the predicate but a substantive property behind it.

## Epistemic Theory Of Truth

- secondary_domains: [metaphysics-of-truth, epistemology, anti-realism]
- aliases: [epistemic conception of truth, anti-realist theory of truth]
- broader: [theories of truth]
- related: [coherence theory of truth, pragmatic theory of truth, deflationary theory of truth, epistemic relativism]

**definition**: The Epistemic Theory Of Truth holds that truth is to be analysed in terms of epistemic notions such as warranted assertibility, justification under ideal conditions, or knowability in principle, rather than in terms of correspondence to a mind-independent world.

**key_claim**: The Epistemic Theory Of Truth is the natural home for anti-realist positions in philosophy and the philosophy of science; by tying truth to what can in principle be warranted, it forecloses the possibility of recognition-transcendent truths that no finite inquirer could ever access.

**warning**: The Epistemic Theory Of Truth faces a classical objection from the asymmetry of truth and warrant: there appear to be true propositions that no one will ever be in a position to verify, and treating these as untrue (because unverifiable) is a substantial metaphysical commitment, not a clarification of an existing concept.

## Truth Aptness

- secondary_domains: [philosophy-of-language, metaethics, formal-semantics]
- aliases: [truth-aptness, truth-evaluability]
- broader: [properties of statements]
- related: [coherence theory of truth, pragmatic theory of truth, deflationary theory of truth, epistemic theory of truth]

**definition**: Truth Aptness is the property of being capable of being true or false — of being the kind of content to which truth-values apply at all — as distinct from contents (commands, expressions of attitude, performatives) that are not candidates for truth-evaluation.

**key_claim**: Truth Aptness is the prior question that has to be answered before any theory of truth can apply to a given discourse; whether moral, aesthetic, or modal statements are truth-apt is itself a substantive issue, and competing meta-ethical and meta-aesthetic positions are largely positions on this question.

**warning**: Truth Aptness is often presupposed without argument in debates about realism and anti-realism; treating moral or modal claims as obviously truth-apt (or obviously not) skips the very question that the surface grammar of those discourses leaves open and that subsequent metaphysical theorising depends on.
