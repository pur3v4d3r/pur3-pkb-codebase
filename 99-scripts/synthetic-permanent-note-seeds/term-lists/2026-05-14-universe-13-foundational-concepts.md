# Batch: Universe 13 — Foundational Concepts and Cosmological Epochs

## Speed Of Light

- secondary_domains: [special-relativity, electromagnetism]
- aliases: [c, light speed]
- broader: [fundamental physical constants]
- related: [special relativity, mass energy equivalence, photon, planck units]

**definition**: The Speed Of Light c = 299,792,458 m/s (exact, by 1983 SI definition of the metre) is the velocity of electromagnetic radiation in vacuum and the universal speed limit for all signal propagation, the conversion factor between space and time in special relativity, and the structural constant appearing in mass-energy equivalence E = mc².

**key_claim**: The Speed Of Light is invariant under Lorentz transformations — the same in every inertial reference frame — a postulate of special relativity confirmed in over a century of experimental tests including the Michelson–Morley experiment, modern Hughes–Drever-type tests of Lorentz invariance, and high-energy-cosmic-ray observations sensitive to even very small Lorentz violations.

**warning**: The Speed Of Light is conventionally described as "the speed of light"; in fact it is the universal invariant speed of all massless particles and the maximum signal-propagation speed in any inertial frame, with light merely happening to travel at this speed because photons are (effectively) massless — the framing as "the speed of light" obscures that c is a structural constant of spacetime independent of any specific particle.

## Planck Units

- secondary_domains: [theoretical-physics, quantum-gravity]
- aliases: [natural units, Planck system of units]
- broader: [systems of fundamental units]
- related: [planck length, planck time, quantum gravity, speed of light]

**definition**: Planck Units are the system of natural units constructed (Planck 1899) from the four fundamental constants c (speed of light), ℏ (reduced Planck constant), G (gravitational constant), and k_B (Boltzmann constant), yielding base units (Planck length ~1.6×10⁻³⁵ m, Planck time ~5.4×10⁻⁴⁴ s, Planck mass ~2.2×10⁻⁸ kg, Planck temperature ~1.4×10³² K) that contain no arbitrary anthropic factors.

**key_claim**: Planck Units mark the energy and length scales at which gravitational and quantum effects become comparable in magnitude — the regime where any quantum theory of gravity is required — and are the natural units of theoretical physics at the most fundamental level.

**warning**: Planck Units are sometimes presented as if they marked the universal "smallest possible" length and time; while heuristic arguments suggest spacetime concepts may break down at the Planck scale in any quantum gravity, the existence of a literally minimum length is a model-dependent prediction (loop quantum gravity makes it; perturbative string theory does not in the same way) rather than an established fact.

## Planck Epoch

- secondary_domains: [cosmology, quantum-gravity]
- aliases: [Planck era]
- broader: [cosmological epochs]
- related: [planck units, planck time, quantum gravity, big bang theory]

**definition**: The Planck Epoch is the period from the universe's origin to the Planck time (~5.4 × 10⁻⁴⁴ s), during which the energy density of the universe exceeded the Planck scale and a quantum theory of gravity (rather than classical general relativity coupled to quantum field theory) is required to describe the dynamics — placing this epoch beyond the reach of currently-formulated physics.

**key_claim**: The Planck Epoch is the only era in cosmic history where the formalism of general relativity is generally agreed to break down, and any account of the universe's earliest moment requires a successful quantum theory of gravity that does not yet exist; speculative scenarios (Hartle–Hawking no-boundary, loop-quantum-cosmology bounce, string-cosmology pre-Big-Bang) populate the Planck epoch with model-dependent narratives.

**warning**: The Planck Epoch is sometimes described as if its physics were "well understood" via heuristic Planck-scale arguments; in fact essentially nothing about the physics of this epoch is established, and treatments that present specific Planck-epoch dynamics as factual rather than speculative model-output overstate what is known.

## Planck Length

- secondary_domains: [quantum-gravity, fundamental-units]
- aliases: [l_P, Planck distance]
- broader: [planck units]
- related: [planck units, quantum gravity, planck time, holographic principle]

**definition**: The Planck Length l_P = √(ℏG/c³) ≈ 1.616 × 10⁻³⁵ m is the natural length scale constructed from c, ℏ, and G, marking the regime where gravitational effects become comparable to quantum effects and where a quantum theory of gravity is presumptively required to describe spatial geometry.

**key_claim**: The Planck Length is widely conjectured to be the scale below which the classical concept of smooth spacetime geometry breaks down, with various quantum-gravity approaches (loop quantum gravity, causal dynamical triangulations, asymptotic safety) predicting some form of discreteness or modification at this scale.

**warning**: The Planck Length is not established to be the literal "minimum length"; high-energy phenomenology constraints from cosmic-ray observations and precision interferometry (Holometer experiments) have placed bounds on Planck-scale modifications without definitive detection, and statements that "spacetime is discrete at the Planck length" are model-dependent conjectures rather than established physics.

## Planck Time

- secondary_domains: [quantum-gravity, fundamental-units]
- aliases: [t_P]
- broader: [planck units]
- related: [planck units, planck length, planck epoch, quantum gravity]

**definition**: The Planck Time t_P = √(ℏG/c⁵) ≈ 5.391 × 10⁻⁴⁴ s is the natural time scale constructed from c, ℏ, and G, equivalent to the time required for light to traverse one Planck length and serving as the lower bound on cosmic-history times for which classical general-relativistic descriptions are conjecturally valid.

**key_claim**: The Planck Time bounds, on the small-time side, the era during which classical cosmological evolution can be described by general relativity coupled to quantum field theory; before the Planck time the Planck-epoch quantum-gravity regime applies, and "the universe at t = 0" is literally beyond the predictive reach of currently-formulated physics.

**warning**: The Planck Time is sometimes presented as the "first moment" of cosmic time at which the universe could be said to "exist"; in fact the time coordinate itself becomes ambiguous in the Planck-epoch quantum-gravity regime, and treating the Planck time as a sharp temporal boundary anthropomorphises a more subtle conceptual situation.

## Entropy In Cosmology

- secondary_domains: [thermodynamics, cosmology]
- aliases: [cosmological entropy]
- broader: [statistical-mechanical concepts in cosmology]
- related: [arrow of time, second law of thermodynamics, black hole entropy, holographic principle]

**definition**: Entropy In Cosmology is the application of statistical-mechanical entropy to the universe as a whole, with cosmological entropy budgets dominated by the entropy of supermassive black holes (~10¹⁰⁴ k_B for the observable-universe budget) followed at much smaller scales by the entropy of the cosmic microwave background (~10⁸⁹ k_B), with the past universe in a remarkably low-entropy state relative to its maximum.

**key_claim**: Entropy In Cosmology presents the foundational puzzle that the early universe — despite being hot and dense — was in an extraordinarily low-entropy state when its gravitational degrees of freedom are properly accounted for, providing the cosmological precondition for the thermodynamic arrow of time observed in laboratory and biological systems.

**warning**: Entropy In Cosmology calculations depend on assumptions about how to count gravitational degrees of freedom — a problem with no fully satisfactory solution outside specific frameworks like black-hole thermodynamics — and quantitative claims about "the entropy of the universe" should be read as estimates conditioned on currently-assumed gravitational-entropy formulas rather than as settled measurements.

## Arrow Of Time

- secondary_domains: [foundations-of-physics, cosmology]
- aliases: [thermodynamic arrow, time's arrow]
- broader: [foundational problems in physics]
- related: [entropy in cosmology, second law of thermodynamics, big bang theory, causality in physics]

**definition**: The Arrow Of Time is the observed asymmetry between past and future in macroscopic physical processes — entropy increases, causation propagates forward, memory accumulates, biological organisms age — despite the time-reversal symmetry (or near-symmetry) of the underlying microscopic laws of physics.

**key_claim**: The Arrow Of Time appears to derive ultimately from the cosmological initial condition: the universe began in a state of extraordinarily low entropy (the "past hypothesis," Boltzmann–Penrose), and the second law's monotonic increase of entropy is then a statistical consequence of the asymmetry between this special initial state and the high-entropy generic states the universe evolves toward.

**warning**: The Arrow Of Time is sometimes presented as a fundamental fact about time itself; the more rigorous view is that microscopic laws are essentially time-symmetric and the apparent arrow is a statistical-mechanical consequence of cosmological initial conditions — a distinction important in foundations-of-physics discussions and routinely conflated in popular accounts.

## Causality In Physics

- secondary_domains: [special-relativity, foundations-of-physics]
- aliases: [relativistic causality, causal structure]
- broader: [foundational principles of physics]
- related: [special relativity, light cone, arrow of time, quantum entanglement]

**definition**: Causality In Physics is the principle that physical influences propagate only within or on the future light cone of the originating event, equivalently that no signal can travel faster than light — encoded in special relativity by the Lorentz-invariant structure of light cones and in quantum field theory by the requirement that spacelike-separated operators commute.

**key_claim**: Causality In Physics is built into the structure of all currently-accepted fundamental theories — special relativity, general relativity, quantum field theory — and tests of Lorentz invariance, the no-communication theorem in quantum information, and limits on superluminal propagation in cosmic-ray observations all consistently support it.

**warning**: Causality In Physics is sometimes claimed to be threatened by quantum entanglement, closed timelike curves in some general-relativistic solutions, or hypothetical wormholes; in standard quantum field theory the no-communication theorem rigorously preserves causality despite entanglement, and Hawking's chronology-protection conjecture (still unproven) suggests a quantum-gravity mechanism that prevents CTC formation — popular framings of "causality violation" overstate the implications of these phenomena.

## Conservation Of Energy

- secondary_domains: [classical-mechanics, thermodynamics]
- aliases: [energy conservation, first law of thermodynamics]
- broader: [conservation laws]
- related: [noether's theorem, mass energy equivalence, stress energy tensor, action principle]

**definition**: Conservation Of Energy is the principle that the total energy of an isolated physical system remains constant over time, equivalent (via Noether's theorem) to the time-translation invariance of the system's action and underlying the first law of thermodynamics in its various formulations across mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, and field theory.

**key_claim**: Conservation Of Energy is among the most stringently tested principles in physics — verified across regimes from particle physics (energy-momentum conservation in scattering processes) through chemistry and macroscopic mechanics — and apparent violations have always proved diagnostic of new physics (the prediction of the neutrino by Pauli to preserve energy conservation in beta decay is a textbook example).

**warning**: Conservation Of Energy in its elementary global sense does not hold in cosmological general relativity — the absence of a global timelike Killing vector in expanding spacetimes means that the energy of the cosmological perfect-fluid component is not in general conserved, with photons losing energy via cosmological redshift the most familiar example — and statements that "energy is always conserved" require qualification in cosmological contexts.

## Symmetry Breaking

- secondary_domains: [particle-physics, condensed-matter-physics]
- aliases: [spontaneous symmetry breaking, SSB]
- broader: [field-theoretic phenomena]
- related: [gauge theory, higgs boson, phase transition in cosmology, group theory in physics]

**definition**: Symmetry Breaking is the phenomenon in which a system whose underlying laws are invariant under a symmetry group settles into a ground state that is not invariant under the full symmetry, reducing the effective symmetry of the low-energy physics — distinguished into spontaneous (the laws retain the symmetry, the ground state breaks it) and explicit (the symmetry is broken at the Lagrangian level).

**key_claim**: Symmetry Breaking is the mechanism by which the Higgs field gives mass to the W and Z gauge bosons (electroweak symmetry breaking), by which a uniform ferromagnet aligns its spins below the Curie temperature, and by which cosmological phase transitions in the early universe shaped the spectrum of subsequent physics — making spontaneous symmetry breaking a central organising principle of modern physics.

**warning**: Symmetry Breaking in finite-volume systems is, strictly speaking, impossible — the symmetry is restored by quantum tunnelling between the symmetry-broken vacua — and the physical concept of spontaneous symmetry breaking emerges only in the thermodynamic limit; statements about "symmetry breaking in finite systems" require careful interpretation and frequently mislead in textbook treatments.

## Phase Transition In Cosmology

- secondary_domains: [early-universe-cosmology, particle-cosmology]
- aliases: [cosmological phase transition]
- broader: [early-universe phenomena]
- related: [symmetry breaking, baryogenesis, electroweak phase transition, cosmic string]

**definition**: A Phase Transition In Cosmology is a change in the ground state of a quantum field as the universe cooled through a critical temperature in the early universe — the QCD chiral / confinement transition at ~150 MeV (~10⁻⁴ s), the electroweak transition at ~160 GeV (~10⁻¹¹ s), and possibly higher-energy GUT-scale transitions at 10¹⁵–10¹⁶ GeV — typically associated with spontaneous symmetry breaking.

**key_claim**: A Phase Transition In Cosmology of strongly first-order type produces relic gravitational waves, possibly observable today in the millihertz band by LISA, and the electroweak phase transition in particular is a candidate site for electroweak baryogenesis — making the order and dynamics of early-universe phase transitions a target of substantial theoretical and observational interest.

**warning**: A Phase Transition In Cosmology in the Standard Model is now known to be a smooth crossover for both the QCD and electroweak transitions (not first-order), removing the Standard Model as a viable site for electroweak baryogenesis; results derived from old assumptions of strongly first-order Standard-Model transitions are obsolete and require beyond-Standard-Model physics to recover the original conclusions.

## Recombination Epoch

- secondary_domains: [cosmology, atomic-physics]
- aliases: [recombination, cosmological recombination]
- broader: [cosmological epochs]
- related: [cosmic microwave background radiation, last scattering surface, big bang theory, reionization]

**definition**: The Recombination Epoch is the period at cosmic age ~370,000 years (redshift z ≈ 1090, temperature ~3,000 K) during which free electrons and protons combined into neutral hydrogen atoms, decoupling photons from matter and producing the cosmic-microwave-background "last scattering surface" we observe today — the earliest direct electromagnetic image of the universe.

**key_claim**: The Recombination Epoch sets the surface from which CMB photons free-stream to the present, with the detailed physics of recombination (carefully treated by codes like RECFAST, CosmoRec, HyRec) determining the precision of CMB-derived cosmological-parameter constraints — a sub-percent-level accuracy in recombination physics is essential for precision cosmology.

**warning**: The Recombination Epoch term is etymologically misleading — protons and electrons were never previously combined into atoms, so "combination" rather than "recombination" would be more accurate — but the misleading historical name is now entrenched in cosmological literature and unlikely to change.

## Reionization

- secondary_domains: [cosmology, extragalactic-astronomy]
- aliases: [reionisation, epoch of reionization, EoR]
- broader: [cosmological epochs]
- related: [recombination epoch, dark ages of the universe, cosmic dawn, james webb space telescope]

**definition**: Reionization is the cosmic epoch (roughly z ≈ 6–15, ages 250 Myr to 1 Gyr after the Big Bang) during which ultraviolet radiation from the first stars, galaxies, and quasars re-ionised the neutral hydrogen and helium of the intergalactic medium produced at recombination — the universe's last global phase transition.

**key_claim**: Reionization is observationally constrained from the optical depth measured in CMB polarisation (Planck 2018: τ ≈ 0.054 ± 0.007), Lyman-alpha-forest absorption in high-redshift quasars (the Gunn–Peterson trough), and 21-cm radiation observations (EDGES, HERA, LOFAR upper limits), with JWST now providing direct observations of the galaxies driving reionisation.

**warning**: Reionization's history is not yet pinned down — the relative contributions of dwarf galaxies vs more massive galaxies vs AGN to the UV ionising photon budget remain debated, and the epoch's precise duration and morphology depend on assumed escape fractions of UV photons — and quantitative reionisation history claims should be read as model-dependent reconstructions rather than direct measurements.

## Last Scattering Surface

- secondary_domains: [cosmology, cmb-research]
- aliases: [surface of last scattering, LSS]
- broader: [cosmic microwave background origins]
- related: [recombination epoch, cosmic microwave background radiation, planck satellite, big bang theory]

**definition**: The Last Scattering Surface is the surface in spacetime at cosmic age ~370,000 years from which the cosmic-microwave-background photons we observe today were last scattered before free-streaming to the present, located at comoving distance ~14 Gpc and observed today as a thin spherical shell centred on the Earth at redshift z ≈ 1090.

**key_claim**: The Last Scattering Surface is the deepest direct electromagnetic image of the universe we can ever obtain — the universe was opaque to electromagnetic radiation at earlier epochs — and its temperature anisotropies (the patterns of the CMB) carry the fingerprint of the universe's matter and energy content, encoding the cosmological parameters measured by COBE, WMAP, Planck, and ground-based CMB experiments.

**warning**: The Last Scattering Surface is not infinitesimally thin — the recombination process took ~10⁵ years, corresponding to a finite shell thickness that limits the angular resolution of the CMB to ~7 arcminutes (small-scale anisotropies are washed out by the LSS thickness) — and treatments of CMB physics that ignore this finite-thickness damping ("Silk damping") miss an important physical effect.

## Dark Ages Of The Universe

- secondary_domains: [cosmology, observational-astronomy]
- aliases: [cosmic dark ages]
- broader: [cosmological epochs]
- related: [reionization, cosmic dawn, james webb space telescope, recombination epoch]

**definition**: The Dark Ages Of The Universe is the period between recombination (~370,000 years after the Big Bang) and the formation of the first luminous structures (~150–250 million years after the Big Bang), during which the universe contained neutral hydrogen, the CMB photons had decoupled, and there was no source of significant ultraviolet or visible radiation.

**key_claim**: The Dark Ages Of The Universe end at the appearance of the first Population III stars and the onset of cosmic dawn, and the most direct observational probe of the dark ages and their termination is redshifted 21-cm hydrogen-line emission — the target of ongoing experiments (HERA, SKA, LOFAR) and the principal scientific objective of next-generation low-frequency radio facilities.

**warning**: The Dark Ages Of The Universe are observationally inaccessible to optical and infrared astronomy — there is no visible light to detect — and "dark ages" claims based on deep imaging surveys (HST, JWST) are actually probing the cosmic dawn and reionisation eras, not the dark ages proper; the popular framing that "JWST sees the dark ages" misuses the technical terminology.

## Cosmic Dawn

- secondary_domains: [cosmology, extragalactic-astronomy]
- aliases: [first light, cosmic dawn era]
- broader: [cosmological epochs]
- related: [dark ages of the universe, reionization, james webb space telescope, population iii stars]

**definition**: Cosmic Dawn is the cosmic epoch (~150–500 Myr after the Big Bang, redshifts z ≈ 10–30) during which the first stars (Population III), the first galaxies, and the first accreting black holes formed, ending the cosmic dark ages and beginning the process of reionisation that completed by z ≈ 6.

**key_claim**: Cosmic Dawn is an observationally emerging epoch, with JWST detections of luminous galaxies at z > 12 (cosmic age < 400 Myr) and the EDGES claimed 21-cm absorption signal at z ≈ 17 (still controversial after non-detection by SARAS-3) providing the first observational glimpses; the SKA-1 / -2 and the planned LuSEE-Night (lunar far-side 21-cm) facilities are designed to characterise cosmic dawn in detail.

**warning**: Cosmic Dawn observations from JWST are sensitive to photometric-redshift systematics — early "z > 14" candidates have been substantially revised downward following spectroscopic follow-up — and the claimed EDGES 21-cm absorption signal (Bowman et al 2018) has not been confirmed by SARAS-3, leaving the existence of a strong absorption signal at cosmic dawn observationally unsettled.

## Holographic Principle

- secondary_domains: [theoretical-physics, quantum-gravity]
- aliases: [holography, holographic conjecture]
- broader: [conjectured principles of quantum gravity]
- related: [ads-cft correspondence, black hole information paradox, hawking radiation, quantum gravity]

**definition**: The Holographic Principle is the conjecture (Hooft 1993, Susskind 1995) that the information content of a region of space with gravity is fully encoded on the region's boundary surface area — measured in Planck-area units divided by 4 — generalising the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy bound for black holes to all gravitating regions.

**key_claim**: The Holographic Principle is concretely realised in the AdS/CFT correspondence (Maldacena 1997), in which a quantum theory of gravity in (d+1)-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime is exactly equivalent to a non-gravitational quantum field theory living on the d-dimensional boundary — the most explicit and well-tested example of holography in theoretical physics.

**warning**: The Holographic Principle is firmly established only for asymptotically anti-de Sitter spacetimes (AdS/CFT); its applicability to our universe (which is asymptotically de Sitter) requires a "dS/CFT" or analogous duality whose status is much more conjectural, and statements that "the universe is a hologram" overstate the empirical and theoretical reach of the principle.

## AdS CFT Correspondence

- secondary_domains: [theoretical-physics, string-theory, quantum-gravity]
- aliases: [AdS/CFT, gauge-gravity duality, Maldacena duality]
- broader: [holographic principle]
- related: [holographic principle, string theory, quantum gravity, gauge theory]

**definition**: The AdS CFT Correspondence is the duality, conjectured by Juan Maldacena (1997), between Type IIB string theory on AdS_5 × S^5 (anti-de Sitter spacetime crossed with a five-sphere) and N = 4 supersymmetric SU(N) Yang–Mills gauge theory in four-dimensional Minkowski space — equating a quantum theory of gravity in five-dimensional curved space with a non-gravitational quantum field theory in four-dimensional flat space.

**key_claim**: The AdS CFT Correspondence is one of the most-cited results in theoretical physics, providing a concrete realisation of the holographic principle, a tool for non-perturbative calculations in strongly-coupled gauge theory (used in heavy-ion physics, condensed-matter physics, and quark-gluon plasma calculations), and an example of how a quantum theory of gravity can be defined.

**warning**: The AdS CFT Correspondence applies rigorously only to asymptotically anti-de Sitter spacetimes (negative cosmological constant, the opposite sign to our observed universe), and the existence of a similar duality for asymptotically de Sitter or Minkowski spacetimes remains conjectural; presenting AdS/CFT as if it directly applied to our universe overstates the duality's established domain.
