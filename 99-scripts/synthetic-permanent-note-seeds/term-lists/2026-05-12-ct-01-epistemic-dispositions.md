## Intellectual Courage

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, ethics]
- aliases: [epistemic courage]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [epistemic cowardice, open mindedness, fair mindedness]
- prerequisites: [intellectual humility]

**definition**: Intellectual Courage is the disposition to engage fairly with ideas, evidence, and viewpoints that one finds threatening or socially costly, and to assert well-founded conclusions even when they conflict with one's prior commitments or surrounding consensus.

**key_claim**: Intellectual Courage is what converts private rational assent into public rational behavior; without it, a thinker may correctly identify a sound argument and still refuse to follow where it leads, allowing social cost to override epistemic warrant.

**warning**: Intellectual Courage is often confused with contrarianism or rhetorical bravado; the disposition is governed by evidence, not by the discomfort it produces, so a stance taken purely because it is unpopular fails the criterion just as surely as one held purely because it is safe.

## Intellectual Perseverance

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, learning-science]
- aliases: [epistemic perseverance, intellectual persistence]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [intellectual conscientiousness, truth seeking disposition]
- prerequisites: [intellectual humility]

**definition**: Intellectual Perseverance is the disposition to remain engaged with difficult, frustrating, or unresolved problems long enough for genuine understanding to develop, rather than terminating inquiry at the first sign of cognitive strain.

**key_claim**: Intellectual Perseverance is the disposition that protects depth of understanding from the local minimum of "good-enough" comprehension, because most non-trivial questions yield surface plausibility quickly and structural insight only after sustained effort.

**warning**: Intellectual Perseverance is not the same as stubbornness; the disposition requires continuing to think, not continuing to believe, so a perseverant thinker who encounters defeating evidence updates rather than digging in.

## Fair Mindedness

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, ethics]
- aliases: [epistemic fairness, fair-mindedness]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [intellectual empathy, open mindedness, intellectual integrity]

**definition**: Fair Mindedness is the disposition to evaluate all viewpoints by the same standards regardless of whether they are one's own, those of an ally, or those of an opponent, treating the strength of an argument as independent of its source.

**key_claim**: Fair Mindedness operationalises impartiality as a procedural commitment rather than a feeling; it requires that the criteria one applies to weak versions of opposing views be the same criteria one applies to weak versions of one's own.

**warning**: Fair Mindedness is frequently mistaken for false balance — granting equal credibility to unequally supported positions; the disposition demands equal standards of evaluation, which typically produces unequal verdicts.

## Intellectual Empathy

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, communication]
- aliases: [epistemic empathy, perspective-taking disposition]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [fair mindedness, steelmanning, open mindedness]

**definition**: Intellectual Empathy is the disposition to reconstruct another person's reasoning from the inside — recovering the premises, evidence, and inferential moves that make their conclusion appear rational to them — before evaluating or responding.

**key_claim**: Intellectual Empathy is a precondition for accurate disagreement; without it, a critic typically attacks a position the holder does not actually hold, which guarantees that the exchange will neither persuade nor inform.

**warning**: Intellectual Empathy does not require agreement or moral endorsement; the disposition is descriptive reconstruction, and conflating it with sympathy leads thinkers either to refuse the exercise on ethical grounds or to slide from understanding into capitulation.

## Intellectual Integrity

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, ethics]
- aliases: [epistemic integrity]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [intellectual courage, fair mindedness, epistemic responsibility]

**definition**: Intellectual Integrity is the disposition to hold oneself to the same standards of evidence and consistency that one demands of others, and to acknowledge openly any inconsistencies, weaknesses, or unresolved tensions in one's own position.

**key_claim**: Intellectual Integrity is what makes a thinker's reasoning trustworthy across contexts; it eliminates the asymmetry whereby strong evidence is required to overturn a favoured belief and weak evidence suffices to dismiss a disfavoured one.

**warning**: Intellectual Integrity is not a one-time audit but a continuing practice; thinkers who treat it as a settled identity ("I am an intellectually honest person") tend to detect their own inconsistencies less reliably than those who treat it as a method.

## Open Mindedness

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, decision-making]
- aliases: [open-mindedness, epistemic openness]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [intellectual humility, fair mindedness, intellectual empathy]

**definition**: Open Mindedness is the disposition to take seriously the possibility that one's current beliefs are mistaken and to give genuine consideration to evidence and arguments that might revise them, without prejudging the outcome.

**key_claim**: Open Mindedness is a regulatory disposition over inquiry rather than a positive attitude toward any particular conclusion; it specifies how one weighs new information, not what one ends up believing after weighing it.

**warning**: Open Mindedness is routinely conflated with credulity or with the obligation to give every claim equal hearing; the disposition is selective engagement governed by relevance and evidential bearing, not indiscriminate acceptance.

## Intellectual Autonomy

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, philosophy-of-education]
- aliases: [epistemic autonomy, intellectual independence]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [epistemic responsibility, intellectual courage, epistemic trust]

**definition**: Intellectual Autonomy is the disposition to think for oneself — to form beliefs through one's own evaluation of evidence and argument rather than by deferring uncritically to authority, tradition, or peer pressure.

**key_claim**: Intellectual Autonomy is compatible with extensive reliance on testimony; the disposition concerns who is finally responsible for the verdict, not whether one's inputs include the work of others.

**warning**: Intellectual Autonomy is often misread as a mandate to reject expert testimony; the disposition requires that deference be reasoned rather than abolished, so a calibrated autonomous thinker defers more often than a contrarian and for explicit reasons.

## Epistemic Cowardice

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, vice-epistemology]
- aliases: [intellectual cowardice]
- broader: [epistemic vices]
- related: [intellectual courage, fair mindedness, intellectual integrity]

**definition**: Epistemic Cowardice is the vice of withholding or softening one's well-supported beliefs in order to avoid social, professional, or personal cost, even when the situation calls for sincere assertion.

**key_claim**: Epistemic Cowardice is not a failure of belief but a failure of avowal; the cowardly thinker may know what is true and still allow audience pressure to determine what is said, which corrupts the public record without any underlying confusion.

**warning**: Epistemic Cowardice should not be diagnosed merely from silence; legitimate reasons to withhold testimony — incomplete evidence, inappropriate venue, risk to third parties — must be ruled out before attributing the vice.

## Epistemic Humility

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, philosophy-of-mind]
- aliases: [intellectual humility]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [open mindedness, fair mindedness, intellectual autonomy]

**definition**: Epistemic Humility is the disposition to recognise the limits of one's own knowledge, the fallibility of one's reasoning, and the possibility that any of one's current beliefs may turn out to be mistaken.

**key_claim**: Epistemic Humility is structurally distinct from low confidence; it is a calibrated awareness of where the edges of one's competence actually lie, which can coexist with strong, well-justified conviction inside those edges.

**warning**: Epistemic Humility is often performed as generic self-deprecation, but the disposition requires localised acknowledgement of specific gaps; vague confessions of fallibility offer no traction for revision and can function as a rhetorical shield against criticism.

## Intellectual Conscientiousness

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, ethics]
- aliases: [epistemic conscientiousness]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [epistemic responsibility, intellectual perseverance, truth seeking disposition]

**definition**: Intellectual Conscientiousness is the disposition to investigate questions with the care, thoroughness, and attention to detail that the stakes of the question warrant, rather than settling for the first plausible answer.

**key_claim**: Intellectual Conscientiousness scales effort to consequence; it is what distinguishes responsible inquiry from both intellectual laziness on important questions and pointless over-investigation of trivial ones.

**warning**: Intellectual Conscientiousness can degrade into perfectionism or analysis paralysis; the disposition is bounded by the practical decision the inquiry serves, so a conscientious thinker treats "good enough for the decision at hand" as a legitimate stopping criterion.

## Epistemic Responsibility

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, ethics]
- aliases: [doxastic responsibility, epistemic accountability]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [intellectual conscientiousness, intellectual autonomy, intellectual integrity]

**definition**: Epistemic Responsibility is the disposition — and the corresponding normative requirement — to manage one's belief-formation processes with the kind of care that one's epistemic situation and social role demand.

**key_claim**: Epistemic Responsibility holds that thinkers can be praised or blamed not only for what they believe but for how they came to believe it; this extends accountability from the content of belief to the conduct of inquiry.

**warning**: Epistemic Responsibility does not entail voluntarism about belief; the disposition concerns the procedures one can control — evidence-gathering, attention, deliberation — not a pretence that one can simply choose what to believe.

## Truth Seeking Disposition

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, philosophy-of-inquiry]
- aliases: [truthfulness disposition, alethic motivation]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [intellectual integrity, intellectual courage, inquisitiveness as disposition]

**definition**: A Truth Seeking Disposition is the standing motivation to pursue accurate beliefs even when accuracy conflicts with comfort, identity, or social reward, and to prefer well-supported answers to congenial ones.

**key_claim**: A Truth Seeking Disposition reorders the thinker's incentive landscape so that being right outranks being seen as right; this is the trait that makes other intellectual virtues stable rather than situational.

**warning**: A Truth Seeking Disposition is routinely simulated by motivated reasoners who frame their preferred conclusions as "just following the truth"; the disposition is identified by behaviour under disconfirming evidence, not by the rhetoric of objectivity.

## Systematicity As Disposition

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, methodology]
- aliases: [systematicity, methodical disposition]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [intellectual conscientiousness, judiciousness]

**definition**: Systematicity As Disposition is the standing tendency to approach questions in an orderly, methodical, and structured way — laying out alternatives, examining them in turn, and resisting the temptation to settle on a verdict by impression alone.

**key_claim**: Systematicity As Disposition compensates for the well-documented limits of unaided intuition by externalising the reasoning structure, which makes both the conclusion and the path to it available for inspection and critique.

**warning**: Systematicity As Disposition can ossify into procedural fetishism in which the form of method substitutes for the substance of judgement; the disposition is in service of better reasoning, not a ritual that licenses worse reasoning when the steps are followed.

## Judiciousness

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, decision-making]
- aliases: [practical wisdom in inquiry, judicious disposition]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [fair mindedness, intellectual humility, systematicity as disposition]

**definition**: Judiciousness is the disposition to render measured, well-proportioned judgements that take into account the full range of relevant considerations rather than overweighting any single one.

**key_claim**: Judiciousness is the trait that prevents virtues from becoming vices in isolation; it is what makes a thinker know when to persevere versus when to update, when to defer versus when to dissent, when to scrutinise versus when to act.

**warning**: Judiciousness is often confused with risk-aversion or compromise; the disposition can deliver decisive and even radical verdicts when the considerations actually point that way, and treating it as synonymous with moderation is itself a failure of judgement.

## Inquisitiveness As Disposition

- secondary_domains: [virtue-epistemology, philosophy-of-education]
- aliases: [intellectual curiosity, inquisitiveness]
- broader: [intellectual virtues]
- related: [truth seeking disposition, open mindedness, intellectual perseverance]

**definition**: Inquisitiveness As Disposition is the standing tendency to ask questions, to find unfamiliar territory interesting rather than threatening, and to seek out information beyond what immediate utility would require.

**key_claim**: Inquisitiveness As Disposition is the engine of inquiry rather than its method; many epistemic virtues describe how one investigates, but inquisitiveness describes why one investigates at all.

**warning**: Inquisitiveness As Disposition can be hijacked by novelty-seeking that mimics curiosity without producing understanding; the virtuous form is sustained interest that survives the discovery that an answer will require effort.
