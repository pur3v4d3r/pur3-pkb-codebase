# Batch: Motivation & Self-Belief Gaps

## Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Motivation

- domain: motivational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [self-determination-theory, educational-psychology]
- aliases: [intrinsic-extrinsic distinction, IM vs EM]
- broader: [motivation-theory]
- related: [intrinsic-motivation, extrinsic-motivation, autonomous-motivation, controlled-motivation, self-determination-theory-sdt]
- prerequisites: [intrinsic-motivation, extrinsic-motivation]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Motivation is the foundational classification scheme in motivation science that distinguishes behaviour driven by inherent interest, enjoyment, or volitional engagement from behaviour driven by separable consequences such as rewards, deadlines, or social approval.

**key_claim**: The Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Motivation distinction is not a clean dichotomy but a continuum, and self-determination theory's organismic integration sub-theory shows that extrinsic motivations differ enormously among themselves in how autonomously they regulate behaviour.

**warning**: The Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Motivation distinction is frequently misread as "intrinsic = good, extrinsic = bad", but well-internalised extrinsic motivation can be as functional and durable as intrinsic motivation, and treating Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Motivation as a moral binary leads to design failures in school and workplace incentive systems.

## Autonomy Supportive Teaching

- domain: educational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [self-determination-theory, motivational-psychology]
- aliases: [autonomy-supportive instruction, ASI]
- broader: [autonomy-support]
- related: [autonomy-need, controlling-teaching-styles, structure, self-determination-theory-sdt]
- prerequisites: [autonomy-support, self-determination-theory-sdt]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Autonomy Supportive Teaching is the instructional style in which teachers nurture students' inner motivational resources by acknowledging students' perspectives, providing meaningful rationales, offering choice within structure, and minimising controlling language and contingent rewards.

**key_claim**: Autonomy Supportive Teaching is empirically associated with deeper conceptual learning, higher persistence, and lower dropout than controlling teaching styles, and the effect is mediated by satisfaction of the basic psychological needs that self-determination theory specifies.

**warning**: Autonomy Supportive Teaching is often confused with permissive or unstructured teaching; in fact Autonomy Supportive Teaching combines high responsiveness with high structure, and removing structure in the name of "giving students freedom" undermines the very autonomy Autonomy Supportive Teaching is meant to support.

## Mastery vs Performance Goals

- domain: motivational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [achievement-goal-theory, educational-psychology]
- aliases: [task-vs-ego goals, learning-vs-performance goals]
- broader: [achievement-goal-theory]
- related: [mastery-goal, performance-approach-goal, performance-avoidance-goal, 2x2-framework]
- prerequisites: [achievement-goal-theory, achievement-goal]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Mastery vs Performance Goals is the central distinction in achievement-goal theory between goals oriented toward developing competence relative to the task or one's prior self versus goals oriented toward demonstrating competence relative to others.

**key_claim**: Mastery vs Performance Goals predict different downstream patterns: mastery goals reliably link to deep processing, persistence after failure, and help-seeking, whereas performance goals — especially performance-avoidance — link to surface strategies, anxiety, and avoidance of challenging tasks.

**warning**: Mastery vs Performance Goals are not mutually exclusive; learners commonly hold both, and the empirically interesting question is the goal-complex pattern rather than which single Mastery vs Performance Goals category dominates, so dichotomous classifications misrepresent the construct.

## Explanatory Style

- domain: motivational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [attribution-theory, clinical-psychology]
- aliases: [attributional style, optimistic-pessimistic explanatory style]
- broader: [attribution-theory]
- related: [learned-helplessness, attribution-retraining, locus-of-control, controllability-dimension, stability-dimension]
- prerequisites: [attribution-theory, attributional-vocabulary]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Explanatory Style is the habitual cross-situational pattern by which a person explains the causes of negative and positive events along three dimensions — internal versus external, stable versus unstable, and global versus specific — and it forms the cognitive substrate of learned helplessness theory.

**key_claim**: A pessimistic Explanatory Style — internal, stable, and global causes for failure — predicts depression, low achievement, and poor health outcomes more reliably than situational measures, while an optimistic Explanatory Style predicts persistence and recovery, making Explanatory Style a stable individual-difference moderator of stress.

**warning**: Explanatory Style is sometimes flattened into a generic "optimism vs pessimism" trait, but the diagnostic and clinical value of Explanatory Style depends on assessing each of the three dimensions independently; treating Explanatory Style as a single bipolar variable loses the predictive precision the construct provides.

## Self Serving Bias

- domain: cognitive-science
- secondary_domains: [attribution-theory, social-psychology]
- aliases: [self-enhancement bias]
- broader: [cognitive-bias]
- related: [attribution-theory, fundamental-attribution-error, ego-protective-attribution, self-esteem]
- prerequisites: [attribution-theory, attributional-vocabulary]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Self Serving Bias is the systematic tendency to attribute one's successes to internal, stable, controllable causes while attributing one's failures to external, unstable, uncontrollable causes, producing an asymmetry in causal explanations that protects self-esteem.

**key_claim**: Self Serving Bias is robust across cultures but moderated by self-construal, achievement domain, and audience; the magnitude of Self Serving Bias drops in private settings and in collectivist contexts, indicating that Self Serving Bias is partly a self-presentation strategy rather than a purely automatic cognitive distortion.

**warning**: Self Serving Bias is not always maladaptive — moderate Self Serving Bias correlates with persistence and well-being — but extreme Self Serving Bias blocks learning from failure and corrupts feedback loops, which is why feedback-design must distinguish protective Self Serving Bias from pathological externalisation.

## Self Esteem

- domain: motivational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [clinical-psychology, social-psychology]
- aliases: [global self-worth]
- broader: [self-concept]
- related: [self-efficacy, self-worth-theory, self-serving-bias, contingent-self-esteem]
- prerequisites: [self-concept]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Self Esteem is a person's overall affective evaluation of their own worth as a person, distinct from domain-specific competence beliefs such as self-efficacy and from descriptive self-concept content, and it is typically measured as a stable trait with state-level fluctuations.

**key_claim**: Self Esteem is correlated with — but does not causally produce — achievement, well-being, or pro-social behaviour; the meta-analytic evidence shows that interventions raising Self Esteem without changing the underlying competencies fail to improve outcomes, overturning the 1980s "self-esteem movement" assumption.

**warning**: Self Esteem is often confused with self-efficacy or narcissism; conflating Self Esteem with task confidence misdirects intervention design, and conflating high Self Esteem with narcissism — which is contingent and fragile — leads to the misreading that all high Self Esteem is socially harmful.

## Possible Selves

- domain: motivational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [self-concept, identity-psychology]
- aliases: [Markus-Nurius possible selves]
- broader: [self-concept]
- related: [feared-self, ideal-self, identity-based-motivation, future-self-continuity, goal-setting-theory]
- prerequisites: [self-concept]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Possible Selves are the cognitively elaborated images a person holds of who they might become, who they would like to become, and who they fear becoming, and they function as personalised motivational structures that link the present self to specific future trajectories.

**key_claim**: Possible Selves are most motivationally effective when they are paired with concrete strategies for reaching the desired Possible Selves and avoiding the feared Possible Selves; without this strategy linkage, Possible Selves operate as fantasies that can actually depress goal pursuit.

**warning**: Possible Selves are often mistaken for general aspirations or values; the empirical construct requires vivid, self-relevant, and contextually situated representations, so generic "be successful" goals do not function as Possible Selves and do not produce the motivational effects the literature documents.

## Goal Orientation Framework

- domain: motivational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [achievement-goal-theory, organizational-psychology]
- aliases: [goal-orientation theory, GO framework]
- broader: [achievement-goal-theory]
- related: [mastery-vs-performance-goals, 2x2-framework, implicit-theory-of-intelligence, learning-goal-orientation]
- prerequisites: [achievement-goal-theory]
- confidence: high

**definition**: The Goal Orientation Framework is a body of theory in achievement and organisational psychology that classifies the dispositional and situational reasons learners or workers engage with achievement tasks, originally as mastery versus performance and later refined into the 2x2 Elliot-McGregor matrix.

**key_claim**: The Goal Orientation Framework predicts a coherent pattern of cognitive, affective, and behavioural outcomes — strategy use, persistence, anxiety, help-seeking — that the simpler intrinsic-vs-extrinsic motivation distinction does not, which is why the Goal Orientation Framework remains the dominant taxonomy in achievement-motivation research.

**warning**: The Goal Orientation Framework is often applied as a static trait classification, but goal orientations are partly situational and partly induced by classroom and workplace climate; treating the Goal Orientation Framework as a fixed personality categorisation misses the design lever that situational induction provides.

## Implicit Theory of Intelligence

- domain: motivational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [educational-psychology, social-cognition]
- aliases: [Dweck mindsets, lay theories of intelligence]
- broader: [implicit-theories]
- related: [growth-mindset, entity-theory-of-intelligence, learned-helplessness, mastery-vs-performance-goals, attribution-theory]
- prerequisites: [implicit-theories]
- confidence: high

**definition**: An Implicit Theory of Intelligence is a learner's lay belief about whether intelligence is a fixed trait or a malleable quality that can be developed through effort and strategy, and it is the cognitive antecedent of the broader fixed-versus-growth mindset distinction.

**key_claim**: An Implicit Theory of Intelligence shapes downstream goal choice, attribution, and persistence: an entity Implicit Theory of Intelligence funnels learners toward performance goals and helpless attributions after failure, while an incremental Implicit Theory of Intelligence supports mastery goals and effort-based attributions.

**warning**: An Implicit Theory of Intelligence is sometimes treated as a stable personality trait, but it is domain-specific and modifiable through brief interventions; the reproducibility crisis around generic mindset interventions reflects misuse of the construct rather than failure of Implicit Theory of Intelligence research itself.

## Pintrich's 4x4 Matrix

- domain: educational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [self-regulated-learning, metacognition]
- aliases: [Pintrich SRL matrix, four-phases-by-four-areas]
- broader: [self-regulated-learning]
- related: [pintrich-self-regulation-foundational-report-2026-04-19, forethought-phase, control-phase, self-regulation, cyclical-model-of-self-regulated-learning]
- prerequisites: [self-regulated-learning]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Pintrich's 4x4 Matrix is the organising framework of Paul Pintrich's self-regulated-learning model in which the four temporal phases — forethought, monitoring, control, and reaction — are crossed with the four areas of regulation — cognition, motivation/affect, behaviour, and context — yielding sixteen distinct self-regulatory activities.

**key_claim**: Pintrich's 4x4 Matrix advances on earlier SRL models by treating motivation, behaviour, and context as first-class targets of self-regulation alongside cognition, which is why Pintrich's 4x4 Matrix remains the most comprehensive descriptive scaffold for educational interventions targeting self-regulated learning.

**warning**: Pintrich's 4x4 Matrix is descriptive rather than mechanistic; it organises the territory of self-regulation but does not by itself specify which cells are causally upstream of which others, so Pintrich's 4x4 Matrix should be paired with process-level models such as Winne's COPES when explanation rather than classification is required.

## Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire MSLQ

- domain: educational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [self-regulated-learning, psychometrics]
- aliases: [MSLQ, Pintrich MSLQ]
- broader: [educational-assessment-instrument]
- related: [self-regulated-learning, pintrich-s-4-4-matrix, cognitive-strategy-use, metacognitive-strategy, self-efficacy]
- prerequisites: [self-regulated-learning, educational-assessment]
- confidence: high

**definition**: The Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire MSLQ is a self-report instrument developed by Pintrich and colleagues that assesses college students' motivational orientations and use of cognitive, metacognitive, and resource-management strategies across 81 items organised into 15 sub-scales.

**key_claim**: The Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire MSLQ is the most widely used assessment in self-regulated-learning research and has been translated into dozens of languages, providing the empirical bridge between Pintrich's theoretical SRL model and decades of correlational and intervention studies.

**warning**: The Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire MSLQ is a self-report instrument and shares the well-documented limitations of self-report — desirability bias, limited insight into automatic processes, course-context confounds — so Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire MSLQ scores should not be treated as direct measurements of strategy use without behavioural triangulation.

## Motivation Science

- domain: motivational-psychology
- secondary_domains: [psychology, educational-psychology]
- aliases: [motivation research, science of motivation]
- broader: [psychology]
- related: [self-determination-theory, achievement-goal-theory, expectancy-value-theory, attribution-theory, control-value-theory]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Motivation Science is the interdisciplinary research field that studies the processes initiating, directing, sustaining, and terminating goal-directed behaviour, integrating evidence from cognitive psychology, social psychology, neuroscience, and educational research.

**key_claim**: Modern Motivation Science is not a single grand theory but a family of mid-range theories — SDT, achievement-goal theory, expectancy-value theory, control-value theory, attribution theory — each addressing a different slice of the motivational system, and Motivation Science is most useful when these theories are read as complementary rather than competing.

**warning**: Popular accounts of Motivation Science routinely cherry-pick a single mechanism — dopamine, mindset, autonomy, grit — and present it as the whole story; serious Motivation Science treats the mechanism in question as one constituent of a multi-process system, and any monocausal claim should be read as a red flag.
