# Batch: Neuroscience of Learning

## Acetylcholine and Memory

- domain: neuroscience-of-learning
- secondary_domains: [cognitive-neuroscience, memory-science]
- aliases: [cholinergic system and memory, ACh and memory]
- broader: [neuromodulation-and-learning]
- related: [hippocampal-neocortical-transfer, attention, alzheimers-disease]
- prerequisites: [neurotransmission]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Acetylcholine and Memory refers to the modulatory role of the cholinergic neurotransmitter acetylcholine — released by basal-forebrain projections to the hippocampus and neocortex — in gating attentional selection during encoding and biasing the hippocampus toward storage of new information rather than retrieval of old patterns.

**key_claim**: The Acetylcholine and Memory literature shows that pharmacological blockade of muscarinic receptors during encoding selectively impairs subsequent recall while leaving recognition relatively intact, supporting a model in which acetylcholine specifically supports the binding of contextual detail into episodic traces.

**warning**: Acetylcholine and Memory is often summarized as "ACh = memory chemical," but the cholinergic system's contribution is to biasing the encoding/retrieval balance and to attentional gating; treating it as a uniform amplifier of "memory" obscures the dissociations actually observed in the lesion and pharmacology data.

## Amygdala and Learning

- domain: neuroscience-of-learning
- secondary_domains: [cognitive-neuroscience, emotion, memory-science]
- aliases: [amygdala memory modulation, amygdala fear learning]
- broader: [emotional-memory-enhancement]
- related: [emotional-memory-enhancement, fear-conditioning, mood-congruent-memory]
- prerequisites: [limbic-system, neurotransmission]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Amygdala and Learning refers to the role of the amygdaloid complex — particularly the basolateral nucleus — in modulating the consolidation of memories for emotionally arousing events, primarily through stress-hormone-driven enhancement of hippocampal and neocortical encoding.

**key_claim**: Amygdala and Learning research shows that the amygdala does not store explicit emotional memories itself; rather, it tags concurrent activity in other memory systems as "important," producing the well-attested enhancement of emotional content over neutral content in long-term retention.

**warning**: Amygdala and Learning is often equated with fear learning specifically, but the same modulatory mechanism enhances memory for positive arousing events; framing the amygdala as a "fear centre" misrepresents the broader role of arousal-driven consolidation.

## Glial Cells and Cognition

- domain: cognitive-neuroscience
- secondary_domains: [neuroscience-of-learning]
- aliases: [neuroglia and cognition, astrocyte computation]
- broader: [cellular-neuroscience]
- related: [synaptic-plasticity, hippocampal-neocortical-transfer, neuromodulation-and-learning]
- prerequisites: [neurotransmission]
- confidence: medium

**definition**: Glial Cells and Cognition refers to the increasingly documented contribution of non-neuronal brain cells — principally astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, and microglia — to information processing, learning, and memory through synapse modulation, myelination, and structural remodelling rather than action-potential signalling.

**key_claim**: Glial Cells and Cognition research has overturned the long-standing "support-cell" view of glia: astrocytic calcium signalling actively gates synaptic transmission, and activity-dependent myelination by oligodendrocytes is a learning mechanism that operates on the timing of axonal conduction itself.

**warning**: Glial Cells and Cognition is a rapidly moving area, and many strong cognitive claims about glial contributions rest on a small number of preparations or species; readers should treat strong glia-centric reframings of cognition as suggestive rather than as a stable consensus.

## Mirror Neurons

- domain: cognitive-neuroscience
- secondary_domains: [neuroscience-of-learning, social-cognition]
- aliases: [mirror neuron system, MNS]
- broader: [neural-coding]
- related: [theory-of-mind, observational-learning, embodied-cognition]
- prerequisites: [motor-cortex]
- confidence: medium

**definition**: Mirror Neurons are a class of cells, originally identified in macaque ventral premotor cortex, that fire both when an animal performs a goal-directed action and when it observes another agent performing a similar action, providing a candidate neural substrate for action understanding and imitation.

**key_claim**: Mirror Neurons gained outsized influence because the simple "I see, I do" mapping was offered as a unified neural explanation for imitation, empathy, language evolution, and theory of mind, becoming the textbook example of how a clean experimental finding can be over-extended into a master explanation.

**warning**: Mirror Neurons in the strict single-cell sense have been confirmed only in macaques; human evidence is largely indirect (fMRI suppression, EEG mu-rhythm) and does not establish the existence of cells with the macaque mirror property, so theoretical claims that import the macaque circuitry into human cognition should be discounted accordingly.

## Neuromodulation and Learning

- domain: neuroscience-of-learning
- secondary_domains: [cognitive-neuroscience, memory-science]
- aliases: [neuromodulatory systems in learning]
- broader: [synaptic-plasticity]
- related: [acetylcholine-and-memory, norepinephrine-and-learning, dopamine-and-reward, amygdala-and-learning]
- prerequisites: [neurotransmission, synaptic-plasticity]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Neuromodulation and Learning refers to the role of slow-acting, broadly projecting neurotransmitter systems — dopaminergic, noradrenergic, cholinergic, and serotonergic — in setting the gain on synaptic plasticity, so that the same Hebbian event produces more or less durable change depending on the modulatory state at encoding.

**key_claim**: Neuromodulation and Learning frames learning rate as a context-dependent variable rather than a fixed property of synapses; arousal, novelty, reward, and uncertainty selectively bias which experiences are tagged for long-term storage and which decay as background.

**warning**: Neuromodulation and Learning is often summarized as "neuromodulators turn plasticity on," but each system has antagonistic and time-locked effects depending on receptor subtype and circuit; lumping them together hides the functional dissociations the literature actually requires.

## Norepinephrine and Learning

- domain: neuroscience-of-learning
- secondary_domains: [cognitive-neuroscience, memory-science, stress-physiology]
- aliases: [noradrenergic system and learning, NE and learning]
- broader: [neuromodulation-and-learning]
- related: [acetylcholine-and-memory, amygdala-and-learning, emotional-memory-enhancement, locus-coeruleus]
- prerequisites: [neurotransmission, neuromodulation-and-learning]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Norepinephrine and Learning refers to the role of the noradrenergic system — projecting from the locus coeruleus to the amygdala, hippocampus, and cortex — in tagging arousing, surprising, or behaviorally significant events for enhanced consolidation through phasic noradrenaline release at encoding.

**key_claim**: Norepinephrine and Learning research shows an inverted-U relationship between noradrenergic activation and memory performance: too little produces under-encoding, too much produces over-consolidation of central detail at the expense of peripheral context, the Yerkes-Dodson curve at the cellular level.

**warning**: Norepinephrine and Learning is often invoked to justify "high-arousal study" interventions, but the inverted-U is steep and individual differences are large, so generic "make studying exciting" advice can land learners on either side of the optimum without principled calibration.
