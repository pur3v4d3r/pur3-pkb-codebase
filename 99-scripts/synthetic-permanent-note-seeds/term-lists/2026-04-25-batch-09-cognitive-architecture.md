# Batch: Cognitive Architecture

## Mind Wandering

- secondary_domains: [attention, consciousness]
- aliases: [task-unrelated thought, stimulus-independent thought]
- broader: [attention-and-cognitive-control]
- related: [executive-function, default-mode-network, boredom-and-learning, cognitive-flexibility]
- prerequisites: [attention-and-cognitive-control]

**definition**: Mind Wandering is the spontaneous shift of attention from an ongoing primary task to internally generated, task-unrelated thoughts, typically without explicit decision and often without awareness at the moment of onset, and is one of the most empirically common states of waking cognition.

**key_claim**: Mind Wandering is not a unitary failure mode of attention: meta-aware mind-wandering (caught and acknowledged) and tuned-out mind-wandering (ongoing without awareness) have distinct cognitive profiles and distinct relationships to performance, and a measure that lumps them together obscures the meta-awareness component that mediates whether the mind-wandering episode is repaired or extended.

**warning**: Mind Wandering is often framed as uniformly costly, but the empirical record shows context-dependent benefits — for creative incubation, autobiographical planning, and prospective memory — so a blanket prescription against mind-wandering misreads a state that is sometimes the cognitive operation the situation calls for.

## Neuroplasticity

- secondary_domains: [neuroscience, learning-science]
- aliases: [brain plasticity]
- broader: [cognitive-architecture]
- related: [memory-consolidation, sleep-and-memory-consolidation, deliberate-practice, the-power-law-of-practice]
- prerequisites: [cognitive-architecture]

**definition**: Neuroplasticity is the capacity of the nervous system to modify its structural and functional organization in response to experience — through synaptic strengthening and weakening, dendritic remodeling, and in limited regions adult neurogenesis — and is the biological substrate that makes learning possible across the lifespan.

**key_claim**: Neuroplasticity is heavily constrained: the organism does not rewrite arbitrary structure on demand but exhibits experience-dependent changes governed by molecular and circuit-level rules, which is why "neuroplasticity" is an enabling condition for learning rather than an explanation of any specific learning outcome.

**warning**: Neuroplasticity is the most over-claimed concept in popular brain literature, routinely invoked to license claims that any desired change is achievable through any preferred practice; the appropriate inference from plasticity is that change is possible under appropriate conditions, not that any specific intervention will produce any specific change.

## Inhibitory Control

- secondary_domains: [executive-function, developmental-psychology]
- aliases: [response inhibition, behavioral inhibition]
- broader: [executive-function]
- related: [working-memory-capacity, cognitive-flexibility, self-regulation, the-stop-signal-task]
- prerequisites: [executive-function]

**definition**: Inhibitory Control is the executive-function component that suppresses pre-potent or automatic responses in favor of goal-relevant ones — encompassing both behavioral response inhibition (stopping an action) and cognitive interference control (suppressing irrelevant information) — and is one of the three core executive functions in the Miyake unity-and-diversity model.

**key_claim**: Inhibitory Control is causally implicated in a wide range of self-regulatory outcomes — from delay of gratification to academic achievement — and Diamond's developmental work shows that it can be specifically improved by interventions that load it under conditions of joy and competence, distinguishing genuine inhibitory training from generic executive-function programs that fail to transfer.

**warning**: Inhibitory Control is often promoted as a master variable that explains broad self-regulatory differences, but the empirical pattern shows that the construct is composite at best — behavioral and cognitive inhibition correlate only modestly — and treating any single inhibition task as a measure of "inhibition in general" over-aggregates a structured set of distinct sub-abilities.

## Cognitive Ease

- secondary_domains: [metacognition, dual-process-theory]
- aliases: [processing fluency, ease of processing]
- broader: [processing-fluency]
- related: [fluency-illusion, illusion-of-knowing, system-1, dual-process-theory, cognitive-miserliness]
- prerequisites: [dual-process-theory]

**definition**: Cognitive Ease is the subjective sense that mental processing is unfolding smoothly, fluently, and without strain, and is the affective signal — popularized in Kahneman's writing — that the mind uses as a default cue for truth, familiarity, liking, and confidence in the absence of more diagnostic information.

**key_claim**: Cognitive Ease is the central mechanism by which System 1 substitutes a fluency judgment for a substantive judgment of accuracy: the felt smoothness of processing systematically biases the mind toward accepting fluent claims as true, which is why repeated exposure to a claim increases belief in it independent of evidence (the illusory-truth effect).

**warning**: Cognitive Ease cannot be reliably countered by knowing about it; the cue is generated automatically and the warning lives in System 2, so corrective interventions must operate at the structural level (e.g., altering the surface fluency of presented information) rather than relying on the deliberate vigilance the situation systematically dis-affords.

## Overconfidence Bias

- secondary_domains: [metacognition, decision-making]
- aliases: [overconfidence effect, miscalibration]
- broader: [cognitive-bias]
- related: [calibration, retrospective-confidence-judgment, dunning-kruger-effect, illusion-of-knowing]
- prerequisites: [metacognitive-calibration]

**definition**: Overconfidence Bias is the empirical pattern in which subjective probability judgments systematically exceed objective accuracy — most cleanly demonstrated by calibration curves in which 90%-confidence answers are correct substantially less than 90% of the time — and decomposes into overprecision, overestimation, and overplacement variants that have distinct empirical signatures.

**key_claim**: Overconfidence Bias is not a uniform deficit of self-knowledge: the three variants dissociate empirically (a person can be overprecise but well-calibrated on absolute accuracy), and the most consequential variant for decision-making is overprecision, the systematic narrowness of subjective probability distributions relative to the actual error distribution of one's estimates.

**warning**: Overconfidence Bias is sometimes invoked as a universal human flaw, but the bias is moderated by domain familiarity, feedback frequency, and question format; the diagnostic question is not whether the person is overconfident but whether the conditions that produce calibration (rapid, frequent, outcome-coupled feedback) are present in the domain at hand.
