# Batch: Memory Science

## Emotional Memory Enhancement

- domain: memory-science
- secondary_domains: [cognitive-neuroscience, emotion]
- aliases: [emotional enhancement of memory, EEM]
- broader: [memory-consolidation]
- related: [amygdala-and-learning, mood-congruent-memory, flashbulb-memory]
- prerequisites: [long-term-memory]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Emotional Memory Enhancement is the empirical phenomenon in which events with high emotional salience — positive or negative — are encoded more strongly and retained longer than affectively neutral events, mediated by amygdala-driven modulation of hippocampal consolidation.

**key_claim**: Emotional Memory Enhancement does not improve memory uniformly: it sharpens central, emotionally relevant details (the "weapon focus" effect) while degrading peripheral details, producing memories that feel vivid and accurate but are systematically narrowed.

**warning**: Emotional Memory Enhancement is often misread as evidence for the high reliability of vivid memories, but the same neuromodulatory cascade that strengthens central detail also increases susceptibility to post-event distortion, so subjective vividness is not a credible cue to factual fidelity.

## Errorless Learning

- domain: learning-science
- secondary_domains: [memory-science, instructional-design, neuropsychology]
- aliases: [errorless training]
- broader: [instructional-method]
- related: [productive-failure, scaffolding, retrieval-practice, desirable-difficulties]
- prerequisites: [explicit-memory, implicit-memory]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Errorless Learning is an instructional approach in which the learner is prevented from making errors during acquisition by tightly scaffolded prompts and gradual fading, so that no incorrect responses are encoded into the trace.

**key_claim**: Errorless Learning shows the largest benefit for populations with impaired explicit memory — most famously amnesic patients — because their preserved implicit system would otherwise consolidate erroneous responses without the explicit corrective tagging healthy learners use.

**warning**: Errorless Learning is sometimes generalized as superior for all learners, but in intact populations the literature on desirable difficulties shows that productive errors followed by feedback typically outperform Errorless Learning on long-term retention and transfer.

## Forward Testing Effect

- domain: memory-science
- secondary_domains: [learning-science, cognitive-psychology]
- aliases: [test-potentiated new learning, FTE]
- broader: [testing-effect]
- related: [retrieval-practice, proactive-interference, retrieval-induced-facilitation]
- prerequisites: [testing-effect, working-memory]
- confidence: high

**definition**: The Forward Testing Effect is the finding that testing on an earlier list of material improves the learning of subsequent, unrelated material, in addition to the well-known benefit testing confers on the tested material itself.

**key_claim**: The Forward Testing Effect is theoretically important because it cannot be reduced to direct strengthening of the tested items; it shows that retrieval acts as a metacognitive reset that releases the learner from accumulated proactive interference and recalibrates encoding for what comes next.

**warning**: The Forward Testing Effect is sometimes mis-cited as evidence that any quiz, of any kind, helps subsequent learning; the experimental literature shows the effect requires meaningful retrieval — recognition tests and cued cues with low retrieval demand often fail to produce it.

## Hippocampal Neocortical Transfer

- domain: cognitive-neuroscience
- secondary_domains: [memory-science, neuroscience-of-learning]
- aliases: [systems consolidation, complementary learning systems]
- broader: [memory-consolidation]
- related: [sleep-dependent-consolidation, schema-theory, declarative-memory]
- prerequisites: [hippocampus, long-term-memory]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Hippocampal Neocortical Transfer is the model of long-term memory consolidation in which initially hippocampus-dependent episodic traces are gradually integrated into distributed neocortical representations through repeated reactivation, especially during sleep, eventually becoming retrievable without hippocampal mediation.

**key_claim**: Hippocampal Neocortical Transfer explains why retrograde amnesia following hippocampal damage typically follows a temporal gradient — recent memories are lost while remote memories are spared — because remote memories have already been transferred to networks the lesion does not affect.

**warning**: Hippocampal Neocortical Transfer is easily caricatured as a one-way "upload" from hippocampus to cortex; the modern view treats consolidation as continuous re-encoding and re-integration, so a "consolidated" memory is not a fixed file but a recurrently re-constructed representation.

## Imagination Inflation

- domain: memory-science
- secondary_domains: [cognitive-psychology, false-memory]
- aliases: [imagined-event inflation]
- broader: [false-memory, source-monitoring]
- related: [misinformation-effect, source-monitoring-error, cryptomnesia]
- prerequisites: [episodic-memory, source-monitoring]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Imagination Inflation is the experimentally demonstrated phenomenon in which merely imagining a counterfactual event increases the subjective confidence that the event actually occurred in one's past, with the inflation effect rising as imagination is repeated and elaborated.

**key_claim**: Imagination Inflation reflects a failure of source monitoring: the perceptual and contextual richness produced by vivid imagination resembles the cues people normally use to distinguish memory from imagination, eroding the boundary precisely as elaboration increases.

**warning**: Imagination Inflation has direct forensic implications: any therapeutic, journalistic, or interrogative practice that asks a subject to repeatedly imagine a possible past event is likely to produce false confidence in that event, whether or not the practitioner intends to suggest it.

## Keyword Mnemonic Method

- domain: learning-science
- secondary_domains: [memory-science, vocabulary-acquisition]
- aliases: [keyword method, Atkinson keyword method]
- broader: [mnemonic-technique]
- related: [method-of-loci, dual-coding, elaborative-encoding]
- prerequisites: [working-memory, encoding-specificity]
- confidence: high

**definition**: The Keyword Mnemonic Method is a vocabulary-learning technique in which a foreign word is bound to a phonetically similar native-language "keyword" and an interactive mental image links that keyword to the word's meaning, leveraging dual coding to anchor an arbitrary form-meaning mapping.

**key_claim**: The Keyword Mnemonic Method consistently produces large initial gains in vocabulary acquisition relative to rote rehearsal, because the technique converts a sparse phonological cue into a richly interconnected episodic representation that retrieval can re-enter through multiple paths.

**warning**: The Keyword Mnemonic Method shows weaker advantages on long-term, productive use of vocabulary; the keyword scaffolding can interfere with fluent retrieval once it must be dropped, so the technique should be staged into spaced retrieval practice rather than treated as a stable end-state.

## Method of Loci

- domain: memory-science
- secondary_domains: [learning-science, mnemonic-technique]
- aliases: [memory palace, loci method]
- broader: [mnemonic-technique]
- related: [keyword-mnemonic-method, dual-coding, spatial-memory]
- prerequisites: [spatial-memory, episodic-memory]
- confidence: high

**definition**: The Method of Loci is an ancient mnemonic technique in which items to be remembered are placed at distinct locations along a well-known mental route, so that retrieval consists of mentally walking the route and "picking up" the items in order.

**key_claim**: The Method of Loci works because it offloads sequential information onto pre-existing spatial schemata, exploiting the brain's evolutionarily over-trained navigational system to provide structured retrieval cues for arbitrary content.

**warning**: The Method of Loci is sometimes presented as a panacea for all memory problems, but its benefits concentrate on serial-list recall and decline sharply for material that must be retrieved in flexible, query-driven ways rather than as a fixed sequence.

## Mood Congruent Memory

- domain: cognitive-psychology
- secondary_domains: [memory-science, emotion]
- aliases: [mood congruence effect, MCM]
- broader: [memory-bias, encoding-specificity]
- related: [emotional-memory-enhancement, state-dependent-memory, depression-cognition]
- prerequisites: [emotion, episodic-memory]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Mood Congruent Memory is the empirical regularity that material whose affective valence matches the rememberer's current mood is encoded more deeply and retrieved more readily than material whose valence is incongruent with mood.

**key_claim**: Mood Congruent Memory provides a cognitive mechanism for the maintenance of affective disorders: a depressed mood selectively recruits negatively-valenced memories, which in turn sustain the mood, producing a self-perpetuating loop that is targeted directly by cognitive therapies.

**warning**: Mood Congruent Memory is often confused with state-dependent memory, but the two effects are dissociable: mood congruence concerns the valence match between content and mood, whereas state dependence concerns reinstatement of the same internal state regardless of content valence.
