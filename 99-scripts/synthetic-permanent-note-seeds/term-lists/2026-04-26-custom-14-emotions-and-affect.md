# Batch: Emotions and Affect

## Affect Labeling

- domain: emotion
- secondary_domains: [emotion-regulation, neuroscience]
- aliases: [name it to tame it, emotional labeling]
- broader: [emotion-regulation]
- related: [emotional-granularity, cognitive-reappraisal, amygdala-regulation, interoception]
- prerequisites: [emotion-regulation]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Affect Labeling is the process of putting feelings into words by assigning a verbal label to a current emotional state, and the consistent finding that doing so attenuates the intensity of the labeled emotion at both subjective and physiological levels.

**key_claim**: Neuroimaging work by Matthew Lieberman and colleagues shows that Affect Labeling produces increased activation in right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex coupled with decreased activation in the amygdala — a top-down regulatory signature — even when subjects are not instructed to regulate, suggesting Affect Labeling functions as an incidental form of emotion regulation rather than mere description.

**warning**: Affect Labeling is often misread as a universal emotion-regulation panacea, but its effectiveness depends on accurate, granular labeling; vague or inaccurate labels can entrench rumination rather than reduce it, and chronic over-labeling in clinical populations can shade into the experiential avoidance patterns that emotion-focused therapies explicitly try to undo.

## Alexithymia

- domain: emotion
- secondary_domains: [clinical-psychology, personality-psychology]
- aliases: [emotional blindness, no-words-for-feelings]
- broader: [emotion-processing-disorders]
- related: [interoception, affect-labeling, emotional-granularity, autism]
- prerequisites: [emotion]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Alexithymia is a personality construct denoting difficulty identifying feelings, difficulty describing feelings to others, and an externally-oriented thinking style, operationalized chiefly through the Toronto Alexithymia Scale and present at clinically elevated levels in roughly ten percent of the general population.

**key_claim**: Alexithymia accounts for substantial variance in social and emotional difficulties previously attributed to overlapping conditions: when controlling for Alexithymia, much of the empathy deficit attributed to autism disappears, suggesting Alexithymia is a transdiagnostic dimension whose effects have been silently absorbed into other diagnostic categories.

**warning**: Alexithymia is frequently conflated with emotional suppression or avoidance, but the construct as defined targets the upstream identification problem rather than a downstream regulation choice; treating Alexithymia interventions as exposure therapy for "people who avoid feelings" misunderstands a deficit that is more about interoceptive granularity than about motivated avoidance.

## Emotional Granularity

- domain: emotion
- secondary_domains: [emotion-regulation, affective-science]
- aliases: [emotion differentiation, fine-grained emotion experience]
- broader: [emotion-regulation]
- related: [affect-labeling, alexithymia, constructed-emotion, interoception]
- prerequisites: [emotion]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Emotional Granularity is the degree to which an individual experiences and represents emotional states with specificity — distinguishing, for example, "frustrated" from "irritated" from "resentful" — rather than collapsing them into broad valence-arousal categories such as "bad" or "upset."

**key_claim**: Lisa Feldman Barrett's research program has shown that high Emotional Granularity is associated with more flexible emotion regulation, lower psychopathology, and better health outcomes, and her constructed-emotion theory frames Emotional Granularity as the conceptual vocabulary the brain uses to make predictions about its own affective states.

**warning**: Emotional Granularity is sometimes promoted as if simply learning more emotion words automatically increases it, but the construct depends on differentiated experience, not differentiated vocabulary; vocabulary expansion without corresponding interoceptive practice can produce the appearance of granularity in self-report while leaving the underlying representational structure unchanged.

## Emotional Suppression

- domain: emotion
- secondary_domains: [emotion-regulation, clinical-psychology]
- aliases: [expressive suppression, response-focused regulation]
- broader: [emotion-regulation]
- related: [cognitive-reappraisal, gross-process-model, experiential-avoidance, rumination]
- prerequisites: [emotion-regulation]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Emotional Suppression, in James Gross's process model of emotion regulation, is a response-focused strategy in which the individual inhibits the outward behavioral expression of an already-generated emotion, contrasted with antecedent-focused strategies such as cognitive reappraisal that intervene earlier in the emotion-generation sequence.

**key_claim**: Emotional Suppression is consistently associated with worse outcomes than reappraisal across affective, cognitive, and physiological measures: it does not reduce subjective negative emotion, it impairs memory for the emotional event, and it elevates sympathetic-nervous-system arousal, identifying it as a high-cost regulation strategy that pays an inner price for outer composure.

**warning**: The cross-cultural psychology of Emotional Suppression complicates the standard "suppression is bad" reading; in cultures with stronger interdependent self-construals, expressive suppression has weaker or even reversed associations with wellbeing, indicating that the costs of Emotional Suppression are partly mediated by whether suppression is experienced as self-betrayal or as appropriate social calibration.

## Interoception

- domain: emotion
- secondary_domains: [neuroscience, affective-science]
- aliases: [interoceptive awareness, visceral perception]
- broader: [perception]
- related: [emotional-granularity, alexithymia, predictive-processing, insula]
- prerequisites: [perception]
- confidence: high

**definition**: Interoception is the perception of the internal state of the body — heartbeat, respiration, gut sensations, temperature, fatigue — and is theoretically partitioned into interoceptive accuracy (objective task performance), interoceptive sensibility (self-reported attention to bodily signals), and interoceptive awareness (the metacognitive correspondence between the two).

**key_claim**: Interoception is now widely argued to be the bodily substrate of subjective emotional experience: theories from Damasio's somatic markers to Barrett's constructed emotion treat affective feelings as the brain's interpretation of Interoceptive signals, predicting that disorders of Interoception should produce characteristic affective and decision-making disturbances, a prediction supported by anxiety, depression, and eating-disorder research.

**warning**: Interoception's three components do not move together, and treating "interoception" as a single ability is the most common error in the literature; high interoceptive sensibility (paying a lot of attention to the body) without accuracy is in fact a vulnerability factor for anxiety and somatic symptom disorders, so Interoception interventions need to target the awareness-accuracy alignment rather than simply increase attention to the body.
