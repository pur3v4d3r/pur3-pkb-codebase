## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Level 3: Substructure — The Components Beneath the Mechanism
> **Zoom progression:** at the previous level, element interactivity appeared as a count of mutually constraining elements. This level opens the construct itself and asks what an "element" *is* — a question whose answer is neither stable across the literature nor unproblematic in practice.
> **What you'll see at this level:** the operational definitions and ambiguities surrounding the *element*, the relational structure that constitutes *interactivity*, the connection to relational reasoning research outside CLT (Halford et al.), and why the construct's apparent precision conceals genuine indeterminacy.
> **Specialist value:** anyone reading the CLT literature critically will encounter the question "what counts as an element?" in nearly every empirical paper, often answered tacitly and in mutually incompatible ways. Without seeing this layer one cannot evaluate the literature; with it one acquires a critical apparatus that the standard exposition does not provide.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-element-interactivity-deep-dive-2026-04-20]]*

> [!evidence] Level 3: Substructure — The Components Beneath the Mechanism
> **Zoom progression:** Level 2 revealed that walking-while-reading has low structural resource overlap, with residual interference concentrated at the Central Executive. This level reveals the **specific neural circuits and cognitive subsystems** that produce this pattern — the cerebellar-cortical loops that automate locomotion, the left-lateralized language network that drives reading, and the prefrontal executive circuits that mediate between them.
> **What you'll see at this level:** The neural implementation of resource separation, the specific conditions under which automaticity breaks down, and the circuitry that makes walking either cognitively "free" or cognitively "expensive."
> **Specialist value:** Moving from box-and-arrow cognitive models to neural circuit-level analysis, which reveals important constraints and predictions that the cognitive models alone miss.
> *— [[walking-academic-reading-working-memory-deep-dive-2026-04-13_report]]*
