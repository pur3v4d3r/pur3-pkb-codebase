## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Diagnostic Power of a Failure
> Imagine two students, both of whom fail the same difficult exam. Student A, operating under mastery goals, asks: "What did I misunderstand? What do I need to study differently?" She schedules more practice, seeks a study partner, asks the instructor about the concepts she missed, and returns to the next exam with stronger understanding. Student B, operating under performance-avoidance goals, asks: "What does this say about my ability?" He concludes that he is not a "maths person," avoids asking for help (which would reveal his confusion publicly), reduces the effort he invests in subsequent assessments (offering himself the excuse of "not really trying"), and eventually withdraws from maths-related coursework. These divergent responses are not determined by the failure itself — they are determined by the goal framework through which the failure is interpreted.
> *— [[achievement-goal-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-10_report]]*
