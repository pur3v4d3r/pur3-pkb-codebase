## Examples

> [!example] Control Orientation in Occupational Contexts
> A manager with high control orientation frames their professional identity primarily through hierarchical status and the approval of superiors. When a project succeeds, they experience satisfaction primarily through recognition and credit attribution. When a project encounters difficulties, their first concern is how it reflects on their reputation and standing. They monitor organizational feedback closely, are highly sensitive to perceived slights in evaluation processes, and regulate their behavior primarily through contingencies of social approval and consequence avoidance. Their motivation is high but brittle, dependent on a continuous stream of positive external validation.
