## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] The Depth-Breadth Trade-off in Deliberate Vocabulary Practice
> **Position A:** Vocabulary depth (full lexical quality — collocations, register, connotation, morphological family) should be the primary target of deliberate practice, because deep knowledge of a smaller vocabulary is more cognitively functional than shallow knowledge of a larger one.
>
> **Position B:** Vocabulary breadth (recognition-level knowledge of as many word families as possible) should be the primary target, because coverage threshold effects mean that hitting 98% coverage of diverse text is more important than any individual word's depth.
>
> **Current Evidence State:** The two positions address different use cases. For reading comprehension (especially at challenging levels), breadth is primary — you need to know enough words to achieve coverage. For professional/academic communication, depth is primary — precise use of technical vocabulary requires deep collocational knowledge. For most adult learners, initial prioritization of breadth (to reach the coverage threshold) should give way to depth once coverage is established.
>
> **This Report's Stance:** The coverage threshold analysis strongly supports a breadth-first strategy for readers who have not yet reached 98% coverage of their target genre. The depth-frequency multiplier then argues for depth once coverage is established — not as either/or but as sequential.
> *— [[reading-vocabulary-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
