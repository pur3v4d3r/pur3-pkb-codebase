## Examples

> [!example] Derivation Example: The Worked Example Effect
> **Surface prescription:** "Use worked examples rather than problem-solving for novices."
>
> **Mechanism-level derivation:**
> 1. Problem-solving with novel material requires the learner to search for a solution path. This search generates element interactivity between the current problem state, the goal state, possible operators, and subgoals — none of which are encapsulated in schemas because the material is novel.
> 2. Worked examples *borrow and reorganize* — they present the expert's solution schema directly, allowing the learner to process the solution steps and their relationships without the additional element interactivity of means-ends search.
> 3. Therefore, worked examples reduce effective element interactivity relative to problem-solving. They succeed because they reduce interactivity, and they reduce interactivity because they provide schema-level organization that the novice lacks.
>
> This derivation also immediately predicts the expertise reversal effect: once the learner has acquired the relevant schemas, worked examples become *redundant information* that adds extraneous element interactivity (the learner must process both their own schema and the presented solution, integrating or reconciling them). The same mechanism — element interactivity — generates both the original effect and its reversal.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*
