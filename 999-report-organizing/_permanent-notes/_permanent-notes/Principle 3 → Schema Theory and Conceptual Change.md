## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Principle 3 → Schema Theory and Conceptual Change
> The Integration Paradox has a precise structural analogue in [[schema-theory]] and [[Conceptual-Change-Theory]]. Schema accommodation — the revision of existing cognitive frameworks in response to anomalous data — requires a foundational schema stable enough to recognize the anomaly as anomalous. A student who lacks the foundational schema cannot experience the disequilibration that triggers accommodation; they can only ignore or distort the anomalous input through assimilation. Exactly like SDT's Integration Paradox: the process that is supposed to revise the cognitive structure requires a stable cognitive structure as a prerequisite. *Transfer encoding*: When encountering a learner who does not respond to conceptual change interventions, check whether the prerequisite schema is in place — not merely whether the student has encountered the anomalous information. The integration paradox applies to cognitive as well as motivational development.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-19_report]]*
