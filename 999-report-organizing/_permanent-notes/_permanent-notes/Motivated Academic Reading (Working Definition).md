# Motivated Academic Reading (Working Definition)

> [!definition] Motivated Academic Reading (Working Definition)
> Motivated academic reading refers to the self-initiated, sustained engagement with expository, argumentative, or scholarly texts in which the reader actively processes content for meaning, pursues understanding beyond surface recall, and maintains this engagement under conditions of cognitive effort and difficulty. It is distinguished from both passive reading (attention without comprehension pursuit) and compliance-driven reading (reading to satisfy external requirements rather than internal understanding goals).
>
> **Boundary:** The term does not presuppose enjoyment in every reading session, nor does it exclude reading assigned texts — the crucial variable is the reader's regulatory orientation (autonomous vs. controlled) and the depth of processing pursued, not the text's origin or genre.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This definition frames reading motivation as a quality of engagement, not a personality property — which means it is malleable and subject to evidence-based intervention.
>
> **See also:** [[intrinsic-motivation]], [[autonomous-motivation]], [[deep-processing]], [[self-regulated-learning]]
