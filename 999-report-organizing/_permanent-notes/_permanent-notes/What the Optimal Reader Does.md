## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] What the Optimal Reader Does
> Open the YAML frontmatter and scan it **before reading a single line of body text**. The metadata is a high-density orientation layer. It tells you what frameworks are in play, what the author's analytical focus is, and which PKB nodes you should have active before diving in.
> *— [[worked-example-optimal-reading-session_report]]*

> [!evidence] What the Optimal Reader Does
> Before reading, write down — without consulting any notes — what you already know about the domain. This serves two functions: (1) it activates relevant schema in long-term memory, priming retrieval pathways for what you're about to read; (2) it creates a baseline against which to measure genuine learning.
> *— [[worked-example-optimal-reading-session_report]]*

> [!evidence] What the Optimal Reader Does
> Formulate 3–5 specific learning goals before reading. These should be proximal (achievable this session) and process-focused (what you'll be able to *do* with the knowledge, not just *know* abstractly). Then generate predictive questions — forced attempts to answer the report's central questions before you encounter the author's answers.
> *— [[worked-example-optimal-reading-session_report]]*
