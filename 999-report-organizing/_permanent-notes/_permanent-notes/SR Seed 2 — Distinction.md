## Flashcards

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 2 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the critical distinction between intrinsic cognitive load and extraneous cognitive load?
> **A:** Intrinsic load is determined by the element interactivity inherent in the material-learner interaction and can only be managed (through sequencing and scaffolding), not eliminated. Extraneous load is imposed by suboptimal instructional design and can and should be minimized, as it represents waste of limited working memory resources.
> **Source:** Section 2
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Tags:** #clt #intrinsic-load #extraneous-load #distinction

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 2 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the key distinction between mastery-approach and performance-approach goals in Elliot's 2×2 Achievement Goal framework?
> **A:** Mastery-approach goals are oriented toward developing competence (learning, improving); performance-approach goals are oriented toward demonstrating competence relative to others (outperforming, proving ability). Both have approach valence, but mastery goals are associated with deeper processing and intrinsic interest while performance goals are associated with surface strategies.

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 2 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the difference between storage strength and retrieval strength in the Bjork framework?
> **A:** Storage strength reflects how well information is encoded in long-term memory (increases with encoding, does not decay). Retrieval strength reflects current accessibility (decays over time even when storage strength is high). Knowledge can be stored but inaccessible.
> **Source:** Section 4
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 2 — Distinction
> **Q:** How does SDT distinguish between autonomy and independence?
> **A:** Autonomy means endorsement — the experience that behavior emanates from one's own values. Independence means self-reliance and separation from others. One can be autonomous while interdependent (freely choosing to follow another's guidance).
> **Source:** Section 2, Section 9
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
