## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Self-Regulated Learning as a Resource Allocation Problem Under Uncertainty
> Synthesizing the mechanisms examined in Phases II through V, this analysis proposes a reconceptualization that resolves several tensions in the existing literature: **SRL is fundamentally a problem of resource allocation under uncertainty, governed by a decision architecture that is simultaneously cognitive, motivational, and metacognitive.**
>
> The learner in a learning situation faces continuous allocation decisions: How much cognitive effort should be devoted to the current strategy versus exploring alternatives? How much attentional capacity should be dedicated to monitoring versus to task execution? How much motivational resource should be invested in this domain given the expected return on regulatory effort? These allocation decisions are made by the self-regulatory system under conditions of inherent uncertainty about the quality of one's current understanding (the monitoring uncertainty), the effectiveness of one's current strategy (the control uncertainty), and the expected return on continued investment (the efficacy-value uncertainty).
>
> This reconceptualization resolves the domain-generality tension: domain-specific knowledge reduces monitoring uncertainty (the learner knows what good understanding looks like), which is why regulatory transfer across domains is difficult — it is not that self-regulatory skills don't transfer, but that the monitoring uncertainty that those skills operate under is substantially higher in new domains. It resolves the known-doing gap: knowing what effective self-regulation looks like does not solve the allocation problem — learners may know that monitoring is valuable while allocating insufficient resources to it because the motivational calculus (goal orientation, task value, efficacy expectations) suppresses the perceived expected return on monitoring investment. And it suggests a novel intervention target: interventions that reduce monitoring uncertainty directly — through feedback that makes comprehension quality more visible, through worked examples that establish reference standards for quality, or through AI-assisted process tracking — may be more effective than interventions that teach monitoring strategies in the abstract, because they address the root resource allocation problem rather than expanding the toolkit available for solving it.
> *— [[self-regulated-learning-focused-analysis-2026-03-06_report]]*
