> [!person] Albert Bandura (1925–2021)
> Social cognitive psychologist at Stanford; originator of Social Learning Theory and its successor Social Cognitive Theory, with self-efficacy as the central motivational mechanism. Self-Efficacy Theory represents the most extensively validated theory in motivational psychology, with the largest meta-analytic base for educational outcomes. Key works: *Social Learning Theory* (1977); *Social Foundations of Thought and Action* (1986); *Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control* (1997).
>
> **Lineage:** Extended behaviorist accounts of observational learning (Skinner) while fundamentally departing from behaviorism through cognitive agency constructs; influenced Zimmerman's SRL model (via self-efficacy as a forethought mechanism), Schunk's instructional research, and Pajares's work on academic self-efficacy.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the self-efficacy framework and, specifically, the verbal persuasion source that explains why vocabulary choice functions mechanistically rather than decoratively.
> *— [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25_report]]*

> [!person] Albert Bandura (1925–2021)
> Canadian-American psychologist, Stanford University. Developer of self-efficacy theory (1977, 1997) and social cognitive theory. Identified mastery experience as the primary source of self-efficacy information, but the attributional interpretation of that experience — not the experience itself — determines its efficacy impact. The attribution-self-efficacy interface is one of the most productive theoretical connections in motivation research.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!person] Albert Bandura (1925–2021)
> **Core Contribution:** Developed Social Cognitive Theory and Self-Efficacy Theory, identifying four sources of self-efficacy (mastery experiences, vicarious learning, verbal persuasion, physiological/affective cues) and establishing the role of self-efficacy in motivation, strategy use, and persistence.

> **Relationship to Other Figures:** Social Cognitive Theory influenced virtually all subsequent work in educational psychology; his emphasis on verbal persuasion as a self-efficacy source provides the theoretical basis for this report's treatment of inner speech as self-administered verbal persuasion.

> [!person] Albert Bandura (1925–2021)
> **Core Contribution:** Social Cognitive Theory and, for SRL specifically, the self-efficacy construct and its updating mechanisms through mastery experiences, vicarious learning, verbal persuasion, and physiological states.
>
> **Relationship to Other Figures:** Zimmerman was a doctoral student in Bandura's tradition; the SRL cycle's motivational architecture (self-efficacy as state variable) is directly Bandura's. Bernard Weiner's attribution theory was incorporated by Zimmerman as the mechanism through which mastery experiences (performance outcomes) are interpreted and translated into self-efficacy updates.
>
> **Key Works:** *Social Foundations of Thought and Action* (1986); *Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control* (1997).
>
> **Relevance to This Analysis:** Bandura's mastery experience construct provides the mechanistic grounding for the self-efficacy updating function analyzed in Phase IV. The asymmetric risk in self-efficacy updating (ability attributions for failure damage self-efficacy more durably than defensive attributions after success) follows directly from Bandura's treatment of self-efficacy sources and stability.
> *— [[zimmerman-cyclical-srl-focused-analysis-2026-03-23_report]]*
