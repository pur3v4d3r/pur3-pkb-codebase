## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Readiness Trap
> One of the most serious limitations in SDL research and practice is the risk of assuming that because SDL is a valued educational goal, SDL-structured learning environments are appropriate for all learners at all stages. The evidence consistently suggests that learners with low SDL readiness perform worse, not better, in fully SDL-structured environments without additional support — experiencing confusion, anxiety, and feelings of abandonment rather than empowerment. Placing low-readiness learners in unscaffolded SDL environments in the name of promoting autonomy is a failure of instructional design, not an application of SDL theory. SDL theory actually calls for graduated support that is progressively withdrawn as readiness develops — a process that requires sophisticated ongoing diagnosis of where each learner is in their development.
> *— [[self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*
