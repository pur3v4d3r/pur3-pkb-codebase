## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Attainment Paradox: Goal Achievement Is Not a Universal Good
> The Niemiec et al. findings constitute the most theoretically provocative evidence in the GCT literature, and they deserve careful interpretation. The finding that attaining extrinsic goals produces little or no well-being gain — and can produce ill-being increases — directly challenges a widespread assumption embedded in both folk psychology and much of goal-setting research: that the core function of goals is to direct effort toward outcomes, and that achieving goals is good. GCT's evidence suggests this is only half right. The direction-setting and effort-mobilizing functions of goals are important, but the *content* of what goals aim toward determines whether attainment produces the anticipated psychological returns. This is what makes GCT a genuinely important contribution: it forces a qualitative question into a domain that has often been treated in purely quantitative terms.
> *— [[goal-contents-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-13_report]]*
