# Metacognitive Calibration (Nelson, 1996)

> [!definition] Metacognitive Calibration (Nelson, 1996)
> [[metacognitive-calibration|Metacognitive calibration]] refers to the correspondence between a learner's metacognitive judgments and the learner's actual cognitive performance. A perfectly calibrated learner who assigns a 70% probability to recalling an item would recall items so rated exactly 70% of the time. Calibration is typically assessed through calibration curves (plotting mean confidence against mean accuracy at each confidence level) and through summary statistics such as the calibration score, the over/underconfidence index, and the resolution score (the extent to which confidence discriminates between correct and incorrect responses).
>
> **Boundary:** Calibration is not the only measure of monitoring quality. Resolution — the ability to discriminate between items that will and will not be recalled — can be high even when calibration is poor (the learner may overestimate recall across the board but still accurately rank items by relative difficulty).
>
> **See also:** [[calibration]], [[metacognitive-accuracy]], [[Metacognitive-Monitoring-Accuracy-and-Calibration]]
