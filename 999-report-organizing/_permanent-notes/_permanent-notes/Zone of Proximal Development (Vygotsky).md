# Zone of Proximal Development (Vygotsky)

> [!definition] Zone of Proximal Development (Vygotsky)
> The Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) designates the distance between what a learner can accomplish independently and what the same learner can accomplish with the guidance or collaboration of a more capable partner — teacher, peer, or any more competent interlocutor. It is the space in which learning, on Vygotsky's account, actually occurs: not below the learner's independent capability (where no challenge exists) and not beyond the reach of supported performance (where the task remains incomprehensible), but in the dynamic zone where competence is emerging and can be catalyzed by appropriate social support.
>
> **Boundary:** The ZPD is not a fixed property of the individual but a relational property between the individual and the task in a social context. It shifts as learning occurs. The concept is sometimes reduced to a simple performance deficit, but Vygotsky's intent was richer: the ZPD captures the developmental potential of the learner — what they are in the process of becoming, not merely what they currently lack.
>
> **Common Misconception:** The ZPD is frequently equated with "scaffolding." Vygotsky himself never used the term "scaffolding" — it was coined by [[jerome-bruner]], David Wood, and Gail Ross to operationalize the instruction-in-the-ZPD concept. The ZPD is the zone; scaffolding is one mechanism for working within it.
>
