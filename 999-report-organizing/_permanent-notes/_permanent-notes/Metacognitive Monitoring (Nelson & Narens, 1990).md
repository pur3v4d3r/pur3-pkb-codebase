# Metacognitive Monitoring (Nelson & Narens, 1990)

> [!definition] Metacognitive Monitoring (Nelson & Narens, 1990)
> The process by which a learner's meta-level representation is updated by information flowing from the object-level — that is, the process of generating internal assessments of one's own cognitive states, processes, and products. In PKM, monitoring manifests as the capacity to evaluate one's own understanding of a concept, to detect gaps or inconsistencies in one's knowledge structure, and to assess whether one's current learning strategy is producing genuine comprehension or merely the experience of familiarity.
>
> **Boundary:** Monitoring accuracy must be distinguished from monitoring *confidence*. A learner can monitor confidently and inaccurately (the illusion of comprehension) or monitor tentatively and accurately (productive uncertainty). The calibration between monitoring confidence and actual performance is a key indicator of metacognitive expertise.
>
> **See also:** [[metacognition]], [[metacognitive-monitoring]], [[nelson-narens-model]], [[calibration]], [[metacognitive-regulation]]

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Monitoring (Nelson & Narens, 1990)
> Metacognitive monitoring is the meta-level process of acquiring information about the current state of object-level cognitive processes. It is operationalized through prospective and retrospective judgments: Ease of Learning (EOL) judgments made before study, Judgments of Learning (JOL) made during or after study, Feelings of Knowing (FOK) about accessible but currently unrecalled information, and Retrospective Confidence Judgments post-response. Monitoring provides the informational input to both real-time metacognitive control and episodic metacognitive reflection.
>
> **Boundary:** Metacognitive monitoring is NOT identical to metacognitive reflection — monitoring is continuous, often automatic, and provides raw signals; reflection is episodic, effortful, and works on accumulated monitoring outputs to generate metacognitive knowledge revisions.
>
> **See also:** [[metacognitive-monitoring]], [[nelson-narens-model]], [[feeling-of-knowing]], [[judgment-of-learning]]

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Monitoring (Nelson & Narens, 1990)
> [[metacognitive-monitoring|Metacognitive monitoring]] is the upward flow of information from the object-level to the meta-level, through which the meta-level constructs and updates its model of the object-level's state. Monitoring is cue-based and inferential: the meta-level does not have direct access to the object-level's state but infers it from available cues such as processing fluency, retrieval latency, and partial information accessibility.
>
> **Boundary:** Monitoring is not equivalent to accurate self-assessment. The accuracy of monitoring depends on the diagnosticity of the cues available, which varies across conditions.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The cue-based nature of monitoring is the mechanism that makes the meta-level's model imperfect, generating the framework's characteristic predictions about calibration and metacognitive illusions.
>
> **See also:** [[Metacognitive-Monitoring-Accuracy-and-Calibration]], [[cue-utilization-framework]], [[processing-fluency]]
