> [!flashcard] Seed 12 — Definition
> **Q:** What is the predicative compression problem in inner speech, and why does it create vocabulary risk during academic reading?
> **A:** Vygotsky's inner speech is condensed and predicative — only the dynamic predicate is retained, not the full propositional subject. During reading, a complex comprehension failure may compress to a single predicate like "can't" or "lost," which bundles together radically different attribution types (ability failure, strategy failure, prior knowledge gap). The risk is that compressed predicates automatically activate ability attributions (the most cognitively economical inference) rather than the more accurate, more controllable strategy or knowledge attributions — initiating the vicious attribution-efficacy spiral.
> **Source:** Phase II
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #inner-speech #vygotsky #attribution #predicative-compression

> [!flashcard] Seed 12 — Definition
> **Q:** What does "self-regulated reading" mean and how does it relate to Zimmerman's cyclical SRL model?
> **A:** Self-regulated reading is metacognitive, goal-directed, and strategically enacted text comprehension. Maps onto Zimmerman's three phases: (1) Forethought = goal-setting, prior knowledge activation, strategy planning; (2) Performance = comprehension monitoring, strategy deployment, attention regulation; (3) Self-reflection = outcome evaluation, attribution, strategy adaptation. Distinguishes reading as self-directed enterprise from externally regulated strategy use.
> **Source:** Phase III, Section 3.4; Phase II, Section 2.1
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #self-regulated-learning, #reading, #metacognition

> [!flashcard] Seed 12 — Definition
> **Q:** What is pseudometacognition, and what practical risk does it pose?
> **A:** Pseudometacognition is the performance of external metacognitive behavior patterns (completing monitoring checklists, articulating study plans, answering self-questioning prompts) without genuine internal monitoring or regulatory adjustment. The practical risk is that learners taught explicit metacognitive protocols may comply with the external script without developing genuine regulatory competence — the protocol becomes a performance rather than a cognitive tool.
> **Source:** Phase V; Appendix Section 1
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #metacognition, #pseudometacognition, #metacognitive-instruction

> [!flashcard] Seed 12 — Definition
> **Q:** What is the hierarchical goal architecture for SRL, and what is the function of each level?
> **A:** Three levels: (1) Distal Learning Goal — the broad capability being developed over months; provides directional motivation and frames the learning agenda. (2) Session Outcome Goal — what will be produced this session; defines scope and provides a milestone. (3) Proximal Process Goals — the specific strategies to be enacted during performance; these are most important for learning quality and are the primary targets of self-evaluation. The levels function together: the distal provides direction, the outcome provides scope, the process goals provide operational guidance.
> **Source:** Phase II
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Tags:** #srl, #goal-architecture, #process-goals, #forethought
> *— [[srl-forethought-selfreflection-pkb-report-2026-03-24_report]]*
