# Fundamental Attribution Error (FAE)

> [!definition] Fundamental Attribution Error (FAE)
> The **Fundamental Attribution Error** (Ross, 1977) is the systematic human tendency to over-attribute another person's behavior to stable dispositional properties and under-attribute it to situational forces. The "fundamental" designation reflects the claim that this is not a peripheral error but a deep architectural feature of the person-perception system — perhaps driven by attentional salience (persons are perceptually figural against situational backgrounds) and by cultural frameworks (particularly in individualistic societies) that conceptualize behavior as primarily person-caused. The FAE is culturally modulated and most pronounced for negative behaviors and in evaluative contexts.
