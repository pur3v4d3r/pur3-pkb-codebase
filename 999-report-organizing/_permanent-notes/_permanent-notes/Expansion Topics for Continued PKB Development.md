## Open Threads

> [!open-question] Expansion Topics for Continued PKB Development
> > [!topic-idea] [[Basic-Psychological-Needs-Theory-BPNT|Basic-Psychological-Needs-Theory-(BPNT)]]
> > *Priority: High* — The SDT mini-theory most closely connected to OIT, specifying autonomy, competence, and relatedness as universal psychological needs whose satisfaction facilitates and whose frustration impedes internalization. Deep engagement with BPNT provides the theoretical infrastructure for OIT's environmental facilitation claims.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[Cognitive-Evaluation-Theory-(CET)]]
> > *Priority: High* — OIT's sibling mini-theory addressing the conditions under which external events (rewards, feedback, surveillance) enhance or diminish intrinsic motivation. Understanding CET's account of functional significance of events is essential for the complete OIT picture.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[contingent-self-worth]]
> > *Priority: High* — The psychological construct most directly related to introjected regulation. Deep engagement with the contingent self-worth literature (Kernis, Crocker, Park) provides complementary perspective on the ego-involvement mechanisms OIT's introjection construct identifies.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[motivational-interviewing]]
> > *Priority: High* — The clinical application framework most directly aligned with OIT's facilitation principles. Understanding MI's evidence base and theoretical structure provides insight into how OIT concepts are operationalized in practice.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[self-regulated-learning]]
> > *Priority: Medium* — The learning science domain most directly connected to OIT's educational applications. Systematic engagement with the SRL literature would allow integration of OIT's motivational framework with cognitive and metacognitive strategy frameworks.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[Cognitive-Dissonance-Theory]]
> > *Priority: Exploratory* — Festinger's theory of attitude change through behavioral enactment has structural similarities to OIT's account of internalization through engaged activity: both posit mechanisms by which behavior under external pressure can, under appropriate conditions, produce genuine attitude/value change.
> *— [[organismic-integration-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*
