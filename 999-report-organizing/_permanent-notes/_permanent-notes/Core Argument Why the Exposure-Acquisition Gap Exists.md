> [!diagram] Core Argument: Why the Exposure-Acquisition Gap Exists
> ```
> PREMISE 1: Durable vocabulary encoding requires deep,
>            semantic-elaborative processing
>            (Levels of Processing Theory — Craik & Lockhart)
>                         ↓
> PREMISE 2: Naturalistic reading for comprehension
>            generates shallow processing of novel words
>            (Goal-structure suppression mechanism)
>                         ↓
> PREMISE 3: Cognitive load dynamics amplify shallow
>            processing for lower-vocabulary readers
>            (Working memory bottleneck mechanism)
>                         ↓
> PREMISE 4: Metacognitive miscalibration prevents readers
>            from detecting partial knowledge states
>            (Fluency-masquerades-as-knowledge mechanism)
>
>  P1 + P2 + P3 + P4
>        ↓
> ════════════════════════════════════════════════
>  CONCLUSION: Reading produces far less vocabulary
>  acquisition per encounter than exposure-quantity
>  models predict because it systematically fails to
>  generate the processing depth that acquisition requires
> ════════════════════════════════════════════════
>        ↓
>  IMPLICATION: Increasing reading volume without
>  restructuring processing depth conditions will
>  produce marginal vocabulary gains
> ```
> **Reading Guide:** Premises 1 and 2 establish the mismatch between what acquisition requires and what reading provides. Premises 3 and 4 explain why the mismatch is worse for lower-ability readers and is self-concealing. The conclusion is the logical product of all four premises.
> *— [[vocabulary-acquisition-reading-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
