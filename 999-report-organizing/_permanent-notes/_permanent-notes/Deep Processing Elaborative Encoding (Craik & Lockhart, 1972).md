# Deep Processing / Elaborative Encoding (Craik & Lockhart, 1972)

> [!definition] Deep Processing / Elaborative Encoding (Craik & Lockhart, 1972)
> The encoding of information at a semantic, relational, and meaningful level, as contrasted with shallow processing of phonological or orthographic features. Deep processing is associated with more durable memory traces, greater resistance to interference, and superior transfer.
>
> **Boundary:** Depth of processing is better understood as a continuum of elaborative richness than as a binary shallow/deep distinction. EI induces processing toward the deep-elaborative end of this continuum, but the richness of the elaboration is itself variable.
>
> **Common Misconception:** Deep processing does not automatically mean slow processing. A highly knowledgeable learner can process a familiar domain at a deep semantic level rapidly because relevant schemata are highly automated.
>
> **See also:** [[levels-of-processing-theory]], [[processing-depth]], [[shallow-processing]], [[fergus-i.-m.-craik]], [[meaningful-learning-theory]]
