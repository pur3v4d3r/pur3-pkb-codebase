# The Internalization Continuum (OIT)

> [!definition] The Internalization Continuum (OIT)
> [[organismic-integration-theory]] describes how externally prompted behavior can be taken in and made progressively more self-determined. The continuum runs from [[external-regulation|external regulation]] (compliance to obtain reward or avoid punishment) through [[introjected-regulation|introjected regulation]] (taking in but not accepting external pressure; ego-involvement, "should") to [[identified-regulation|identified regulation]] (endorsing the value of an activity even if not intrinsically enjoyable) to [[integrated-regulation|integrated regulation]] (full congruence of the activity's value with one's identity and other values). [[intrinsic-motivation|Intrinsic motivation]] — engaging because the activity is inherently interesting or enjoyable — sits outside the internalization continuum as qualitatively distinct. OIT's key claim is that internalization is a natural developmental process that tends toward integration under need-supportive conditions.
