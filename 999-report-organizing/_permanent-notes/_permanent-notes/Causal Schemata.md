# Causal Schemata

> [!definition] Causal Schemata
> **Causal schemata** (Kelley, 1972) are abstract knowledge structures — templates for causal reasoning — that are applied when complete covariation information is unavailable. Rather than performing full ANOVA-style analysis, people apply pre-formed schemas such as the **Multiple Sufficient Causes Schema** (if any one of several possible causes would be sufficient to produce the effect, the presence of one cause discounts inference to others) and the **Multiple Necessary Causes Schema** (if multiple causes are all required to produce an effect, the presence of one supports inference that others also occurred). Causal schemata are cognitive shortcuts that maintain reasonable causal inference under incomplete information.
