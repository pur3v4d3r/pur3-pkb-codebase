> **Q:** What structural analogue connects the vocabulary-modifiability principle from Attribution Theory to clinical therapeutic dialogue?
> **A:** In both attribution research and clinical frameworks (narrative therapy, CBT), the key structural move is from trait vocabulary (what someone *is* — stable, uncontrollable) to process vocabulary (what someone *does* or *how they approached something* — unstable, controllable). In academic reading: "I'm not good at this" → "My strategy for this text type needs development." In therapy: "I am an anxious person" → "I experience anxiety in specific contexts." Same dimensional reassignment principle; different domain surface content.
> **Source:** Phase VII, FAR Transfer, Abstract Principle 1
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Connection + Transfer

> **Q:** What are temporal marker words, and why are they high-leverage in attributional vocabulary for SRL?
> **A:** Temporal markers encode the stability dimension of attributions at the grammatical level: "always/never" = stable/fixed; "so far / not yet / until now" = developmental trajectory (unstable, growth-oriented); "today / in this session / with this type of text" = situationally bounded (low stability, specificity-preserving). They are high-leverage because they directly encode the fixed/growth distinction in a single word — "I can't do this" vs. "I can't yet do this" differ only in the temporal marker "yet," but carry radically different motivational architectures.
> **Source:** Appendix Lexicon — Temporal Marker Vocabulary; Phase III
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Type:** Definition
> **Tags:** #temporal-markers #attribution #vocabulary #growth-mindset

> **Q:** For what population of learners is Pintrich's framework most applicable, according to the Transitional Self-Regulation synthesis?
>
> **A:** Learners in the middle zone of regulatory development — those who have sufficient intrinsic motivation to begin engaging but who lack the regulatory architecture to sustain engagement through difficulty, ambiguity, and extended challenge. Not novice learners with externally regulated motivation (they lack the bootstrap), and not expert learners with fully internalized motivation (they don't need deliberate regulation). This is the modal population of higher education learners.
>
> **Source:** Phase VI, Original Synthesis

> **Type:** Application

> [!flashcard] Seed 10
> **Q**: What is the "disruption before resources" principle, and to which non-cognitive domains does it transfer?
> **A**: Systems in stable attractor states (optimized for current patterns) resist change by assimilating new inputs to old patterns. Disruption — experienced inadequacy of the current pattern — is the engine of deep restructuring. This principle transfers to: (1) organizational change (Lewin's unfreeze-change-refreeze), (2) product design (strategic friction that drives deeper engagement with new features), and (3) psychotherapy (productive confrontation of maladaptive patterns before introducing alternative frameworks).
> **Source**: Phase VI, Abstract Principle 3
> **Difficulty**: Advanced
> **Type**: Application

> [!flashcard] Seed 10
> **Q:** What specific monitoring practice does the reconstructed understanding recommend that the received view never recommends?
>
> **A:** The reconstructed understanding requires situation-model-level monitoring even in the *absence* of confusion — a proactive global coherence check that does not wait for a negative monitoring signal. Specifically: periodically closing or looking away from the text and attempting to reconstruct the situation model (who is involved, what is happening, why it is happening, how it is happening, and where/when it takes place). This is the opposite of the received view's implicit guidance, which treats the absence of confusion as evidence of comprehension. The metacognitive inversion required: felt comprehension (fluency) is evidence of text-base success, not situation model success.
>
> **Source:** Phase V, Metacognitive Inversion section

> **Type:** Application

> [!flashcard] Seed 10
> **Q:** What does the SDT research add to the language design requirements for SRL tools that Achievement Goal Theory does not cover?
> **A:** SDT's autonomy support research adds that *controlling language* — even self-directed controlling language without any external agent — undermines intrinsic motivation. "I must understand this" generates internal pressure that is phenomenologically equivalent to external control and activates the same amotivation mechanisms. Achievement Goal Theory covers the mastery/performance orientation distinction but does not address the autonomy dimension — the difference between "I want to understand X because it connects to [valued goal]" (autonomy-supportive) and "I must understand X" (controlling). Both are mastery-framed; only one is autonomy-supportive.
> **Source:** Phase V, Language Architecture section
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Distinction

> [!flashcard] Seed 10
> **Q:** What is the Cold Reconstruction protocol and what problem does it solve?
> **A:** The Cold Reconstruction protocol involves writing everything you can remember about a text immediately after reading, before consulting any notes, and then comparing this reconstruction to the actual text. It solves the Fluency Illusion problem: because recognition is contaminated by familiarity (making comprehension feel higher than it is), only retrieval-based testing — like reconstruction from memory — produces an accurate assessment of what was genuinely understood.

> [!flashcard] Seed 10
> **Q:** How does the active word-noticing disposition differ from standard fluent reading, and why is this distinction critical for the reconstructed model?
> **A:** Fluent reading optimises for comprehension: it tolerates local ambiguity, skips processing unfamiliar words when the passage meaning is sufficient, and prioritises speed and global coherence. The active word-noticing disposition deliberately slows at unfamiliar words, attempts contextual inference, and flags items for follow-up — sacrificing some reading speed for vocabulary acquisition. The distinction is critical because the same reading volume produces dramatically different vocabulary outcomes depending on which stance the reader maintains: the reconstructed model holds that active noticing, not reading volume per se, is the key mediator of reading's vocabulary acquisition efficiency.
> **Source:** Phase V Reconstructive Synthesis; Original Synthesis
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Application

> [!flashcard] Seed 10
> **Q:** What is the prescriptive-descriptive gap in the Zimmerman model, and why does it matter for educational practice?
>
> **A:** The prescriptive-descriptive gap is the distance between what the model *describes* (what expert, high-agency SRL looks like) and what *teaching the model prescribes* (the capacity to enact it). Knowing the cycle's three phases does not produce the capacity to cycle effectively; that capacity requires calibrated self-efficacy, domain-specific metacognitive vocabulary, emotional regulation for non-defensive attribution, and sufficient prior knowledge to set meaningful goals — prerequisites the model does not specify or develop. For educational practice, it means that SRL interventions focused on teaching the cycle abstractly are systematically insufficient; what is needed is scaffolded practice with calibration feedback embedded in authentic learning contexts.
>

>
