# Basic Psychological Needs (Deci & Ryan, 2000)

> [!definition] Basic Psychological Needs (Deci & Ryan, 2000)
> **Autonomy**: The need to experience one's actions as self-determined, volitional, and self-endorsed rather than controlled by external forces or internal compulsion. **Competence**: The need to feel effective in one's interactions with the environment — to experience growth and mastery. **Relatedness**: The need to feel connected to and cared for by significant others. SDT's core claim is that social environments (including educational ones) that support all three needs will tend to produce intrinsically motivated, fully functioning individuals; environments that thwart them will produce controlled, amotivated, or passively compliant learners.

> [!evidence] Basic Psychological Needs (Deci & Ryan, 2000)
> Innate, universal psychological requirements whose satisfaction is necessary and sufficient for ongoing psychological growth, integrity, and well-being, and whose thwarting reliably predicts psychological fragmentation, ill-being, and defensive or rigid functioning. In SDT, the basic psychological needs are autonomy, competence, and relatedness. They are termed "basic" in that they are not learned or socially constructed but are inherent features of human organismic functioning, analogous in their universality to biological needs for food and water, though expressed and satisfied through culturally specific means.

> [!evidence] Basic Psychological Needs (Deci & Ryan, 2000)
> Innate, organismic requirements for autonomy (volition and self-endorsement), competence (effectance and mastery), and relatedness (meaningful connection with others), whose satisfaction is essential for psychological growth, integrity, and well-being and whose frustration produces predictable patterns of ill-being — regardless of whether the individual consciously values or recognizes them.
>
> **Boundary:** "Basic" means essential for optimal functioning, not that these needs are the only psychological needs. SDT does not deny the existence of other desires and preferences, but claims that only these three function as universal nutriments.
>
> **Research Status:** The universality claim is supported by evidence from 100+ countries, though the [[WEIRD-Sample-Bias]] in the evidence base is acknowledged.
