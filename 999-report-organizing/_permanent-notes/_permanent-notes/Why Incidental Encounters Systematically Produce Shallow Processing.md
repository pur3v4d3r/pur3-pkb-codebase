## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Why Incidental Encounters Systematically Produce Shallow Processing
> Readers engaged in text comprehension are pursuing a comprehension goal, not a word-learning goal. This goal structure systematically shapes what aspects of the reading experience receive elaborative attention. A reader encountering an unfamiliar word during comprehension-directed reading will typically: (1) allocate brief attention to the word form; (2) attempt rapid context inference to assign a "good enough" approximate meaning; and (3) continue reading, using the inferred meaning to support ongoing comprehension. This processing sequence rarely goes deep enough to produce durable encoding. The inferred meaning is processed at just sufficient depth to serve the immediate comprehension goal — not at the depth required to produce a generalisable, production-accessible representation. The comprehension goal is epistemically satisfied by shallow processing; it does not require and does not reward the elaborative processing that would produce durable vocabulary acquisition.
> *— [[vocabulary-acquisition-reading-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
