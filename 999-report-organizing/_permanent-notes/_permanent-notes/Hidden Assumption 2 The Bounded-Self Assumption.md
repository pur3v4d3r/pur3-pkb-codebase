## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Hidden Assumption 2: The Bounded-Self Assumption
> **The assumption**: SDT's autonomy need presupposes that there is a coherent, bounded self from which authentic action originates — a self whose values can be distinguished from external pressures, whose integration is both possible and developmentally desirable.
>
> **Why it's hidden**: SDT does not make this metaphysical commitment explicit. It is expressed through the language of "authentic self," "true self vs. false self compliance," and "integrated regulation" — language that sounds psychological rather than philosophical but carries substantial commitments about the nature of selfhood.
>
> **What happens if it's wrong**: Buddhist psychology — a tradition with sophisticated empirical claims about human psychology — holds that the self is a construction rather than a stable entity, that reifying a coherent self and acting from "its" values is itself a form of attachment that creates suffering rather than relieving it. Phenomenological traditions (Merleau-Ponty, Heidegger) challenge the Cartesian picture of a bounded self that SDT's language implies, arguing that the self is constitutively intersubjective, never simply "one's own." Post-structuralist critiques (Foucault, Butler) argue that the subject who "authors" their own action is itself a product of discursive practices rather than a pre-given source of authentic motivation. These critiques do not collectively refute SDT's pragmatic value — but they destabilize the theoretical foundation on which the autonomy need rests.
>
> **Assessment**: The strong version of the assumption — a fully coherent, individuated self awaiting authentic expression — does not survive philosophical scrutiny. A minimal-self version — sufficient continuity of values, preferences, and relational identity to distinguish "acting from one's own values" from "acting from pure external compulsion" — survives and is sufficient to ground SDT's empirical program. The reconstruction will operate from this minimal-self version.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-19_report]]*
