>
> **Identified Limitations:**
> 1. The neuroscience of metacognition is covered at the level of broad PFC involvement; the specific neural circuits, neuroimaging studies, and clinical implications of metacognitive disorders deserve deeper treatment in a companion report.
> 2. Cultural context is limited: the research synthesized is primarily North American and European; metacognitive practices embedded in non-Western learning traditions (e.g., Buddhist mindfulness-metacognition tradition, Confucian educational philosophy) are not represented.
> 3. The Metacognitive Skill Stack and Metacognitive Failure Taxonomy are original theoretical integrations; they merit independent evaluation against the literature and possibly an empirical research program, and should not be cited as established models without qualification.
> 4. The report covers the metacognitive implications of several learning strategies (retrieval practice, interleaving, spacing) but does not provide the full implementation guidance that practitioners deploying these strategies need; the SR Seeds and Protocols sections partially address this, but companion Practitioner's Reports would add substantial value.
>
> **Recommendations for Future Revision:**
> - Add a section on metacognitive assessment instruments (how to formally measure metacognitive knowledge, monitoring accuracy, and regulation quality)
> - Expand the neuroscience section when neuroimaging evidence is more settled
> - Add a cross-cultural comparative analysis of metacognitive practices and their developmental contexts
> - Commission companion Practitioner's Field Guide on metacognitive training implementation
