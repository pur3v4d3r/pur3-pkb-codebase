## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Financial Modeling → Reading Comprehension
> In quantitative finance, there is a well-documented failure mode in which models that produce internally consistent numbers — that "balance" and produce plausible outputs — are taken as validated when they are structurally flawed. The fluency of the calculation (the numbers add up, the spreadsheet runs without errors) produces confidence in the model's validity that the model has not earned. The model's internal arithmetic is the equivalent of sentence-level processing: locally correct, globally misleading. The transfer encoding: when a process produces a smooth, error-free phenomenology — when the work feels like it's going well — the absence of errors is evidence of local correctness, not global validity. Dedicated global-validity checks are required independently of the smooth-processing experience.
> *— [[situation-model-construction-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-29_report]]*
