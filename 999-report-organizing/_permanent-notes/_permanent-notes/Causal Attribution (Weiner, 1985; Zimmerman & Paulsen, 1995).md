# Causal Attribution (Weiner, 1985; Zimmerman & Paulsen, 1995)

> [!definition] Causal Attribution (Weiner, 1985; Zimmerman & Paulsen, 1995)
> The explanatory account a learner generates to explain performance outcomes. Attributions are characterised by two critical dimensions: locus (internal vs. external) and controllability (within vs. outside the learner's agency). Adaptive attributions for SRL are internal and controllable (specifically, strategy-level): they preserve agency and generate actionable next steps.
>
> **Boundary:** Attribution is a belief, not an accurate analysis. Learners can make accurate or inaccurate attributions, and the motivational consequences follow from the attribution made, not from the actual cause. This is why attribution training matters: it is possible to cultivate accurate, adaptive attribution patterns through deliberate practice.
>
> **See also:** [[attribution-theory]], [[Attribution-Retraining-Interventions]], [[growth-mindset]], [[self-efficacy]]
