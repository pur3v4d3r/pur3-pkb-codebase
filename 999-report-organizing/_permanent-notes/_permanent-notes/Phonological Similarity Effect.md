# Phonological Similarity Effect

> [!definition] Phonological Similarity Effect
> **Phonological Similarity Effect**: The finding that sequences of phonologically similar items (letters B, C, D, G, P, T; words cat, bat, hat, mat) are recalled less accurately than sequences of phonologically distinct items, indicating that working memory stores phonological codes rather than semantic or orthographic representations. This demonstrates that the phonological loop operates on sound-based representations, and that confusability in that dimension directly degrades recall. See: [[phonological-loop]], [[phonological-awareness]].

> [!evidence] Phonological Similarity Effect
> Sequences of phonologically similar items (e.g., *b, d, g, p, t*) are harder to recall in correct order than phonologically distinct items. Crucially, this effect persists even with visually presented letters — demonstrating that the phonological store encodes speech-based representations even when input is visual (Conrad & Hull, 1964).
> *— [[baddeley-working-memory-model-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!evidence] Phonological-Similarity-Effect
> The finding that sequences of phonologically similar items (sharing many speech sounds) are harder to recall in correct serial order than phonologically distinct items — demonstrating that the phonological store codes information in phonological, not semantic or orthographic, format.
