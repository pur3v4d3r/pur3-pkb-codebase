## Reflections

> [!reflection] Phase II Reflections
> 1. The declarative-procedural-conditional framework suggests that many educational interventions teach declarative metacognitive knowledge (describing effective strategies) without adequately developing procedural or conditional metacognitive knowledge. Can you identify a context in your own learning where you possessed adequate declarative knowledge of a strategy but lacked the procedural or conditional knowledge to deploy it effectively? What would explicit instruction in the missing dimension have needed to include?
> 2. Flavell's person-task-strategy taxonomy and Schraw's declarative-procedural-conditional taxonomy are described here as complementary. If you were designing a comprehensive assessment of a student's metacognitive knowledge, how would you structure it to capture both the *object* (person/task/strategy) and the *form* (declarative/procedural/conditional) dimensions?
> 3. The distinction between metacognitive knowledge and metacognitive experience is theoretically important but phenomenologically subtle — in practice, stored knowledge and current experience interact continuously. What is the educational value of maintaining this distinction, and where does its utility reach its limits?
> *— [[metacognitive-knowledge-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*
