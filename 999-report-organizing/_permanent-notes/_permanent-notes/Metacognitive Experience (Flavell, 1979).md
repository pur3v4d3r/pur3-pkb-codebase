# Metacognitive Experience (Flavell, 1979)

> [!definition] Metacognitive Experience (Flavell, 1979)
> Phenomenological signals that accompany cognitive activity: the feeling that one understands, the sense that an answer is just out of reach, the subjective ease or difficulty of processing. Metacognitive experiences are the *proximate* inputs to monitoring judgments — they are what monitoring actually detects and interprets. See [[metacognitive-experience]].

> The conscious, affective-cognitive experiences that accompany reading — feelings of understanding, confusion, familiarity, or difficulty that arise during a reading episode. [[metacognitive-experience]] includes the "feeling of knowing," the sense that a passage is or isn't making sense, and the nagging sense that something was just missed. These experiences are the *interface* between the reading process and the monitoring system.

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Experience (Flavell, 1979)
> The conscious affective-cognitive experiences that accompany cognitive activity — including feelings of knowing, confusion, familiarity, and difficulty. In reading, metacognitive experiences are the primary interface between the comprehension process and the monitoring system: they serve as the trigger signals that should activate regulatory action. The fluency trap operates precisely at this level, generating false "feeling of comprehending" experiences that suppress regulatory activation.
> *— [[metacognition-silent-reading-focused-analysis-2026-03-21_report]]*

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Experience (Flavell, 1979)
> The online, moment-to-moment phenomenological sense of one's cognitive state during a task — the felt sense of understanding or confusion, ease or difficulty, familiarity or strangeness. Metacognitive experiences are the raw data from which monitoring judgments are constructed. They are also profoundly unreliable: the feeling of knowing does not reliably track actual knowing, and the feeling of understanding does not reliably track actual comprehension.
> *— [[metacognitive-scaffolding-focused-analysis-2026-03-20_report]]*

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Experience (Flavell, 1979)
> In-the-moment affective or cognitive signals about the quality of one's current cognitive processing — feelings of knowing, of confusion, of fluency, or of effort. Serve as the monitoring signals that activate control processes. Inaccurate metacognitive experiences (feeling that you understand when you don't) constitute the primary failure mode of metacognitive monitoring.
