## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Beck, McKeown & McCaslin (1983): Contextual Support Categories
> Beck and colleagues coded naturally occurring contexts for their ability to help a reader infer word meaning, identifying four categories: (1) *Misdirective* contexts — contexts that actively point toward an incorrect meaning (a word used in a sentence that is plausible with either the correct meaning or an incorrect one); (2) *Non-directive* contexts — contexts that neither help nor hinder inference; (3) *Generally directive* contexts — contexts that suggest the word is positive or negative, or has something to do with a general domain, without specifying the meaning; (4) *Directive* contexts — contexts that provide enough information to infer the meaning with confidence. Their finding: in naturally occurring children's literature, misdirective and non-directive contexts together constitute approximately 50% of all contexts in which unfamiliar words appear. Directive contexts are a minority.
> *— [[reading-vocabulary-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
