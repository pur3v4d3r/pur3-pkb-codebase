# Heutagogy (Hase & Kenyon, 2000)

> [!definition] Heutagogy (Hase & Kenyon, 2000)
> A theory of self-determined learning extending beyond pedagogy (teacher-directed) and andragogy (self-directed within a structure) to emphasise the learner's capacity to determine not only how they learn but what they learn and whether they have learned it. Heutagogy describes the aspirational endpoint of the developmental trajectory from other-regulated through co-regulated to fully self-regulated learning.
>
> **Boundary:** Heutagogy assumes a level of metacognitive sophistication and motivational autonomy that must be developed rather than assumed — hence the importance of scaffolded developmental stages.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Frames the lifelong learning horizon (Section 7) toward which the entire PKM goal-setting architecture is oriented.
>
> **See also:** [[heutagogy]], [[andragogy]], [[self-directed-learning]], [[lifelong-learning]]

> [!evidence] Heutagogy (Hase & Kenyon, 2000)
> An extension of SDL toward fully self-determined learning, in which learners not only take initiative in pursuing pre-specified goals but define their own competencies and learning pathways. Where SDL still operates within an institutional context of needs as diagnosed against some external standard, heutagogy imagines the learner as the author of both the standard and the process. Associated with double-loop learning (revision of underlying assumptions, not merely strategies) and capability development. Most applicable in professional and post-formal learning contexts where competencies cannot be fully prescribed in advance.

> [!evidence] Heutagogy (Hase & Kenyon, 2000)
> A theory of self-determined learning that extends andragogy by emphasizing the learner's capacity to determine not only what and how to learn but also to modify and reconstruct their own conceptual frameworks and meaning-making structures. Where andragogy describes learners who direct their own learning within established domains, heutagogy describes learners who can fundamentally revise their understanding of the domains themselves.
>
> **Boundary:** Heutagogy describes a developmental aspiration rather than a universal capacity. It presupposes a level of metacognitive sophistication and epistemic maturity that represents the most advanced end of the self-direction continuum.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Represents the theoretical ceiling of self-directed learning — the point at which self-direction extends to the revision of one's own epistemological frameworks.
>
