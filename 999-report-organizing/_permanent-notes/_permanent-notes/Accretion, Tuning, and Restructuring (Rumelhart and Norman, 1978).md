# Accretion, Tuning, and Restructuring (Rumelhart and Norman, 1978)

> [!definition] Accretion, Tuning, and Restructuring (Rumelhart and Norman, 1978)
> **Accretion** is the addition of new information to an existing schema without modifying the schema's fundamental structure — new facts or examples are stored as instances of the schema, filling its slots with new specific values. Most day-to-day learning is accretion: we learn that Abraham Lincoln was 6'4" without revising our schema for presidents or for historical figures. **Tuning** is the gradual refinement of a schema's parameters through experience — the slot values, default settings, and variable constraints are adjusted to fit the evidence more accurately. A novice chess player who initially represents "good piece position" in terms of board-centre proximity gradually tunes this schema to include more complex positioning principles. **Restructuring** is the creation of genuinely new schematic organization — either through *patterned generation* (inducing a new schema from a set of experienced instances) or *schema induction* (an educator or model deliberately constructing and transmitting a new schema). Restructuring is cognitively costly, tends to occur when existing schemas fail systematically, and corresponds roughly to Piaget's accommodation at the macro-level.
