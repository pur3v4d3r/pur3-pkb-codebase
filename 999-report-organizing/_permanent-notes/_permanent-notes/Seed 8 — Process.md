> [!flashcard] Seed 8 — Process
> **Q:** What is the "accuracy-activation dialectic" and why does it matter for expert use of EI?
> **A:** The accuracy-activation dialectic (original synthesis) names the tension between two facts: (1) EI is most powerful when prior knowledge is richest; but (2) rich prior knowledge creates the risk that fluent but inaccurate schemata will distort encoding of genuinely discrepant new information. Expert EI use requires maintaining calibrated skepticism toward self-generated elaborations — neither assuming accuracy because elaboration felt fluent nor abandoning elaboration because the domain is difficult.
> **Source:** Phase V
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #elaborative-interrogation, #accuracy-constraint, #metacognition

> [!flashcard] Seed 8 — Process
> **Q:** What is the monitoring-control coupling problem, and what empirical evidence documents it?
> **A:** Monitoring-control coupling refers to the functional integration between monitoring signals and regulatory responses. Low coupling — monitoring accurately detects a problem but control does not respond — is documented in: strategy selection studies where comprehension monitoring does not produce strategy switching (Pressley et al.); calibration studies where good JOL accuracy does not predict adaptive study time allocation (Metcalfe & Finn). The Calibration-Action Gap is the theoretical account of this empirical pattern.
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #monitoring-control-coupling, #monitoring-regulation-decoupling, #calibration-action-gap, #metacognitive-regulation

> [!flashcard] Seed 8 — Process
> **Q:** What is the Morphological Leverage Effect, and how does it multiply the return on vocabulary-building efforts?
>
> **A:** The Morphological Leverage Effect is the principle that knowledge of a single morpheme (root, prefix, or suffix) increases the inferrability of a network of derived words — not just one target word. For example, knowing the Latin root *spect-* (see/look) improves inference for spectator, inspection, perspective, retrospective, circumspect, speculate, spectacle, and dozens more. The "leverage" is that one investment (learning a morpheme) creates multiple inference pathways simultaneously. Nagy & Anderson (1987) estimated that roughly 80,000 of the 100,000+ words in printed school English are morphologically related to words in the first 5,000 most frequent — meaning morphological knowledge multiplies the productive coverage of a foundation vocabulary.
>
> **Source:** Phase IV, Morphological Knowledge section
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Process
> **Tags:** #morphological-knowledge, #vocabulary-acquisition, #learning-efficiency, #reading

> **Q:** What are the three key outputs of the Self-Reflection Phase that feed into the next Forethought Phase?
> **A:** (1) Revised strategy knowledge — updated information about which strategies work, under which conditions, with which material types. (2) Calibrated self-efficacy — mastery experiences (verified by genuine performance, not fluency) or accurate gap-detection that informs efficacy calibration. (3) Revised goal architecture — adjustments to process goal ambition level based on consistent patterns of met/unmet goals.

> [!flashcard] Seed 8 — Process
> **Q:** What is the attention-allocation paradox in incidental vocabulary learning, and why is it "paradoxical" rather than merely a design limitation?
>
> **A:** The paradox: the conditions that maximise accurate inferential processing (rich, redundant context) also allow shallow processing to succeed (you can comprehend without deep word-level elaboration). The conditions that would force deep processing (sparse context) reliably produce inferential errors and comprehension failure. There is no naturalistic reading condition that simultaneously forces deep, accurate semantic processing AND supports adequate text comprehension. The paradox is structural, not correctable by better attention management, because it arises from the incompatible processing requirements of the comprehension goal and the acquisition goal within naturalistic reading contexts.
>
> **Source:** Phase IV

> **Type:** Process
> **Tags:** #incidental-learning, #attention, #cognitive-load, #vocabulary
