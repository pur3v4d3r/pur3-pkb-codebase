## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Matthew Effect Operates Through Context Quality, Not Just Exposure Quantity
> The standard account of the Matthew Effect emphasises quantity: strong readers read more and thus encounter more words. This is true, but it misses a more subtle and theoretically important mechanism: strong readers encounter words in *better contexts*. A skilled reader navigating a sophisticated text has the background knowledge and syntactic processing fluency to construct rich, redundant inferential contexts for unfamiliar words. A struggling reader navigating a text at or above the threshold of their comprehension cannot construct these rich contexts — too much of their working memory is consumed by decoding and basic comprehension. The Matthew Effect is thus not merely a quantity effect but a *quality-of-processing effect*: strong readers get more encoding depth per encounter, not just more encounters. This reframes the Matthew Effect as a compounding advantage in processing conditions, not just in input volume.
> *— [[vocabulary-acquisition-reading-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
