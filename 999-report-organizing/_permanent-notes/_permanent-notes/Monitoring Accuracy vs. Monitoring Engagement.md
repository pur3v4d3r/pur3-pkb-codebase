## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Monitoring Accuracy vs. Monitoring Engagement
> **The Tension:** Metacognitive reading research implicitly assumes that the primary problem for poor comprehenders is *metacognitive monitoring failure* — failing to detect comprehension breakdowns. However, an alternative hypothesis holds that the primary problem is *monitoring disengagement* — detecting but not responding to breakdowns, often for motivational reasons (monitoring is threatening when comprehension failure confirms negative self-concepts about reading ability).
>
> **Position A — Cognitive Failure (Baker, Brown):** Poor comprehenders genuinely lack the monitoring sensitivity to detect comprehension failures; their monitoring system is insufficiently calibrated to the situational-model level.
>
> **Position B — Motivational Disengagement (Pintrich, Zimmerman):** Poor comprehenders often detect failure but suppress the regulatory response to protect self-efficacy; the deficit is motivational rather than purely cognitive.
>
> **Current Evidence:** Both positions have empirical support; the research base suggests genuine heterogeneity — some struggling readers exhibit monitoring failure in the cognitive sense, others exhibit motivational disengagement, and many exhibit both.
>
> **Why It Matters:** The distinction has direct instructional implications: monitoring training will not help a student who monitors accurately but disengages motivationally; motivational intervention will not help a student who genuinely cannot detect coherence failures.
>
> **This Report's Position:** Both pathways are empirically operative; effective reading comprehension instruction should assess which deficit predominates in individual students rather than assuming a uniform mechanism.
>
> **See also:** [[metacognitive-monitoring]], [[self-efficacy]], [[monitoring-gap]], [[comprehension-monitoring]]
