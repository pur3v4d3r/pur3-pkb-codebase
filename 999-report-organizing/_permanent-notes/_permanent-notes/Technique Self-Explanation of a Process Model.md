## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technique: Self-Explanation of a Process Model
> *Why here: The five feedback channels are enumerable. [[self-explanation-effect|Self-explanation]] of enumerable processes is especially high-value because it tests whether you can reproduce both the items AND their logic — not just recall their names.*
>
> **Self-explanation produced (report closed, 5-minute timer):**
>
> The five channels through which Self-Reflection regenerates Forethought:
>
> 1. **Self-efficacy recalibration** — after the session, your confidence in your ability to do this type of reading is updated. If you successfully met your process goals, confidence stabilizes or rises, which makes the next Forethought more ambitious.
>
> 2. **Prior knowledge update** — the PKB gets richer after a good session, so the next Forethought's "reconnaissance" phase has better material to draw from. Better prior knowledge = better gap mapping = better goals.
>
> 3. **Strategic knowledge refinement** — you now know more about which strategies worked and which didn't. This feeds directly into next session's strategy planning.
>
> 4. **Goal standard recalibration** — if you consistently hit your goals, they were probably too easy; if you consistently miss them despite following process, they may be miscalibrated. Either way you adjust.
>
> 5. **Motivational trajectory** — whether you drew adaptive or defensive inferences determines whether the next session starts with maintained or degraded motivation.
>
> **Gap detected:** I almost certainly have channels 1–3 right. I am less confident about 4 and 5. Channel 4 — goal standard recalibration — feels like a second-order effect, not a direct "channel." Re-read to check. *(Post-check: Correct — the report calls it "recalibrated outcome expectations, based on actual experience with this type of text." My framing was close enough.)*
