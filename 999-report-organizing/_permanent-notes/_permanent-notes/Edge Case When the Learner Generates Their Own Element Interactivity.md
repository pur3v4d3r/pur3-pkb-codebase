# Edge Case: When the Learner Generates Their Own Element Interactivity

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Edge Case: When the Learner Generates Their Own Element Interactivity
> **The case:** [[self-regulated-learning]] situations where the learner controls instructional sequencing, pacing, and strategy selection.
>
> **What standard element interactivity predicts:** Element interactivity is determined by the material (intrinsic) and the instructional format (extraneous). The learner receives these as given and must process them within WM constraints.
>
> **What actually happens:** Self-regulating learners actively generate element interactivity through their strategic choices. A learner who chooses an ineffective strategy (e.g., attempting to solve problems they should study) creates extraneous element interactivity through their own metacognitive decisions. Conversely, a learner with strong [[self-regulated-learning|self-regulated learning]] skills may effectively manage element interactivity by adaptively choosing when to study examples, when to attempt problems, and when to review — functioning as their own adaptive instructional system.
>
> **Why this matters:** The standard CLT model treats the learner as a *recipient* of element interactivity determined by external instructional design. Self-regulated learning introduces the learner as an *agent* who generates, manages, and sometimes mismanages their own element interactivity. This agent-level behavior is not captured by the element interactivity mechanism as specified — the mechanism describes what happens *given* a level of interactivity, but not how learners *self-select* their interactivity levels.
>
> **Implications:** CLT's element interactivity mechanism may need supplementation with a metacognitive control layer — a model of how learners monitor and regulate their own element interactivity. This connects CLT to work on [[metacognition]], [[Ease-of-Learning|ease-of-learning judgments]], and the concept of [[Cognitive-Load-Measurement-and-Self-Monitoring-in-PKM-Practice|cognitive load self-monitoring]]. The frontier question (Level 6) is whether such a metacognitive layer can be integrated into CLT's evolutionary framework or requires a separate theoretical apparatus.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*
