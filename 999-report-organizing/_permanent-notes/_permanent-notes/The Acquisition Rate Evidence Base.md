## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Acquisition Rate Evidence Base
> Nation (2001) synthesised the existing research to estimate that a reader would need approximately 10–15 encounters with a novel word in running text before achieving anything resembling "partial knowledge," and substantially more (perhaps 20–30 encounters) before achieving robust, productive mastery. This estimate is itself optimistic, as it assumes encounters in high-quality inferential contexts — a condition that real texts do not reliably provide. Studies that have tracked actual encounter frequencies in natural reading corpora find that the vast majority of low-frequency words never accumulate to the 10-encounter threshold across a typical year of reading, regardless of volume.
> *— [[vocabulary-acquisition-reading-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
