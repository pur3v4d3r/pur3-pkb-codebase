> [!flashcard] Seed 7 — Connection
> **Q:** What is the temporal grammar difference between fixed mindset and growth mindset inner speech, and how does it connect to Weiner's stability dimension?
> **A:** Fixed mindset inner speech is atemporally stable — "I can't" implies "I never will" because ability is treated as static. Growth mindset inner speech is temporally open — "I can't *yet*" grammatically marks the current limitation as time-limited. This maps directly onto Weiner's stability dimension: growth mindset reframes ability as unstable (developable) rather than stable (fixed), which preserves expectancy for future success.
> **Source:** Phase III

> [!flashcard] Seed 7 — Connection
> **Q:** How does the generation effect in memory research (Slamecka & Graf, 1978) relate to the self-explanation effect in reading comprehension (Chi et al., 1989)?
>
> **A:** Both establish the same structural principle — the Generation Constraint — from different methodological directions. Slamecka & Graf showed that words actively generated by participants were remembered better than identical words passively read, demonstrating the memory advantage of active production over passive reception. Chi et al. showed that students who explained worked examples during study — generating causal accounts rather than reading passively — built substantially better knowledge representations. Both converge on the claim that generation (producing new content from partial cues) is the mechanism that forces active encoding and model construction.
>
