# Motivational Climate (Ames, 1992)

> [!definition] Motivational Climate (Ames, 1992)
> **Definition:** The goal-relevant structure of an achievement environment — the constellation of signals, practices, and evaluative procedures that implicitly communicate which type of achievement is valued, how success is defined, and how ability is evaluated. A mastery climate emphasizes personal improvement, effort, and learning; a performance climate emphasizes normative comparison, relative ability, and competitive success.
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Motivational climate is the primary environmental lever for shaping goal adoption distributions — it determines the goal type ecology in which individual learners operate. This report argues that mastery climate design should prioritize avoidance-reduction over mastery-promotion as its first-order target.
> **Operational Indicators:** Climate type can be assessed via structural features (grading type, ranking practices, feedback language) and perceptual reports (perceived task vs. ego climate instruments).
> **Etymology/Intellectual Lineage:** Developed by Carole Ames (1992) building on Nicholls; extended through the TARGET framework (Epstein, 1988) and extensive sport psychology research (Ntoumanis & Biddle, 1999).
> **Cross-References:** [[motivational-climate-design]], [[achievement-goal-theory]], [[mastery-goal-orientation]]

> [!evidence] Motivational Climate (Ames, 1992)
> The goal structure perceived to be operative in a particular achievement environment, shaped by structural features including task design, authority distribution, recognition practices, grouping, evaluation methods, and time management (TARGET). Motivational climate is distinct from individual goal orientation — it is an environmental variable that powerfully shapes which goals individuals adopt in a given setting.
