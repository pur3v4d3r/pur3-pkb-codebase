# The 2×2 Achievement Goal Framework (Elliot & McGregor, 2001)

> [!definition] The 2×2 Achievement Goal Framework (Elliot & McGregor, 2001)
> **Definition:** The 2×2 framework cross-classifies two conceptually independent dimensions — *definition of competence* (mastery vs. performance) and *valence of motivation* (approach vs. avoidance) — to yield four distinct achievement goal constructs: mastery-approach (MAP), mastery-avoidance (MAV), performance-approach (PAP), and performance-avoidance (PAP). The definition-of-competence dimension distinguishes whether the referent for competence evaluation is absolute/intrapersonal (mastery) or normative/interpersonal (performance). The valence dimension distinguishes whether the motivational impetus is moving toward a desired positive state or away from a feared negative state.
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This framework is the primary analytical object of this report. Its theoretical significance lies not in providing four categories where two existed before, but in revealing that the approach-avoidance distinction was always implicitly operative in the mastery-performance dichotomy — but obscured by conflating two independent dimensions into one.
> **Operational Indicators:** Goal type can be assessed via validated measures (e.g., the Achievement Goal Questionnaire-Revised) and inferred behaviorally — MAV goals characteristically produce vigilance toward incremental improvement failures; PAV goals produce defensive withdrawal or surface processing strategies.

> [!evidence] The 2×2 Achievement Goal Framework (Elliot & McGregor, 2001)
> A taxonomic model that classifies goal orientations along two orthogonal dimensions: *definition of competence* (mastery vs. performance) and *valence of striving* (approach vs. avoidance). The resulting four cells are:
> - **Mastery-approach:** Focused on developing competence, deepening understanding, learning from challenge. Associated with deep processing, intrinsic interest, and adaptive help-seeking.
> - **Mastery-avoidance:** Focused on avoiding misunderstanding, not losing skills, preventing knowledge decay. Less studied but associated with perfectionism and anxiety in learning.
> - **Performance-approach:** Focused on demonstrating competence relative to others, outperforming peers, appearing intelligent. Associated with surface processing but sometimes positive performance outcomes.
> - **Performance-avoidance:** Focused on avoiding the appearance of incompetence, not looking unintelligent, preventing failure exposure. Consistently associated with maladaptive patterns: anxiety, withdrawal, self-handicapping.
>
> **Boundary:** Goal orientations are *not* fixed traits. They are contextually activated patterns influenced by environmental cues, feedback structures, task framing, and instructional design. A learner can shift orientations across tasks or even within a single task depending on the goal structure and feedback environment.
