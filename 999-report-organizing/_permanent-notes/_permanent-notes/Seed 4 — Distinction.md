> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the fluency illusion, and why does it distort metacognitive monitoring?
> **A:** The fluency illusion is the systematic over-inference from subjective processing ease to genuine encoding depth and retention probability. Smooth, familiar processing generates high JOLs and comprehension confidence even when encoding is shallow. It distorts monitoring because mnemonic fluency cues — which are easily accessible — carry excessive weight in JOL construction relative to their actual validity as encoding-quality indicators.
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate

> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Distinction
> **Q:** What distinguishes comprehension failure from the Textbase Trap from comprehension failure from cognitive overload?
>
> **A:** Cognitive overload failure (Mechanism 1) is experienced as difficulty — the reader knows they are struggling. The Textbase Trap (Mechanism 2) is experienced as confidence — the reader feels they understood. This is the critical diagnostic distinction: a reader who feels confused after reading may have an overload problem; a reader who feels confident but cannot answer inference questions has a Textbase Trap problem. The Textbase Trap produces "confident incomprehension."
>
> **Source:** Phase IV, Mechanisms 1 and 2

> **Type:** Distinction

> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Distinction
> **Q:** What distinguishes an adaptive self-reaction from a defensive self-reaction in the SRL cycle?
> **A:** An adaptive reaction modifies the SRL system toward improvement: revising strategy, adjusting goal level, maintaining productive approaches. A defensive reaction protects self-esteem at the cost of regulatory information: lowering goals to guarantee success, attributing failure externally, withdrawing effort (self-handicapping). The key diagnostic: does the reaction generate actionable next steps or protect against evaluation?
> **Source:** Phase III
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #srl, #self-reaction, #adaptive-defensive
