# Covariation Principle (Kelley's ANOVA Model)

> [!definition] Covariation Principle (Kelley's ANOVA Model)
> The **covariation principle** — articulated by Harold Kelley (1967) — holds that people attribute behavior to the cause with which it most consistently co-occurs across conditions. Kelley formalized this through an analogy with ANOVA experimental design: people behave as intuitive scientists, varying the potential causal factors of Persons (consensus), Entities (distinctiveness), and Occasions (consistency) and attributing behavior to whichever factor shows the strongest covariation with the behavior. High consensus + high distinctiveness + high consistency = entity (situational) attribution; low consensus + low distinctiveness + high consistency = person (dispositional) attribution.
