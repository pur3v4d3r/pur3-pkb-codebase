# Incremental Knowledge Model (Nation, 2001; Schmitt, 2014)

> [!definition] Incremental Knowledge Model (Nation, 2001; Schmitt, 2014)
> **Definition:** A model of vocabulary knowledge according to which knowing a word involves gradual acquisition across multiple independent dimensions: spoken/written form, word parts, syntagmatic relationships (collocations), paradigmatic relationships (synonyms/antonyms), semantic range and polysemy, register and contextual appropriateness, and availability for production. Each dimension can be known receptively (recognition in context) or productively (availability for use). Word knowledge is not binary (known/unknown) but a probability distribution across dimensions and modalities.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Provides the philosophical lens challenge — reading efficiently builds receptive knowledge but systematically underbuilds productive depth, collocational precision, and register sensitivity. The model reveals that the reading-vocabulary correlation, based primarily on receptive vocabulary measures, overstates reading's contribution to full word knowledge.
>
> **Cross-References:** [[semantic-memory]], [[schema-theory]], [[elaborative-rehearsal]]
