# Worked Example Effect

> [!definition] Worked Example Effect
> The empirically robust finding that novice learners acquire competence more efficiently by studying worked examples of solved problems than by solving equivalent problems themselves. Mechanistically explained by the finding that novices lack the schemas required for efficient problem-solving and therefore engage in working memory-intensive means-ends analysis that consumes resources without producing schemas. Worked examples provide schema-ready demonstrations that reduce this inefficiency. Effect size is consistent and substantial in well-structured domains; weakens and reverses with increasing expertise.

> [!evidence] Worked Example Effect
> The [[worked-example-effect]] is the empirical finding that studying worked examples — complete, step-by-step solutions to problems — produces superior learning outcomes compared to solving equivalent problems independently, particularly for novice learners facing high element interactivity tasks. First documented systematically by Sweller and Cooper (1985), it is CLT's founding empirical result.
>
> **Boundary:** The effect is robust for novices but reverses for advanced learners (the [[expertise-reversal-effect]]). It applies most strongly to well-structured domains with clear solution procedures.
>

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Worked Example Effect
> One of the most reliably demonstrated findings in Cognitive Load Theory is the **worked example effect**: novice learners benefit from studying worked examples (problems with complete solutions) more than from problem-solving, because worked examples reduce extraneous and intrinsic load, freeing capacity for germane processing. As learners acquire expertise and the problem-solving elements begin to chunk, this advantage reverses (the **expertise reversal effect**) — experts are actually hindered by the redundant detail of worked examples that they could now process independently. This has direct implications for instructional sequencing.
> *— [[memory-systems-working-memory-long-term-memory-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*
