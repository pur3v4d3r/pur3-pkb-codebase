## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Cross-Domain Test
> Consider a philosopher trained in formal logic and argumentation who encounters a highly technical genomics paper. The philosopher brings sophisticated argumentation skills and strong metacognitive habits — they will notice logical gaps, question hidden assumptions, and carefully track the structure of the argument. But they may entirely miss that the study is underpowered for the effect size it claims to detect, that the statistical analysis is inappropriate for the data structure, or that the proposed mechanism contradicts established biochemistry. Now consider a genomics expert with weaker formal argumentation training who reads the same paper. They will notice the methodological flaws but may accept certain logical leaps in the interpretation uncritically. What does this thought experiment suggest about the relationship between domain knowledge and general critical thinking skill?
> *— [[metacognition-and-critical-thinking-foundational-report-2026-03-05_report]]*
