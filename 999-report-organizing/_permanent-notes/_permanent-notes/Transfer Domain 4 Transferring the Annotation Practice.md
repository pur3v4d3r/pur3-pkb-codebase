# Transfer Domain 4: Transferring the Annotation Practice

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Transfer Domain 4: Transferring the Annotation Practice
> **Structural principle:** The practice of annotating your own claims with source basis, confidence level, and alternatives considered — which gives this report its distinctive form — is not limited to formal analysis. It represents an epistemic practice applicable whenever the reliability of your own reasoning matters.
>
> Applications: (a) **Decision memos:** Annotate recommendations with confidence levels and key assumptions. Reviewers can then interrogate the assumptions rather than only evaluating the recommendation. (b) **Strategic planning:** Annotate projections with the evidence basis and explicit confidence. Teams working from annotated plans are less vulnerable to overconfident anchoring on weak projections. (c) **Internal dialogue:** Practicing claim-annotation privately — "I believe X; the evidence for this is [source]; I'm [confidence] confident; the main alternative I've considered is [Y]" — is essentially the formalized version of what critical thinkers do naturally. Teaching this explicitly is an effective [[metacognition|metacognitive]] training tool.
>
> **Boundary condition:** Annotation is most valuable when stakes are high and evidence is mixed. For routine, well-established procedures, annotation adds overhead without proportionate benefit. The practice is calibrated to uncertainty: the more confident and well-established the claim, the lighter the annotation needed.
>
