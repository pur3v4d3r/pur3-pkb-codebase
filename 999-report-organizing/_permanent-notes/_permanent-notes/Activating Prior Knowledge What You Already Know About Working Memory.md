> [!schema-activation] Activating Prior Knowledge: What You Already Know About Working Memory
> Before you encounter Baddeley's formal model, you already possess rich intuitive knowledge about the cognitive phenomena it describes. When you pause mid-sentence while reading to hold a sentence's beginning in mind long enough to integrate it with the end, you are using your [[phonological-loop]]. When you mentally rotate a floor plan to figure out where furniture will fit, you are using your visuospatial sketchpad. When you simultaneously follow a conversation while monitoring background noise for your name — shunting attention between competing streams — you are deploying your [[central-executive]]. And when you watch a film scene, integrate its visual content with the dialogue, connect it to story events from twenty minutes earlier, and feel an emotional response to the synthesis, you are engaging what Baddeley calls the [[episodic-buffer]].
>
> These phenomena connect to notes you may have on [[information-processing-theory]], [[multi-store-model]], and [[cognitive-architecture]]. The guiding question for this report is: **What is the structural architecture through which the mind does its real-time cognitive work — and what are the implications of that architecture's limits for how we learn, reason, and design teaching?** As you read, attend especially to the ways in which Baddeley's model resolved genuine theoretical puzzles while simultaneously creating new ones, and to how a forty-year programme of experimental research produced a theory that is simultaneously foundational and contested.
>
> *This report assumes familiarity with basic memory terminology (encoding, storage, retrieval) and with the Atkinson-Shiffrin multi-store model. If those are unfamiliar, consult [[memory-systems]] and [[multi-store-model]] first.*
> *— [[baddeley-model-of-working-memory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
