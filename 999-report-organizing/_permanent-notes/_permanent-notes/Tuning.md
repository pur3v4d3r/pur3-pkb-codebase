# Tuning

> [!definition] Tuning
> A moderate form of schema change in which the *parameters* or *variable constraints* of a schema are adjusted to better fit experience, without altering the schema's fundamental structure. Tuning changes what values are expected in particular slots, or adjusts the weights of different interpretations, but leaves the schema's architecture intact.
>
> **Example:** A new medical resident initially has a DIAGNOSIS schema with widely distributed priors (many equally likely diagnoses for chest pain). With experience, the schema is tuned so that the most common diagnoses (MI, GERD, musculoskeletal pain) receive high prior weights, while rare diagnoses receive low weights. The schema structure hasn't changed — the physician still considers all the same diagnostic categories — but the prior probabilities have been calibrated to clinical reality.
>
> **When tuning is the response:** Tuning occurs when experience reveals that the current schema parameters are miscalibrated — the schema is structurally correct but quantitatively wrong. Prediction errors that are consistent across many instances (always wrong in the same direction) trigger tuning.
>
> **Educational implication:** Tuning is the mechanism behind calibration through practice. It explains why experts develop appropriate intuitions about case difficulty, probability, and typical presentations — not because they have different schemas, but because their schemas have been tuned by extensive feedback. This is why mere exposure to examples (without feedback on accuracy) produces poor tuning.

> [!evidence] Tuning
> The process of refining schema parameters — adjusting the precision of slot constraints, modifying default value assignments, and strengthening or weakening associations between schema elements — in response to accumulated experience. The primary mechanism of skill development through deliberate, feedback-driven practice. Distinguished from accretion (adding facts) and restructuring (reorganizing framework) by being *parameter adjustment* rather than *content addition* or *framework change*.
