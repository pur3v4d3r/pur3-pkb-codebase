# Self-Regulated Learning

> [!definition] Self-Regulated Learning
> **Term:** Self-Regulated Learning (Zimmerman, 2000; Pintrich, 2000)
> **Definition:** The process by which learners activate and sustain cognitions, affects, and behaviours that are systematically oriented toward the attainment of personal goals through forethought, performance monitoring, and self-reflection. The metacognitive regulatory cycle applied specifically to learning.
> **Boundary conditions:** Requires metacognitive development sufficient to monitor learning quality; scaffolding may be needed for novice learners; effectiveness varies by domain familiarity.
> **Report significance:** Connects metacognitive regulation (Section 6) to learning practice; foundational for PKB integration.
> **See also:** [[self-regulated-learning]], [[metacognition]], [[self-directed-learning]], [[Zimmerman-Self-Regulated-Learning-Model]]

> The capacity of learners to monitor, direct, and regulate their own cognitive, motivational, and behavioural processes in service of self-set or internalised learning goals. Self-regulated learners can generate their own feedback through self-monitoring, identify discrepancies between current and desired performance, and select and deploy strategies to close those gaps. The developmental endpoint toward which autonomy-supportive feedback design is aimed.

> [!evidence] Self-Regulated Learning
> [**Self-Regulated-Learning**:: A cyclical process through which learners proactively direct their learning by setting goals, selecting strategies, monitoring progress, evaluating outcomes, and adjusting behaviour. Major SRL models include Zimmerman's (2000) three-phase cyclical model (forethought → performance → self-reflection), Winne and Hadwin's (1998) four-phase COPES model, and Pintrich's (2000) four-phase framework. All models foreground metacognition as the regulatory engine that operates across phases.]
