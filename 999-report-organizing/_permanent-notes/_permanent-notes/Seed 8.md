> [!flashcard] Seed 8
> **Q:** Why does criterion-referenced grading support mastery-approach goal orientations while norm-referenced grading activates performance goal orientations?
> **A:** Criterion-referenced grading defines success against a fixed absolute standard — making competence evaluation self-referenced and controllable (the learner's performance determines their grade independently of others). Norm-referenced grading defines success relative to the performance of peers — making success normative, partially uncontrollable, and zero-sum. The normative structure activates the differentiated ability concept (my relative performance says something about my relative ability) and raises the motivational stakes of comparative failure, generating conditions conducive to PAP and PAV goal adoption.
> **Source:** Phase V, Assessment Design; Phase IV, Mechanisms
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Application

> **Q:** What does the Two-Stage Intervention Architecture propose, and what is the theoretical rationale?
> **A:** Stage 1: cognitive reattribution (shifting stable to unstable causal explanations). Stage 2: affective reorganization (using behavioral experiments to generate visceral confirmation of the new attribution's emotional implications). The rationale: if emotion mediates the attribution-behavior link, cognitive change without affective change fails to propagate through to behavior.
> **Source:** Phase VI, Section 6.2

> **Type:** Application

> **Q:** Why does the Scaffolded-Recalibration Gradient predict that maintaining formative assessment scaffolds indefinitely is counterproductive?
> **A:** If external calibration (formative tests, teacher feedback) is always available, students may develop accurate monitoring only when external support is present, without developing autonomous internal monitoring infrastructure. The gradient prescribes fading external calibration tools as monitoring accuracy improves, shifting responsibility to self-testing and self-prediction — otherwise scaffold-dependence replaces the autonomous self-regulation that formative assessment was meant to build.
> **Source:** Phase IV, scaffolding dimension; Phase VII, transfer to athletic coaching
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Process
> **Tags:** #scaffolded-fading, #scaffold-dependence, #self-regulated-learning

> **Q:** How does the structural analogue between habit formation and SDT's internalization continuum illuminate both frameworks?
> **A:** Both describe a transition from effortful, externally-triggered action to autonomous, internally-triggered action: external regulation → integrated regulation in SDT; goal-directed behavior → habitual behavior in habit formation. Both involve a "scaffolding" phase (external support / goal-directed effort) that must progressively cede to autonomous execution. The convergence suggests that the scaffolding-withdrawal model is a general architectural principle across motivational and neural learning processes.

> **Q:** What is the Motivational Lexicon, and how does it differ from domain vocabulary?
> **A:** The Motivational Lexicon is the vocabulary repertoire available for narrating, evaluating, and directing one's own motivational and cognitive engagement during academic learning — specifically in the attributional, regulatory, and goal-construction registers. Domain vocabulary is for understanding content; the Motivational Lexicon is for understanding and directing your motivational experience of engaging with that content. Both are cognitive resources; the Motivational Lexicon enables motivational metacognition that is impossible without it.

> **Q:** What is the "planfulness assumption" and why is it a limitation of Pintrich's framework?
>
> **A:** The framework models the learner as a deliberate, rational agent who plans, monitors, controls, and reflects. This is an aspirational model — in practice, a significant proportion of learning behavior is driven by automatic, non-deliberate processes (habit, affect, implicit goal activation) that are not accessible to the kind of conscious monitoring the framework describes. The framework provides little guidance on when deliberate SRL is possible vs. when the automatic system has taken over.
>
> **Source:** Phase V, Limitations
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Concept

> [!flashcard] Seed 8
> **Q:** What is the Analogy-First Instructional Principle, and how does it differ from Ausubel's advance organizer?
>
> **A:** The Analogy-First Principle holds that for categorically novel content, the first instructional act should establish the structural analogue (a *different* concept with isomorphic structure), not introduce the target concept. Ausubel's advance organizer introduces the *target concept itself* at a higher level of abstraction. The difference: the Analogy-First Principle introduces a different domain's structure; advance organizers introduce the target domain's structure at higher generality. The Analogy-First Principle addresses categorical novelty where no high-level target structure exists to be organized.
>
> **Source:** Phase V, VI
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Distinction

> **Q**: What are Rumelhart & Norman's three mechanisms of schema change, and which is underdescribed by schema theory?
> **A**: Accretion (adding new information without structural change), tuning (adjusting variable constraints), and restructuring (creating qualitatively new schema organizations). Restructuring is the underdescribed mechanism — schema theory's term "accommodation" implies a gentle adjustment, but conceptual change research shows genuine restructuring is cognitively costly, emotionally aversive, and more like conceptual revolution than database schema migration.

> [!flashcard] Seed 8
> **Q:** What is the Construction-Integration model's account of why poor situation models form?
>
> **A:** In the CI model, comprehension proceeds through cycling construction phases (building a network of propositions from text plus activated prior knowledge) and integration phases (refining the network through spreading activation to produce coherence). Poor situation models form when the integration phase fails — when the spreading-activation convergence that should produce a coherent situation model does not reach a coherent outcome. This failure is most commonly caused by inadequate prior knowledge (insufficient integration constraints) or failure to generate the elaborative inferences that bridge propositions to a world-representation.
>
> **Source:** Phase IV, Alternative Framework 1
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Process
> **Tags:** #construction-integration #situation-model #inference-generation

> [!flashcard] Seed 8
> **Q:** What is the Handoff Mechanism, and why is it structurally necessary rather than merely helpful?
> **A:** The Handoff Mechanism is a physical link (a specific field in the Self-Reflection template that feeds directly into the next session's Forethought template) that transfers the adaptive inference from one cycle to the next. It is structurally necessary because adaptive inferences — the most valuable output of the Self-Reflection Phase — evaporate without explicit capture and forward connection. In a solo learning context without social accountability structures, the handoff mechanism is the only reliable mechanism for actually applying inferences across sessions rather than generating and discarding them.
> **Source:** Phase V, Application 2
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Application

Without active monitoring during the Performance Phase — comprehension checkpoints, confusion flagging, goal tracking — the Self-Reflection Phase has only subjective impressions to evaluate, which are distorted by the Fluency Illusion. > [!flashcard] Seed 8
> **Q:** What makes formative assessment "externally scaffolded cycle closure" within Zimmerman's model?
>
> **A:** Effective formative feedback compensates for deficient self-reflection by providing the three components that produce adaptive cycle closure: (1) performance information relative to a reference standard; (2) attribution information (specific causal account in terms of controllable strategy use); and (3) adaptive inference scaffolding (specific recommendations for strategy modification). Feedback that provides only outcome information (grades) closes the cycle evaluatively but not adaptively.
>
> **Source:** Phase V, Formative Assessment section

> **Type:** Application

> [!flashcard] Seed 8
> **Q:** What is the structural analogy between the Zimmerman cycle's monitoring-regulation decoupling and software quality assurance?
>
> **A:** A test suite that monitors the codebase but tests the wrong things (inaccurate monitoring) provides a false safety signal analogous to a learner who monitors but miscalibrates. In both cases, the system *appears* to be self-correcting while the underlying state deteriorates. Software QA addresses this with *test quality* metrics (coverage, mutation testing) that assess monitoring accuracy, not just monitoring presence. The corresponding SRL intervention is explicit calibration activity — self-testing against external performance criteria — not just self-monitoring prompting.
>
