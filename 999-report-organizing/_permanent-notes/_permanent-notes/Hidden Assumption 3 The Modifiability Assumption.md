# Hidden Assumption 3: The Modifiability Assumption

> [!definition] Hidden Assumption 3: The Modifiability Assumption

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Hidden Assumption 3: The Modifiability Assumption
> **The assumption**: SDT's practical recommendations implicitly assume that the relevant environments — classrooms, workplaces, therapeutic relationships — can be redesigned to be more need-supportive, and that practitioners have the autonomy and structural support to do this redesigning.
>
> **Why it's hidden**: The theory is framed at the level of psychological mechanisms, not structural conditions. It describes what environments should do without accounting for why they often cannot — understaffed classrooms, incentive structures that punish autonomy-supportive behavior, institutional constraints that make rationale-provision impractical, populations whose basic psychological need satisfaction history creates responses that autonomy-supportive interventions are not designed to address.
>
> **What happens if it's wrong**: When practitioners apply SDT recommendations in contexts where the structural conditions for implementation are absent, they may experience both the theory's failure to produce expected outcomes and a subtle accusation of personal inadequacy (failure to be sufficiently autonomy-supportive) that SDT's framework doesn't help them process.
>
> **Assessment**: This assumption does not undermine SDT's theoretical validity, but it substantially limits the scope conditions under which its practical prescriptions can be straightforwardly applied. The theory's dissemination has not been proportionally attentive to these scope conditions.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-19_report]]*
