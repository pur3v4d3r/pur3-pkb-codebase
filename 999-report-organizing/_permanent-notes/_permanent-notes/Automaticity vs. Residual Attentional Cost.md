## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Automaticity vs. Residual Attentional Cost
> **Position A (Strong Automaticity):** Walking in a familiar environment is fully automatic and imposes zero attentional cost (classical Shiffrin & Schneider view). **Advocates:** Logan (instance theory suggests overlearned skills bypass central processing entirely).
> **Position B (Graded Automaticity):** Walking always retains some central executive monitoring requirement, particularly for balance, turning, and gaze stabilization (Woollacott & Shumway-Cook, 2002). **Advocates:** Yogev-Seligmann et al. (2008), Al-Yahya et al. (2011).
> **Current Evidence:** Dual-task gait research consistently shows measurable gait changes during concurrent cognitive tasks even in young adults, supporting Position B.
> **Why It Matters:** If Position A is correct, walking-reading should be cost-free; if Position B is correct, the cost is real but may vary with conditions.
> **This Report's Stance:** This report sides with Position B based on the weight of dual-task gait evidence and the graded automaticity framework of Moors & De Houwer (2006).
> *— [[walking-and-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-13_report]]*
