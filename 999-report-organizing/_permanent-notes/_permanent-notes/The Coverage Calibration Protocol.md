> [!protocol] The Coverage Calibration Protocol
> **Context:** Use when selecting reading material for deliberate vocabulary building. The goal is to identify text at approximately 98-99% coverage level — challenging enough to expose you to new vocabulary, easy enough to permit cognitive slack for inference.
>
> **Derived From:** Phase II (coverage threshold analysis) and Phase IV (Cognitive Slack Model).
>
> **Steps:**
> 1. **Sample the text:** Read a passage of approximately 250 words at your normal reading pace.
> 2. **Count unknown words:** Mark every word you are uncertain about (unfamiliar or imprecise). Count the marked words.
> 3. **Calculate coverage:** Coverage = (250 - unknown count) ÷ 250. Multiply by 100 for percentage.
> 4. **Interpret the result:** Coverage below 95%: too difficult — comprehension will suffer and inference will be unreliable. Coverage 95-97%: moderately productive — some acquisition but cognitive slack may be limited. Coverage 98-99%: optimal zone — proceed with this material. Coverage 99.5%+: likely below challenge threshold — vocabulary growth will be minimal.
> 5. **Adjust selection accordingly:** If too easy, seek more specialized or literary material. If too hard, read introductory material in the domain first.
>
> **Success Criteria:** You find yourself encountering roughly 1-3 unfamiliar words per page, comprehension is smooth and fluent, and you can often infer approximate meanings from context.
>
> **Common Pitfalls:** Underestimating familiarity (marking words you know but use rarely) — only mark words you genuinely do not know. Overestimating familiarity (skipping words you only vaguely recognize) — a word you "sort of know" is a low-quality representation worth counting as unknown for calibration purposes.
> *— [[reading-vocabulary-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
