# The Incremental Knowledge Model (Multi-Dimensional Word Knowledge)

> [!definition] The Incremental Knowledge Model (Multi-Dimensional Word Knowledge)
> A model of vocabulary knowledge according to which knowing a word is not binary (known or unknown) but involves gradual acquisition across multiple independent dimensions: (1) spoken/written form, (2) word parts and morphology, (3) syntagmatic relationships (collocations, typical sentence positions), (4) paradigmatic relationships (synonyms, antonyms, hyponyms), (5) semantic range and polysemy, (6) register and contextual appropriateness, (7) frequency and availability for production. Nation (2001) identifies nine aspects of word knowledge across three dimensions of form, meaning, and use, each of which can be known receptively or productively. Word knowledge is thus better understood as a *probability distribution* of knowledge states than as a toggle switch.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance**: This model is central to the philosophical challenge in Phase III: the question is not just *how many* words reading builds but *what kind* of word knowledge it builds. The argument will be that reading predominantly develops shallow receptive knowledge while leaving productive depth, morphological awareness, and collocational precision underdeveloped.
>
