# Restructuring

> [!definition] Restructuring
> The most radical and rarest form of schema change, in which the schema's fundamental structure — its variable architecture, its coverage predicate, its inter-schema relationships — is modified or replaced. Restructuring produces genuine *conceptual change* ([[conceptual-change-theory-and-schema-restructuring]]).
>
> **Two types of restructuring:**
> - **Schema creation (inducing):** A new schema is created from scratch (or assembled from existing sub-schemas) to explain a pattern of experience that no existing schema handles
> - **Schema revision (differentiation/coalescence):** An existing schema is split into two more specific schemas (differentiation) or two schemas are merged into a single more comprehensive one (coalescence)
>
> **Example:** Naive physics treats "impetus" as a property things acquire from being pushed (analogous to how an arrow "carries" the archer's push). The Newtonian restructuring creates force-as-interaction rather than force-as-property, velocity-as-state rather than velocity-as-stored-impetus. This is not adding facts to the impetus schema or tuning its parameters — it destroys the impetus schema and replaces it with an incompatible structure.
>
> **When restructuring is the response:** Restructuring is triggered by encounter with *genuinely anomalous* data — information that cannot be assimilated to *any* existing schema and that is sufficiently striking to motivate the cognitive effort of restructuring. The anomaly must be noticed, recognized as anomalous (not simply discarded as error), and judged to warrant reconstruction effort.
>
> **Educational implication:** Conceptual change instruction (Posner et al. 1982; Chi 2008) attempts to engineer the conditions for restructuring by making anomalies salient, demonstrating the failure of existing schemas, providing intelligible alternative schemas, and showing the alternative to be more plausible and fruitful. This is why conceptual change is so hard to achieve — it requires all four conditions simultaneously.
