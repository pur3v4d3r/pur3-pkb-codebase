# Need Frustration

> [!definition] Need Frustration
> Active thwarting of a basic psychological need — distinct from mere need absence (unsatisfied need). Need frustration activates qualitatively different psychological responses than need absence: specifically, avoidance-oriented defensive motivational states including ego-involvement, controlled motivation, and behavioral rigidity (Vansteenkiste & Ryan, 2013).

> [!evidence] Need Frustration
> The active thwarting of basic psychological needs, the experience of their being undermined or blocked by contextual conditions. Theoretically distinguished from mere non-satisfaction (needs not addressed but not actively blocked) because need frustration predicts more severe ill-being outcomes than non-satisfaction and appears to operate through distinct psychological mechanisms. In close relationships, autonomy need frustration occurs through controlling behavior; competence need frustration through chronic criticism and incompetence-signaling; relatedness need frustration through rejection, dismissal, and non-responsiveness. *See also*: [[need-thwarting]], [[satisfaction-frustration-asymmetry]]
> *— [[relationship-motivation-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*

> [!evidence] Need Frustration

Need frustration occurs when the environment actively undermines need satisfaction: forced compliance produces autonomy frustration; failures without competence support produce competence frustration; conditional regard or exclusion produces relatedness frustration. BPNT proposes that need frustration is the primary predictor of psychological ill-health, distinct from mere absence of satisfaction. **See also**: [[need-frustration]], [[need-thwarting]], [[basic-psychological-needs-theory]]

> [!evidence] Need Frustration
> The active thwarting of a basic psychological need (in contrast to mere need deprivation, the absence of need satisfaction). Empirically distinct from low need satisfaction in its consequences: produces the specific signatures of compensatory behavior, defensive functioning, and ill-being that need-deprivation alone does not produce. Methodologically requires separate measurement from need satisfaction.
