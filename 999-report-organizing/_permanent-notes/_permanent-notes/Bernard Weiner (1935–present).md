## Additional Material

> [!person] Bernard Weiner (1935–present)
> American psychologist, UCLA. The architect of the three-dimensional causal taxonomy (locus, stability, controllability) and the attribution-emotion mediation model. Weiner's contributions span from 1972 to the present, with his 1985 and 1986 publications representing the mature statement of his intrapersonal theory. His extension to interpersonal motivation (1995, 2006) — examining how attributions for others' outcomes generate emotions that drive helping behaviour and social judgments — extended the theory's reach into social psychology, moral judgment, and public policy.
>
> **Relationship to other figures:** Built on Heider's internal/external distinction; extended it dimensionally. Influenced by Rotter's locus of control concept but carefully distinguished locus of causality from locus of control. His work intersects with Seligman's on learned helplessness through the stability-expectancy mechanism.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!person] Bernard Weiner (1935–present)
> **Core Contribution:** Developed [[attribution-theory|attribution theory]] in achievement contexts (1985, 2010), providing the three-dimensional framework (locus, stability, controllability) for understanding how causal explanations for performance outcomes determine motivational consequences.
> **Relationship to Other Figures:** Extended Heider's general attribution theory to achievement motivation; influenced Dweck's early helpless-mastery response pattern research; integrated by Zimmerman into the Self-Reflection Phase architecture.
> **Key Works:** *An Attributional Theory of Achievement Motivation and Emotion* (1985), *An Attribution Theory of Motivation* (2010).
> **Relevance to This Analysis:** The attribution mechanism analyzed in Phase IV and operationalized in the Self-Reflection Phase tools is Weiner's framework applied to the PKB academic reading context.
> *— [[srl-forethought-self-reflection-pkb-focused-analysis-2026-03-24 (1)_report]]*
