## Additional Material

> [!section-summary] Section 6 Takeaways
> 1. Metacognitive monitoring — the ongoing evaluation of one's own comprehension state — is the executive prerequisite for effective academic reading; without it, readers cannot detect failures and therefore do not deploy repair strategies.
> 2. The illusion of knowing (fluency illusion + subjective certainty) means that self-reported comprehension is a poor proxy for actual comprehension, and that comprehension monitoring must be trained as a deliberate skill.
> 3. Fix-up strategies (re-reading, self-explanation, schema-building) are powerful only when deployed in response to accurate monitoring; the SRL cycle provides the framework for integrating monitoring and regulation into a cohesive reading practice.
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 6 Takeaways
> 1. The three-tier strategy hierarchy (Cognitive Foundation → Comprehension-Oriented → Motivational Architecture) sequences interventions according to prerequisite logic: lower tiers create conditions that higher tiers presuppose. Starting at the wrong tier produces wasted effort.
> 2. Tier 2 strategies — particularly self-explanation, elaborative interrogation, and retrieval practice — are among the most robustly evidence-supported learning strategies across the empirical literature and produce direct motivational benefits through competence need satisfaction.
> 3. Tier 3 strategies address regulatory quality and goal orientation rather than cognitive technique; their effects are more diffuse but more fundamental, shaping the motivational architecture that determines whether Tier 1 and Tier 2 strategies are persistently deployed over extended reading programs.
> *— [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 6 Takeaways
> 1. Background knowledge/schema richness is the strongest cognitive predictor of new domain learning, partly because schemas extend effective working memory capacity by enabling efficient chunking of new information.
> 2. Reading comprehension is schema construction — using text as a set of activation cues to build a situation model — which explains why reading comprehension is domain-specific and why background knowledge is a prerequisite for deep reading comprehension.
> 3. The expert-novice difference in most domains is primarily a difference in schema organization — experts have schemas organized by deep structural features (domain principles), while novices have schemas organized by surface features (perceptual characteristics).
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 6 Takeaways
> 1. [[elaboration]] — actively connecting new information to existing knowledge through why/how/what-if questions — produces deep, transferable encodings by integrating new nodes into existing schema networks, with elaborative interrogation being one of the highest-utility study strategies.
> 2. The [[self-explanation-effect]] demonstrates that self-explanation during study produces dramatically better learning than passive reading, because it forces construction of accurate situation models and — crucially — surfaces the gaps in comprehension that fluent reading conceals.
> 3. Elaboration and self-explanation operate in opposite directions: elaboration builds the relational network (outward to existing knowledge), while self-explanation builds the conceptual model (inward to mechanism and logic), and both are necessary for deep, transferable understanding.
> *— [[strategic-reading-systems-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*
