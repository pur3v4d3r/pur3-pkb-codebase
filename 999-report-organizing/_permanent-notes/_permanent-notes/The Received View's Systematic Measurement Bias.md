## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Received View's Systematic Measurement Bias
> The reading-primary hypothesis was developed and tested primarily with measures of *receptive* vocabulary breadth — the number of words a reader can recognise and approximately define. These are precisely the dimensions of word knowledge that reading builds most efficiently. Had the research tradition used measures of productive vocabulary depth, collocational accuracy, or register sensitivity as its primary outcome measures, the reading-primary hypothesis would have found much weaker support in the empirical literature. The hypothesis is, in part, a consequence of what the field chose to measure — and what it chose to measure was shaped, in part, by what was easiest to operationalise. This is not a defect unique to vocabulary research, but it is a defect with significant practical consequences.
> *— [[vocabulary-building-reading-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-28_report]]*
