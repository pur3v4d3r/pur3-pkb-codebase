## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Automaticity in Reading → Automaticity in Music: The Same Architecture
> When a beginning piano student learns a piece, their attention is entirely consumed by identifying the notes, finding the keys, and producing individual sounds. When a piece becomes automatic — when note-reading and finger-placement are handled by procedures running below conscious attention — the musician can attend to phrasing, dynamics, expression, and musical communication. The parallel to reading is structural, not merely metaphorical. Both cases involve the same cognitive architecture: (1) a limited attentional resource, (2) lower-level processes that initially consume that resource, (3) practice-driven automatization of those lower-level processes, and (4) consequent liberation of attention for higher-order processing. The Focal-Subsidiary-Attention-Distinction describes both. In music as in reading, the art — the meaning-making — only becomes possible after the mechanics become automatic.
> *— [[reading-fluency-automaticity-focused-analysis-2026-03-20_report]]*
