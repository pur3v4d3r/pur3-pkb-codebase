## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Resolution: Autonomy and Relatedness as Mutually Constitutive in Secure Relationships
> The apparent tension between autonomy and relatedness dissolves under close examination in well-functioning relationships, and the resolution reveals something important about the nature of each need. Autonomy need satisfaction does not require that the other person have no influence on you — it requires that your engagement with the relationship be self-endorsed. A person who deeply values honesty, care, and growth can experience influence from an autonomy-supportive partner as *autonomy-consistent* rather than autonomy-threatening, because accepting that influence is an expression of their own values. Similarly, relatedness need satisfaction does not require that you be identical to the partner or that the partner approve of everything you are — it requires that the partner understand and genuinely care for *who you actually are*. When both these conditions obtain, the partner who knows you deeply is the partner with whom you are most free to be yourself — autonomy and relatedness become mutually reinforcing rather than mutually limiting. The integration is not a compromise between two competing demands; it is the emergence of a qualitatively different relational experience in which each need supports the other.
> *— [[relationship-motivation-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-13_report]]*
