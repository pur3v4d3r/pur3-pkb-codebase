> [!flashcard] Seed 2 — Process
> **Q:** What are the five dimensions of the Event Indexing Model (CATSM), and which is most vulnerable to disruption in academic expository text?
>
> **A:** Causality, Agency, Time, Space, Motivation. Causality is most vulnerable in academic text because causal relations are frequently implicit — the text presents X and Y without explicitly marking the causal connection, requiring the reader to generate bridging inferences using prior knowledge. When prior knowledge is insufficient, the causal dimension collapses, disrupting all other dimensions.
>

> [!flashcard] Seed 2 — Process
> **Q:** Describe the three-mechanism account of why naturalistic reading suppresses vocabulary acquisition. What is each mechanism's specific contribution?
>
> **A:** (1) *Goal-structure suppression*: readers pursuing comprehension as their primary goal process novel words at just sufficient depth for the comprehension goal — they do not elaborate further. (2) *Cognitive load dynamics*: for lower-vocabulary readers, the intrinsic load of managing unknowns while comprehending consumes working memory, leaving insufficient resources for elaborative word processing. (3) *Metacognitive miscalibration*: fluent recognition of a word's form generates a familiarity signal that feels like knowledge, preventing the monitoring system from triggering deeper processing.
>
