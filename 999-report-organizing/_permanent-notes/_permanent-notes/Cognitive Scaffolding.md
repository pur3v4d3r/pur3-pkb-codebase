# Cognitive Scaffolding

> [!definition] Cognitive Scaffolding
> Environmental, social, or material structures that support cognitive performance by organising, simplifying, and augmenting the cognitive work an individual must perform. Scaffolding can be temporary (faded as competence develops) or permanent (constituting an ongoing cognitive extension). The concept derives from Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development and has been developed in instructional design, where it describes structured support for learners. Extended Mind Theory provides a theoretical account of how scaffolding can transition from external support to genuine cognitive extension.
