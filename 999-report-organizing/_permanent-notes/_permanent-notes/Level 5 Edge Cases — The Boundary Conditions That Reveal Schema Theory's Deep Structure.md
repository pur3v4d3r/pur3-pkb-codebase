## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Level 5: Edge Cases — The Boundary Conditions That Reveal Schema Theory's Deep Structure
> **Zoom progression:** We have the full schema dynamics picture: distributed or symbolic architecture, hierarchical organization, threshold-based competition, three-mode update, sleep-based consolidation, and Fitts-stage automation. Level 5 now turns to the cases where this standard account is complicated or falsified by specific phenomena. These are not peripheral anomalies — they are where the theory's most revealing insights live, because failures and exceptions expose the mechanisms that normal-functioning systems conceal.
> **What you'll see at this level:** Schema intrusion errors and the evidential base from Bartlett's experimental program; the expert blindness paradox; traumatic memory as schema disruption; cultural variability in schema content and its theoretical implications; accommodation resistance and why schemas don't update when they should; and the counterintuitive schema-incongruent memory advantage — the finding that information that *violates* schemas is sometimes *better* remembered than information that confirms them.
> **Specialist value:** Edge cases in schema theory are where the most important applied insights reside. Clinicians need schema disruption to understand PTSD; educators need accommodation resistance to understand why conceptual change is hard; cognitive psychologists need schema-incongruent memory to refine models of encoding priority.
> *— [[schema-and-how-they-work-deep-dive-2026-04-08_report]]*
