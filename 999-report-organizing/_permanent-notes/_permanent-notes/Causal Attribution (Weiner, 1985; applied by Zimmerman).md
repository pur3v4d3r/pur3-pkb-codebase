# Causal Attribution (Weiner, 1985; applied by Zimmerman)

> [!definition] Causal Attribution (Weiner, 1985; applied by Zimmerman)
> The cognitive process by which a learner explains the cause of their performance outcome. Attribution has two key dimensions that determine its consequences: (1) *Locus of control* — whether the cause is internal (ability, effort) or external (task difficulty, luck); and (2) *Controllability* — whether the cause can be influenced by the learner's future actions. The combination of these dimensions produces radically different motivational consequences, which is why attribution is the mechanistically critical component of the Self-Reflection Phase.
