# The Universal Intellectual Standards

> [!definition] The Universal Intellectual Standards
> Paul and Elder identify the following core standards, each accompanied by probing questions that operationalize the standard for practical application:
>
> - **Clarity** — Is the reasoning understandable? Could it be elaborated, illustrated, or exemplified? Clarity is the gateway standard: if reasoning is not clear, its other qualities cannot be assessed.
> - **Accuracy** — Is the reasoning free from errors and distortions? Do the claims correspond to reality as best we can determine it?
> - **Precision** — Is the reasoning sufficiently specific and detailed for the purpose at hand? Precision prevents the vagueness that allows ambiguity to masquerade as depth.
> - **Relevance** — Does the reasoning actually bear on the question at issue? Irrelevant information, however accurate, does not advance understanding.
> - **Depth** — Does the reasoning address the complexities and difficulties inherent in the question? Shallow reasoning ignores the deep structures that make problems genuinely difficult.
> - **Breadth** — Does the reasoning consider alternative perspectives and other relevant points of view? Narrow reasoning, even if deep, remains parochial.
> - **[[Logic|Logic]]** — Does the reasoning hang together? Do the conclusions genuinely follow from the premises? Is the overall argument internally consistent?
> - **Significance** — Does the reasoning focus on what is most important? Is the thinker prioritizing the central issues rather than being distracted by peripheral concerns?
> - **Fairness** — Is the reasoning free from bias and self-interest? Does it represent different viewpoints equitably, or does it privilege the thinker's own position?
