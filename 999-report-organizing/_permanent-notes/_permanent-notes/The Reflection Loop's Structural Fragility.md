## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Reflection Loop's Structural Fragility
> Attribution theory predicts that the utility of self-reflection for future performance depends entirely on whether the attributed cause is both controllable and unstable. Only when a learner attributes a performance shortfall to strategy choice (controllable and unstable — it can be changed) does reflection generate productive regulatory adjustment. Attribution to effort (controllable but potentially stable) generates motivational change without strategic change. Attribution to ability (uncontrollable and stable) generates withdrawal. The reflection loop is therefore structurally fragile: it requires a specific attribution pattern to close the cycle productively, and that pattern is not the default. The default attribution under threat and ego-involvement is ability attribution — the very attribution that severs the reflection-forethought connection. SRL frameworks theorize the loop as if it closes naturally; the evidence suggests it closes naturally only for learners who already have adaptive attributional styles.
> *— [[self-regulated-learning-focused-analysis-2026-03-06_report]]*
