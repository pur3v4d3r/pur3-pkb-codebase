## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] Metacognitive Instruction Must Precede Strategy Instruction
> A systematic review of comprehension instruction research reveals an important sequencing principle that is frequently violated in instructional practice: **metacognitive awareness of comprehension processes should be developed before or simultaneously with strategy instruction, not after**. Many reading strategy programs fail or have weak effects because they teach strategies without first developing students' metacognitive awareness of the comprehension problems those strategies address. Students who do not yet recognize that they construct textbases without situation models, who do not yet notice comprehension monitoring failures, who do not yet experience the difference between surface familiarity and deep understanding — these students have no motivational and functional context for strategy use. They learn strategies as arbitrary procedures rather than as solutions to comprehension problems they have actually experienced. Effective instruction begins by developing comprehension awareness — creating conditions where students can observe their own monitoring failures, compare shallow and deep comprehension products, and notice the gap between fluency and understanding. Only on this foundation does strategy instruction achieve its full potential. This sequencing principle applies equally to self-directed adult learners developing academic reading skill: diagnosis of metacognitive awareness gaps should precede prescriptive strategy training.
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-and-metacognition-for-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*
