> [!section-summary] Section 5 Takeaways
> 1. Situation models are constructed through inference generation that connects explicit text content with activated prior knowledge — the CI model explains this as alternating construction and integration phases across proposition cycles.
> 2. Bridging inferences (closing coherence gaps between text segments) are the most comprehension-critical inference type; academic texts are dense with unexplained bridges requiring disciplinary knowledge to complete.
> 3. Inference generation in academic reading is partially domain-specific — disciplinary schemas generate disciplinary inferences, which means general academic reading skill instruction has inherent transfer limits.
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 5 Takeaways
> 1. Goal orientation is a proximal determinant of academic reading quality: mastery goals predict deep processing and persistence; performance-avoidance goals predict surface processing and avoidance. Goal orientation diagnosis should precede strategy prescription.
> 2. The 2×2 framework reveals that performance-approach goals sometimes produce adequate achievement in assessed contexts while simultaneously undermining genuine comprehension development — a distinction critical for learners whose primary goal is understanding rather than certification.
> 3. Implicit theories of reading ability directly modulate achievement goal orientation: entity theory promotes performance-avoidance and helplessness under difficulty; incremental theory promotes mastery goals and strategic adaptation. Belief-change interventions targeting reading ability malleability are legitimate and practical.
> *— [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 5 Takeaways
> 1. Memory is reconstructive, not reproductive — encoding is schema-guided selection and organization of experience, and retrieval is schema-guided reconstruction from partial traces, with default values filling the gaps.
> 2. Schema assimilation is the primary mechanism of long-term memory distortion: distinctive memories gradually and systematically transform toward schema-consistent default values over time.
> 3. Transfer of learning is fundamentally schema transfer — the abstract organizing framework that generalizes across instances, not the specific training example — explaining why varied practice and principle-focused instruction support better transfer.
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 5 Takeaways
> 1. The [[spacing-effect]] — one of the most reliable findings in cognitive science — demonstrates that distributing practice across time produces dramatically better long-term retention than massing equivalent practice, via mechanisms of implicit retrieval, encoding variability, and memory consolidation windows.
> 2. [[interleaving-effect|Interleaved practice]] outperforms blocked practice on delayed tests and transfer problems despite feeling worse and performing worse in-session, because interleaving builds the discriminative recognition and retrieval skills that transfer actually demands.
> 3. Spacing and interleaving are unified expressions of **encoding diversity** — both create more robust, context-independent memories by ensuring that each encounter with material occurs under varied cognitive conditions.
> *— [[strategic-reading-systems-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*
