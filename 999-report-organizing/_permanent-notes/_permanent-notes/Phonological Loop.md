# Phonological Loop

> [!definition] Phonological Loop
> **Phonological Loop**: The verbal working memory subsystem, comprising a *phonological store* (passive temporary holding of phonological representations, susceptible to trace decay over approximately 2 seconds) and an *articulatory rehearsal process* (sub-vocal rehearsal that refreshes phonological store traces and converts visual items to phonological form). Demonstrated through four core phenomena: phonological similarity effect, word-length effect, irrelevant speech effect, articulatory suppression. Neuroanatomically: left inferior parietal cortex (store) and Broca's area / supplementary motor area (rehearsal). See: [[phonological-loop]], [[phonological-awareness]].

> [!evidence] Phonological Loop
> The component of working memory responsible for maintaining and rehearsing verbal and acoustic information. It consists of two subcomponents: a *phonological store* (a passive acoustic buffer holding sounds for approximately 2 seconds before decay) and an *articulatory rehearsal process* (an inner voice that refreshes traces in the phonological store through sub-vocal rehearsal, effectively resetting the 2-second decay clock). Evidence for the phonological loop comes from the *phonological similarity effect* (sequences of similar-sounding words are harder to recall than dissimilar ones), the *word-length effect* (longer words are harder to hold than shorter ones), and *articulatory suppression* effects (repeating an irrelevant word disrupts verbal working memory).
> *— [[memory-systems-working-memory-long-term-memory-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*

> [!evidence] Phonological Loop
> A specialised subsystem of working memory comprising a temporary phonological store (with a decay period of approximately 1.5–2 seconds) and an articulatory rehearsal process (analogous to inner speech) that refreshes decaying representations. It is the primary mechanism for maintaining verbal-sequential information.
>
> **Boundary:** The phonological loop does not process meaning. It operates on sound-based codes. Semantic processing requires the involvement of the [[central-executive]] and [[long-term-memory]].
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The phonological loop provides the clearest illustration of how a domain-specific subsystem can be characterised through converging experimental effects, and it anchors the broader claim that working memory is multicomponent rather than unitary.
>
