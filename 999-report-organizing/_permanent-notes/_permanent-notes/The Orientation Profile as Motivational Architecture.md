## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] The Orientation Profile as Motivational Architecture
> I find COT's profile conception one of its most theoretically valuable but least-developed features. The idea that a person carries a three-dimensional motivational architecture — not assignable to a categorical type — has significant implications for assessment and intervention. Most motivational interventions, whether in education, health, or organizational contexts, treat motivation as a unidimensional variable (more vs. less motivated, autonomous vs. controlled). But if a person has a profile — say, high autonomy orientation in intellectual domains combined with high control orientation in interpersonal-evaluative contexts — then interventions need to be domain- and context-sensitive in ways that current practice rarely achieves. The profile conception suggests that we should ask not "what is this person's motivation type?" but "in what domains, under what conditions, does each orientation predominate?"
> *— [[causality-orientations-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*
