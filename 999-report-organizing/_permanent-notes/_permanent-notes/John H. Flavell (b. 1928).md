> [!person] John H. Flavell (b. 1928)
> American developmental psychologist at Stanford, credited with establishing metacognition as a formal scientific construct. His research on children's memory monitoring in the 1970s initiated four decades of metacognition research.
>
> *Core contribution:* The metacognition framework distinguishing metacognitive knowledge (person, task, and strategy knowledge) from metacognitive experiences, and the formal definition of metacognition as cognition about cognition with self-regulatory functions.
>
> *Relationship to other figures:* His framework was extended by Ann Brown to reading comprehension contexts; Schraw & Moshman refined his knowledge taxonomy; Nelson & Narens formalized his monitoring-control distinction.
>
> *Key work:* Flavell (1976, 1979)
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-and-metacognition-for-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*

> [!person] John H. Flavell (b. 1928)
> Stanford University developmental psychologist. Inaugurated the modern study of metacognition with his 1976 definition and subsequent elaboration of the metacognitive knowledge taxonomy (person, task, and strategy knowledge). Flavell's framework established metacognition as a distinct construct separable from both intelligence and executive function.
>
> **Relationship to other figures:** Student of Piaget's developmental tradition; influenced Brown and Schoenfeld; his work on metamemory preceded Nelson-Narens.
> **Key works:** *Metacognitive aspects of problem solving* (1976); *Metacognition and cognitive monitoring* (1979).

> [!person] John H. Flavell (b. 1928)
> Developmental psychologist, Stanford University. Coined the term "metacognition" and developed the foundational tripartite taxonomy distinguishing metacognitive knowledge, metacognitive experiences, and metacognitive regulation. His 1979 paper "Metacognition and Cognitive Monitoring" in *American Psychologist* established the field's conceptual architecture and launched the empirical program that would produce the reading comprehension monitoring literature.
>
> **Lineage:** Drew on Piaget's developmental tradition; influenced Ann Brown, who applied the framework to reading. Influenced by the information-processing revolution; influenced virtually the entire metacognition field.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the foundational conceptual taxonomy; essentially all reading metacognition research derives from or responds to his framework.
> *— [[metacognition-and-reading-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] John H. Flavell (b. 1928)
> John Flavell is the founding figure of modern metacognition research. Professor at Stanford University for decades, Flavell coined the term "metacognition" in 1976 from his earlier work on "metamemory" and children's knowledge about memory. His theoretical framework distinguishing metacognitive knowledge (person, task, strategy) from metacognitive experiences established the conceptual architecture that subsequent researchers elaborated and tested. Flavell's developmental orientation — studying metacognition primarily through child development research — grounded the field in empirically observable growth rather than abstract theorizing.
> **Key works referenced:** *Metacognition and Cognitive Monitoring* (1979), *Cognitive Development* (1977).
> **Relationship to other figures:** Intellectual progenitor of Ann Brown's developmental work; Nelson and Narens built on his monitoring concept in the information-processing tradition; Zimmerman synthesized his metacognitive framework into motivational SRL theory.
> *— [[metacognitive-skills-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

> [!person] John H. Flavell (b. 1928)
> [[john-h.-flavell|John Flavell]], Professor Emeritus at Stanford University, is the originator of the term "metacognition" and the developer of the first comprehensive taxonomy of metacognitive phenomena. His 1979 framework — distinguishing metacognitive knowledge, metacognitive experience, goals/tasks, and actions/strategies — established the conceptual landscape that Nelson and Narens later formalized.
>
> **Core contribution:** Coined "metacognition"; developed the tripartite taxonomy of metacognitive knowledge (person, task, strategy variables); established developmental metacognition research.
> **Relationship to other figures:** Provided the taxonomic foundation that Nelson and Narens formalized architecturally; influenced the entire field of metacognition research.
> **Key works:** Flavell (1976, 1979).
