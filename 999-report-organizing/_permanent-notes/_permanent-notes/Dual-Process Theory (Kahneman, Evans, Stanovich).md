# Dual-Process Theory (Kahneman, Evans, Stanovich)

> [!definition] Dual-Process Theory (Kahneman, Evans, Stanovich)
> A broad theoretical framework distinguishing two types of cognitive processing: System 1 (fast, automatic, associative, low-effort, resistant to conscious override) and System 2 (slow, deliberate, rule-governed, effortful, sensitive to instruction). Deductive reasoning is paradigmatically a System 2 activity; deductive errors — including belief bias and affirmation of the consequent — are often explained as failures of System 2 to override System 1's rapid, heuristically generated responses. Individual differences in deductive performance correlate with System 2 capacity measures (working memory, fluid intelligence) and with the disposition to engage System 2 (actively open-minded thinking).
