# Articulatory Suppression

> [!definition] Articulatory Suppression
> **Articulatory Suppression**: The concurrent production of an irrelevant spoken utterance (e.g., "the, the, the" or "blah, blah, blah") that prevents articulatory rehearsal, thereby blocking the refreshment of phonological store traces and eliminating the acoustic coding advantage for verbal material presentation. Articulatory suppression is the primary behavioral manipulation used to isolate phonological store contributions from rehearsal contributions in working memory experiments.

> [!evidence] Articulatory Suppression
> Having participants repeat an irrelevant sound (e.g., "the, the, the...") during memorisation eliminates the word length effect for visually presented items and attenuates the phonological similarity effect. This suppression occupies the articulatory rehearsal mechanism, preventing sub-vocal recoding — **the clearest evidence that the articulatory rehearsal process, not merely the store, is critical for maintenance of phonological representations.**
> *— [[baddeley-working-memory-model-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!evidence] Articulatory-Suppression
