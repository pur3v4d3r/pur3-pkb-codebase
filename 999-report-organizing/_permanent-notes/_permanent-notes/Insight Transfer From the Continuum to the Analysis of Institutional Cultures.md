## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Insight Transfer: From the Continuum to the Analysis of Institutional Cultures
> The OIT typology was developed primarily for individual-level regulation, but the underlying mechanism transfers to the analysis of institutional cultures — to the question of why some organizations produce sustained, autonomous engagement from their members while others produce compliance that collapses the moment incentives shift. The same architecture applies: an organizational culture that operates primarily through external contingencies (compensation, surveillance, formal sanctions) will produce externally regulated engagement; one that operates through reputation and shame will produce introjected engagement; one that successfully articulates rationales that members can identify with will produce identified engagement; one that integrates its values with the broader value structures of its members' lives will produce integrated engagement; and the few organizations that find ways to make the work itself intrinsically interesting will produce intrinsic motivation directly. The mechanism, articulated for individuals, generalizes — though the institutional case introduces complications (collective need-satisfaction substrates, organizational analogues of relatedness) that the standard framework would need to be extended to handle adequately.
