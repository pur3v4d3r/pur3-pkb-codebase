# Amotivation

> [!definition] Amotivation
> The absence of intentional motivation — behavior is not driven by any identified or intrinsic reason, or is not engaged with at all. The motivational state associated with the impersonal orientation. In OIT's motivational continuum, amotivation represents the complete absence of self-determination, contrasting with externally regulated, introjected, identified, integrated, and intrinsic motivation.

> [!evidence] Amotivation
> A state in which the person lacks any sense of agency with respect to an activity — neither intrinsic motivation (engagement for its own sake) nor extrinsic motivation (engagement for separable outcomes). Amotivation is the motivational consequence of events that simultaneously frustrate perceived competence and externalise PLOC to the point of helplessness. It is the deepest form of motivational damage in the SDT framework and is associated with disengagement, helplessness, and eventually dropout. Amotivation is distinct from low intrinsic motivation — one can be extrinsically motivated (working for rewards) without being amotivated.
> *— [[cognitive-evaluation-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-13_report]]*

> [!evidence] Amotivation
> The absence or near-absence of motivation, characterized by lack of both intrinsic and extrinsic regulation. [[amotivation]] may result from lack of competence (the person does not believe their behavior can produce desired outcomes), lack of outcome contingency (behavior does not seem to affect outcomes), or lack of value (outcomes are not cared about). SDT distinguishes three subtypes of amotivation that differ in their attributional character.

> [!evidence] Amotivation
> The absence of intentional regulation — the state in which a person either does not engage in a behavior at all, or engages without any intention behind the action. In learning contexts, amotivation is typically the result of sustained need thwarting: the learner has given up any sense that engagement will satisfy their needs. Distinguished from disinterest (which can coexist with intrinsic motivation for other topics) by its global quality and its association with helplessness, passivity, and disengagement from academic life broadly.

> [!evidence] Amotivation
> The absence of intentional regulation — neither intrinsic nor extrinsic motivation is operative. In SDT, amotivation arises when a person does not perceive a connection between their behaviour and outcomes (attributing outcomes to luck, others' power, or uncontrollable forces), when they believe the activity is not worth attempting, or when they lack the perceived competence to engage. Amotivation is NOT the same as laziness or disengagement from all activity; it is contextually specific — a student may be amotivated in mathematics but highly motivated in art. It is predicted by chronic need thwarting, particularly competence thwarting, and by low perceived locus of causality.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*
