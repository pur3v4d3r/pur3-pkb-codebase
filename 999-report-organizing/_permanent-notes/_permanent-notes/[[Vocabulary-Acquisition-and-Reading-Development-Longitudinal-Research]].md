## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] [[Vocabulary-Acquisition-and-Reading-Development-Longitudinal-Research]]
> **Description:** Longitudinal studies tracking vocabulary development and reading comprehension from early schooling through secondary and tertiary education provide the empirical evidence base for the Matthew Effect and for vocabulary instruction priorities. A dedicated note synthesising the major longitudinal datasets (Hart & Risley, NICHD Early Child Care Research Network, the Rotterdam studies) would provide the developmental evidence grounding that this report's cross-sectional synthesis cannot supply.
>
> **Connection to this report:** The Matthew Effect claim (Section 2, Section 7) is based on cross-sectional and short-term longitudinal evidence; the developmental trajectory claim needs long-term longitudinal evidence to stand on its strongest footing.
>
> **Priority:** Medium
>
> **Suggested report type:** Historical-Genealogical Report — tracing the development of vocabulary and reading research from Hart & Risley (1995) through the 30-million word gap debate through contemporary precision.
>
