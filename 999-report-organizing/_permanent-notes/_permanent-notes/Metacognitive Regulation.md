# Metacognitive Regulation

> [!definition] Metacognitive Regulation
> The executive processes by which individuals actively manage their own cognition: planning (selecting and sequencing strategies before engaging a task), monitoring (tracking comprehension, progress, and quality during task performance), and evaluation (assessing outcomes and cognitive processes after task completion). Regulation is the functional payoff of metacognitive knowledge — it is where knowledge translates into improved performance.

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Regulation
> [[metacognitive-regulation|Metacognitive regulation]] encompasses the executive processes through which a cognitive agent controls and directs their own cognitive activity. Following Brown (1987) and Schraw (1998), these processes are typically decomposed into three phases: **planning** (goal-setting, strategy selection, resource allocation before a task), **monitoring** (tracking comprehension and performance during a task), and **evaluating** (assessing outcomes and process effectiveness after a task). Regulation converts monitoring information into adaptive action.
>
> **Boundary:** Regulation is not the same as the strategies it deploys. [[elaborative-interrogation|Elaborative interrogation]], for example, is a cognitive strategy; the *decision* to use elaborative interrogation in a particular study context is a regulatory act.
>

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Regulation
> Metacognitive regulation encompasses the executive processes by which learners manage their cognitive activities in pursuit of learning goals. Three core regulatory processes are most frequently identified: (a) *planning* — the anticipatory selection of strategies, allocation of resources, and formulation of approach before cognitive engagement; (b) *monitoring* — the ongoing evaluation of comprehension, learning progress, and strategy effectiveness during task engagement; and (c) *evaluation* — retrospective assessment of outcomes and strategy effectiveness after task completion, with updating of metacognitive knowledge and revision of future approach plans.
>
> **Boundary:** Metacognitive regulation is not equivalent to executive function more broadly, though it draws on executive function resources (particularly working memory and inhibitory control). Regulation is specifically goal-directed management of cognition in service of learning objectives; executive function encompasses a broader range of cognitive control processes that include non-metacognitive functions such as task-switching and working memory updating in general contexts.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Regulation is the component of metacognition most directly targeted by instructional interventions and most proximal to learning outcomes. Without regulation, monitoring information generates awareness without behavioral consequence.
>
> **See also:** [[metacognitive-self-regulation]], [[metacognitive-regulation]], [[self-regulated-learning]], [[cyclical-model-of-self-regulated-learning]]
> *— [[metacognitive-skills-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Regulation
> The executive processes by which learners manage their own cognitive activities in pursuit of learning goals. Three core regulation processes: planning (anticipatory strategy selection and resource allocation), monitoring-based updating (strategy adjustment in response to monitoring signals), and evaluation (retrospective assessment of outcomes with updating of metacognitive knowledge). Regulation is the behavioral consequence component of the metacognitive system — the only layer whose action is directly observable in learning behavior.
>
> **Boundary:** Not equivalent to executive function broadly defined, though it draws on PFC-implemented executive resources. Regulation in the metacognitive sense is specifically directed toward learning and understanding goals.
>
