## Additional Material

> [!person] Thomas O. Nelson (1943–2005) & Louis Narens
> Cognitive psychologists at the University of Maryland who developed the meta-level/object-level monitoring-control model that formalized metacognition as an information-processing architecture. Their 1990 model describes how the meta-level receives information from the object level (monitoring) and sends regulatory signals back (control), creating a feedback loop. In this report, the Nelson-Narens model provides the structural vocabulary for understanding how PKBs externalize the meta-level.
>
> **Key works referenced:** "Metamemory: A Theoretical Framework and New Findings" (1990).
>

> [!person] Thomas O. Nelson (1943–2005) & Louis Narens
> University of Maryland. Developed the monitoring-control framework that underpins Section 2's analysis of metacognitive architecture. Their model — in which monitoring produces information that feeds control decisions, and control actions produce new monitoring data — is the structural basis for this report's argument that PKB scaffolds must support both monitoring and control in coupled pairs. Key work: "Metamemory: A Theoretical Framework and New Findings" (1990).
> **Relationship to other figures:** Complemented Flavell's content taxonomy with a process architecture; influenced Dunlosky and Metcalfe's later work on metacognitive judgments.
