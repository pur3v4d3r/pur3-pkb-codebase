## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Organisational Leadership and Performance Evaluation
> The actor-observer asymmetry and the fundamental attribution error combine to create a predictable pattern in organisational performance reviews: managers attribute employees' poor performance disproportionately to dispositional factors (low motivation, lack of skill, character flaws), while employees attribute the same performance to situational factors (insufficient resources, unclear direction, competing demands). This attributional divergence is not a communication problem but a structural consequence of the information asymmetry between observers and actors. Effective leadership, in attributional terms, requires the deliberate practice of *situational attribution* — the disciplined habit of asking "What in the environment might be producing this behaviour?" before defaulting to "What kind of person produces this behaviour?" The dimensional framework provides concrete tools: when evaluating poor performance, a leader trained in attributional reasoning might ask whether the cause is stable or unstable (can it change?), controllable or uncontrollable by the employee (is it fair to hold them responsible?), and internal or external (is this about the person or about the position?).
>
> **Structural principle:** The dual architecture of bias (cognitive + motivational) means that organisational debiasing requires both structural interventions (reducing information asymmetry) and motivational interventions (reducing ego-protective attributional patterns in leaders).
