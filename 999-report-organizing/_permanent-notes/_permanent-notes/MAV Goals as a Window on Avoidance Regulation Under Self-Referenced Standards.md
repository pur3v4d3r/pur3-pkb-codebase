## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] MAV Goals as a Window on Avoidance Regulation Under Self-Referenced Standards
> The existence and profile of MAV goals provides a natural experiment on what happens when avoidance regulation is directed at a self-referenced standard rather than a normative one. The finding that MAV goals reduce the severity of maladaptive outcomes compared to PAV goals — while still producing more anxiety and lower intrinsic interest than MAP goals — suggests that the *standard* dimension modulates the intensity of avoidance-mode pathology. Comparing MAV to an anxious self-critic perfecting their craft to protect a self-image of excellence. This is less socially threatening than PAV, but cognitively and emotionally expensive in ways that MAP is not. The MAV evidence thus supports the claim that avoidance regulation *per se* carries cognitive and affective costs, independent of whether the comparison standard is internal or external.
> *— [[achievement-goal-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-24_report]]*
