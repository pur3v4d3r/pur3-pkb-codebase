# Adaptive vs. Defensive Inference** *(Zimmerman, 2000)*

> [!definition] Adaptive vs. Defensive Inference** *(Zimmerman, 2000)*
> **Definition:** The two possible outcomes of the Self-Reaction subsystem. *Adaptive inference* leads the learner to adjust, persist, and re-engage with appropriate modifications. *Defensive inference* leads the learner to protect self-esteem through withdrawal, lowered standards, or avoidance — at the cost of learning and self-efficacy.
> **Operational Indicators:** Defensive inference can be disguised as wisdom ("I'm not ready for this yet") or self-care ("I need to come back to this when I'm better prepared"). The diagnostic question: does the inference generate a *specific, controllable action* that will be taken? If yes, it may be adaptive. If it generates withdrawal without a specific return plan, it is more likely defensive.
> **Cross-References:** [[self-reflection-phase]], [[attribution-theory]], [[learned-helplessness]]
