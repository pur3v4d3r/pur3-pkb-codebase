## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Active Reading Prompt
> Before continuing, pause to consider what you now know about the journey from molecular cloud to protostar. You have followed a narrative of increasing density, rising temperature, and evolving geometry — from diffuse interstellar gas, through gravitational collapse, to a disk-and-jet system centred on a growing embryonic star. The next section addresses the final threshold: what happens when the temperature at the centre of the protostar becomes high enough to ignite nuclear fusion? As you read, consider how the transition from gravitational contraction to nuclear burning changes the star's relationship with its own energy budget — and what determines whether that transition succeeds at all.
> *— [[how-stars-are-created-in-the-universe-foundational-report-2026-04-17_report]]*

> [!evidence] Active Reading Prompt
> You have now traced the full physical pathway of star formation — from raw material through collapse, accretion, and nuclear ignition — and explored the diversity of outcomes that this process produces. The remaining two sections shift perspective: from the physics of what happens to the question of how we know it happens and what we do not yet understand. As you read, consider the relationship between [[empiricism|empirical observation]] and [[Theoretical-Framework|theoretical modelling]] in a field where the timescales of the processes being studied vastly exceed the timescales of human inquiry.
> *— [[how-stars-are-created-in-the-universe-foundational-report-2026-04-17_report]]*
