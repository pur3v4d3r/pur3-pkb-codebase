## Reflections

> [!reflection] Reflecting on Section 6
> 1. The CTML principles are derived from studies using specific multimedia formats (narration + animation, text + static diagrams) in specific content domains. How confident should we be in generalizing these principles to emerging educational technologies — immersive virtual reality environments, interactive simulations, large language model tutors? What aspects of the underlying cognitive architecture remain constant, and what might change?
> 2. CTML's twelve principles were largely derived from studies with young adults in laboratory-like conditions. How might the principles apply differently for younger children, older adults, or learners with cognitive differences? Do the principles represent universal features of human cognitive architecture or features specific to the college-undergraduate populations in which most CTML research was conducted?
> 3. The finding that generative drawing outperforms studying pre-made diagrams has direct implications for how we use AI-generated images and visualizations in education: providing learners with AI-generated diagrams might actually *reduce* learning compared with prompting learners to generate their own. What are the design implications of this for AI-assisted educational tools?
> *— [[generative-learning-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
