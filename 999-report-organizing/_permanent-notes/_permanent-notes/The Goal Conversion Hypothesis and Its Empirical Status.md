> [!tension-identified] The Goal Conversion Hypothesis and Its Empirical Status
> A theoretically important but empirically contested claim in achievement goal research is that PAP goals are unstable under competitive pressure — that they tend to convert toward PAV dynamics when normative comparison becomes threatening. Elliot and colleagues have provided experimental evidence for this conversion process. Critics (notably Harackiewicz and collaborators) have argued that the evidence for conversion is limited and that PAP goals can be held stably over time without generating PAV-like consequences. The current state of evidence suggests a conditional claim: goal conversion is more likely in populations with low perceived competence, in high-stakes competitive environments, and when PAP goal strength is matched by strong normative threat. The implication for educational practice is that the adaptive value of PAP goals is competence-contingent in a way that MAP goals are not — an asymmetry that is itself revealing about the fundamental difference between approach and avoidance as regulatory modes.
> *— [[achievement-goal-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-24_report]]*
