> [!methodology-and-sources] Scope Statement
> **This report's focus:** Element interactivity as the unifying mechanism underlying all of [[cognitive-load-theory]]'s predictions, effects, and design principles — its definition, measurement, theoretical role, boundary conditions, and frontier debates.
>
> **Drawn from broader topic:** Cognitive Load Theory (the full field encompassing dozens of instructional effects, three load types, evolutionary foundations, and 40+ years of research)
>
> **What this report covers:**
> - Element interactivity as the core explanatory mechanism of CLT
> - The relationship between element interactivity and the three load types
> - What constitutes an "element" and how interactivity is formally defined
> - How element interactivity generates specific CLT instructional effects
> - Boundary conditions and edge cases where the framework breaks down
> - The germane load reconceptualization controversy
> - The measurement problem for element interactivity
> - Frontier research including neural correlates and adaptive instruction
>
> **What this report does NOT cover:**
> - Comprehensive survey of all CLT instructional effects (see [[cognitive-load-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-11|CLT Foundational Report]])
> - Richard Mayer's [[cognitive-theory-of-multimedia-learning]] as a separate framework
> - Detailed treatment of [[four-component-instructional-design-4cid|4C/ID]] as an instructional design model
> - Historical chronological development narrative of CLT
> - Practical instructional design cookbook for applying CLT effects
>
> **Intended audience:** Specialists and serious investigators with existing familiarity with CLT's basic framework who want to understand the theory at the mechanism level — why it works, not just what it prescribes.
>
> **Prerequisites:** Familiarity with [[working-memory]], [[long-term-memory]], the basic tripartite load model, and at least surface awareness of CLT instructional effects (worked examples, split attention, redundancy, expertise reversal).
>
> **Why narrow scope matters:** A Deep Dive earns its value through exhaustive treatment of a focused subject. Broader coverage of Cognitive Load Theory is available in the [[cognitive-load-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-11|CLT Foundational Report]]. This report assumes the reader already has general familiarity and wants to GO DEEP on the single mechanism — element interactivity — that makes the entire theory cohere.

> [!methodology-and-sources] Scope Statement
> **This report's focus:** [[Element-Interactivity]] as the deep mechanism of intrinsic cognitive load in [[Cognitive-Load-Theory]].
>
> **Drawn from broader topic:** Cognitive Load Theory as developed by [[John-Sweller]] and colleagues (Sweller, [[Fred-Paas]], van Merriënboer, [[Kirschner,-Sweller-&-Clark]]).
>
> **What this report covers:** the construct of element interactivity itself; how it generates intrinsic load; what counts as an element and what counts as an interaction; how expertise transforms the count; the edge cases that complicate the construct (biologically primary knowledge, expertise reversal, germane load reformulation); current quantification and measurement disputes; the frontier of neural and computational accounts.
>
> **What this report does NOT cover:** comprehensive history of CLT, the full catalog of CLT effects in their breadth, [[Multimedia-Learning-Theory]] in its broad form, applications of CLT to specific instructional domains, PKM-specific applications. Each of these merits a separate report and several already exist (`cognitive-load-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-12`, `cognitive-load-theory-and-pkm-foundational-report-2026-04-12`).
>
> **Intended audience:** specialists, researchers, advanced practitioners with prior familiarity with CLT in its standard exposition. Readers new to CLT should consult the foundational report first.
>
> **Prerequisites:** working familiarity with [[Cognitive-Architecture-Working-Memory-&-Long-Term-Memory]], the basic three-load taxonomy, and the standard CLT effects ([[Worked-Example-Effect]], [[Split-Attention-Effect]], [[Modality-Effect]], [[Redundancy-Effect]]).
>
> **Why narrow scope matters:** A Deep Dive earns its value through exhaustive treatment of a focused subject. The intrinsic load construct, more than any other element of CLT, has shifted from a peripheral notion in the early formulations to the load-bearing concept on which the modern theory rests. Any practitioner or researcher working at the level where CLT is currently being theorized must understand element interactivity in detail; broader treatments do not provide that detail.

> - Systematic calibration failures: illusions of knowing, overconfidence, the [[dunning-kruger-effect]]
> - The monitoring-control coupling problem: how monitoring quality propagates to regulatory decisions
> - Computational models of metacognitive monitoring (signal detection theory approaches, Bayesian models)
> - Neural substrates of monitoring accuracy ([[prefrontal-cortex|prefrontal cortex]], anterior cingulate)
> - Technology-mediated calibration interventions
> - The emerging question of algorithmic metacognition

> - [[metacognitive-regulation]] strategies in detail (planning, evaluation) except as monitoring outputs feed into them
> - Educational interventions for metacognition broadly (covered in [[metacognition-in-learning-focused-analysis-2026-03-20]])
> - Developmental trajectory of metacognition in children
>
> **Intended audience:** Specialists and serious investigators with prior knowledge of cognitive psychology and metacognition fundamentals.

> [!methodology-and-sources] Scope Statement
> **This report's focus:** The internalization continuum within Organismic Integration Theory — specifically, the mechanisms by which externally regulated behavior becomes autonomously endorsed.
>
> **Drawn from broader topic:** Self-Determination Theory (SDT) as a whole, which comprises six sub-theories: [[cognitive-evaluation-theory-cet|Cognitive Evaluation Theory]], Organismic Integration Theory, [[basic-psychological-needs-theory-BPNT|Basic Psychological Needs Theory]], Goal Contents Theory, Causality Orientations Theory, and Relationships Motivation Theory.
>
> **What this report covers:**
> - The four extrinsic regulatory styles (external, introjected, identified, integrated) and their qualitative distinctions
> - The role of the three basic psychological needs ([[autonomy-need|autonomy]], [[competence-need|competence]], [[relatedness-need|relatedness]]) as the substrate enabling internalization
> - The mechanism by which need satisfaction produces movement along the continuum
> - Edge cases where the standard model breaks down
> - Current research frontier
> - Speculation about internalization in algorithmically mediated environments
>
> **What this report does NOT cover:**
> - [[cognitive-evaluation-theory-cet|Cognitive Evaluation Theory]] and the undermining effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation — covered in dedicated CET reports
> - Goal Contents Theory and the intrinsic/extrinsic life aspirations distinction
> - Causality Orientations Theory and individual-difference dimensions
> - Relationships Motivation Theory and need satisfaction in close relationships
> - Broad survey of all six sub-theories — covered in [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01|foundational SDT reports]]
>
> Each excluded topic merits its own dedicated report. The narrowing here is what allows the present report to achieve specialist-level depth on the internalization mechanism specifically.
>
> **Intended audience:** Specialists and serious investigators who already have general familiarity with SDT vocabulary and want exhaustive treatment of one mechanism within it.
>
> **Prerequisites:** Reader is presumed to know what SDT is, who [[Edward-L.-Deci|Deci]] and [[richard-ryan|Ryan]] are, that there are three basic psychological needs, and that SDT distinguishes intrinsic from extrinsic motivation. Readers needing this background should consult the foundational SDT reports first.
>
> **Why narrow scope matters:** A Deep Dive earns its value through exhaustive treatment of a focused subject. Broader coverage of SDT is available in foundational reports.
