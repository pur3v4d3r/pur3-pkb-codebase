## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] + ❓ Question — The Mastery Language Bank
> The report includes a language bank distinguishing mastery-oriented from performance-oriented phrasing. I noticed that mastery-oriented language is consistently **specific, behavioral, and controllable**: "I identified specific gaps — sections 3 and 4" rather than "I failed to grasp the main argument."
>
> **My question:** Does this language really matter that much? Or is this a surface-level reframing that doesn't change the underlying evaluative content?
>
> *Working through it:* The research cited (Ames, 1992; Black & Wiliam, 1998) suggests language matters precisely because it determines *what gets activated* cognitively when you evaluate performance. Global failure language ("I failed") activates identity-level schemas. Behavioral, specific language ("I didn't pause at section breaks") activates procedure-level schemas. The first produces affect and rumination; the second produces planning. The activation difference is not trivial — it shapes what the brain does next.
> *— [[worked-example-optimal-reading-session_report]]*
