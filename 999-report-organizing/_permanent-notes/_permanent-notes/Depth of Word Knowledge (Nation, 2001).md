# Depth of Word Knowledge (Nation, 2001)

> [!definition] Depth of Word Knowledge (Nation, 2001)
> The multidimensional representation of a lexical item in long-term memory, comprising at minimum: (1) *form knowledge* — orthographic and phonological representation; (2) *meaning knowledge* — core denotative meaning, semantic range, and connotative associations; (3) *use knowledge* — syntactic behaviour, collocational constraints, register appropriateness, and pragmatic application. Full productive mastery requires all three dimensions to be robustly encoded and fluently accessible; receptive reading comprehension requires a lower threshold, but even this threshold is higher than is commonly assumed.
>
> **Boundary:** Partial word knowledge — knowing a word "when I see it" without being able to retrieve it productively, knowing a vague approximate meaning without knowing its boundaries — does not constitute the kind of knowledge that reliably supports text comprehension in novel syntactic contexts or transfers to productive use.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The partial-knowledge problem means that incidental acquisition studies may overestimate true learning by counting any post-test advantage, however marginal, as "acquisition." It also means that readers may be systematically overestimating their own vocabulary size by conflating "vague familiarity" with "knowledge."
>
