> **Q:** What does Ausubel's meaningful learning theory imply for instructional design?
> **A:** Instruction should begin by activating and organizing relevant prior knowledge using advance organizers — abstract, superordinate frameworks that create conceptual anchors for new content. Without relevant prior organization, new material is learned only rote (verbal association without genuine understanding).

> [!flashcard] Seed 9 — Application
> **Q:** How does EI contribute specifically to situation model construction during reading?
> **A:** Situation models have multiple dimensions (spatial, temporal, causal, referential). EI contributes specifically to the causal dimension: by generating why-answers, the reader explicitly constructs the causal network linking text propositions. This produces a more causally coherent mental model of the described state of affairs, enabling inference and transfer beyond verbatim text recall.
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate

> [!flashcard] Seed 9 — Application
> **Q:** Why do most learners choose massed study over distributed practice, and what does this reveal about metacognitive regulation?
> **A:** Massed study produces higher subjective fluency and higher immediate JOLs than spaced study, even though spaced study produces superior long-term retention. Learners regulate study behavior partly on the basis of JOL signals — which are systematically distorted by fluency. This is the fluency illusion in study strategy self-regulation: learners are regulating (using monitoring to guide behavior) but their monitoring cues are invalid for long-term learning, producing the systematic preference for inferior strategies.
> **Source:** Phase V
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #fluency-illusion, #spaced-repetition, #desirable-difficulties, #metacognitive-regulation

> [!flashcard] Seed 9 — Application
> **Q:** A reader reports that they have been reading extensively for years but their vocabulary does not seem to be growing. Based on this report's analysis, identify the three most likely causes.
>
> **A:** (1) *Below-threshold material*: Most reading is in material far below the 98% coverage level — too comfortable to encounter new vocabulary. Fix: deliberately choose more challenging material at the coverage edge. (2) *Pure incidental mode without depth*: Encountering unfamiliar words but processing them shallowly (skipping or surface inference) — the depth-frequency multiplier is not activating. Fix: practice enhanced incidental acquisition with morphological analysis and post-reading review. (3) *Wrong vocabulary domain*: Reading general text when target vocabulary is domain-specific — incidental acquisition through general reading cannot reach technical/low-frequency vocabulary. Fix: combine extensive domain reading with intentional study of domain-specific terms.
>
> **Source:** Phase III, V, Practical Protocols

> **Type:** Application
