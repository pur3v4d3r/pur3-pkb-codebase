## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Far Transfer Domain 3: Leadership, Influence, and Organizational Communication
> **Structural Principle:** The psychological safety research (Edmondson), the double-loop learning framework (Argyris), and the autonomy-support literature (Self-Determination Theory) converge on a common organizational communication principle: the quality of information that flows through an organization is determined primarily by the communicative behavior of its leaders, specifically whether they create conditions in which honest, complete, and timely communication is rewarded or suppressed.
> **Concrete Application:** Leaders who deploy [[autonomy-support]]ive communication, model vulnerability and uncertainty, respond to bad news with curiosity rather than blame, and practice genuine inquiry rather than pure advocacy create the communicative infrastructure for [[double-loop-learning]] at organizational scale. Leaders who communicate in ways that punish dissent, signal that only certain information is welcome, or focus feedback at the self rather than process level systematically degrade their organizations' communicative capacity — often without knowing they are doing so.
> **Boundary Condition:** Organizational communication is embedded in formal power structures, incentive systems, and cultural histories that individual communication behavior alone cannot overcome; structural conditions (anonymized reporting channels, formal psychological safety assessments, separation of development- and evaluation-oriented feedback contexts) are necessary complements to skilled interpersonal communication.
> **See also:** [[growth-mindset]], [[self-efficacy]], [[communities-of-practice]]
