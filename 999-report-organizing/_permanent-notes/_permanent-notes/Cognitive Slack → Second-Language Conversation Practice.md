## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Cognitive Slack → Second-Language Conversation Practice
> Speaking a second language in natural conversation provides rich implicit input for language acquisition — but only when the speaker has sufficient L2 fluency that the act of speaking does not consume all available working memory. Beginners who must consciously search for words, construct grammar, and monitor pronunciation while also attempting to comprehend their interlocutor have no cognitive slack for the kind of implicit pattern extraction that drives naturalistic acquisition. This explains why language acquisition accelerates dramatically at intermediate levels when production becomes more automatic — not because the input becomes richer, but because cognitive slack becomes available for processing that input. The recommendation to "immerse yourself" in a second language is dramatically more effective above a proficiency threshold.
> *— [[reading-vocabulary-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
