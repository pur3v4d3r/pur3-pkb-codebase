## Additional Material

> [!person] John Sweller (b. 1946)
> *University of New South Wales, Australia*
>
> **Core contribution:** Developed [[cognitive-load-theory|cognitive load theory]] (CLT), the primary theoretical foundation upon which the 4C/ID model rests. Established the empirical basis for [[intrinsic-cognitive-load|intrinsic]], [[extraneous-cognitive-load|extraneous]], and [[germane-cognitive-load|germane]] cognitive load categories, the [[the-worked-example-effect|worked example effect]], the [[split-attention-effect|split-attention effect]], the [[redundancy-effect|redundancy effect]], the [[modality-effect|modality effect]], and the [[expertise-reversal-effect|expertise reversal effect]].
>
> **Relationships:** Sweller's CLT provides the cognitive-architectural constraints that the 4C/ID model translates into design prescriptions. Van Merriënboer's model is, in significant part, an application of Sweller's theory to the specific problem of complex learning.
>
> **Key works:** "Cognitive Load During Problem Solving" (1988); *Cognitive Load Theory* (2011)
> *— [[four-component-instructional-design-model-4cid-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!person] John Sweller (b. 1946)
> University of New South Wales. Developer of [[cognitive-load-theory|cognitive load theory]], which integrates working memory limitations with instructional design. His three-load taxonomy (intrinsic, extraneous, germane) provides the design constraint framework used throughout this report, particularly in Section 7.
>
> **Key work in this report:** Sweller, J. (1988). Cognitive load during problem solving: Effects on learning; Sweller, J., Ayres, P., & Kalyuga, S. (2011). *Cognitive Load Theory*.
> *— [[pkb-pkm-planning-and-cognitive-science-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*
