## Additional Material

> [!person] David Rumelhart (1942–2011)
> **Core Contribution:** Formalized Schema Theory computationally, defining schemas as data structures with variables, default values, and hierarchical subschema relations. With Donald Norman, proposed the three-mode learning model (accretion, tuning, restructuring) that is the theoretical foundation for this report's analysis.
>
> **Relationship to Other Figures:** Rumelhart's schema formalization drew on Bartlett, Piaget, and early AI work on frames (Minsky) and scripts (Schank & Abelson). His parallel distributed processing (PDP) work with McClelland later provided an alternative computational account that partially undermines the discrete schema model.
>
> **Key Works:** "Schemata: The Building Blocks of Cognition" (1980); *Parallel Distributed Processing* (1986, with McClelland).
>
> **Relevance to This Analysis:** Rumelhart's restructuring mode is the primary theoretical resource Schema Theory offers for schema construction — and the analysis here argues it is substantially underdeveloped relative to accretion and tuning.
> *— [[schema-construction-problem-focused-analysis-2026-03-22_report]]*

> [!person] David Rumelhart (1942–2011)
> American cognitive psychologist and AI researcher; provided the first rigorous computational specification of schemas. His connectionist work and schema theory contributions (1975–1984) formalized schemas as data structures with slots, defaults, and constraints. Co-developed the "interactive activation model" of reading and worked on parallel distributed processing. Key works: "Notes on a Schema for Stories" (1975), "Schemata: The Building Blocks of Cognition" (1980). Relationship: formalized Bartlett's insight; influenced Cognitive Load Theory researchers.
>
> **See also:** [[David-Rumelhart]], [[schemas]], [[schema-theory]]

> [!person] David Rumelhart (1942–2011)
> American cognitive psychologist at UC San Diego who provided the first formal, computationally explicit account of schemas in cognitive science (1975, 1980). Rumelhart formalized the slot-default architecture, the goodness-of-fit account of schema activation, and the interactive activation model of processing. With Donald Norman, he developed the accretion-tuning-restructuring account of schema modification. Later contributed to the PDP/connectionist revolution, creating a productive tension with his own earlier symbolic schema theory. See also: [[David-Rumelhart]], [[rumelhart]].
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
