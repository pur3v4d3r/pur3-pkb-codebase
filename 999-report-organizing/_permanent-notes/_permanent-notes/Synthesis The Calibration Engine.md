## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Synthesis: The Calibration Engine
> This is the report's most ambitious analytical move. The claim: the Forethought-Reflection cycle's *emergent output* is not domain knowledge but **an increasingly accurate self-model** — knowing what you know, at what depth, with what confidence. The engine metaphor captures the key structural feature: calibration is produced by the cycle even if the learner never explicitly targets it. You don't try to become calibrated; you build the cycle well, and calibration is the output.
>
> *Why is this non-obvious?* Because most people think of SRL as a tool for learning *content*. The Calibration Engine framing says it's also (maybe primarily) a tool for learning *yourself as a learner*. The self-model that develops over hundreds of well-executed cycles is the deep long-term asset — not any individual session's content acquisition.
>
> *Connection:* This maps to [[metacognitive-calibration]] but extends it — calibration here isn't a static skill to be trained once but a *developmental trajectory* produced by accumulated cycles. → This reframes the value proposition of building the SRL practice: it's not just about each session but about the cumulative self-knowledge that builds across them.
> *— [[reading-session-worked-example-srl-forethought-reflection-2026-03-25_report]]*
