# Self-Regulated Learning (SRL)

> [!definition] Self-Regulated Learning (SRL)
> [[self-regulated-learning|Self-regulated learning]] refers to the degree to which learners are metacognitively, motivationally, and behaviourally active participants in their own learning process. Unlike self-directed learning, which encompasses the broader question of who controls the learning agenda (goals, resources, evaluation criteria), self-regulated learning operates primarily within the bounds of a given learning task, addressing how the learner manages their own cognition, motivation, and behaviour to achieve a goal that may have been externally assigned.
>
> **Boundary:** SRL and SDL are related but not identical. A learner can be highly self-regulated (excellent at managing their attention, strategies, and motivation within assigned tasks) without being self-directed (without taking initiative to define their own learning goals and agendas). The relationship is hierarchical: SRL provides the internal regulatory mechanisms that SDL requires to function, but SDL additionally requires the capacity to identify learning needs, locate resources, and evaluate outcomes in the absence of external guidance.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Curriculum for self-direction must develop SRL competencies as foundational capacities before — or while — gradually extending learner control over the broader SDL dimensions of goal-setting, resource identification, and self-evaluation.
>
> **See also:** [[zimmerman-srl-model]], [[Pintrich]], [[metacognitive-monitoring]], [[metacognitive-regulation]], [[self-reflection-phase]], [[forethought-phase]]

> [!evidence] Self-Regulated Learning (SRL)
> [[self-regulated-learning]] denotes the cluster of cognitive, metacognitive, motivational, and behavioural processes through which learners proactively direct their own learning by setting goals, selecting strategies, monitoring their effectiveness, and adaptively adjusting their approach in response to ongoing feedback. The construct, as developed principally by [[barry-zimmerman|Zimmerman]] (1986, 2000) and [[paul-pintrich|Pintrich]] (2000, 2004), reconceptualises the learner not as a passive recipient of instruction but as an active agent who regulates the conditions, processes, and outcomes of their own learning.
>
> **Boundary:** SRL is not synonymous with autonomous or independent learning; it operates within social contexts and can be scaffolded externally. It is also distinct from (though overlapping with) [[metacognition]], which addresses specifically the monitoring and control of cognition, whereas SRL encompasses additional motivational and behavioural dimensions.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** SRL provides the integrative mechanistic framework through which goals (Section 1), motivation (Section 2), and metacognition (Section 4) are coordinated into a functional self-regulatory system — and the PKB, properly designed, can serve as the externalised infrastructure of that system.
>

> [!evidence] Self-Regulated Learning (SRL)
> Self-regulated learning is the metacognition-driven process by which learners proactively plan, monitor, control, and evaluate their learning activities in pursuit of personally set goals. SRL integrates metacognitive, motivational, and behavioral components: metacognitive monitoring and regulation provide the self-knowledge and adaptive control; goal orientation and self-efficacy provide motivational architecture; active strategy use provides the behavioral expression of SRL. Zimmerman's cyclical model is the most influential structural account of SRL.
>
> **Boundary:** SRL is not equivalent to independent learning (working without a teacher). Students can self-regulate in highly structured classroom environments; students working independently can fail to self-regulate at all. The defining characteristic is goal-directed monitoring and adaptation, not the absence of external structure.
>

> [!evidence] Self-Regulated Learning (SRL)
> [[self-regulated-learning|Self-regulated learning]] refers to the cyclical, self-directive processes by which learners transform their mental abilities into academic and performance skills. In Zimmerman's formulation, SRL is not a trait but a set of proactive processes that learners deploy, monitor, and adjust across three phases. It encompasses motivational, behavioural, and metacognitive dimensions, making it broader than metacognition alone — though metacognition provides the cognitive engine that drives the regulatory cycle.
>
> **Boundary:** SRL is not equivalent to self-discipline or willpower. It is a skill set, not a personality characteristic, and it is teachable through explicit instruction and scaffolding.
>
> **See also:** [[cyclical-model-of-self-regulated-learning]], [[barry-zimmerman]], [[metacognition-and-self-regulated-learning]]

> [!evidence] Self-Regulated Learning (SRL)
> A cyclical process in which learners actively direct their own learning through three recursive phases: forethought (planning and goal-setting before learning), performance (monitoring and strategy deployment during learning), and self-reflection (evaluation and adaptive planning after learning). Principally theorised by [[barry-zimmerman|Zimmerman]] (2000).
>
> **Boundary:** SRL is not synonymous with autonomous learning or self-teaching. A learner can self-regulate while receiving instruction; the "self" in self-regulation refers to the locus of regulatory control, not the absence of external support.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Zimmerman's cyclical model provides the temporal architecture that organises the three categories of PKB scaffolding: forethought scaffolds, performance scaffolds, and reflection scaffolds.
>
> **See also:** [[cyclical-model-of-self-regulated-learning]], [[metacognition-and-self-regulated-learning]], [[forethought-phase]], [[performance-phase]]
