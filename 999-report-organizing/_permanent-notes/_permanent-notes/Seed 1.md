> **Q:** What is the operative causal variable in habit automaticity development, and why is it more important than repetition count?
> **A:** Context stability — the consistency with which the same contextual configuration precedes the behavioral sequence. Repetitions in variable contexts produce weak, conditional associations; repetitions in stable contexts produce strong, reliable context-action encoding in the dorsolateral striatum.
> **Source:** Phase III (Lally et al. evidence), Phase IV (context-action encoding mechanism)
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Distinction
> **Tags:** #habit-formation, #context-stability, #automaticity

> **Q:** What is the core distinction between the expressive and constitutive accounts of inner speech vocabulary function?
> **A:** Expressive: vocabulary reflects pre-formed motivational states (downstream symptom). Constitutive: vocabulary partially *creates* the motivational state it articulates — the words chosen are among the causal mechanisms producing the subsequent motivational outcome, not just its report.

> [!flashcard] Seed 1
> **Q:** What is the Schema Construction Problem, and why is it a problem *for Schema Theory specifically* rather than just a gap in cognitive science generally?
>
> **A:** Schema Theory explains comprehension primarily through schema activation — existing schemas guide processing. But schemas had to be built at some point. Schema Theory's construction mechanism (restructuring) is schema-dependent, creating a bootstrapping problem: building schemas requires schemas. This is a problem for Schema Theory specifically because the theory's explanatory power is grounded in schema activation, leaving construction underspecified within the same theoretical framework.
>
> **Source:** Phase I, II
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Connection
> *— [[schema-construction-problem-focused-analysis-2026-03-22_report]]*

> [!flashcard] Seed 1
> **Q**: What is the "construction problem" in schema theory, and why is it significant?
> **A**: The construction problem is the difficulty that schema theory cannot explain the formation of schemas without presupposing schemas — assimilation and accommodation both require an existing schema, so the first schema is inexplicable within the theory's own resources. It is significant because it reveals that schema theory cannot claim foundational status — it requires supplementary accounts (statistical learning, embodied regularization) for genesis.
> **Source**: Phase III, Philosophical Challenge
> **Difficulty**: Advanced
> **Type**: Concept
> *— [[schema-theory-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-22_report]]*

> [!flashcard] Seed 1
> **Q:** What are the three levels of text representation in van Dijk & Kintsch's model, and which one constitutes genuine comprehension?
>
> **A:** Surface code (exact wording, fades rapidly), propositional text base (explicit semantic content, supports recognition), and situation model (mental representation of the described world, supports inference and application). The situation model is the gold standard — the level at which genuine, transferable comprehension occurs.
>
> **Source:** Phase II, Perspectival Framework

> **Type:** Definition
