> **Q:** What four conditions must be met for conceptual change to occur (Posner et al., 1982)?
> **A:** (1) Dissatisfaction with the current conception; (2) intelligibility of the new conception (it can be understood); (3) plausibility (it seems potentially true); (4) fruitfulness (it opens productive new questions or applications).

> [!flashcard] Seed 6 — Process
> **Q:** What is the generation advantage in EI, and why does it specifically arise in elaborative interrogation rather than from the generic generation effect alone?
>
> **A:** The generation advantage is the finding that self-produced elaborations outperform experimenter-provided elaborations in EI. While the generic generation effect (Slamecka & Graf, 1978) contributes, EI's generation advantage also includes a specific construction premium: generating a causal connection requires actively traversing the conceptual path from text proposition to prior knowledge schema, reinforcing both the path and the endpoint. Provided elaborations allow recognition of a connection; generated elaborations require its construction — and the construction is the learning.
>
> **Source:** Phases III and IV
>
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
>
> **Type:** Process
>
> **Tags:** #generation-effect, #elaborative-interrogation, #construction-premium

> [!flashcard] Seed 6 — Process
> **Q:** What is the delayed JOL effect, and what is the theoretical explanation?
> **A:** Delayed JOLs (made 1-2 minutes after study) are substantially more accurate predictors of subsequent recall than immediate JOLs. The explanation is cue-based: delayed JOLs allow time for an accessibility test (partial retrieval attempt), making retrieval fluency the primary cue — which is more diagnostic than the processing fluency cues available immediately after study.
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #metacognition, #judgment-of-learning, #delayed-jol, #cue-utilization

> [!flashcard] Seed 6 — Process
> **Q:** What is the Knowledge-Activation Gap, and what mechanisms are hypothesized to produce it?
> **A:** The Knowledge-Activation Gap is the systematic failure of stored, declaratively accurate metacognitive knowledge to activate in relevant regulatory contexts during task performance. Three mechanisms are proposed: (1) indexing insufficiency (knowledge indexed to instruction contexts, not task-embedded monitoring signals); (2) proceduralization deficit (insufficient supported practice to proceduralize declarative knowledge); (3) monitoring-knowledge decoupling (weak associative links between monitoring state representations and metacognitive knowledge elements). [Note: speculative synthesis requiring validation]
> **Source:** Phase IV.3
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #knowledge-activation-gap #metacognitive-knowledge #regulation #original-construct

> [!flashcard] Seed 6 — Process
> **Q:** What does Verbal Efficiency Theory claim is the mechanism connecting vocabulary to reading comprehension?
> **A:** Effortful, slow lexical access consumes working memory resources that would otherwise be available for higher-level comprehension processes (inference, integration, monitoring). Fast, automatic lexical access frees those resources. Vocabulary depth → lexical automaticity → more cognitive capacity for comprehension.
> **Source:** Phase III, Theoretical Landscape
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #verbal-efficiency, #perfetti, #working-memory, #cognitive-load, #reading
