## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Hidden Assumption: What Counts as "Knowing a Word" Is Unitary
> **The assumption**: The reading-primary hypothesis treats vocabulary acquisition as essentially the accumulation of more word-meaning pairs, where reading provides encounters that build these pairs. Word knowledge is treated as roughly binary or at most as varying along a single dimension (strength of association).
>
> **Why it's hidden**: Measurement practices reinforce this assumption. Most vocabulary tests (including large-scale assessments) measure something close to receptive vocabulary — recognition of word meanings. When studies report strong correlations between reading and vocabulary, they are typically measuring this unidimensional receptive knowledge.
>
> **What happens if it's wrong**: If word knowledge is multi-dimensional — if knowing a word fully means knowing its forms, meanings, collocations, register, morphological family, and syntactic behaviour — then reading builds only part of word knowledge. Specifically, reading efficiently builds receptive vocabulary (breadth) but inefficiently builds productive depth, collocational precision, register sensitivity, and morphological awareness. A learner who has read widely has large receptive vocabulary but may have a substantially smaller productive vocabulary — a gap that reading alone is structurally poorly positioned to close.
>
> **Assessment**: This assumption clearly fails. Productive vocabulary is systematically smaller than receptive vocabulary for all learners; the gap is larger for words acquired primarily through incidental reading than for words that have been used, practised, and actively processed. Reading builds an impressive but shallow semantic knowledge base; deep, productive, flexible word knowledge requires active processing that reading rarely demands.
> *— [[vocabulary-building-reading-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-28_report]]*
