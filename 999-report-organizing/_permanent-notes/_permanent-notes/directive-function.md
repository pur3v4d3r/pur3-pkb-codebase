# Directive Function

> [!definition] **Directive Function** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> The mechanism by which a committed goal organizes attention preferentially toward goal-relevant activities and away from goal-irrelevant activities, including the suppression of competing tasks, the filtering of competing stimuli, and the privileged occupancy of working memory by goal-related representations.
> 
> **Boundary:** The directive function operates on activities the performer recognizes as relevant to the goal; activities whose relevance is not perceived will not be selected even if they would advance the goal, which is one mechanism by which goals can paradoxically narrow performance on tasks requiring exploration.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The directive function is the cleanest example of the theory's signature pattern — a mechanism that is unambiguously beneficial in narrow tasks but that produces tunnel vision in tasks requiring breadth.
> 
> **See also:** [[selective-attention]], [[attention-and-learning]]

## Key Figures & Intellectual Lineage

> [!person] **Edwin A. Locke (b. 1938)** — Professor Emeritus, University of Maryland** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Co-architect of Goal Setting Theory; conducted the foundational laboratory studies in the 1960s that established the basic specificity-difficulty relationship; primary theoretical synthesizer of the inductive findings into the integrative monograph (1990) and subsequent refinements. **Key works referenced:** *A Theory of Goal Setting and Task Performance* (1990, with Latham); "Building a Practically Useful Theory of Goal Setting and Task Motivation" (2002, with Latham).

> [!person] **Gary P. Latham (b. 1945)** — Professor of Organizational Behavior, University of Toronto (Rotman)** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Co-architect of Goal Setting Theory; primary investigator on the field studies in industrial settings (logging crews, engineers, clerical workers) that established the theory's external validity beyond laboratory conditions. **Relationship:** Decades-long collaboration with Locke producing the field-laboratory complementarity that gives the theory its unusual evidentiary breadth. **Key works:** *New Developments in Goal Setting and Task Performance* (2013, with Locke).

> [!person] **Albert Bandura (1925–2021)** — Stanford University** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Developer of [[Social-Cognitive-Theory-—-Bandura]] and [[self-efficacy-theory]]; not a Goal Setting theorist but provided the conceptual machinery (self-efficacy as a determinant of perceived attainability) that Locke and Latham integrated into their account of commitment formation. **Relationship:** Bandura's framework became the bridge connecting Goal Setting Theory's commitment moderator to a broader cognitive-motivational architecture.

> [!person] **Peter M. Gollwitzer (b. 1950)** — New York University** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Developer of [[implementation-intention]] research; provided the volitional complement to Goal Setting Theory's motivational machinery, with the "when-then" specification framework that closes the intention-action gap. **Relationship:** Independent program of work that has subsequently integrated productively with Goal Setting Theory in behavior-change applications.

> [!person] **Lisa Ordóñez (Eller College of Management)** — University of Arizona** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Lead author of "Goals Gone Wild" (2009) with Maurice Schweitzer, Adam Galinsky, and Max Bazerman; catalysed the contemporary dark-side literature on Goal Setting Theory. **Relationship:** Provides the most consequential structural critique of the classical theory's prescriptive recommendations.

> [!person] **Barry J. Zimmerman (1942–2020)** — CUNY Graduate Center** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Developer of the [[Zimmerman's-Cyclical-Model-of-Self-Regulation|Cyclical Model of Self-Regulation]]; provided the framework within which Goal Setting Theory's High Performance Cycle integrates with the broader [[self-regulated-learning]] literature. **Relationship:** Independent framework whose integration with Goal Setting Theory has substantially refined the theory's applicability to learning contexts.

## Conceptual Tensions

> [!tension] **Performance Gains vs. Performance Pathologies** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*

> **Position B (Ordóñez, Schweitzer, Galinsky, Bazerman, 2009 and after):** The dark-side phenomena are not aberrations but predictable consequences of the same mechanisms that produce performance gains; the theory's failure to integrate the systematic risks into its prescriptive…

> [!tension] **Specific Performance Goals vs. Learning Goals on Complex Tasks** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Position A:** Specific, difficult performance goals produce higher performance across task types (classical Locke & Latham 1990).
> **Position B:** On tasks where strategy must be acquired, learning goals outperform performance goals; the original prescription must be refined accordingly (Locke & Latham 2002 refinement; [[achievement-goal-theory]]).
> **Current state:** Largely resolved in favor of Position B, with the boundary now formally part of the mature theory.
> **This report's stance:** Position B is correct and the resolution represents a genuine sharpening of the theory's predictive…

> [!tension] **Goal Setting as Productivity Tool vs. Goal Setting as Powerful Intervention Requiring Caution** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Position A:** Goal setting is a relatively safe productivity intervention with technical caveats (Locke & Latham, sustained position).
> **Position B:** Goal setting is a powerful intervention whose mechanisms predictably produce pathology under common conditions, requiring substantial training in its conditions of safe use (Ordóñez et al. 2009).
> **Current state:** Active and unresolved.
> **This report's stance:** Position B substantially strengthens the theory by sharpening the conditions of safe application; the translation-window framework offered in Section 5 is a constructive integration.

> [!tension] **Commitment as Quantitative Variable vs. Quality-Differentiated Variable** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Position A:** Commitment varies in degree and is well-treated as a single moderator (classical formulation).
> **Position B:** Commitment varies qualitatively (autonomous vs. controlled) with substantial consequences for sustained performance and well-being ([[self-determination-theory]] integration).
> **Current state:** Empirical evidence accumulating in favor of Position B; theoretical integration into Goal Setting Theory in progress.
> **This report's stance:** Position B; the report's "Goal Setting–SDT Reconciliation" synthesis is offered as a well-motivated integrative proposal.

## Section Summaries

> [!section-summary] **What This Section Established** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Goal Setting Theory is the inductively constructed product of more than five decades of empirical research demonstrating that specific, difficult, accepted goals produce higher performance than vague or easy goals on quantifiable, individually performed tasks. The theory's evidentiary strength is unusual in motivational psychology, but its inductive origins narrow the conditions under which its predictions can be confidently extended, a constraint whose practical consequences will recur as a central thread throughout the report.

> [!section-summary] **What This Section Established** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Goals exert their effects on performance through four distinct but interlocked mechanisms: the directive function organizes attention; the energizing function intensifies effort; the persistence function extends effort across time; the strategic function initiates the search for adequate strategy. The mechanisms are conditional rather than universal; their compounding produces the theory's characteristic performance gains; their failure under unmet conditions produces the theory's characteristic performance losses.

> [!section-summary] **What This Section Established** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Five moderators govern whether and how powerfully goals translate into performance: commitment (the master moderator without which nothing operates), feedback (the informational substrate of self-regulation), task complexity (which strains the strategic mechanism), situational constraints (which can bind the entire relationship), and ability (which sets the ceiling). Each is a condition of adequacy rather than a variable for fine-tuning, and the theory's predictions assume all five are at least minimally satisfied.

> [!section-summary] **What This Section Established** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> The High Performance Cycle integrates the static elements of Goal Setting Theory into a dynamic loop in which goal achievement produces rewards that reinforce commitment to subsequent goals, producing sustained excellence under conditions of adequacy and sustained pathology under conditions of inadequacy. The cycle's morally neutral amplification of input conditions is the structural reason both the theory's proudest achievements and its most concerning failures are best understood through the same framework.

> [!section-summary] **What This Section Established** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> The dark-side phenomena — narrow attention beyond useful boundaries, distorted risk preference, eroded cooperation, and unethical behavior under unattainable difficulty — are not aberrations of Goal Setting Theory but predictable consequences of its mechanisms operating outside their proper conditions. The translation window provides a diagnostic frame for distinguishing applications likely to produce benefits from those likely to produce pathology, and the integration of this frame into the theory's prescriptive recommendations is the most consequential refinement still in progress.

> [!section-summary] **What This Section Established** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Three modern integrations substantially refine Goal Setting Theory: the SDT integration introduces the qualitative dimension of commitment quality; the implementation-intention integration closes the intention-action gap; the self-regulated-learning integration situates the High Performance Cycle within the metacognitive infrastructure required for sustained learning. Each integration responds to a specific limitation of the classical theory, and together they constitute the contemporary frontier of theoretical work in this area.

> [!flashcard] **Card 1** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q:** What two properties of goals jointly produce the strongest effect on performance, according to Goal Setting Theory?

> **A:** **A:** Directive (attention), energizing (effort), persistence (over time), and strategic (planning and strategy discovery).
**Source:** Section 2.

> **A:** **A:** Goal commitment; determined jointly by perceived importance and perceived attainability (where attainability is closely tied to [[self-efficacy]]).
**Source:** Section 3.

> [!flashcard] **Card 4** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q:** When does Goal Setting Theory recommend a learning goal rather than a performance goal?
> **A:** **A:** When the performer does not yet possess the strategy required to achieve the outcome — i.e., when strategy must be acquired rather than deployed.
**Source:** Section 3, Section 5.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate.
**Tags:** distinction, performance-vs-learning.

> [!flashcard] **Card 5** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q:** Describe the High Performance Cycle in one sentence.
> **A:** **A:** Specific, difficult, accepted goals (with adequate moderators) activate the four mechanisms, produce performance, generate commensurate rewards, increase satisfaction with internal attribution, and thereby renew commitment to subsequent goals — completing a self-sustaining loop.
**Source:** Section 4.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate.
**Tags:** process, high-performance-cycle.

> [!flashcard] **Card 6** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q:** What four phenomena constitute the dark-side pattern documented by Ordóñez et al. (2009)?
> **A:** **A:** (1) Narrowed attention beyond useful boundaries, (2) distorted risk preference under threshold rewards, (3) eroded cooperation under individual targets, (4) ethical compromise under unattainable difficulty.
**Source:** Section 5.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate.
**Tags:** definition, dark-side.

> [!flashcard] **Card 7** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q:** What four conditions define the open Translation Window?
> **A:** **A:** Proxy fidelity, honest attainability, measurement adequacy, and failure tolerance — all four must hold for the window to be open.
**Source:** Section 5 (original synthesis).
**Difficulty:** Advanced.
**Tags:** application, translation-window.

> [!flashcard] **Card 8** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q:** How does autonomous commitment differ from controlled commitment in its predicted effects on goal pursuit?
> **A:** **A:** Autonomous commitment produces greater sustained performance under failure, greater preservation of the translation window, and greater long-term well-being than controlled commitment of equal quantitative strength — because autonomous regulation is less dependent on success for renewal and does not produce ego-depletion costs.
**Source:** Section 6.
**Difficulty:** Advanced.
**Tags:** distinction, commitment, SDT-integration.

> [!flashcard] **Card 9** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q:** What gap in classical Goal Setting Theory does the integration with implementation intentions address?
> **A:** **A:** The gap between goal commitment and goal-directed action; implementation intentions ("when X arises, I will perform Y") pre-link action to triggering cues, increasing the rate at which committed goals translate into actual behavior.
**Source:** Section 6.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate.
**Tags:** connection, implementation-intentions.

> [!flashcard] **Card 10** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q:** Why is it accurate to say Goal Setting Theory has become harder to apply well even as its predictive precision has improved?
> **A:** **A:** Because the mature theory's prescriptions are conditional on multiple moderators, qualitative commitment specifications, volitional engineering, metacognitive infrastructure, and an open translation window — making correct application more demanding even though correct applications produce more robust outcomes than the simpler classical formulation.
**Source:** Section 6, Synthesis.
**Difficulty:** Advanced.
**Tags:** connection, theory-maturity.

## Protocols & Methods

> [!protocol] **Goal Design Protocol — Pre-Setting Checklist** *(from [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> Before committing to a specific, difficult goal, work through:
> 1. **Specificity audit** — Is the criterion of achievement unambiguous and discriminable?
> 2. **Difficulty calibration** — Is the goal substantially above default but plausibly achievable given current ability?
> 3. **Strategy adequacy** — Do you have or can you develop the strategy required, or is this actually a learning task in disguise?
> 4. **Commitment quality** — Is your commitment autonomously regulated (identification with values) or controlled (external pressure)?
> 5. **Moderator inventory** — Are feedback, ability, task simplicity (or strategy support), and commitment all in place?
> 6. **Translation window** — Do the four window conditions all hold?
> 7. **Implementation intention** — Have you specified when, where, and how…

## References

- **Locke, E. A., & Latham, G. P. (1990).** *A Theory of Goal Setting and Task Performance.* Englewood Cliffs, NJ: Prentice-Hall.**: The foundational integrative monograph synthesizing the first quarter-century of empirical work into the formal theory; remains the primary reference for the four mediating mechanisms and the original specification of the moderators. **Recommended sections:** Chapters 4-6 (mechanisms), Chapters 7-8 (moderators).
- **Locke, E. A., & Latham, G. P. (2002).** Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation: A 35-year odyssey. *American Psychologist, 57*(9), 705-717.**: The most cited refinement paper; introduces the performance-goal vs. learning-goal distinction and integrates the High Performance Cycle into the theory's prescriptive recommendations. Essential reading for anyone applying the theory.
- **Locke, E. A., & Latham, G. P. (Eds.). (2013).** *New Developments in Goal Setting and Task Performance.* New York: Routledge.**: Comprehensive update covering theoretical refinements, cross-cultural validation, team-level extensions, and applications across domains; the de facto contemporary handbook for the theory.
- **Ordóñez, L. D., Schweitzer, M. E., Galinsky, A. D., & Bazerman, M. H. (2009).** Goals gone wild: The systematic side effects of overprescribing goal setting. *Academy of Management Perspectives, 23*(1), 6-16.**: The catalysing paper of the dark-side literature; documents the systematic pathologies and argues for substantially more conditional prescriptive recommendations. **Companion reading:** Locke & Latham's 2009 response, which contests the prescriptive implications while accepting the descriptive findings.
- **Bandura, A. (1997).** *Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control.* New York: W. H. Freeman.**: Comprehensive treatment of [[self-efficacy-theory]]; essential context for understanding the perceived-attainability component of commitment formation in Goal Setting Theory.
- **Gollwitzer, P. M. (1999).** Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans. *American Psychologist, 54*(7), 493-503.**: The foundational paper introducing implementation intentions; essential for understanding how committed goals translate into action.
- **Gollwitzer, P. M., & Sheeran, P. (2006).** Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes. *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 38*, 69-119.**: Meta-analytic confirmation of implementation-intention effects across 94 studies and several thousand participants; the empirical anchor for the integration with Goal Setting Theory.
- **Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2017).** *Self-Determination Theory: Basic Psychological Needs in Motivation, Development, and Wellness.* New York: Guilford Press.**: The comprehensive contemporary statement of [[self-determination-theory]]; essential context for the autonomous-vs-controlled commitment refinement discussed in Section 6.
- **Zimmerman, B. J. (2000).** Attaining self-regulation: A social cognitive perspective. In M. Boekaerts, P. R. Pintrich, & M. Zeidner (Eds.), *Handbook of Self-Regulation* (pp. 13-39). San Diego: Academic Press.**: The accessible statement of the cyclical model of self-regulation; the foundational reference for the integration with [[self-regulated-learning]] frameworks.
- **Latham, G. P., & Locke, E. A. (2007).** New developments in and directions for goal-setting research. *European Psychologist, 12*(4), 290-300.**: A useful intermediate update between the 2002 *American Psychologist* paper and the 2013 *New Developments* volume; covers cross-cultural and subconscious-goal extensions.
