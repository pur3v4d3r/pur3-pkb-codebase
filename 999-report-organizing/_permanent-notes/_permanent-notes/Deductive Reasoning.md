# Deductive Reasoning

> [!definition] Deductive Reasoning
> Deductive reasoning is a form of inference in which a conclusion is drawn from premises in such a way that, if the premises are true, the conclusion *must* be true. The relationship between premises and conclusion is one of logical necessity — the conclusion is contained within, or guaranteed by, the premises. Deductive reasoning moves from the general to the specific: from universal rules or principles to particular instances or applications. It is fundamentally distinct from reasoning in which the conclusion is merely probable, plausible, or well-supported by evidence. **What it is NOT**: deductive reasoning is not the same as careful, rigorous, or thoughtful reasoning in general. One can reason carefully using inductive or analogical inference, and one can reason deductively in sloppy or invalid ways.

> [!evidence] Deductive Reasoning
> A form of inference in which the conclusion follows *necessarily* from the premises — if the premises are true and the argument is valid, the conclusion cannot be false. Deductive reasoning is *truth-preserving*: it can never produce a false conclusion from true premises. However, it is not *ampliative*: deductive conclusions contain only what was already implicitly present in the premises. Geometry and formal mathematics are paradigmatically deductive domains. Example: All mammals are warm-blooded. Whales are mammals. Therefore, whales are warm-blooded.

> [!evidence] Deductive Reasoning
> Deductive reasoning is a form of inference in which the conclusion follows necessarily from the premises — that is, if the premises are true and the argument is valid, the conclusion *must* be true. Deduction is validity-preserving: it cannot generate more information than is already contained in the premises, but it guarantees the truth of its conclusions given the truth of its inputs. The classical form is the syllogism: "All humans are mortal; Socrates is human; therefore, Socrates is mortal." Deduction is not the same as certainty about facts — it is certainty about the relationship between premises and conclusion.
> *— [[types-of-reasoning-foundational-report-2026-03-05_report]]*
