> [!section-summary] Section 1 Key Takeaways
> 1. Reading comprehension is a multi-level construction process, and genuine understanding requires building a *situation model* — not merely a textbase — of the text's content.
> 2. Inference is the keystone mechanism: readers who generate rich bridging and elaborative inferences comprehend more deeply and transfer more effectively to novel problems.
> 3. The Simple View of Reading correctly identifies the foundational role of decoding and language comprehension, but is insufficient for explaining advanced academic comprehension, which depends critically on domain knowledge, metacognition, and strategic processing.
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-and-metacognition-for-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 1 Key Takeaways
> 1. **Reaction to passivity**: GLT arose as a reaction to both behaviorist and early cognitive models that treated the learner as a passive recipient of information. Its central innovation was placing the learner's constructive cognitive activity at the center of the learning process.
> 2. **Intellectual lineage**: GLT synthesizes and extends Piaget's constructivism, Ausubel's meaningful learning theory, and Craik & Lockhart's levels of processing framework into a pedagogically actionable model.
> 3. **Core principle**: Learning is self-generated meaning construction, not information reception. The quality of learning depends primarily on the nature and quality of the cognitive activity the learner performs, not merely on exposure to content.
> *— [[generative-learning-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 1 Key Takeaways
> - Metacognition denotes a two-layer cognitive architecture: a meta level that monitors and controls an object level that performs cognitive tasks.
> - The field was formally established by [[john-h.-flavell|John Flavell]] and [[ann-l.-brown|Ann Brown]] in the 1970s, drawing on the cognitive revolution's conceptual resources but with deep philosophical roots in the self-knowledge problem.
> - The [[nelson-narens-model|Nelson-Narens model]] provides the most rigorous formal architecture: monitoring flows upward from object to meta level; control flows downward.
> - The central epistemological challenge is that metacognitive monitoring is interpretive inference, not transparent introspection — a fact with direct implications for how metacognitive development proceeds and how it can be trained.
> *— [[metacognitive-development-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 1 Key Takeaways
> 1. Metacognitive reflection is a deliberate, primarily offline, evaluative process of thinking about one's own cognitive processes — distinguished from monitoring (online, automatic), general self-reflection (not cognitively specific), and rumination (repetitive, negatively valenced, non-generative).
> 2. Its defining features are: cognitive specificity, retrospective/prospective temporal orientation, effortful intentionality, and generative function — it produces new metacognitive knowledge and adaptive strategy revision.
> 3. Its importance spans education, clinical psychology, professional practice, and knowledge management — wherever the quality of cognition matters, the capacity to reflect on and improve that cognition is decisive.
> *— [[metacognitive-reflection-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 1 Key Takeaways
> 1. Metacognitive skills determine the efficiency and adaptiveness of cognitive effort; without them, motivated learners tend toward low-efficacy, high-fluency strategies rather than high-efficacy, high-difficulty ones.
> 2. The confidence-competence asymmetry (Dunning-Kruger) demonstrates that metacognitive limitations are self-concealing: those who most need metacognitive correction are least capable of spontaneously performing the self-assessments that would generate it.
> 3. Hattie's synthesis and expertise research both converge on metacognitive instruction as among the highest-leverage educational interventions, with effects that transfer across domains in ways content knowledge alone does not.
> *— [[metacognitive-skills-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*
