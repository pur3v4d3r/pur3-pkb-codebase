> The attributional mechanism does not operate with normative accuracy; it is subject to systematic distortions that arise from both cognitive and motivational sources. The fundamental attribution error (overweighting disposition in others' behaviour) reflects perceptual salience and effortful correction failure. The actor-observer asymmetry (situational for self, dispositional for others) reflects differential information access. The self-serving bias (credit for success, external blame for failure) reflects motivational self-protection. These biases interact to produce systematic attributional conflicts between actors and observers — conflicts with practical consequences in educational, organisational, and clinical settings.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 5 Summary
> The central executive is the attentional-control center of working memory, responsible for focusing, dividing, and switching attention and for interfacing with long-term memory. Drawing on Norman and Shallice's supervisory attentional system, it performs four cardinal functions: focusing, dividing, switching, and long-term memory activation. Individual differences in working memory capacity are best understood as differences in controlled attention — the ability to maintain goal-relevant representations against interference — rather than as passive storage capacity differences. The central executive is neuroanatomically associated with prefrontal cortex and connects theoretically to cognitive load theory (where central executive overload prevents schema formation) and to metacognitive self-regulation (where executive control is deployed to monitor and adjust one's own cognitive processing).
> *— [[baddeley-model-of-working-memory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> Schema theory provides the cognitive mechanism through which constructivist learning operates at the individual level: all new learning is assimilation into existing schemas or accommodation of those schemas in response to perturbation. > Specific curriculum design mechanisms — learning contracts, backward design, inquiry-based learning, evidence-based learning strategies, and deliberate practice — each develop particular dimensions of self-direction and are optimally deployed at different points in the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression. Learning contracts develop self-regulatory capacity through negotiation and are most effective during the Negotiated Autonomy phase. > [!section-summary] Section 5 Summary
> Transfer-Appropriate Processing (TAP) offers a procedural account of encoding-retrieval compatibility: memory is best when the type of processing at test matches the type at study. Both TAP and the ESP reject simple depth-of-processing predictions and affirm encoding-retrieval match as the key variable. They differ in level of analysis: TAP is procedural (type of cognitive operation), the ESP is content-specific (encoded cue-target relationships). Unified accounts treat both as instances of a general encoding-retrieval compatibility principle operating at different levels of description. In applied learning contexts, both frameworks converge on the recommendation that study should simulate retrieval conditions — but the ESP adds the important caveat that specific encoded cues, not just processing procedures, determine what will and will not be effectively retrieved.

> The attainment paradox represents one of GCT's most practically significant and theoretically distinctive contributions. It demonstrates that goal attainment is not a uniform good — its well-being consequences depend on goal content. Extrinsic goal attainment produces transient hedonic spikes without lasting well-being gains because the goals are structurally unable to satisfy basic psychological needs. > The elaboration and distinctiveness constructs emerged as necessary supplements to the original depth hypothesis in explaining variation in memory performance *within* processing levels. > [!section-summary] Section 5 Summary
> Calibration error is the persistent finding that confidence in comprehension correlates only weakly with actual comprehension, with bias toward overconfidence. Two formally separable properties — sensitivity (discriminative accuracy) and calibration (absolute alignment) — can dissociate, and instructional interventions tend to affect one without the other. The mechanism is the misuse of processing fluency (familiarity, visual clarity, topic recognition) as a comprehension proxy, generating high confidence under conditions where comprehension is uncertain. The illusion of knowing and the illusion of explanatory depth are particular manifestations. The calibration problem is structural rather than informational: the monitoring system was optimized for relative comparison, not absolute accuracy, which is why training monitoring sensitivity rarely improves calibration. Effective intervention requires external checkpoints (testing, explanation, application) that supply ground truth the monitoring system cannot generate.
> *— [[metacognition-and-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
