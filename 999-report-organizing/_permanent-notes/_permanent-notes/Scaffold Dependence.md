# Scaffold Dependence

> [!definition] Scaffold Dependence
> The failure mode in which a learner develops reliable performance in a scaffolded environment without developing the internal capacity to perform independently when the scaffold is removed. Scaffold dependence results from scaffolding that is too effective at reducing challenge, from insufficient fading, or from failure to transfer metacognitive strategies to conditions that lack the scaffolding's contextual cues.

> [!evidence] Scaffold Dependence
> The risk that reliance on external cognitive or metacognitive support atrophies internal capacity rather than supplementing it. In the PKB context, the concern that users become unable to perform metacognitive monitoring, regulation, or calibration without their external system.
>
> **Boundary:** Scaffold dependence is a risk, not an inevitability. The writing-to-learn evidence suggests that externalization typically *triggers* rather than *replaces* internal processing. The risk is most acute when external tools automate functions rather than supporting the user's performance of those functions.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Represents the primary limitation of the Externalized Metacognition Thesis — the dark side of cognitive extension.
