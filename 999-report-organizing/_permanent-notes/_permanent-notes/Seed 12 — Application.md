> [!flashcard] Seed 12 — Application
> **Q:** What is the expertise reversal effect, and why does it matter for constructivist instructional design?
> **A:** The expertise reversal effect (Kalyuga) is the finding that instructional supports (e.g., worked examples, guided exploration) that are effective for novices become counterproductive for experts who have the prior knowledge to work without them. It shows that the optimal constructivist instructional design is profoundly prior-knowledge-dependent rather than universal.
> **Source:** Phase V

> [!flashcard] Seed 12 — Application
> **Q:** What distinguishes a "robust" vocabulary learning strategy from a shallow one?
> **A:** Robust strategies engage multiple dimensions of word knowledge (meaning, use, collocational range), apply elaborative processing (connecting to prior knowledge, generating examples), exploit spacing and varied contexts, and target both recognition and production. Shallow strategies (bare definition pairs, reading-once-without-attending) engage only definitional processing, produce context-dependent recognition without deployment flexibility, and fade quickly without review.
> **Source:** Phase V, Applications
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #vocabulary, #learning-strategy, #elaboration, #robust-instruction
