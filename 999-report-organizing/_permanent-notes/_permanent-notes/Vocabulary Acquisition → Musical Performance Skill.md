## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Vocabulary Acquisition → Musical Performance Skill
> The Frequency-Indexed Dual-Channel Model maps precisely onto skill development in musical performance. The *technical threshold* analogy: until a musician has sufficient technical foundation (scales, arpeggios, rhythmic accuracy, basic harmonic knowledge), extended repertoire playing does not efficiently build technique — the musician encounters too many obstacles to sustain the attention required for learning. Above the technical threshold, extensive repertoire performance is the best consolidation and enrichment mechanism — the analogue to extensive reading. The *active noticing* analogy: musicians who listen critically to their own playing, attending to specific technical and expressive elements (the analogue to active word-noticing), acquire far more from practice sessions than musicians who play through pieces passively. The Passive Exposure Fallacy operates identically: hours logged at the instrument without focused attention produce performance experience but not skill acquisition.
> *— [[vocabulary-building-reading-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-28_report]]*
