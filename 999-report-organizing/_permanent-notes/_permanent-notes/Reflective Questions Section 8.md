## Reflections

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions: Section 8
> 1. The profile of the metacognitive lifelong learner describes a reader who has internalized comprehensive reading-regulation capabilities. Honestly evaluating yourself against this profile: what are the two or three dimensions where you are currently strongest, and what are the two or three where you have the most development work to do?
> 2. The Fluency Illusion — mistaking processing ease for comprehension depth — is particularly insidious for lifelong learners revisiting familiar texts. When you re-read foundational texts in your areas of expertise, how do you protect against this? Do you test comprehension depth or mostly trust the familiar feeling of understanding?
> 3. Writing to learn treats every reading experience as the beginning of a knowledge construction project, not its endpoint. What would it look like to design your academic reading practice so that writing (synthesis, explanation, connection-making) is normatively expected after significant reading, not merely an occasional activity?
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-and-metacognition-for-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*
