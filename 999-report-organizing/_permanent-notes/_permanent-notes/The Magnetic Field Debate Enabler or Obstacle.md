## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Magnetic Field Debate: Enabler or Obstacle?
> The role of magnetic fields in regulating star formation remains one of the most contentious issues in the field. Two broad schools of thought have historically competed. The "strong-field" school holds that molecular clouds are supported against collapse primarily by magnetic fields, and that star formation proceeds slowly as ambipolar diffusion gradually weakens this support — implying that star formation is a slow, quasi-static process. The "weak-field" school holds that turbulence, not magnetic fields, is the primary support mechanism, and that star formation proceeds rapidly once turbulence decays locally — implying a more dynamic, stochastic process. Observational measurements of magnetic field strengths in molecular clouds (using the [[Zeeman-Effect|Zeeman effect]] and polarisation of dust emission) have yielded mixed results, with some clouds appearing magnetically supercritical (gravity-dominated) and others subcritical (magnetically supported). The emerging consensus is that both turbulence and magnetic fields play important roles, with their relative importance varying between different environments and evolutionary stages — but this synthesis, while pragmatic, has not yet been cast into a quantitative framework capable of making falsifiable predictions.
