## Open Threads

> [!open-question] Recommended Expansion Areas
> > [!topic-idea] Cognitive Load Theory — Deep Dive
> > **Connection**: CLT is directly derived from the working memory model and represents its most influential applied extension. **Why explore**: Understanding CLT's full suite of effects (split-attention, worked example, expertise reversal, redundancy, modality) and their moderating conditions provides practical tools for instructional design and self-directed learning optimization. **Priority**: High — immediate practical applicability.
>
> > [!topic-idea] Cowan's Embedded-Processes Model
> > **Connection**: The primary theoretical alternative to Baddeley's framework. **Why explore**: A deeper engagement with Cowan's model reveals the genuine theoretical tensions in the field and provides a richer understanding of working memory architecture than either model alone supplies. Understanding the debate between modular and activated-LTM views of working memory sharpens theoretical reasoning about cognition. **Priority**: High — essential for theoretical depth.
>
> > [!topic-idea] Executive Function Development Across the Lifespan
> > **Connection**: The central executive of working memory is closely related to the broader construct of executive function in developmental and clinical psychology. **Why explore**: Understanding how executive functions develop from infancy through old age, and how they are impaired in ADHD, TBI, dementia, and frontal lobe disorders, connects the theoretical working memory model to clinical reality, developmental prediction, and intervention possibilities. **Priority**: High — bridges theory to clinical and developmental application.
>
> > [!topic-idea] Deliberate Practice and Long-Term Working Memory
> > **Connection**: Ericsson and Kintsch's long-term working memory theory proposes that experts effectively expand their working memory capacity through retrieval structures in long-term memory built through deliberate practice. **Why explore**: This framework reframes the relationship between practice, expertise, and cognition — suggesting that the limits of working memory studied in novices are not the limits of expert cognition, and that the primary path to expanded cognitive capability passes through deliberate knowledge acquisition rather than capacity training. **Priority**: High — directly relevant to the learning and expertise acquisition agenda.
>
> > [!topic-idea] Neuroimaging Studies of Working Memory
> > **Connection**: The neural correlates of working memory components are extensively studied via fMRI, EEG, and TMS paradigms. **Why explore**: Understanding the neuroimaging evidence for and against Baddeley's model's component distinctions provides a reality-check on the behavioral model and connects cognitive architecture to neuroscientific findings, including neural plasticity effects of training. **Priority**: Medium — deepens mechanistic understanding.
>
> > [!topic-idea] Working Memory Assessment in Education
> > **Connection**: Gathercole and Alloway's programme of applied research translating working memory theory into educational assessment and intervention. **Why explore**: This represents the most directly impactful application of working memory theory in educational practice and provides tools for identifying children in need of support and for designing learning environments that accommodate working memory variability. **Priority**: Medium-High — high practical relevance to educational contexts.
>
> > [!topic-idea] Self-Regulated Learning and Working Memory
> > **Connection**: Self-regulated learning requires ongoing executive monitoring, strategy selection, and attentional management — all central executive functions. **Why explore**: Articulating the working memory foundations of self-regulated learning produces a more mechanistic and instructionally actionable account of what effective self-regulation actually requires cognitively — and what conditions support or undermine it. **Priority**: High — directly relevant to lifelong learning and PKB-mediated self-development.
>
> > [!topic-idea] Global Workspace Theory and Consciousness
> > **Connection**: Baars' Global Workspace Theory and Baddeley's episodic buffer share structural similarities — both involve a limited-capacity central workspace that makes information globally available and may correspond to conscious awareness. **Why explore**: The intersection of working memory and consciousness theories raises profound questions about the relationship between cognitive architecture, attention, and phenomenal experience that go to the heart of both cognitive psychology and philosophy of mind. **Priority**: Exploratory — philosophically rich but technically demanding.
> *— [[baddeley-model-of-working-memory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
