# Autonomy Orientation

> [!definition] Autonomy Orientation
> The disposition to notice, respond to, and be regulated by the intrinsically interesting, personally valuable, and choice-promoting features of situations. High autonomy orientation is associated with autonomous motivation, psychological well-being, vitality, and relatedness quality. NOT synonymous with preferences for independence or low need for social support — autonomy orientation can include valuing social relationships when those relationships are experienced as freely chosen rather than obligatory.

> [!evidence] Autonomy Orientation
> The autonomy orientation reflects the tendency to attend to and extract the informational, choice-affording, and personally relevant aspects of situations, and to regulate behavior in accord with one's genuine interests, values, and integrated self. Persons high in autonomy orientation characteristically experience their behavior as self-initiated, select contexts that afford personal expression, and respond to external evaluations and feedback as informational rather than as controlling.
> *— [[causality-orientations-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*
