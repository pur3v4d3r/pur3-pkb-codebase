## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technical Detail: The Memory Signature of Schema Intrusion
> Systematic experimental research following Bartlett has identified the precise conditions under which intrusion errors occur:
>
> - **Temporal delay increases intrusion:** Intrusion errors are more frequent at longer retention intervals. Immediately after encoding, fragments are available; after delay, reconstruction from schema becomes necessary.
> - **Schema strength predicts intrusion magnitude:** Stronger, more automated schemas produce more intrusion into schema-inconsistent memories. This is why experts sometimes misremember domain instances more systematically than novices (more powerful schemas, more powerful schema-based reconstruction).
> - **Narrative schemas are particularly intrusion-prone:** Stories have strong schema-structures (beginning-complication-resolution) that template reconstruction strongly. Recall of narrative is especially susceptible to schema intrusion.
> - **The "boundary extension" phenomenon** (Intraub & Richardson 1989): When viewing photographs, people systematically misremember the scene as extending *beyond* the image's actual borders — a visual intrusion effect driven by the scene schema's expectation of a wider context than the photograph captured.
>
> The boundary extension finding is important because it extends schema intrusion from semantic content to *perceptual* memory, suggesting that schema-based reconstruction occurs at early stages of visual memory, not just in later semantic processing.
> *— [[schema-and-how-they-work-deep-dive-2026-04-08_report]]*
