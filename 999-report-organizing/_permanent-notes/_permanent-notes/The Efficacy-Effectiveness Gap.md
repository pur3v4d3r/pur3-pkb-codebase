## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Efficacy-Effectiveness Gap
> A treatment can demonstrate strong efficacy in RCTs — outperforming control conditions in carefully controlled settings — while having modest effectiveness in routine practice. Multiple factors drive this gap: RCTs typically exclude the comorbid presentations that dominate clinical practice; manualized protocols may not accommodate the session-to-session flexibility that effective practice requires; RCT therapists are often more specialized and supervised than routine practitioners; and the Hawthorne-like effect of being in a closely monitored research study may itself contribute to outcomes. Any synthesis of the clinical psychology evidence base should weight effectiveness data (often from naturalistic, benchmarking, or benchmarking studies) alongside efficacy data.
> *— [[clinical-psychology-foundational-report-2026-03-19_report]]*
