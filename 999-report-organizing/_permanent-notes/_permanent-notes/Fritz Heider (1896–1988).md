## Additional Material

> [!person] Fritz Heider (1896–1988)
> **Core Contribution:** Established the fundamental internal/external causal distinction and the "naive psychology" framework — the observation that people spontaneously construct systematic causal explanations for social events. His book *The Psychology of Interpersonal Relations* (1958) initiated the attribution research program.
>
> **Relationship to Other Figures:** Intellectual grandfather of Weiner, Kelley, Jones and Davis. His phenomenological approach to social cognition positioned attribution as a study of common sense reasoning rather than formal logic.
>
> **Key Works:** *The Psychology of Interpersonal Relations* (1958).
>
> **Relevance to This Analysis:** The internal/external distinction that Heider established is the foundation on which Weiner's three-dimensional model builds.
> *— [[attribution-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-24_report]]*

> [!person] Fritz Heider (1896–1988)
> **Role:** Founder of the Attribution Theory tradition. Austrian-born Gestalt psychologist whose work on interpersonal perception, balance theory, and causal attribution established the foundational framework. **Key Works:** *The Psychology of Interpersonal Relations* (1958). **Core Contribution:** The locus-of-causation distinction (personal/environmental causality); the concept of "naive psychology" as a legitimate object of psychological study; the distinction between behavior caused by personal and impersonal forces.

> [!person] Fritz Heider (1896–1988)
> Austrian-American psychologist, University of Kansas. Foundational figure who established the study of causal attribution with *The Psychology of Interpersonal Relations* (1958). Introduced the naive psychologist metaphor and the fundamental internal/external causal distinction. His work seeded both correspondent inference theory (Jones & Davis) and the covariation model (Kelley). All subsequent attribution research descends from Heider's core insight that ordinary people are intuitive causal theorists.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
