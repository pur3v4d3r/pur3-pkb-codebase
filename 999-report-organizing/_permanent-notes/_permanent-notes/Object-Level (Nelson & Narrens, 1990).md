# Object-Level (Nelson & Narrens, 1990)

> [!definition] Object-Level (Nelson & Narrens, 1990)
> The [[Meta-Level-Object-Level-Model|object-level]] is the lower-order component of the Nelson–Narrens architecture where first-order cognitive operations occur — encoding, retrieval, problem-solving, comprehension, and other domain-specific processes. The object-level generates cues that are transmitted upward to the meta-level through the monitoring channel and receives control directives from the meta-level that alter its operations.
>
> **Boundary:** The object-level is not "unintelligent" — it encompasses all of the sophisticated cognitive processing that the individual engages in. The term "lower-order" refers to the informational hierarchy, not to cognitive complexity.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The object-level defines the scope of what can be metacognitively monitored and controlled; the meta-level can only model aspects of the object-level that produce detectable cues.
>
> **See also:** [[Meta-Level-Object-Level-Model]], [[working-memory]], [[long-term-memory]]
