# Relatedness Need

> [!definition] Relatedness Need
> The basic psychological need for genuine connection with others — to feel that one belongs, matters to others, and that others matter to oneself. Satisfied through mutual care and genuine significance in specific relationships, not by social contact quantity or network size.

> [!evidence] Relatedness Need
> The need for relatedness is satisfied when a person experiences genuine mutual care, understanding, and belonging with another — feeling that they matter to the other person, that the other person understands who they actually are, and that the connection involves authentic mutual investment. Crucially, relatedness need satisfaction is *qualitative* rather than *quantitative*: it is not reduced by the number of relationships maintained but by the phenomenological quality of connection within any given relationship. Superficial contact with many people can leave the relatedness need unsatisfied; genuine understanding from a single person can fully satisfy it.
> *— [[relationship-motivation-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-13_report]]*

> [!evidence] Relatedness Need
> The basic psychological need to feel connected to significant others — to care for others and to experience being genuinely cared for. In SDT, relatedness requires *unconditional* positive regard (care that is not contingent on performance or compliance) for full need satisfaction. Conditional regard — approval given or withheld based on behavior — produces controlled rather than autonomous motivation and creates [[contingent-self-worth]]. **See also**: [[relatedness-need]], [[conditional-regard]], [[relationship-motivation-theory]]
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01 (1)]]*
