# Internalization

> [!definition] Internalization
> The broad process of taking in a value, rule, or regulation from the social environment and making it one's own to varying degrees. Internalization spans the entire range from introjection to integration — it is the overarching process, not a single outcome. One can internalize a value incompletely (introjection), partially (identification), or fully (integration).

> [!evidence] Internalization
> The broad process of taking in externally-presented values, norms, or regulations and making them one's own to varying degrees. Internalization spans the full range from introjection (partial, identity-discrepant) through identification to integration (full, identity-synthesized). All positions on OIT's continuum beyond external regulation represent varying degrees of internalization. The *quality* of internalization — not merely whether it has occurred — is OIT's primary analytical concern.
> *— [[organismic-integration-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-13_report]]*

> [!evidence] Internalization
> The process by which a person takes in a value, attitude, or regulatory process that was initially external and transforms it into a personally endorsed and internally regulated motivational resource. Distinguished from mere compliance (behavioral but not motivational), internalization involves genuine psychological incorporation of the value into the self-system. The term derives from [[vygotsky]]'s concept of higher psychological function development through social interaction.
> *— [[organismic-integration-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*

> [!evidence] Internalization
> The process by which an initially externally imposed regulation is taken in by the individual and progressively transformed from a conformity-based behavior into a genuinely valued, self-endorsed one. SDT identifies three degrees of internalization: introjection (taking in the regulation but not yet accepting it as genuinely one's own, accompanied by ego-involvement and shame-based management); identification (recognizing the value and consciously claiming it); and integration (the value becomes fully incorporated into the coherent self-system). **See also**: [[internalization]], [[organismic-integration-theory]], [[ego-involvement]]
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01 (1)]]*
