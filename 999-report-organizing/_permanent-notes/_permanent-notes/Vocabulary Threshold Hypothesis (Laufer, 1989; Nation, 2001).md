# Vocabulary Threshold Hypothesis (Laufer, 1989; Nation, 2001)

> [!definition] Vocabulary Threshold Hypothesis (Laufer, 1989; Nation, 2001)
> The empirically derived claim that fluent, unassisted comprehension of unsimplified text requires knowledge of approximately 95–98% of the running words in a passage. Below this threshold, comprehension breaks down because too many unknowns prevent the construction of a coherent [[situation-models-in-text-comprehension|situation model]]. For typical academic English texts, this corresponds to knowing the most frequent 8,000–9,000 word families.
>
> **Boundary:** The threshold is not a fixed point but depends on text type, topic familiarity, and the reader's ability to tolerate ambiguity. Narrative texts may be navigable at lower coverage ratios than expository texts with dense technical vocabulary.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The threshold hypothesis creates a paradox: you need vocabulary knowledge to read texts that would provide vocabulary-building exposure, but you can only acquire that vocabulary by reading such texts. This bootstrapping problem is central to Phase IV.
>
> **See also:** [[simple-view-of-reading]], [[schema-theory]], [[cognitive-load-theory]]
