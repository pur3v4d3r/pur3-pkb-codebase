# Theory-Ladenness of Observation (Hanson, 1958)

> [!definition] Theory-Ladenness of Observation (Hanson, 1958)
> The philosophical thesis that observations are not theory-neutral sense-data but are already organized and interpreted by the theoretical frameworks of the observer. "Seeing" in a scientific context is always "seeing as" — as a metal, as a bacterium, as evidence for a hypothesis. The theory-ladenness thesis has direct consequences for abduction: what counts as an anomaly, and therefore what triggers abductive inference, is determined by the observer's background theory. This makes the development of richer and more accurate background knowledge a prerequisite for, not merely complementary to, the development of abductive reasoning skill.
