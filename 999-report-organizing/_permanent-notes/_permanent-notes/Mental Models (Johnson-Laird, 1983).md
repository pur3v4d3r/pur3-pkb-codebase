# Mental Models (Johnson-Laird, 1983)

> [!definition] Mental Models (Johnson-Laird, 1983)
> Internal representational structures that capture the states of affairs described by premises in a deductive argument. On Johnson-Laird's Mental Models Theory, deductive reasoning proceeds by (a) constructing one or more mental models of the premises, (b) drawing a putative conclusion that is true in the initial model, and (c) checking whether alternative models of the premises exist in which the conclusion is false. Reasoning difficulty correlates with the number of models required to represent the premises. What it is NOT: mental models are not visual images, though they may have visuospatial properties; they are semantic representations with formal structure.
