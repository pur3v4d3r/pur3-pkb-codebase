> [!tension-identified] The Self-Protection Paradox in PAV Goals
> There is a deep irony embedded in PAV goal dynamics that the literature has not fully theorized. PAV goals are adopted precisely because the learner cares intensely about demonstrating competence — they wish to avoid the inference of incompetence by others. But the motivational and cognitive consequences of PAV adoption (surface processing, defensive help-seeking avoidance, reduced engagement with challenging material) systematically reduce the development of genuine competence over time. The goal adopted to protect the appearance of competence undermines the reality of it. This self-undermining dynamic is structurally similar to what [[carol-dweck|Dweck]] identified in the helpless response pattern but operates through a goal-mediation mechanism rather than an implicit theory mechanism. The convergence of these independent theoretical routes on the same self-undermining structure is analytically significant.
> *— [[achievement-goal-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-24_report]]*
