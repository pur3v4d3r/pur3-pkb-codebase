## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technical Detail: Arousal Classification of Indoor Walking
> Self-paced indoor walking at a comfortable pace falls firmly within the **light-to-moderate aerobic activity** category:
>
> - **Heart rate:** Typically 90-110 bpm (50-60% of age-predicted maximum for a healthy adult) — well below the moderate-intensity threshold of ~65-75% HRmax
> - **Metabolic equivalent (MET):** Approximately 2.5-3.5 METs, compared to 1.0-1.5 METs for sitting. This is in the "light activity" range per WHO classification (moderate begins at ~3.0-6.0 METs)
> - **Rate of perceived exertion (RPE):** Very low (2-3 on a 10-point scale). The walker can easily maintain conversation, read, and walk simultaneously without breathlessness
> - **Catecholamine response:** Light aerobic activity produces a modest increase in norepinephrine and dopamine release — sufficient to enhance [[prefrontal-cortex]] function without the excessive catecholamine levels that impair it (see inverted-U catecholamine model below)
>
> **Arousal classification:** Indoor walking positions the reader in the **ascending limb** of the inverted-U — above resting baseline, approaching but not reaching the optimal arousal peak. This is the zone where the arousal benefit is positive and the risk of over-arousal is negligible.
>
> **Critical comparison:** Jogging or running while reading (if possible) would push up the ascending limb and potentially past the peak; sitting in a slightly warm, post-meal state would push the reader down toward sub-optimal under-arousal. Walking occupies a favorable position.
> *— [[walking-academic-reading-working-memory-deep-dive-2026-04-13_report]]*
