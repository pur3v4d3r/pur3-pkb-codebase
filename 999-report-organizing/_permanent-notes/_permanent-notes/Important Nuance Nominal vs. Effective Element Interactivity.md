## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Important Nuance: Nominal vs. Effective Element Interactivity
> A crucial distinction often lost in application: *nominal* element interactivity (the interactivity that an expert task analysis identifies in the material) differs from *effective* element interactivity (the interactivity actually experienced by a specific learner). Effective interactivity is always ≤ nominal interactivity for learners with relevant prior knowledge, because [[schema|schemas]] compress elements. For learners with *incorrect* prior knowledge (misconceptions), effective interactivity can actually *exceed* nominal — the learner must simultaneously process the presented elements AND their conflicting existing schemas.
>
> **When the distinction matters:** Instructional design for populations with heterogeneous prior knowledge, and studies attempting to measure element interactivity empirically.
> **When it doesn't:** When working with uniformly novice populations encountering genuinely novel material.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*
