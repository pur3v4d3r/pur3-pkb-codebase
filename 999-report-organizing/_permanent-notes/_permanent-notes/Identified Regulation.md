# Identified Regulation

> [!definition] Identified Regulation
> A regulatory style in which the person has consciously endorsed a behavior or value as personally important — they "identify" with it as their own. Identified regulation is autonomous: the person acts from their own valuation rather than from external pressure or internal approval-seeking. It is distinct from integrated regulation in that the value, while endorsed, may not yet be fully synthesized with the person's broader self-conception. It is the first autonomous regulatory style on OIT's continuum.

> [!evidence] Identified Regulation
> A more autonomous form of extrinsic motivation, in which the person has consciously identified with and endorsed the value of an activity or goal, even if it is not intrinsically interesting. [[Identified-Regulation-(Motivation)]] is experienced as self-chosen in the sense that the person genuinely believes the activity matters. Activities regulated by identification are engaged with more fully, retained after external monitoring is removed, and experienced with substantially better affect than externally or introjectedly regulated activities.

> [!evidence] Identified Regulation
> The regulatory style in which the learner consciously values the goal and engages with the activity because they judge it personally important, even if it is not intrinsically interesting. Identified regulation is classified as autonomous because the source of regulation is the learner's own evaluative judgment. A student who studies for a required course because they genuinely see its value for their goals — even if they find the specific material dry — is exhibiting identified regulation.

> [!evidence] Identified Regulation
> A form of extrinsic motivation in which the person has personally identified with and endorsed the value or importance of an activity, even if they do not find the activity inherently interesting. The locus of causality is substantially internal; the person acts because they genuinely value the goal, not because of external pressure or internal shame. Identified regulation is an autonomous form of extrinsic motivation and predicts psychological outcomes (well-being, persistence, learning quality) substantially superior to introjected or external regulation, though typically inferior to intrinsic motivation for affective outcomes.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*
