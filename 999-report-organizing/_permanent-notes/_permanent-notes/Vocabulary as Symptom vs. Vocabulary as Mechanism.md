## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Vocabulary as Symptom vs. Vocabulary as Mechanism
> **The assumption**: In the received view, vocabulary interventions work indirectly — they trigger associated memories of success, prime growth-oriented schemas, or provide information that changes beliefs. The vocabulary is the delivery mechanism, not the active ingredient.
>
> **Why it's hidden**: This interpretation is never explicitly tested in intervention research; researchers typically do not compare "vocabulary practice alone" against "information provision alone" with sufficient rigor to isolate the vocabulary mechanism.
>
> **What happens if it's wrong**: If vocabulary substitution is itself the active ingredient, then the most efficient intervention practice is extended, deliberate vocabulary rehearsal in the specific contexts (during reading, during post-reading reflection) where the target vocabulary is needed — not general attitude change or information provision.
>
> **Assessment**: The evidence **moderately supports** the constitutive account. The intervention literature doesn't definitively rule out the indirect mechanism, but the pattern of evidence (especially the timing of vocabulary change preceding belief change) favors treating vocabulary as the active ingredient.
> *— [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25_report]]*
