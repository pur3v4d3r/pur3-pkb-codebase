## Additional Material

> [!person] Charles Perfetti (b. 1942)
> American cognitive psychologist at the University of Pittsburgh, developer of Verbal Efficiency Theory and the Lexical Quality Hypothesis. Perfetti's work has been central to understanding why vocabulary knowledge predicts reading comprehension — not through a simple definitional-knowledge pathway but through the lexical quality and automaticity mechanisms.
>
> **Key Works (referenced in this report):** Perfetti, C.A. (1985). *Reading Ability*. Oxford University Press. Perfetti, C.A. (2007). "Reading Ability: Lexical Quality to Comprehension." *Scientific Studies of Reading*, 11, 357–383.
>
> **Lineage:** Built on Atkinson-Shiffrin working memory models and early information-processing accounts of reading. Extended and refined by Hart & Risley (vocabulary gaps), Nation & Waring (coverage thresholds), and more recently by connectionist models of lexical access.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Verbal Efficiency Theory is the theoretical foundation for the Cognitive Slack Model — it provides the mechanistic account of why the coverage threshold produces a working-memory tipping point.
> *— [[reading-vocabulary-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*

> [!person] Charles Perfetti (b. 1942)
> Cognitive psychologist at the University of Pittsburgh. Originator of [[verbal-efficiency-theory]] (1985) and a major contributor to the cognitive science of reading comprehension. His work directly links the quality of lexical representations in memory to reading comprehension skill.
>
> **Key Works:** Perfetti, C.A. (1985). *Reading Ability*. Oxford University Press.
>
> **Lineage:** Extended from the information-processing tradition; influenced by Atkinson-Shiffrin and Craik-Lockhart. His Lexical Quality Hypothesis (2007) extended verbal efficiency theory to specify the dimensions of lexical representation quality.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Verbal efficiency theory provides the mechanistic link between lexical representation quality and working memory availability — the engine driving the spiral model's positive trajectory.
> *— [[vocabulary-acquisition-reading-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
