## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Cognitive Slack Model of Incidental Vocabulary Acquisition: An Integrated Framework
> The analysis suggests a unified model that resolves the apparent contradiction between the Matthew Effect's power and the evidence's modesty about acquisition rates. I call this the **Cognitive Slack Model of Incidental Vocabulary Acquisition**. The model has four components:
>
> (1) **Threshold Gate:** Coverage must be above approximately 98% before the acquisition mechanism activates. Below threshold, cognitive resources are consumed entirely by comprehension and decoding, and the inference sequence does not reliably initiate.
>
> (2) **Slack Allocation:** Above threshold, the proportion of available working memory not consumed by comprehension constitutes "cognitive slack." This slack is the resource budget from which vocabulary inference draws. Slack increases with reading fluency, topic familiarity, and morphological competence.
>
> (3) **Depth-Frequency Trade-off:** The learning value of an encounter depends on both frequency and depth, with depth weighted more heavily. The model predicts that a high-slack reader who processes deeply will acquire words faster than a low-slack reader who encounters the same words more frequently but processes shallowly.
>
> (4) **Integration Lag:** Even after successful inference, a word's representation requires consolidation across multiple encounters before it reaches full lexical quality. The consolidation timeline is shortened by post-reading reflection, active morphological analysis, and subsequent encounters in varied contexts.
>
> The model generates testable predictions: (a) Reading-based vocabulary acquisition rates should be higher for readers with larger vocabularies, controlling for exposure; (b) The effect of morphological instruction should be detectable in reading-based vocabulary growth, not just in direct word knowledge; (c) Post-reading reflection should significantly increase the survival rate of provisional form-meaning mappings. All three predictions find support in the existing literature.
> *— [[reading-vocabulary-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
