# Causal Attribution** *(Weiner, 1985)*

> [!definition] Causal Attribution** *(Weiner, 1985)*
> **Definition:** The explanatory accounts that individuals generate for their successes and failures, analyzed along the dimensions of locus (internal/external), stability (stable/unstable), and controllability (controllable/uncontrollable).
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The most consequential distinction for self-regulated learners is between *stable-internal-uncontrollable* attributions ("I failed to understand this because I'm not smart enough for this material") and *unstable-internal-controllable* attributions ("I failed to understand this section because I hadn't adequately reviewed the prerequisite concept beforehand — which I can correct next time"). The first type destroys self-efficacy and produces defensive self-reaction; the second preserves self-efficacy and produces adaptive self-reaction.
>
> **Cross-References:** [[attribution-theory]], [[attribution-retraining]], [[attribution-style]], [[bernard-weiner]], [[carol-dweck]]

> [!evidence] Causal Attribution** *(Weiner, 1985)*
> **Definition:** The explanatory account a learner generates for their performance outcome, analyzed along locus (internal/external), stability (stable/unstable), and controllability (controllable/uncontrollable) dimensions.
> **Operational Indicators:** Attribution can be observed in the language used to describe performance: "I couldn't do it" vs. "I used the wrong strategy" is a locus/controllability distinction. "I never understand this type of material" vs. "I struggled with this specific concept" is a stability distinction.
