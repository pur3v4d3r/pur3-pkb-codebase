## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Approach-Avoidance in Achievement Goals → Regulatory Focus Theory (Higgins, 1997)
> The approach-avoidance asymmetry in achievement goals maps onto [[higgins|Higgins']] regulatory focus theory in a structurally informative way. Promotion-focused individuals — who regulate behavior in relation to ideals, gains, and hoped-for positive end states — show cognitive and behavioral signatures characteristic of approach-mode motivation: broader attentional scope, increased creativity, higher risk tolerance, faster but less accurate performance. Prevention-focused individuals — who regulate in relation to oughts, safety, and feared negative end states — show signatures characteristic of avoidance-mode motivation: narrower attentional scope, lower error rates, reduced creativity, more careful but slower processing. The analogy holds because both frameworks are identifying the same underlying regulatory asymmetry: the approach system is activated by positive-valence representations and supports exploratory behavior; the avoidance system is activated by threat representations and supports protective behavior. Achievement goal researchers have drawn on this convergence to provide mechanistic grounding for the PAV-maladaptive association: PAV goals activate prevention-focus regulatory processes that, while adaptive in genuinely threatening environments, are systematically costly in learning environments requiring exploration, error tolerance, and cognitive flexibility.
