# Pintrich's 4×4 Matrix

> [!definition] **Pintrich's 4×4 Matrix**
> **Definition:** A theoretical architecture for self-regulated learning consisting of four regulatory phases (Forethought/Planning/Activation; Monitoring; Control; Reaction/Reflection) crossed with four regulated areas (Cognition; Motivation/Affect; Behavior; Context), generating 16 cells each representing a distinct regulatory activity. The matrix is analytic — it decomposes simultaneous regulatory processes for examination — not sequential.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The matrix structure is both Pintrich's principal contribution and the source of the tractability problem identified in Phase VI. It generates comprehensive theoretical coverage at the cost of empirical fragmentation.
> 
> **Operational Indicators:** A learner demonstrating full 4×4 regulation would: set cognitive and motivational goals before task engagement; monitor comprehension, self-efficacy, effort, and environment during engagement; apply cognitive, motivational, behavioral, and contextual regulation strategies in response to monitoring signals; and reflect on performance, attributions, and regulatory effectiveness afterward.
> 
> **Etymology/Intellectual Lineage:** Developed through Pintrich's 1980s–2000s theoretical work at the University of Michigan, integrating Bandura's social cognitive theory, Corno's volition theory, and Flavell's metacognition framework.
> 
> **Cross-References:** [[self-regulated-learning]], [[zimmerman-srl-model]], [[metacognitive-regulation]], [[achievement-goal-theory]]
