> [!methodology-and-sources] Transfer Research Grounding
> The following section applies insights from Basic Psychological Needs Theory to domains beyond its primary empirical base, drawing on the concept of [[transfer-of-learning]] and specifically on *far transfer* — the application of structural principles from one domain to significantly different contexts (Halpern, 2010; Perkins & Salomon, 1992; Barnett & Ceci, 2002). Far transfer requires identifying the *deep structural principles* of BPNT rather than its surface features, and asking: what does this theory's logic imply outside its original domain of empirical application? The following examples are analytical and speculative, not empirically validated extrapolations, and should be read accordingly.
> *— [[basic-psychological-needs-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*

> [!methodology-and-sources] Transfer Research Grounding
> The application of domain-specific insights to new contexts depends on what learning researchers call [[transfer-of-learning|transfer of learning]] — the extent to which knowledge and skills acquired in one context are deployed in another. *Near transfer* involves applying learned material to highly similar situations; *far transfer* involves applying abstract principles and structural patterns to superficially dissimilar domains. The constructivist principle that understanding requires active construction of meaning — not passive reception — is structural at a deep level and should transfer wherever human knowledge-building occurs. The four domains below represent substantive far-transfer applications of constructivist principles, extending them from formal education settings to professional practice, personal knowledge management, organizations, and psychotherapy.
> *— [[constructivist-learning-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!methodology-and-sources] Transfer Research Grounding
> The following far transfer analysis applies the transfer framework of Halpern (2010) and Barnett & Ceci (2002), who distinguish near transfer (same surface features, different problem instances), far transfer (same structural principles, different domain or context), and very far transfer (abstract principles applied across domains with minimal surface similarity). GLT's generative processing principles — the SOI mandate for active meaning-making through selection, organization, and integration — are abstract enough to constitute candidates for very far transfer: they describe cognitive operations that are domain-independent in principle, even if their specific instantiations vary by domain.
> *— [[generative-learning-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!methodology-and-sources] Transfer Research Grounding
> The concept of *transfer of learning* — the application of knowledge or skills learned in one context to a different context — is central to why foundational principles are worth learning at depth. Near transfer involves applying learning to closely similar contexts; far transfer involves applying structural principles to significantly different domains. Research by Halpern, Barnett & Ceci, and Perkins and Salomon identifies conditions that maximize far transfer: deep rather than surface processing, explicit identification of underlying structural principles, and deliberate practice of identifying those principles in diverse contexts. The principles developed across the eight sections of this report have high far-transfer potential because they describe structural features of human cognition and communication that operate across contexts.
> *— [[how-to-communicate-effectively-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
