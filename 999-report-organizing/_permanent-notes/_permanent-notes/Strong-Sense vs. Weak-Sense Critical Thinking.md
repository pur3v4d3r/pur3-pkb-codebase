## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Strong-Sense vs. Weak-Sense Critical Thinking
> Richard Paul introduced a distinction that is rarely appreciated outside specialist literature but has profound educational implications: *weak-sense* and *strong-sense* critical thinking. Weak-sense CT is the use of critical thinking skills in service of one's own pre-existing interests, beliefs, and tribal commitments — deploying analytical tools selectively to win arguments rather than to find truth. Strong-sense CT requires the application of the same standards to one's own beliefs and assumptions as to those of one's opponents, including the willingness to discover that one is wrong. Most CT instruction, Paul argues, inadvertently cultivates the weak-sense variety — producing students who are skilled arguers for their own positions but no more willing to question their own assumptions than before.
> *— [[critical-thinking-educational-settings-foundational-report-2026-03-05_report]]*
