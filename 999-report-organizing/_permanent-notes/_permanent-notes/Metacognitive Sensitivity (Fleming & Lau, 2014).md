# Metacognitive Sensitivity (Fleming & Lau, 2014)

> [!definition] Metacognitive Sensitivity (Fleming & Lau, 2014)
> [[metacognitive-accuracy|Metacognitive sensitivity]] refers to the ability of the metacognitive system to discriminate between correct and incorrect cognitive outputs. It is typically measured using signal detection theory metrics such as meta-d' (meta-d-prime), which quantifies the extent to which metacognitive confidence tracks first-order accuracy after controlling for task performance. A high meta-d' indicates that the meta-level is successfully distinguishing between correct and incorrect responses; a low meta-d' indicates that the meta-level's monitoring signals are poorly calibrated to the actual quality of object-level processing.
>
> **Boundary:** Metacognitive sensitivity is a measure of monitoring resolution, not calibration. A system can be highly sensitive (accurately ranking its outputs by confidence) while being systematically miscalibrated (assigning inappropriate absolute confidence values).
>
> **See also:** [[Stephen-Fleming]], [[prefrontal-cortex]], [[metacognitive-accuracy]]
