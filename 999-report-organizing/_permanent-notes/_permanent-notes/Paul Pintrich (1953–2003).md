> [!person] Paul Pintrich (1953–2003)
> Developer of the 4×4 SRL framework crossing phases (forethought, monitoring, control, reflection) with areas (cognition, motivation, behaviour, context). His insight that self-regulation encompasses motivational and contextual regulation — not just cognitive and behavioural — expanded the design space for learning-support systems. Key works: "The Role of Goal Orientation in Self-Regulated Learning" (2000). Relationship: extended Zimmerman's model with the motivational regulation dimension critical for PKM.
> *— [[goal-setting-and-assessment-language-in-pkm-foundational-report-2026-04-15_report]]*

> [!person] Paul Pintrich (1953–2003)
> **Primary Contribution**: Developed [[Pintrich's-Integrative-SRL-Framework|Pintrich's integrated SRL framework]], extending Zimmerman's model to four regulatory areas (cognition, motivation/affect, behavior, context); conducted influential research on achievement goals and SRL in higher education.
> **Key Works**: "A Conceptual Framework for Assessing Motivation and Self-Regulated Learning in College Students" (2004); *Motivation in Education* (with Schunk); contributed the Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire (MSLQ).
> **Legacy**: Most widely used SRL research measurement instrument (MSLQ); theoretical framework integrating motivation and metacognition.

> [!person] Paul Pintrich (1953–2003)
> University of Michigan. Developed the integrative SRL framework crossing four phases (forethought, monitoring, control, reflection) with four areas (cognition, motivation/affect, behaviour, context). Uniquely emphasised the motivational and contextual dimensions of self-regulation that purely metacognitive models neglect.
>
> **Relationship to other figures:** Complemented Zimmerman's cyclical model by adding explicit motivational and contextual dimensions. Extended Brown's regulation concept beyond purely cognitive control.
>
> **Key work:** Pintrich, P. R. (2000). The role of goal orientation in self-regulated learning. In M. Boekaerts, P. R. Pintrich, & M. Zeidner (Eds.), *Handbook of Self-Regulation*.
>
> **See also:** [[Pintrich]], [[paul-pintrich]], [[self-regulated-learning]]
> *— [[metacognition-and-pkm-foundational-report-2026-04-12_report]]*

> [!person] Paul Pintrich (1953–2003)
> University of Michigan educational psychologist. Developed the 4×4 integrative SRL framework that positioned metacognitive regulation as one of four regulatory areas operating simultaneously during academic learning. Created the MSLQ (Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire), which includes metacognitive self-regulation as a key subscale.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by Flavell, Zimmerman, and the motivational psychology tradition. Influenced the integration of motivational and metacognitive dimensions of SRL.

> [!person] Paul Pintrich (1953–2003)
> Educational psychologist, University of Michigan. Pintrich's integrative SRL framework (2000, 2004) situated metacognitive regulation within a four-area, four-phase matrix that explicitly integrated motivational and contextual dimensions, demonstrating that regulation cannot be adequately understood in isolation from motivational context.
>
> **Lineage:** Synthesizes Zimmerman, Bandura's social cognitive theory, and motivation science with the metacognition tradition.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the motivational integration framework that grounds Phase V's implications and the Calibration-Action Gap's motivational interruption mechanism.
> *— [[metacognitive-regulation-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*
