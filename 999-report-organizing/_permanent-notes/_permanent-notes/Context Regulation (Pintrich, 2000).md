# Context Regulation (Pintrich, 2000)

> [!definition] Context Regulation (Pintrich, 2000)
> **Definition:** Regulatory activities directed at managing features of the task and learning environment, including structuring the physical environment for study, managing social resources strategically, selecting and modifying tasks, and seeking or avoiding specific types of help. Context is treated as a first-class regulatory domain rather than a fixed background condition.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The most theoretically underspecified and empirically least-supported area in Pintrich's framework, but potentially the most consequential for learners whose learning failures are primarily environmentally driven rather than strategically or motivationally driven.
>
> **Operational Indicators:** A learner regulating context might: move to a quieter study space when monitoring signals distraction; seek specific partial help from a teacher rather than complete answers; create a study schedule that front-loads difficult material when attention is highest.
>
> **Cross-References:** [[situated-learning]], [[situated-cognition]], [[self-directed-learning]], [[feedback-design]]
