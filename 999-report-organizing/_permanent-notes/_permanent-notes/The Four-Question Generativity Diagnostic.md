## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Four-Question Generativity Diagnostic
> Before deploying any writing technique, ask:
>
> 1. **Source Status:** Is the source closed, available, or in front of the writer? (Closed = high generative demand; present = low generative demand)
>
> 2. **Organisation Authority:** Is the organisational structure provided by the source or generated by the writer? (Writer-generated = high; source-provided = low)
>
> 3. **Connection Mandate:** Does the task require connecting this material to existing knowledge, or only recording what the source contains? (Connection required = high generative demand; recording only = low)
>
> 4. **Predictive or Explanatory Commitment:** Does the task require the writer to commit to a specific, testable claim before checking against the source? (Commitment required = high; no commitment = low)
>
> A technique scoring high on three or four of these dimensions will produce substantially better learning than a technique scoring low on them. The framework is not a prescriptive ranking — it is a diagnostic tool that reveals how much of a writing task's generative potential is being realised.
> *— [[writing-techniques-effective-learning-focused-analysis-2026-03-21_report]]*
