# Important Nuance: The Baseline Matters More Than the Activity

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Important Nuance: The Baseline Matters More Than the Activity
> The arousal benefit of walking is not absolute — it is **relative to the baseline arousal state**. This means:
>
> - A reader who is already at optimal arousal (well-rested, caffeine-primed, mid-morning, actively engaged) gains **little or no arousal benefit** from adding walking. For this reader, walking is a pure dual-task cost — small, but net negative.
> - A reader who is below optimal arousal (afternoon slump, post-meal sedation, bored, sleep-deprived, or struggling with the natural under-stimulation of prolonged sitting) gains a **substantial arousal benefit** from walking. For this reader, walking may produce a net positive effect on reading comprehension.
> - A reader who is above optimal arousal (anxious, stressed, over-caffeinated, agitated) would gain **no arousal benefit** and might even be pushed further from optimal. Walking would add to over-arousal.
>
> **When the distinction matters:** This nuance explains why the same walking reader might find walking-while-reading helpful on some days (when baseline arousal is low) and unnecessary or even slightly distracting on others (when baseline arousal is already optimal). The practice's value is not fixed — it is state-dependent.
> **When it doesn't:** For a reader who consistently reads during low-arousal states (late evening, post-meal), walking consistently provides an arousal benefit.
> *— [[walking-academic-reading-working-memory-deep-dive-2026-04-13_report]]*
