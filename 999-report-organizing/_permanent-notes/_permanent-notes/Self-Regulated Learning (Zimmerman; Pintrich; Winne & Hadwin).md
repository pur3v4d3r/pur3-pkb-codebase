# Self-Regulated Learning (Zimmerman; Pintrich; Winne & Hadwin)

> [!definition] Self-Regulated Learning (Zimmerman; Pintrich; Winne & Hadwin)
> The cyclical process through which learners set goals, select strategies, monitor their progress, and adaptively revise their approach. Typically modelled as three phases — forethought (planning and self-motivation), performance (strategy execution and self-observation), and self-reflection (evaluation and adaptive inference) — operating across cognitive, motivational, behavioural, and contextual domains.
>
> **Boundary:** SRL is a process, not a trait. All learners self-regulate to some degree; expertise lies in the quality, consistency, and metacognitive awareness of regulatory activity, not its mere presence.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** SRL provides the process architecture that connects goal-setting (forethought) to goal-assessment (self-reflection), and the PKM system can scaffold all three phases.
>
> **See also:** [[self-regulated-learning]], [[zimmerman-srl-model]], [[Pintrich]], [[forethought-phase]], [[self-reflection-phase]]
