## Examples

> [!example] The Bransford and Johnson Comprehension Paradigm
> Bransford and Johnson (1972) demonstrated the schema requirement for comprehension in their famous "comprehension without context" studies. Participants who were given the topic (the schema label) before reading a deliberately opaque passage showed dramatically better comprehension and recall than those who received the passage alone or received the topic label only after reading. The critical finding: providing the schema label BEFORE reading enabled comprehension; providing it after could not retroactively organize the already-processed (and poorly encoded) text. This experiment demonstrates that comprehension requires schema activation at the point of processing — schema-mediated comprehension cannot be reconstructed post-hoc from a schema-free initial encoding.
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
