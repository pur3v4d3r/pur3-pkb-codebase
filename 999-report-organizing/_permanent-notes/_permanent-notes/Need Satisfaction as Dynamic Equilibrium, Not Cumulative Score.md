## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Need Satisfaction as Dynamic Equilibrium, Not Cumulative Score
> A second synthetic insight: it is tempting to treat need satisfaction as a kind of psychological bank account — make enough deposits (autonomy-supportive moments, competence-building experiences, warm relational interactions) and the account balance grows, supporting motivation long-term. The evidence suggests a different model. Need satisfaction is more like a dynamic equilibrium than a cumulative score: it must be continuously maintained, because the effects of thwarting are rapid and can quickly override a history of satisfaction. A classroom that has been reliably autonomy-supportive for months can generate acute thwarting damage within a single high-stakes, controlling assessment event. The practical implication is that need support is not something to achieve and sustain in a background sense — it is an ongoing dynamic that requires attention in each instructional interaction, especially during the high-stakes moments that are most tempting to deliver in controlling rather than autonomy-supportive ways.
> *— [[sdt-focused-analysis-2026-03-06_report]]*
