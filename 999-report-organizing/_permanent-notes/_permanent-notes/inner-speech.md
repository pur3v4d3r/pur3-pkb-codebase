# Inner Speech

> [!definition] **Inner Speech** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Definition:** Inner speech is the internalized, abbreviated, and predicate-dominant form of external speech that serves the self-regulatory function of mediating between thought and action. Distinguished from verbal thinking (internal monologue with full sentence structure) and from subvocalization (motor-level phonological encoding), inner speech in [[vygotsky]]'s account is functionally social — it is internalized dialogue — and carries the compressed semantic architecture of the social speech from which it was derived. **Boundary condition**: inner speech is not the same as *all* mental verbal activity; it specifically refers to self-directed, self-regulatory speech rather than merely language-accompanied thought.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The Vygotskian account predicts that inner speech carries social and evaluative structures — including the kinds of attributional and goal-framing structures that shape motivation — because it was formed through the internalization of social evaluations. This is the theoretical foundation for treating vocabulary choice as non-trivial.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> Across dozens of studies, Weiner and colleagues established that the stability dimension of attribution is most strongly predictive of expectancy change (whether the learner expects to do better or worse next time), while the locus dimension most strongly predicts emotional outcomes (pride/shame for internal attributions, gratitude/anger for external). The vocabulary that encodes each dimension differs systematically: ability vocabulary (stable-internal-uncontrollable) reliably produces the most damaging motivational sequence — shame without expectancy change without effort mobilization.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> The convergent evidence across attribution research suggests that ability-referencing vocabulary ("I'm not good at this," "I don't have the mind for this kind of material," "I'm just not a strong reader") is not merely inaccurate — it activates the most motivationally destructive attributional configuration simultaneously: internal, stable, and uncontrollable. Of all vocabulary choices available to a struggling reader, ability vocabulary is uniquely catastrophic because it forecloses all three motivational pathways: it assigns shame (internal), eliminates expectancy change (stable), and removes the basis for effort mobilization (uncontrollable). No other single vocabulary pattern does all three simultaneously.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> Research on source credibility in persuasion establishes that perceived expertise and objectivity are the primary determinants of persuasive impact. Inner speech carries a credibility premium in the domain of self-assessment because we experience it as unmediated access to our own cognitive state, not as an attempt to influence us. This means that negative inner speech — "I'm struggling because I'm not intelligent enough for this material" — may have *higher* persuasive impact on self-efficacy than the same message delivered externally, because it is perceived as an objective self-report rather than a motivated evaluation. The implication is significant: correcting inner speech vocabulary matters more, not less, than correcting externally delivered feedback.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> The specific lexical choices within motivational inner speech — not just the general emotional valence — are functionally significant. "I can't do this" and "I don't have the right strategy yet" may produce similar surface affect but generate categorically different attributional inferences, self-efficacy predictions, and goal constructions. Vocabulary is not packaging for pre-formed meaning; it is partially constitutive of the motivational state itself.

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> A genuine tension exists in the SDT evidence base here. SDT's internalization research suggests that the movement from controlled to autonomous regulation cannot be faked — it requires actual value alignment, not vocabulary substitution. Yet the regulatory language intervention research suggests that vocabulary shifts precede and partly cause the underlying motivational state change. The resolution may lie in [[internalization]] theory itself: the mechanism of internalization involves initially adopting the language of a regulatory style before that style becomes genuinely one's own — exactly as children adopt the language of social norms before those norms are fully internalized. This suggests a bootstrapping possibility: deliberate vocabulary practice with autonomous regulatory language can initiate the internalization process rather than merely describing it. But the tension remains active: purely performative vocabulary without engaged reflection may produce a kind of motivated reasoning about one's own motivation rather than genuine regulatory shift.

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> The most consequential property of the Attributional Vocabulary Loop is not that it produces bad outcomes in a single cycle — it is that it becomes increasingly self-sealing over repeated cycles. Each turn of the loop narrows the vocabulary available to the reader by providing "evidence" for the attributed cause: the reader who uses ability vocabulary and subsequently reads less effectively experiences this as confirmation that they indeed lack ability. Over time, the vocabulary repertoire for self-explanation contracts toward the attributed cause, because alternatives are systematically disconfirmed by experience. This means early vocabulary intervention is dramatically more efficient than late intervention — the loop compounds in the destructive direction just as surely as a growth mindset compounds in the constructive direction.

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> One of the less obvious implications of the self-efficacy calibration mechanism is that positive ability-referencing inner speech ("I'm naturally good at this") is motivationally problematic in a different way than negative ability-referencing speech, but in the same structural family. Both encode efficacy as stable and uncontrollable. Positive ability vocabulary produces two specific failure modes: it reduces the probability of strategic effort allocation (why invest in strategy if you're naturally gifted?), and it creates fragility — when difficulty eventually arrives, as it always does in academic learning, there is no strategy-and-effort vocabulary available to interpret the experience except as evidence that the ability attribution was wrong. Dweck's research on [[growth-mindset]] demonstrates exactly this dynamic: children praised for ability (positive ability vocabulary) subsequently chose easier tasks, showed more fragility under challenge, and performed worse than children praised for effort (strategy-process vocabulary). The vocabulary of praise — delivered externally in Dweck's studies but self-delivered in real-world reading — determines whether efficacy is flexible or brittle.

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> Vocabulary deficits at the superordinate level of goal construction cascade downward through the hierarchy. A reader who can only articulate superordinate goals in controlled regulatory vocabulary ("I should understand this") lacks the motivational architecture to construct specific and challenging intermediate goals, because the energy driving goal construction is controlled rather than autonomous. Autonomous regulatory vocabulary at the superordinate level — "I'm genuinely trying to understand how this argument connects to what I already know about this domain" — generates a motivational pressure toward more specific and more challenging intermediate goals. The cascade implication is that vocabulary development at the superordinate level (the regulatory register) has multiplicative effects on goal quality throughout the hierarchy.

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> There is a specific and underappreciated way that the reports in this knowledge base function as Motivational Lexicon development tools. When you read a report on [[attribution-theory]] that gives you precise vocabulary for the three dimensions — locus, stability, controllability — and the vocabulary to distinguish "effort" attributions from "strategy" attributions from "ability" attributions, you are not only acquiring declarative knowledge of attribution theory. You are acquiring new vocabulary items for your Motivational Lexicon. The next time you finish a difficult reading session, "I didn't have the right strategy for that kind of argument" is now available where previously only "I didn't understand it" was. The reports are building the very inner speech vocabulary that enables better engagement with subsequent reports. This creates a genuine positive feedback loop between PKB development and SRL capability — one that compounds with each new conceptual node added to the knowledge base.

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> - **Locus** → determines *pride/shame* following success/failure. Internal attribution for success generates pride; internal attribution for failure generates shame.
> - **Stability** → determines *expectancy* for future outcomes. Stable attribution for failure predicts continued failure; unstable attribution for failure leaves future success possible.
> - **Controllability** → determines *guilt/anger* and the *judgments others make*. Controllable attribution for failure generates guilt (self-directed) and sympathy from others; uncontrollable attribution generates shame (self) and pity from others.

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> - **External regulation inner speech**: "I have to read this. I'll be behind if I don't. I should just get it done." — The regulation is experienced as wholly external; the words express compliance, obligation, and contingency on external outcomes.
> - **Introjected regulation inner speech**: "I should understand this; I'll feel stupid if I don't. I can't let myself fall behind. I need to prove I can handle this." — The regulation has been partially internalized but as ego-involvement and shame-avoidance; the characteristic words are *should, must, ought to, have to*, and self-critical appraisals.
> - **Identified regulation inner speech**: "I want to understand this because it connects to my SRL project. This matters for what I'm trying to build." — Chosen, instrumentally valued; the characteristic words are *want to, choosing to, because it matters for [goal].*
> - **Integrated regulation inner speech**: "Understanding how attribution works is part of who I am as a deliberate learner. This is exactly the kind of thinking I do." — The activity is experienced as an expression of core identity; the characteristic words invoke identity, values, and intrinsic coherence.
> - **Intrinsic motivation inner speech**: "This is genuinely interesting. I want to follow this argument wherever it goes." — Pure interest and enjoyment; minimal regulatory language because the activity generates its own momentum.

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> When difficulty is encountered during reading:
> - **Mastery-approach inner speech**: "I don't understand this yet. What is it about this argument that's resisting my current schema? What do I need to learn to follow this?" — Difficulty is information about what remains to be learned.
> - **Performance-approach inner speech**: "I should be able to get this. Am I understanding this as well as I should? Would someone reading this alongside me understand it faster?" — Difficulty is a potential competence signal; triggers normative comparison.
> - **Performance-avoidance inner speech**: "If I don't understand this, it means I'm not smart enough for this kind of work. I shouldn't be engaging with this if I can't handle it." — Difficulty is evidence of inadequacy; triggers withdrawal or surface processing.
> - **Mastery-avoidance inner speech**: "I understood this kind of argument better before. I'm losing my grip on this. I must not let my comprehension slip." — Difficulty signals regression; triggers anxiety-based effort.

> [!analytical-insight] Key Insight *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> When a reader encounters comprehension difficulty (the trigger event):
> 1. **Automatic attribution search**: Inner speech rapidly generates a causal explanation. The vocabulary available determines the attribution made: "I can't follow this" (ability, internal, stable, uncontrollable) vs. "I'm not using the right approach here" (strategy, internal, unstable, controllable).
> 2. **Dimensional processing**: The attribution is implicitly assigned to Weiner's dimensions. Stable attributions generate low expectancy for future comprehension; controllable attributions preserve agency.
> 3. **Efficacy update**: The attribution modifies the current self-efficacy estimate for the reading task. Stable/uncontrollable attributions reduce efficacy; unstable/controllable attributions maintain it.
> 4. **Motivational consequence**: Reduced efficacy generates decreased effort allocation, cognitive withdrawal, shallower processing, and reduced persistence. Maintained efficacy generates continued engagement, strategic adaptation, and deeper processing.
> 5. **Comprehension outcome**: The motivated/demotivated reading generates a comprehension outcome that becomes the input for the next attribution search.
> 6. **Cycle**: The loop re-initiates.




































## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application**
> *Describe how this concept applies in practice.*

> [!example] **Application** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> The following vocabulary table provides direct attributional substitutes along the stability and controllability dimensions — the two dimensions most consequential for motivational outcomes (expectancy and effort mobilization, respectively).
> 
> | Destructive Pattern | Dimension Encoded | Constructive Substitute | Dimension Encoded |
> |--------------------|--------------------|------------------------|-------------------|
> | "I can't do this" | Internal, stable, uncontrollable | "I don't have the right approach yet" | Internal, unstable, controllable |
> | "I'm just not good at this kind of reading" | Internal, stable, uncontrollable | "My strategy for this text type needs development" | Internal, unstable, controllable |
> | "This is too hard for me" | Internal, stable, uncontrollable | "This is at the edge of my current comprehension — exactly where growth happens" | Internal, unstable, controllable |
> | "I don't have the background for this" | External/Internal, stable, uncontrollable | "I need to develop specific background — here's what's missing" | Internal, unstable, controllable |
> | "I understand this easily" (ability praise) | Internal, stable, uncontrollable | "My current approach is working well for this material" | Internal, unstable, controllable |
> 
> Note on the table: the constructive substitutes are not merely positive reframings — they are dimensional reassignments. The critical move is always toward unstable and controllable, not simply toward internal or external.

> [!example] **Application** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> This chart translates controlled regulatory inner speech into progressively autonomous alternatives, following the internalization continuum.
> 
> | Controlled Register | Introjected Register | Identified Register | Integrated Register |
> |--------------------|---------------------|--------------------|--------------------|
> | "I have to read this" | "I'll feel guilty if I don't" | "I'm reading this because it matters for my goal" | "I'm reading this because I genuinely want to understand this domain" |
> | "I should focus" | "I'd be embarrassed to not understand this" | "Focusing here serves my real objectives" | "Deep engagement with this is exactly what I'm committed to" |
> | "I must finish this section" | "I'll be disappointed in myself if I don't" | "Finishing this section advances what I'm actually trying to accomplish" | "Working through this fully is consistent with who I am as a learner" |
> 
> Practice instruction: Begin by identifying your natural regulatory register for different reading contexts. The target is not to jump immediately to integrated regulation — progression through identified regulation is a more realistic and sustainable pathway. The vocabulary shift from introjected to identified ("because I'll feel guilty" → "because it serves my goal") is often the most consequential single step.

> [!example] **Application** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> A structured inner speech template for use at the beginning, middle, and end of any significant reading session, designed to engage all three motivational registers.
> 
> **Before reading (Forethought Phase):**
> 1. *Goal vocabulary*: "What specifically do I want to understand from this reading?" (Mastery orientation; specific and challenging)
> 2. *Regulatory vocabulary*: "Why does this matter to me personally?" (Autonomous regulation activation)
> 
> **During reading (Performance Phase):**
> 3. *Monitoring vocabulary*: "Am I following the argument / understanding the concept / or do I need to re-engage?" (Comprehension-cue monitoring, not completion-cue monitoring)
> 
> **After reading (Self-Reflection Phase):**
> 4. *Attributional vocabulary*: "What explains my understanding or confusion — specifically, what approach, strategy, or background knowledge was relevant here?" (Strategy/approach attribution; avoid ability vocabulary)
> 5. *Forward vocabulary*: "What specific next step follows from this reading?" (Seeds the next forethought phase with strategy vocabulary)

> [!example] **Application** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> The following template structures goal vocabulary at three hierarchical levels, integrating SDT regulatory language at the superordinate level and AGT mastery orientation at the intermediate level.
> 
> **Superordinate Goal (Why am I engaging with this domain?):**
> "I am developing understanding of [domain] because [personally meaningful reason connected to genuine values or interests]."
> *Vocabulary principle: autonomous regulation language; first-person ownership; values-connection*
> 
> **Intermediate Goal (What do I want from this reading session?):**
> "By the end of this session, I want to be able to [specific generative capability: explain, apply, critique, connect, evaluate] [specific content]."
> *Vocabulary principle: mastery orientation; specific and generative (not "understand" — "explain," "apply," "critique")*
> 
> **Subordinate Goal (How will I engage with this text?):**
> "For [specific section/argument/concept], I will [specific strategy: map the argument structure, identify the key claims and evidence, locate the tension with what I already know, construct a counterargument]."
> *Vocabulary principle: strategy vocabulary; process-focused; specific enough to generate monitoring checkpoints*
> 
> Note: The vocabulary "understand" is specifically flagged as insufficient for intermediate goal setting, because it fails to generate a monitoring checkpoint — there is no moment when you know whether you "understand" something, whereas there is a clear moment when you can or cannot "explain it in your own words" or "identify the key claims."

> [!example] **Application** *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> **Domain 1: Attribution Vocabulary**
> Replace ability-based, stable, uncontrollable language with strategy-based, unstable, controllable language:
> - *Instead of*: "I can't understand this" → *Use*: "I haven't found the right approach to this yet"
> - *Instead of*: "This is too hard for me" → *Use*: "This requires knowledge/skills I'm still developing"
> - *Instead of*: "I'm not good at abstract reasoning" → *Use*: "Abstract reasoning is an area where I need more practice and examples"
> - *Instead of*: "I keep losing the thread" → *Use*: "My current schema for this argument structure isn't working — I need to reconstruct the argument structure explicitly"
> 
> **Domain 2: Self-Efficacy Vocabulary**
> Use future-oriented, domain-specific, process-anchored language:
> - *Instead of*: "I'll never understand this material" → *Use*: "I can understand this if I work through it systematically"
> - *Instead of*: "I don't think I'm smart enough for this level" → *Use*: "I've understood similarly difficult material before by taking it step-by-step"
> - *Instead of*: "I give up on this section" → *Use*: "I'm not yet understanding this — let me identify specifically where I lost comprehension"
> 
> **Domain 3: Regulatory Vocabulary (SDT)**
> Replace controlled/introjected language with identified/integrated language:
> - *Instead of*: "I have to read this" → *Use*: "I'm choosing to read this because [specific reason]"
> - *Instead of*: "I should understand this by now" → *Use*: "I'm interested in understanding this because it connects to [specific goal]"
> - *Instead of*: "I'll feel stupid if I can't follow this" → *Use*: "Understanding this will help me [specific application]"
> 
> **Domain 4: Goal Construction Vocabulary**
> Use mastery-approach, mechanistic, specific goal language:
> - *Instead of*: "Understand this report" → *Use*: "Understand the *mechanism* by which attribution dimensions cascade into self-efficacy"
> - *Instead of*: "Read through this material" → *Use*: "Construct a situation model of the argument that I can paraphrase in my own words"
> - *Instead of*: "Get through this section" → *Use*: "Identify the two or three claims in this section that I can connect to [existing PKB node]"

> [!warning] **Key Distinction** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> Mechanical vocabulary substitution without genuine engagement produces exactly what the SDT authenticity tension warned against: a kind of motivated self-deception that temporarily elevates mood without changing underlying motivational architecture. The tools above work when used as invitations to genuine reflection, not as scripts to perform. The criterion for genuine use: the vocabulary should surface something true that you did not previously have words for — not dress up a state you're aware of in better clothes.

> [!warning] **Key Distinction** *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> A central limitation of the vocabulary-focused approach in this report is the difficulty of establishing whether changing vocabulary *causes* changes in attribution, efficacy, and motivation, or whether vocabulary merely *reflects* underlying motivational states that change through other mechanisms. Attribution retraining interventions (Försterling, 1985; Perry et al., 1993) provide the strongest evidence that explicitly reframing causal language produces genuine motivational change — but these are structured interventions, not naturalistic vocabulary practice. The claim that deliberate inner speech vocabulary practice produces analogous effects rests on reasonable inference from the intervention literature, not direct evidence. This is worth holding in mind as you work with the practical protocols above.

> [!warning] **Key Distinction** *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> The foundational studies in Attribution Theory, Self-Efficacy Theory, and Achievement Goal Theory are predominantly conducted with Western (primarily American), Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD) samples. Cross-cultural research suggests that the meaning of internal attribution for failure varies substantially across cultural contexts — in collectivist contexts, internal attribution for failure may be less damaging to motivation because it does not carry the same self-defining weight that it does in individualist contexts. The vocabulary prescriptions in this report should be applied with awareness that they reflect cultural assumptions about individual agency, the meaning of effort, and the relationship between self and performance that may not generalize universally.

## Conceptual Tensions

> [!tension] **The Appraisal-Language Tension: Which Comes First?** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> On one side: emotion researchers in the appraisal tradition argue that cognitive evaluations precede and generate emotional responses — suggesting that vocabulary articulates an already-completed appraisal. On the other side: the linguistic relativity literature (Lupyan, Roberson) and the work of Lisa Feldman Barrett on conceptual acts in emotion construction suggest that the verbal category applied to an ambiguous bodily and situational signal is itself what *creates* the emotional experience. The tension is productive: it suggests that the expressive/constitutive distinction is not binary…

> [!tension] **Tension 1: Constitutive vs. Epiphenomenal Vocabulary** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Position A (Received View):** Vocabulary is primarily expressive — it reflects motivational states already generated by deeper processes. Vocabulary change works instrumentally by triggering associated deeper-level changes.
> 
> **Position B (Reconstructed):** Vocabulary is partially constitutive — specific vocabulary choices are among the causal mechanisms through which motivational states are generated and sustained during reading.
> 
> **Current Evidence State:** The intervention literature (attribution retraining, growth mindset) provides strong observational support for the constitutive…

> [!tension] **Tension 2: Spontaneous vs. Deliberate Inner Speech** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Position A:** Much inner speech during reading is automatic, spontaneous, and not available for deliberate monitoring in real time. Vocabulary practice in dedicated practice sessions may not generalize to in-the-moment reading inner speech.
> 
> **Position B:** Extended practice of vocabulary formulations in the SRL context can shift the default vocabulary of spontaneous inner speech — this is what attribution retraining achieves through extended practice.
> 
> **Current Evidence State:** There is strong evidence that extended practice changes default vocabulary tendencies (growth mindset…

> [!tension] **Tension 3: Universal vs. Contextually Indexed Vocabulary** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Position A:** Growth-oriented attributional vocabulary ("not yet," "effort + strategy") represents an objectively superior motivational architecture that is universally applicable.
> 
> **Position B:** What counts as constructive inner speech vocabulary is culturally and contextually indexed. The specific formulations of growth mindset and attribution retraining research carry cultural presuppositions that may not translate across contexts.
> 
> **Current Evidence State:** The intervention evidence base is predominantly WEIRD-sample. Cross-cultural studies show some variation in the effectiveness…

> [!tension] **The Authenticity Problem in Regulatory Vocabulary** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> A genuine tension exists in the SDT evidence base here. SDT's internalization research suggests that the movement from controlled to autonomous regulation cannot be faked — it requires actual value alignment, not vocabulary substitution. Yet the regulatory language intervention research suggests that vocabulary shifts precede and partly cause the underlying motivational state change. The resolution may lie in [[internalization]] theory itself: the mechanism of internalization involves initially adopting the language of a regulatory style before that style becomes genuinely one's own — exactly as children adopt the language of social norms before those norms are fully internalized. This suggests a bootstrapping possibility: deliberate vocabulary practice with autonomous regulatory language can initiate the internalization process rather than merely describing it. But the tension remains active: purely performative vocabulary without engaged reflection may produce a kind of motivated reasoning about one's own motivation rather than genuine regulatory shift.

> [!tension] **Vocabulary Authenticity vs. Vocabulary Practice: Can You Bootstrap Autonomous Motivation?** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> **Position A (SDT orthodoxy):** Authentic autonomous motivation cannot be manufactured through vocabulary substitution. The move from introjected to identified regulation requires genuine value alignment — not performative language change. Changing vocabulary without changing underlying values produces self-deception, not internalization.
> 
> **Position B (Internalization process view):** The internalization process itself involves initially adopting the regulatory language of a more autonomous style before that style is genuinely one's own — exactly as children adopt the language of social norms before full internalization. Vocabulary practice can initiate the internalization process rather than merely describing it.
> 
> **Current Evidence State:** The evidence supports a conditional version of Position B: vocabulary practice produces real motivational change when accompanied by genuine reflection on personal values connection, but not when performed mechanically. The threshold between genuine and performative vocabulary practice is not clearly operationalized in the research.
> 
> **Why This Matters:** The practical tools in Phase V are designed on Position B assumptions. If Position A's stronger claim is correct, they will produce a kind of motivated self-deception rather than genuine motivational development.
> 
> **This Report's Stance:** The analysis accepts the conditional Position B — vocabulary practice as an initiator of internalization rather than a substitute for it — while flagging the authenticity tension as a genuine practical risk requiring reflective engagement, not mechanical vocabulary substitution.

> [!tension] **The Vocabulary-Causality Problem: Does Word Choice Drive Motivation or Merely Label It?** *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> **The Tension:** The central practical claim of this report — that deliberately changing inner speech vocabulary produces genuine motivational change — rests on an assumption that vocabulary is causally upstream of motivational states, not merely downstream of them. This is contested.
> 
> **Position A — Language Constitutes Motivation (Wittgenstein, Vygotsky, Constructionist):** Language is not a passive reporter of pre-linguistic states; it organizes and partly constitutes cognitive and motivational experience. Changing the vocabulary of inner speech does not merely rename existing states — it genuinely reorganizes the cognitive content that language both expresses and structures. Evidence: attribution retraining interventions, which change both causal language and subsequent motivation and performance; theory of constructed emotion (Barrett), which suggests emotional categories are constructed through language-mediated conceptualization.
> 
> **Position B — Motivation is Primary, Language Derivative (Traditional Representationalism):** Motivational states are non-linguistic mental events that are subsequently labeled by language. Changing vocabulary may produce momentary relabeling without changing the underlying state; the observable effects of attribution retraining may work through mechanisms other than language change.
> 
> **Current Evidence:** Attribution retraining intervention studies (Perry et al., 1993; Haynes et al., 2006) do support causal effects of reframing on performance, but the mechanism is not definitively established as linguistic. The most honest position is that language and motivation are mutually constitutive, with neither unambiguously primary.
> 
> **Why It Matters:** If language is only derivative, the vocabulary protocols in this report are misdirected — they address the symptom rather than the cause. If language is constitutive, they address the mechanism directly.
> 
> **See also:** [[attribution-theory]], [[Theory-of-Constructed-Emotion]], [[Language-and-Thought]]




## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **What is the Granularity Limit?** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> The vocabulary granularity principle suggests that finer-grained comprehension monitoring vocabulary enables finer regulatory access. But is there a ceiling? Does very fine-grained inner speech vocabulary create cognitive load that undermines the very comprehension it is trying to monitor? This is an important practical question for designing vocabulary development programs for academic reading.

> [!open-question] **The Automaticity Threshold: When Does Vocabulary Become Infrastructure?** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> At what point does deliberate vocabulary practice become sufficiently internalized that it operates without conscious intention during reading — functioning as genuine motivational infrastructure rather than a metacognitive tax on working memory? The evidence suggests the threshold exists, but neither the timeline nor the conditions for reaching it are well-characterized. This is the most practically consequential open question for implementation of the Motivational Lexicon framework.

> [!open-question] **What is the Natural Ecological Validity of Inner Speech Vocabulary Research?** *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> **The Question:** Nearly all research on inner speech in academic contexts uses verbal protocols (think-aloud) or retrospective self-report, which may not capture the condensed, predicative character of natural inner speech. Is the vocabulary that appears in verbal protocols representative of the vocabulary that actually operates during reading?
> 
> **What We Know:** Fernyhough and colleagues have documented that inner speech in naturalistic settings (experience sampling methodology) often takes non-verbal or partially verbal forms — visual imagery, emotional tone, fragmentary language — rather than full propositions. The rich attribution and efficacy language in this report may correspond more to *potential* inner speech (what could be verbalized if prompted) than to *occurring* inner speech.
> 
> **What We Don't Know:** Whether natural inner speech during academic reading is predominantly verbal (and thus vocabulary-modifiable), whether the non-verbal components carry the same motivational content as their verbal counterparts, and whether deliberate verbal practice generalizes to condensed inner speech.
> 
> **Research Direction:** Experience-sampling studies of inner speech during authentic academic reading, combined with concurrent comprehension measures, would provide important data.
> 
> **See also:** [[inner-speech]], [[metacognitive-monitoring]], [[silent-reading-fluency]]

> [!protocol] **The Motivational Vocabulary Session Protocol** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> **Context:** Use at the beginning and end of each significant reading session to build Motivational Lexicon through deliberate vocabulary practice.
> 
> **Before Reading (5 minutes):**
> 1. *Superordinate Goal*: Write one sentence connecting this reading to a genuine personal value or interest ("I'm reading this because..."). Avoid obligation language.
> 2. *Intermediate Goal*: Write what specific capability you want to have after reading ("By the end, I want to be able to..."). Use a generative verb (explain, apply, critique, connect — not "understand").
> 3. *Subordinate Goal*: Identify the one most challenging section and write what specific strategy you will apply to it.
> 
> **After Reading (5 minutes):**
> 4. *Monitoring review*: What did you notice about your comprehension during reading? Describe in terms of specific passages and specific gaps, not overall impressions.
> 5. *Attribution vocabulary*: Complete this sentence: "Where I struggled, the most accurate explanation is..." — use strategy, approach, or background vocabulary; consciously avoid ability vocabulary.
> 6. *Forward vocabulary*: Write one specific next step that follows from today's reading.
> 
> **Success Criteria:** The protocol is working when the before-reading goal vocabulary becomes increasingly specific and challenging, and the after-reading attribution vocabulary becomes increasingly nuanced and strategy-focused.
> 
> **Common Pitfall:** The most common failure mode is mechanical completion — writing something in each slot without genuine reflection. The criterion for genuine completion: each response should surface something true that you did not previously have words for.

> **Level 1 — Purpose Goal (Why am I reading this?)**
> Frame in identified or integrated regulatory language; connect to values or long-term goals:
> *"I'm reading this because [specific connection to PKB project / intellectual goal / application context]. I choose this."*
> Template: "I am reading [text] because it will help me [specific intellectual/practical outcome], which matters to me because [connection to broader goal]."
> 
> **Level 2 — Comprehension Goal (What do I want to understand?)**
> Frame in mastery-approach, mechanistic language; specify criterion:
> *"By the end of this reading session, I want to understand [specific mechanism / relationship / argument structure] well enough to [specific performance criterion: explain it, connect it to X, apply it to Y]."*
> Template: "I want to understand the **mechanism** by which [A] causes/produces/relates to [B], with enough depth to [apply/explain/connect] it to [specific context]."
> 
> **Level 3 — Process Goal (How will I read?)**
> Frame in strategy language; specify monitoring criteria:
> *"I will [specific reading strategy] and monitor comprehension by [specific criterion]. If I lose the thread, I will [specific recovery strategy] rather than continuing."*
> Template: "I will [read actively / annotate / paraphrase each section] and notice when I can't paraphrase a claim in my own words [monitoring criterion]. When that happens, I will [re-read / look up prerequisite / construct explicit argument structure] before moving on."
> 
> **Level 4 — Attribution Pre-Commitment (How will I interpret difficulty?)**
> Explicitly pre-commit to attribution pattern before encountering difficulty:
> *"If I encounter difficulty in this text, I will interpret it as [strategy/schema mismatch / need for elaboration / unfamiliar vocabulary] rather than [ability inadequacy]. Difficulty is information, not verdict."*

> [!protocol] **Self-Reflection Phase Inner Speech Audit** *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> After completing a reading session, spend 3–5 minutes with these prompts, attending to the vocabulary you reach for:
> 
> **Causal Attribution Audit:**
> "What explains how this session went?" — Notice whether your explanation reaches for ability, effort, strategy, task difficulty, or context. Specifically: is the cause you named stable or unstable? Controllable or uncontrollable?
> 
> **Efficacy Update Audit:**
> "What am I taking away about my capability for this kind of material?" — Notice whether your efficacy estimate has changed and why. Is the update warranted by actual performance data, or is it driven by the *emotional valence* of the session?
> 
> **Regulatory Register Audit:**
> "What was the dominant motivational register of this session?" — Was it introjected (should/must), identified (want to/because), or intrinsic? What would it take to shift the balance toward the more autonomous end?
> 
> **Vocabulary Replacement:**
> For each maladaptive vocabulary item identified, deliberately formulate a replacement using the four-domain vocabulary architecture above. This is not positive thinking; it is accurate attribution reframing.

> [!protocol] **The Pre-Reading Motivational Architecture Protocol (Forethought Phase)** *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> Apply this 5-minute protocol before beginning any Foundational Report or challenging academic text.
> 
> **Step 1 — Regulatory Register Check (1 min):**
> Formulate in one sentence why you are reading this text. Listen to the sentence. Does it contain *have to, should, must, need to* (controlled), or *want to, choosing to, because it matters for* (autonomous)? If controlled: replace with an identified/integrated formulation before proceeding. Do not skip this — the register of the first sentence shapes the entire session.
> 
> **Step 2 — Comprehension Goal (1 min):**
> State the mechanistic goal: "I want to understand the *mechanism* by which [A] produces [B]." Specify the criterion: "Well enough to [paraphrase/connect/apply]." Avoid surface goals ("understand this section") or performance goals ("prove I can handle this material").
> 
> **Step 3 — Attribution Pre-Commitment (1 min):**
> State explicitly: "If I encounter difficulty in this text, I will interpret it as [a strategy/schema mismatch, an unfamiliar concept, a need for more prior knowledge] rather than as evidence of inadequate ability." Pre-commit to this interpretation before the difficulty occurs — post-hoc reframing is harder than prospective commitment.
> 
> **Step 4 — Efficacy Anchoring (1 min):**
> Recall a specific prior instance of successfully engaging with similarly difficult material. This is not positive thinking — it is deliberate activation of the mastery experience source of efficacy. Be specific: "I successfully understood [X difficult material] by [specific strategy]. I can apply the same approach here."
> 
> **Step 5 — Goal Review (1 min):**
> Re-read the comprehension goal. Commit to monitoring comprehension against that criterion (not against "reading the words"). If you cannot paraphrase a claim in your own words, that is a comprehension failure requiring repair strategy — not a signal to continue.

> [!protocol] **The Difficulty-Response Protocol (In-Reading)** *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> Apply this protocol at the moment of encountering comprehension difficulty.
> 
> **Step 1 — Attribution Triage (10 seconds):**
> Name the difficulty without evaluating the self: "I've lost the thread here." (Not: "I can't follow this.") This step prevents the automatic ability attribution.
> 
> **Step 2 — Attribution Classification (20 seconds):**
> Apply Weiner's dimensions: Is the difficulty due to [strategy mismatch / unfamiliar vocabulary / missing prior knowledge / text structure / insufficient effort on this passage]? Select the most accurate and most controllable attribution.
> 
> **Step 3 — Strategy Response (variable):**
> Select a recovery strategy matched to the attribution: vocabulary gap → consult glossary/PKB; schema mismatch → reconstruct argument structure explicitly; missing prior knowledge → identify and fill prerequisite; insufficient attention → re-read with active note-making.
> 
> **Step 4 — Efficacy Maintenance:**
> After applying the strategy, note any improvement in comprehension. Use this as mastery experience input: "I recovered comprehension by [strategy]. That approach works."

## Concrete Examples

> [!example] **Goal Language Comparison: The Same Objective, Multiple Vocabularies** *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> Consider a reader who intends to understand Weiner's three attribution dimensions while reading this report. Here are five different ways to formulate that goal, each encoding a different motivational vocabulary:
> 
> 1. *"I need to memorize the three dimensions so I can use them."* — **Performance-approach, controlled, surface.** The goal is reproduction and instrumental use; the operative word is *need* (mild introjection); the criterion is recall.
> 
> 2. *"I should be able to understand this by the end — it's not supposed to be that hard."* — **Performance-avoidance, introjected.** The goal is competence demonstration; the criterion is normative sufficiency; failure will generate shame.
> 
> 3. *"I want to genuinely understand how locus, stability, and controllability each differentially affect motivation — not just as labels but as mechanisms."* — **Mastery-approach, identified, deep.** The goal is mechanistic understanding; the criterion is self-set and substantive; failure generates curiosity, not shame.
> 
> 4. *"I'm building schema for a framework I'll apply to my own inner speech. I want to understand this well enough to recognize attribution patterns in real-time."* — **Mastery-approach, integrated, applied.** The goal is connected to identity and practical application; the criterion is transfer.
> 
> 5. *"How does this work? I want to follow the argument."* — **Intrinsic.** No explicit goal construction; engagement is pure curiosity-driven; the criterion is felt comprehension.

> [!example] **The Cascade in Action: Two Readers, One Dense Paragraph** *(from [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25]])*
> Consider two readers encountering a particularly abstract passage in a Foundational Report. Both fail to construct a coherent situation model on the first pass. Their inner speech diverges immediately:
> 
> **Reader A**: *"I'm losing the thread again. I've never been good at this level of abstraction. Maybe this is just too advanced for me right now."* → **Internal, stable, uncontrollable** attribution → efficacy erosion → reduced effort allocation → skimming → shallow encoding → confirmation of initial attribution → vicious spiral.
> 
> **Reader B**: *"I'm not tracking the argument here. Let me see where I lost the thread — it was probably at the point where the author moved from describing the phenomenon to explaining the mechanism. I need to re-read that transition."* → **Internal, unstable, controllable** (strategy) attribution → maintained efficacy → strategic re-reading → deeper processing → comprehension improvement → efficacy building → virtuous spiral.
> 
> The difference between these two readers is not, at this moment, one of intelligence, prior knowledge, or reading skill. It is the vocabulary of step 1.
