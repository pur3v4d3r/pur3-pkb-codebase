## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technique: Cold Reconstruction — Written From Memory, Timer Running
> *Why here: The gap between what you think you understood (subjective fluency during reading) and what you can actually reconstruct (genuine comprehension) is your real comprehension map. This is the primary output that feeds into the attribution analysis.*
>
> **Cold Reconstruction — SRL Forethought & Self-Reflection Report:**
>
> The Zimmerman SRL cycle has three phases: Forethought, Performance, Self-Reflection. This report focused on Forethought and Self-Reflection.
>
> **Forethought Phase** has two subsystems: (1) Task Analysis — includes goal-setting and strategic planning, (2) Motivational Beliefs — includes self-efficacy assessment and goal orientation. The key claim is that Motivational Beliefs can *corrupt* Task Analysis — low self-efficacy causes you to set shallower goals before you consciously decide to.
>
> **Self-Reflection Phase** also has two subsystems: (1) Self-Judgment — includes self-evaluation (comparing performance to goals) and causal attribution (why did I perform that way), (2) Self-Reaction — includes self-satisfaction and, most importantly, *adaptive vs. defensive inference*.
>
> **Adaptive inference:** controllable, unstable attribution → "I failed because I didn't review prerequisites → next time I will" → maintains or improves self-efficacy.
>
> **Defensive inference:** stable, uncontrollable attribution → "this material is too advanced for me now" → disguised as good judgment, actually prevents productive struggle.
>
> **The five feedback channels** through which Self-Reflection generates the next Forethought: (1) self-efficacy recalibration, (2) prior knowledge update, (3) strategic knowledge refinement, (4) goal standard recalibration, (5) motivational trajectory. I am less confident about #4 and #5 — the exact phrasing.
>
> **The Monitoring-Reflection Dependency:** Self-Reflection quality depends entirely on monitoring quality during reading. You cannot accurately reflect on what you didn't track.
>
> **Three types of metacognitive knowledge:** declarative (what), procedural (how), conditional (when/why). Conditional is the hardest and is what expertise feels like.
>
