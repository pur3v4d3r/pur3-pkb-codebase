# Regulation of Cognition (Schraw & Dennison, 1994)

> [!definition] Regulation of Cognition (Schraw & Dennison, 1994)
> The ensemble of executive processes through which a learner actively manages her cognitive engagement — planning how to approach a task, monitoring the adequacy of ongoing processing, evaluating the outcomes of cognitive activity, and adjusting strategies when monitoring signals inadequacy. Regulation of cognition operates in real time, during cognitive engagement, and is responsive to the phenomenological signals of metacognitive experience and the outputs of metacognitive monitoring.
>
> **Boundary:** Regulation of cognition is distinguished from automatic executive functions (such as attention management and error detection in highly practiced tasks) by its deliberate, reflective character. Regulation involves bringing metacognitive knowledge to bear on ongoing processing — it is not merely the execution of well-practiced routines, but the adaptive management of cognitive resources in response to task demands.
>
> **Research Status:** The relationship between metacognitive knowledge and metacognitive regulation is contested. Some frameworks treat them as essentially separable — one can have sophisticated knowledge without deploying it regulatorily, and one can regulate effectively without being able to articulate the knowledge underlying one's regulation. Others argue that the distinction is primarily analytic: in vivo metacognition integrates knowledge and regulation inseparably.
>
