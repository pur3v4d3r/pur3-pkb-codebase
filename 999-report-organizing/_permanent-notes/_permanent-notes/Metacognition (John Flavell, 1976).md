# Metacognition (John Flavell, 1976)

> [!definition] Metacognition (John Flavell, 1976)
> Metacognition is cognition about cognition — knowledge and cognitive processes that take one's own cognitive states, processes, and products as their objects. Flavell's original definition distinguished two components: (a) metacognitive knowledge — stored knowledge about factors affecting cognitive performance (person characteristics, task demands, strategy options), and (b) metacognitive experiences — real-time cognitive and affective experiences that arise during cognitive engagements and function as monitoring signals.
>
> **Boundary:** Metacognition is not consciousness, though it involves cognitive processes available to conscious awareness. Not all metacognitive monitoring is conscious or deliberate; some operates automatically (e.g., the fluency signal that produces rapid JOLs). The term also does not refer exclusively to explicit verbal self-commentary; much metacognitive processing is implicit and procedural.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Flavell's definition creates the conceptual terrain for all subsequent theoretical and empirical work. The distinction between knowledge and experience (monitoring signals) establishes the two foundational pillars of the field.
>
> **See also:** [[Metacognition-—-Foundational-Concepts]], [[Flavell's-Metacognitive-Framework]], [[metacognitive-experience]]

> [!evidence] Metacognition (John Flavell, 1976)
> [[metacognition|Metacognition]] refers to cognition about cognition — the knowledge, awareness, and regulatory control exercised over one's own cognitive processes. It encompasses metacognitive knowledge (understanding of one's own cognitive capabilities and strategies), metacognitive monitoring (assessments of current cognitive states), and metacognitive control (decisions that regulate cognitive processing based on monitoring outputs).
>
> **Boundary:** Metacognition is not synonymous with self-awareness in the broader philosophical sense; it refers specifically to cognitive self-monitoring and self-regulation, not to emotional awareness or existential self-knowledge.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Identified as the superstructure within which all other cognitive strategies operate (Section 6) and as a function that PKB architecture can externalize (the PKB as externalized metacognitive architecture).
