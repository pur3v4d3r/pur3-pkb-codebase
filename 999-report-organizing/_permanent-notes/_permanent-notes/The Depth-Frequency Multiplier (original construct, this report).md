# The Depth-Frequency Multiplier (original construct, this report)

> [!definition] The Depth-Frequency Multiplier (original construct, this report)
> The principle that in lexical learning (and, by extension, in other domains of knowledge acquisition), the learning value of a given number of exposures scales non-linearly with the depth of processing at each exposure. A few deep encounters reliably outperform many shallow ones in producing durable, precise, integrated lexical knowledge. The "multiplier" is the ratio of retention per hour under deep processing versus shallow processing conditions, which research suggests is substantially greater than 1 — often estimated at 3:1 or higher.
>
> **Boundary:** This is a theoretical construct integrating depth-of-processing research with vocabulary acquisition research. The precise magnitude of the multiplier varies across individuals, domains, and word types. The claim is directional and comparative (deep > shallow in retention efficiency) rather than precisely quantified.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This construct integrates the depth-of-processing literature with the vocabulary acquisition literature into a single actionable principle for deliberate practice design — prioritizing depth of encounter over frequency of encounter when designing vocabulary practice.
>
> **See also:** [[elaborative-rehearsal]], [[desirable-difficulties]], [[spaced-repetition]], [[retrieval-practice]]
