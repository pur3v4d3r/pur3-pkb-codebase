# Goal Commitment

> [!definition] Goal Commitment
> **Goal Commitment**: The determination to attain a goal and refusal to lower or abandon it despite obstacles. Functions as the primary moderator of the goal difficulty-performance relationship: the performance benefits of challenging goals are realized only when commitment remains high. Moderated by [[self-efficacy]], perceived goal importance, and degree of autonomous vs. controlled regulation.
> See also: [[goal-setting-theory]], [[self-determination-theory]], [[implementation-intention]]

> [!evidence] Goal Commitment
> The degree to which a person is determined to attain a goal, manifested behaviorally as continued effort directed at the goal across time and across resistance, and produced jointly by the perceived importance of the goal and the perceived attainability of the goal.
>
> **Boundary:** Commitment is not the same as goal acceptance, which refers to merely agreeing to pursue a goal; commitment refers to the active determination that sustains pursuit when conditions become difficult. Many goals are accepted without ever being committed to, which is one reason organizational goal-setting interventions frequently fail to produce predicted effects.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Commitment is the master moderator because it is a precondition for all the others — without commitment, no mechanism is activated, no moderator's calibration matters, and the theory has nothing to predict.
>
> **See also:** [[goal-commitment]], [[self-efficacy]], [[volitional-congruence]]
