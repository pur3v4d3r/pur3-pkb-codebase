## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technical Detail: Decision Algorithm for Walk vs. Sit During Reading
> Based on the full analysis, the walking reader can apply the following decision framework in real time:
>
> **Decision point: Should I be walking or sitting right now?**
>
> ```
> IF arousal is LOW (drowsy, restless, post-meal, unfocused)
>   → WALK (arousal regulation is primary need)
>
> IF material difficulty is HIGH (novel framework, complex argument, 
>    struggling to hold the thread)
>   → SIT (full CE capacity needed; metacognitive monitoring critical)
>
> IF making notes is appropriate (key concept identified, claim to 
>    extract, connection to PKB)
>   → SIT (note-making requires full CE + writing capacity)
>
> IF doing first pass on new material (survey, skim, build familiarity)
>   → WALK (walking cost is minimal for first-pass processing;
>     arousal benefit supports sustained attention)
>
> IF reading fluently and comprehension feels solid on moderate material
>   → EITHER (walking is minimally costly; sitting is marginally 
>     better; choose based on arousal needs and restlessness)
>
> DEFAULT: Walk for 20-30 minutes → Sit for 20-40 minutes → Repeat
> ```
>
> **The metacognitive trigger for transition:** The single most important signal is noticing that you are **reading fluently but cannot explain what you just read.** This is the marker of the fluency-comprehension dissociation (Edge Case 4). When this happens: sit down, re-read the passage, make a note.
