## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technique: Elaborative Interrogation — Three Types of Metacognitive Knowledge
> *Why here: The declarative/procedural/conditional distinction in Flavell's framework (introduced in this section) is easy to memorize but hard to use without pushing on WHY the three types are distinct and not reducible to each other.*
>
> **Claim:** You need all three types of metacognitive knowledge — declarative (what), procedural (how), conditional (when/why) — for sophisticated Forethought and Self-Reflection.
>
> **Elaborative interrogation:** Why can't declarative knowledge substitute for conditional knowledge? They seem related — if I know *what* a strategy is and *how* to use it, don't I already know *when* to use it?
>
> **Generated answer:** No — because conditional knowledge is about *fit* between strategy and context, which is a third-order judgment that requires experience with both the strategy and the context under which it succeeds and fails. I can know perfectly well what elaborative interrogation is and how to execute it, while having no reliable sense of *when* it is more valuable than a different strategy. Conditional knowledge is acquired by observing strategy effectiveness across varied conditions — which is exactly what the Zone 3 attribution analysis is designed to produce. The protocol builds conditional knowledge by forcing explicit analysis of "which strategy worked and which didn't, in what kind of text, at what level of prior knowledge."
>
> **Connection made:** This is the same structure as the [[dreyfus-skill-acquisition-model]]'s distinction between novice (rule-following) and expert (situational judgment). Conditional metacognitive knowledge is what expertise in self-regulated reading feels like from the inside. → [[Declarative,-Procedural,-and-Conditional-Metacognitive-Knowledge]]
> *— [[optimal-reading-session-worked-example-2026-03-25_report]]*
