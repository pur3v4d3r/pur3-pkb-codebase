> [!section-summary] Section 6 Core Takeaways
> Attributional processes in social contexts involve systematic biases — the Fundamental Attribution Error, actor-observer asymmetry — that reflect the architecture of human person-perception and attentional deployment rather than random noise. Attributional ambiguity in stigmatized identities reveals that the clarity of attributional diagnosis is itself a privilege; many people face genuine epistemic uncertainty about whether negative outcomes reflect personal or systemic causes, with distinct psychological consequences. Self-handicapping demonstrates that attribution management is not merely retrospective but prospective — people strategically structure causal environments to protect preferred self-attributions. Each of these phenomena has direct implications for instructional design, feedback architecture, and institutional climate.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 6 Core Takeaways
> 1. The pragmatist teacher is a designer of learning environments, not a transmitter of content: the professional challenge is constructing genuinely problematic situations that are appropriately challenging for the learner's current level of development.
> 2. Pragmatist curriculum organizes subject matter functionally — as the tools needed to address genuine problems — rather than logically (as organized disciplines). [[Design-Thinking-Pedagogy|Design thinking]] and project-based approaches are contemporary implementations of this principle.
> 3. [[formative-assessment|Formative assessment]] and [[feedback-design|feedback-rich learning environments]] are the institutional expression of the pragmatist inquiry cycle: they ensure that the learner has the information needed to revise understanding and reconstruct habits in response to the consequences of their intellectual actions.
> *— [[pragmatism-and-learning-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*
