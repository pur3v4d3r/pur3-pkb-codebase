## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Principle 1 → Concept Learning in Science Education
> Students who encounter scientific concepts in passive lecture formats — hearing the term repeatedly without engaging in the elaborative processing required to connect it to prior knowledge and predict its application in novel cases — consistently fail to achieve durable conceptual understanding despite multiple exposures. The mechanism is identical to the vocabulary case: the lecture context does not spontaneously generate the processing depth required for robust encoding. This explains why passive lecture attendance is such a poor predictor of examination performance compared to active problem-solving: the former accumulates shallow exposure; the latter forces the elaborative processing that crosses the encoding threshold. The transfer implication: educators should not infer that "students have been taught" a concept from exposure frequency; they should ask whether the instructional context generated the processing depth that durable encoding requires.
> *— [[vocabulary-acquisition-reading-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
