## Flashcards

> [!flashcard] Seed 13 — Distinction
> **Q:** What does the ICAP framework predict about EI's effectiveness, and why?
> **A:** ICAP predicts that constructive activities (which EI exemplifies) produce superior learning to active, passive, or interactive activities, because constructive activities require the learner to generate knowledge outputs that go beyond the presented information. EI specifically involves generating a causal explanation not contained in the text, placing it squarely in the constructive category. ICAP's constructive engagement hypothesis directly predicts EI's moderate-to-high utility rating.
> **Source:** Phase II
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate

> [!flashcard] Seed 13 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is pseudometacognition, and why does it matter for research and instruction?
> **A:** Pseudometacognition is regulatory-appearing behavior that does not involve genuine meta-level representation of one's cognitive states — a learner who re-reads automatically after a certain page count without monitoring comprehension, or who produces metacognitive vocabulary without actual monitoring. It matters for research because self-report measures cannot distinguish genuine from spurious regulation, and for instruction because behavioral change (more rereading) without genuine regulatory development does not transfer to novel contexts.
> **Source:** Phase V (Limitations)
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #pseudometacognition, #metacognitive-monitoring, #metacognitive-regulation, #self-regulated-learning
