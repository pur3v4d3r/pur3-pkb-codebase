# Elaborative Interrogation (Pressley et al., 1987; McDaniel & Donnelly, 1996)

> [!definition] Elaborative Interrogation (Pressley et al., 1987; McDaniel & Donnelly, 1996)
> A generative learning strategy in which the learner answers why-questions about factual propositions, activating prior semantic knowledge and integrating target information into an existing knowledge network. Distinguished from self-explanation by its factual-relational content target and from free elaboration by the disciplined why-format constraint.
>
> **Boundary:** Does not include: (a) why-questions answered by reading the answer from the text rather than generating it; (b) comprehension questions targeting the learner's overall understanding rather than specific propositions; (c) elaborative strategies that use imagery rather than semantic-causal reasoning.
>
> **Common Misconception:** EI is not "critical thinking questions" appended to textbooks. It is a self-generated, proposition-specific, causal-explanatory answer that the learner produces from prior knowledge.
>
> **Research Status:** Moderate utility (Dunlosky et al., 2013); prior knowledge moderation well-established; long-term retention data sparse.
>
> **See also:** [[generation-effect]], [[generative-learning-theory]], [[levels-of-processing-theory]], [[self-explanation-effect]], [[orienting-task]]
