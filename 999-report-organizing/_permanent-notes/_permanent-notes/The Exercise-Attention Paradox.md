## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Exercise-Attention Paradox
> If walking requires cognitive resources (which it does, at least some), then walking-while-reading should always be worse than reading alone. But many walkers report *better* focus during walking than sitting. Either the self-reports are wrong (possible), or the resource-cost of walking is offset by an arousal or attentional benefit that exceeds the cost (also possible), or the comparison condition — restless sitting — is not cognitively neutral (quite likely).
> *— [[walking-academic-reading-working-memory-deep-dive-2026-04-13_report]]*
