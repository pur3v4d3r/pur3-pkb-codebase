# Personal Knowledge Management (PKM)

> [!definition] Personal Knowledge Management (PKM)
> [[personal-knowledge-management|Personal Knowledge Management]] refers to the integrated set of practices, tools, and cognitive strategies through which an individual captures information, transforms it into usable knowledge, organises that knowledge into retrievable and connectable structures, and applies it across contexts over time. PKM is distinguished from organisational knowledge management by its focus on the individual's cognitive ecosystem, and from simple information management by its emphasis on *transformation* — the movement from information intake to knowledge construction.
>
> **Boundary:** PKM is not mere note-taking or filing. It encompasses the full epistemic lifecycle: encounter, capture, process, connect, retrieve, apply, and review. Systems that merely store information without supporting transformation are information repositories, not knowledge management systems.
>
> **See also:** [[personal-knowledge-base]], [[prior-knowledge-moderation|PKM]], [[zettelkasten]], [[active-note-making]], [[obsidian]]

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Personal Knowledge Management (PKM)
> The practice of building a [[personal-knowledge-management|personal knowledge base]] is itself a cognitive load management exercise. Effective PKM systems function as external schemas — they offload organizational load from working memory to the system's structure (folders, tags, links, MOCs). The design of note-taking templates, the use of [[Atomic-Notes|atomic notes]] that each capture a single concept, the progressive elaboration of connection networks — all of these practices reduce the extraneous load of knowledge organization, freeing cognitive resources for the germane processing of understanding and synthesizing. The wiki-link network in a PKB functions analogously to the schema network in long-term memory: each link represents a learned relationship that, once encoded, no longer requires working memory to reconstruct. [[Cognitive-Load-and-Need-Satisfaction|Cognitive load considerations]] are foundational to sustainable knowledge work.
>

> [!evidence] Personal Knowledge Management (PKM)
> [[personal-knowledge-management|Personal Knowledge Management]] designates the ensemble of strategies, tools, and practices through which an individual captures, organizes, integrates, retrieves, and applies knowledge across the full arc of intellectual and professional life. Unlike organizational knowledge management, which concerns institutional repositories and collective intelligence, PKM operates at the scale of the individual mind and its extensions — notebooks, digital vaults, annotation systems, and the associative networks they instantiate. The term encompasses both the *system* (the tools and structures) and the *practice* (the cognitive strategies deployed within and alongside those tools).
>
> **Boundary:** PKM is not synonymous with information management; the latter concerns data storage and retrieval, while PKM additionally requires meaning-making, integration, and the cultivation of retrievable understanding.
