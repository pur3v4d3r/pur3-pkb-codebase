## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Important Nuance: Expertise Differences Are Schema-Type-Specific
> Expert blindness occurs for patterns that are very well-schematized. If an expert has a detailed, well-tuned schema for atypical presentations of common conditions (experts who have seen many atypical MIs develop an ATYPICAL-MI sub-schema), they will NOT miss atypical presentations with the same mechanism. The blindness is not to "atypical instances" generally but to instances that activate a high-confidence schema and are *also* inconsistent with that schema in ways that haven't been schematized.
>
> This means expertise differences in schema-blind errors are domain-stratified: experts in high-variance domains (emergency medicine, pattern recognition in novel contexts) may have less blindness than experts in low-variance domains (routine clinical medicine, assembly-line quality control). The paradox is worse where standard cases so vastly outnumber atypical cases that the schema for "atypical" never forms.
> *— [[schema-and-how-they-work-deep-dive-2026-04-08_report]]*
