# Strategic Automaticity (Gollwitzer 1999)

> [!definition] Strategic Automaticity (Gollwitzer 1999)
> The cognitive state in which a deliberately formed if-then plan operates with the speed, efficiency, and resistance to interference characteristic of habitual responses, but achieved through prior cognitive engineering rather than long behavioral repetition. Strategic automaticity exhibits three operational signatures: enhanced cue detection, accelerated action initiation, and increased shielding against competing influences.
>
> **Boundary:** Strategic automaticity is not the same as ordinary [[automaticity]]; it lacks the deeply consolidated procedural-memory traces that distinguish true habits, and it can be undone by re-deliberation in ways that genuine habits cannot be undone.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This is the construct that explains *why* if-then plans work and *what* their characteristic failure modes will be when they fail.
>
> **See also:** [[automaticity]], [[habit-formation]], [[dual-process-theory]], [[cue-utilization-framework]]
