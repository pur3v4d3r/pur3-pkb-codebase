## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Process Goals vs. Outcome Goals — When Does the Balance Tip?
> **The Tension:** Zimmerman and Kitsantas (1997, 2002) demonstrate clear advantages of process goals over outcome goals during skill acquisition phases. But the research also suggests that as learners approach expert performance, outcome goals may become more appropriate — the strategies are sufficiently automatised that explicit process focus may actually interfere with performance.
>
> **Position A — Process Goals Throughout:** Process goals should dominate in all non-expert reading contexts because genuine expertise in academic reading across complex domains is rare, and the monitoring benefits of process goals consistently outweigh costs.
>
> **Position B — Context-Sensitive Shifting:** The optimal balance between process and outcome goals should shift as competence in a specific domain develops. Novice readers of theoretical texts: heavy process goal emphasis. Experienced readers in familiar domains: more outcome goal flexibility.
>
> **Current Evidence:** Research supports the novice-toward-expert developmental shift but leaves the exact threshold underspecified. The practitioner heuristic is: default to process goals unless performance monitoring consistently indicates genuine automaticity of the relevant strategies.
>
> **This Report's Position:** Given the range of theoretical domains covered in a comprehensive PKB, process goals should dominate for any new domain or novel type of text, with outcome goals used only for familiar material where specific processing strategies are already well-automatised.
>
