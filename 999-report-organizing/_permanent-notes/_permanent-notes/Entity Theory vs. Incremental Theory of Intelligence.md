# Entity Theory vs. Incremental Theory of Intelligence

> [!definition] Entity Theory vs. Incremental Theory of Intelligence
> **Entity Theory** (also called the Fixed Mindset) is the implicit belief that intelligence and ability are fixed, stable, non-malleable properties — individuals have a certain level of ability, and experiences largely reveal rather than change this level. **Incremental Theory** (also called the Growth Mindset) is the implicit belief that intelligence and ability are malleable, developable, and responsive to effort, learning, and strategy. These implicit theories — measured by items such as "You have a certain amount of intelligence and can't do much to change it" — serve as the upstream schemas from which specific attributions are generated: entity theorists are most at risk for stable ability attribution for failure; incremental theorists most readily make unstable, effort-controllable failure attributions. See also: [[implicit-theory-of-intelligence]], [[growth-mindset-theory]], [[fixed-mindset]].
