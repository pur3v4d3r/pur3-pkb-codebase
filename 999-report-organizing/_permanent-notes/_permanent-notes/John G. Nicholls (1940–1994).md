## Additional Material

> [!person] John G. Nicholls (1940–1994)
> **Core Contribution:** Developed the foundational distinction between task involvement and ego involvement in achievement motivation, and the concept of the differentiated ability concept as its developmental precondition. Established that the standard by which competence is evaluated — self-referenced vs. normative — determines the motivational dynamics of achievement behavior.
> **Relationship to Other Figures:** Influenced by Harter's competence motivation theory and White's effectance motivation concept; parallel development with Dweck's work on achievement patterns; preceded and theoretically grounded Elliot's 2×2 extension.
> **Key Works:** *The competitive ethos and democratic education* (1989); "Achievement motivation: Conceptions of ability, subjective experience, task choice, and performance" (*Psychological Review*, 1984).
> **Relevance to This Analysis:** Nicholls' differentiated ability concept is the psychological foundation of the PAV dynamics analyzed in this report — it explains why normative comparison generates self-worth threat in ways that self-referenced standards do not.
> *— [[achievement-goal-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-24_report]]*

> [!person] John G. Nicholls (1940–1994)
> Developmental and educational psychologist, University of Illinois. Nicholls' foundational contribution was the developmental analysis of ability conceptions — demonstrating that children acquire the differentiated conception of ability (distinguishing ability from effort) across middle childhood, creating the cognitive infrastructure for ego involvement. His construct of task versus ego involvement anchors one pole of the theory's history.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by Atkinson's achievement motivation tradition; developed in parallel with and subsequently integrated with Dweck and Ames.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the developmental and philosophical foundations — particularly the differentiated/undifferentiated ability distinction and the task/ego involvement construct.
> *— [[achievement-goal-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*
