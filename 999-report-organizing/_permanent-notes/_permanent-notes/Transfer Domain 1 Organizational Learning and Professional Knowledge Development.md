## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Transfer Domain 1: Organizational Learning and Professional Knowledge Development
> **Structural principle:** Every mechanism developed for academic text comprehension — metacognitive monitoring, schema-based organization, retrieval practice, self-explanation — applies equally to professional knowledge development from technical reports, industry publications, policy documents, and internal organizational texts. Organizations whose professionals read with situation model depth rather than textbase surface knowledge make decisions based on richer, more accurate models of their domain; those who read for passive exposure produce fluency without understanding.
>
> **Concrete application:** A professional reader engaging with a technical research report should deploy the same forethought phase (goal setting, structured preview, prior knowledge activation), performance phase (strategic slowing at technical content, self-questioning, annotation), and self-reflection phase (post-reading retrieval test, written synthesis note) that an academic reader would use. The specific strategies need contextual adaptation — a legal case brief requires different text structure knowledge than a scientific review — but the metacognitive architecture is identical.
>
> **Boundary condition:** Domain-specific text structure knowledge and epistemic conventions remain necessary even in professional contexts — medical literature, legal opinions, financial analyses, and engineering specifications each constitute technical genres with their own structural conventions requiring explicit genre-schema development.
>
