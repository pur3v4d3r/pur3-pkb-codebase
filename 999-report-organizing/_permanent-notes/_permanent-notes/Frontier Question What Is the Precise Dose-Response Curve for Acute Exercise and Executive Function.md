## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Frontier Question: What Is the Precise Dose-Response Curve for Acute Exercise and Executive Function?
> **The question:** The general model posits an inverted-U relationship between exercise intensity and cognitive performance, with "light-to-moderate" exercise being optimal for executive function tasks. But the shape of this curve — its inflection points, slope, width of the optimal range, and how it varies with task type and individual characteristics — remains poorly characterized for the specific case of [[executive-function]] during (not just after) exercise.
>
> **Current best understanding:** Meta-analyses (McMorris & Hale, 2012; Chang et al., 2012; Lambourne & Tomporowski, 2010) consistently find that:
> - During exercise: Speed improves, accuracy is maintained or slightly reduced, with moderate exercise producing better outcomes than vigorous
> - After exercise: Both speed and accuracy improve, with effects strongest for executive function tasks, peaking at 11-20 minutes post-exercise and lasting up to 60 minutes
> - The inverted-U model is supported but with **substantial heterogeneity** — effect sizes range from small-negative to large-positive within the same meta-analyses
>
> **What we don't know:**
> 1. The **minimum effective dose** — how light can exercise be and still produce measurable cognitive enhancement? Indoor walking may be near or below this threshold for some individuals. Zero exercise effect would eliminate the arousal benefit and leave only the dual-task cost.
> 2. The **optimal zone width** — is the "beneficial zone" a broad plateau or a narrow peak? A broad plateau would mean that moderate variations in walking speed are irrelevant; a narrow peak would mean precise pacing matters.
> 3. **Task-specific dose-response** — reading comprehension involves both simple retrieval (which exercise reliably enhances) and complex inference (which exercise may or may not enhance). The dose-response curve for each may differ.
> 4. **Within-session dose-response dynamics** — does the arousal benefit change across 30-, 60-, or 90-minute exercise bouts? The temporal dynamics addressed in Level 4 are based on limited data for the specific case of sustained light walking with concurrent cognitive demanding tasks.
>
> **Active research directions:** McMorris's group (Chichester, UK) continues dose-response work with catecholamine biomarkers. Pontifex's group (Michigan State) focuses on dose-response in children and ADHD populations. Etnier's group (UNC Greensboro) examines individual difference moderators of the dose-response curve.
>
> **Predicted resolution timeline:** Partial resolution within 5-10 years as wearable technology enables ecological momentary assessment of exercise-cognition interactions in naturalistic settings. Full resolution is unlikely because the variable space (task type × exercise type × fitness level × individual WMC × time course) is enormous.
>
> **What would change if resolved:** A precise dose-response function would allow the walking reader to **calculate** whether their specific walking intensity and duration produces a net positive or negative effect on reading comprehension. Currently, the recommendation is based on qualitative modeling ("light exercise is probably beneficial"); a dose-response function would make it quantitative.
