# Coupling-Constitution Fallacy (Adams & Aizawa)

> [!definition] Coupling-Constitution Fallacy (Adams & Aizawa)
> The alleged error of inferring, from the fact that two systems are causally coupled, that one thereby constitutes part of the other. Adams and Aizawa argue that Clark and Chalmers commit this fallacy: because Otto's notebook is causally coupled to his cognitive processes, they infer it constitutes part of his cognitive system. The objection insists that causal relevance and constitutive membership are distinct relations, and that establishing the former does not establish the latter. The mark of the cognitive — whatever distinguishes genuine cognitive processes from causally relevant non-cognitive ones — must be specified independently of coupling relations.
