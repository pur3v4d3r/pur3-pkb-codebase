## Flashcards

> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Connection
> **Q:** Why does prior knowledge moderate elaborative interrogation effectiveness, and is this moderation peripheral or structural?
>
> **A:** Prior knowledge moderation is structural, not peripheral. Situation model construction — the process EI invokes — requires prior knowledge schemas as integration anchors: points of connection to which new text propositions can be attached within a causal-relational framework. A learner without relevant prior knowledge cannot execute the gateway mechanism because they lack the integration anchors that construction requires. This is why EI effects collapse (not merely diminish) when prior knowledge is absent, and why providing background reading before EI instruction partially restores EI benefits.
>

> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Connection
> **Q:** How does cognitive load theory explain the context quality asymmetry in the Matthew Effect — that is, why do stronger readers get more encoding depth per encounter, not just more encounters?
>
> **A:** Strong readers' lexical representations are more automatised (high lexical automaticity), meaning decoding and basic lexical access consume minimal working memory. This frees WM capacity for elaborative processing of novel words — richer inferential context construction, semantic contrasting, and situational elaboration. Weak readers' less automatised decoding consumes more WM, leaving insufficient capacity for elaborative processing. The Matthew Effect compounds through this WM mechanism: automaticity → spare capacity → deeper encoding → faster acquisition → greater automaticity.
>
