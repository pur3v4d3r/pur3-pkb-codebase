# The Working Memory Homogeneity Assumption

> [!definition] The Working Memory Homogeneity Assumption

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Working Memory Homogeneity Assumption
> **The assumption**: Working memory capacity is sufficiently similar across adult learners that CLT can generate prescriptions for "learners" as a class rather than for individual learners differentiated by WMC.
>
> **Why it's hidden**: CLT research typically uses group means. Individual variation is treated as error variance. The theoretical framework is built around "working memory" as a unitary, approximately equivalent resource across learners.
>
> **What happens if it's wrong**: [[working-memory-capacity]] research (Daneman & Carpenter, Conway et al.) establishes that WMC varies substantially across individuals and is strongly predictive of learning outcomes — correlations with academic achievement are among the largest in cognitive psychology. If WMC varies by a factor of two or more across the learner population, then optimal cognitive load is different for different learners — and a design prescription optimized for the group mean may be suboptimal or even counterproductive for learners at the tails of the distribution.
>
> **Assessment**: This assumption fails. The magnitude of individual WMC differences is incompatible with treating all learners as having equivalent cognitive architecture for instructional purposes. The received CLT acknowledges learner differences primarily through the expertise variable — but expertise is a proxy for schema state, not for underlying WMC. Two learners with identical domain expertise may have substantially different WMC and therefore different optimal load profiles. The practical implication is that personalization is not merely desirable in CLT-informed design — it is theoretically required by the premises of the framework itself.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-22_report]]*
