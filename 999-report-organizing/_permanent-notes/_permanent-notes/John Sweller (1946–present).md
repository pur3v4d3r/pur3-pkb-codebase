## Additional Material

> [!person] John Sweller** (1946–present)
> Educational psychologist at the University of New South Wales; originator of Cognitive Load Theory (1988). Sweller's foundational insight came from studying problem-solving in mathematics: students using means-ends analysis performed worse on subsequent transfer tasks than students working through worked examples, because the problem-solving strategy saturated working memory with subgoal management rather than schema formation. From this observation, CLT developed.
>
> **Core contribution to this report:** The three-load framework; the worked-example effect; the redundancy effect; the expertise-reversal reappraisal (with Kalyuga, 2019).
>
> **Key works:** Sweller, J. (1988). *Cognitive load during problem solving*. *Cognitive Science, 12*(2), 257–285. Sweller, J., van Merriënboer, J. J. G., & Paas, F. (1998). *Cognitive architecture and instructional design*. *Educational Psychology Review, 10*, 251–296. Sweller, J., van Merriënboer, J. J. G., & Paas, F. (2019). Cognitive architecture and instructional design: 20 years later. *Educational Psychology Review, 31*, 261–292.
> *— [[pkm-and-cognitive-load-foundational-report-2026-04-05_report]]*
