# Autonomy Support

> [!definition] Autonomy Support
> [**Autonomy-Support-Definition**:: An interpersonal style characterized by acknowledging others' perspectives, providing meaningful rationale for requests, offering genuine choice, using non-controlling language, and minimizing coercive pressure. In CET, the autonomy-supportive style of authority figures (teachers, managers, parents, healthcare providers) moderates the functional significance of external events: the same reward, deadline, or evaluation carries less controlling significance and more informational significance when delivered within an autonomy-supportive interpersonal context. Contrasted with controlling interpersonal style, which emphasizes compliance, surveillance, comparison, and pressure.]
> *See also: [[autonomy-support]], [[autonomy-supportive-teaching-and-learning-environments]], [[motivational-interviewing]]*

> Environmental provision of conditions that support a person's self-determined functioning — specifically: (1) providing meaningful rationales for required activities; (2) acknowledging the person's feelings and perspective, including negative ones; (3) offering choice where choice is genuine; (4) minimizing external pressure, surveillance, and contingent regard. Autonomy support is compatible with high expectations and firm limits; it is not permissiveness. The hallmark is that the person experiences the locus of causality as internal even when the behavior is initially required.

> [!evidence] Autonomy Support
> An interpersonal style characterised by taking the other person's inner motivational perspective as the reference point for interactions — acknowledging their feelings and perspectives (including negative ones), providing meaningful and non-manipulative rationale for requests, using informational rather than controlling language, and minimising unnecessary pressure and surveillance. Autonomy support is NOT equivalent to permissiveness: it is compatible with, and indeed requires, clear structure and high standards. The key is that standards and structure are implemented in ways that preserve the person's sense of being the origin of their behaviour. The opposite construct is the **controlling style** — pressure, surveillance, contingent rewards, ego-involving comparisons, and the dismissal of internal states.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*

> [!evidence] Autonomy Support
> [[autonomy-support]] in SDT refers to an interpersonal climate characterized by: (1) perspective-taking — working to understand and acknowledge the other's internal frame of reference; (2) providing meaningful rationales for guidelines and constraints; (3) using informational rather than pressuring language; (4) acknowledging and accepting negative affect and ambivalence; and (5) nurturing interest and investment in activities. Autonomy-supportive contexts satisfy all three basic psychological needs simultaneously — supporting volitional engagement (autonomy), providing appropriate challenge and informational feedback (competence), and expressing care and investment in the person (relatedness).
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01 (1)]]*
