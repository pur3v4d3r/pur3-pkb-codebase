## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Competence Problem in Practice
> Imagine a learner who has decided to teach herself statistics through online resources. She diagnoses her needs (she doesn't understand regression), identifies resources (a textbook chapter, some YouTube videos), implements a learning strategy (reading and note-taking), and evaluates her outcomes (she feels she understands the material). But consider: if she doesn't already understand regression, how can she accurately assess whether her new understanding is correct? She may have learned a useful simplification that will mislead her in subsequent applications, without any signal that her learning has gone wrong. This is the competence problem. Now consider: how would a professional colleague, a course with problem sets and feedback, or a community of practice mitigate this problem? This thought experiment illuminates why SDL is not equivalent to isolated, unsupported learning — and why the design of feedback loops is crucial.
> *— [[self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*
