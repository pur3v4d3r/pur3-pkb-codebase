> [!flashcard] Seed 6
> **Q:** What does the "regulatory mode primacy thesis" claim?
> **A:** That the approach-avoidance distinction is the primary axis of motivational architecture in achievement contexts — more theoretically fundamental than the mastery-performance distinction for predicting the stability and directionality of motivational consequences. The mastery-performance axis modulates the magnitude of approach-avoidance effects but does not generate the asymmetry. Evidence: PAV is robustly maladaptive; MAP is robustly adaptive; MAV (avoidance + mastery) is less maladaptive than PAV; PAP (approach + performance) is less maladaptive than PAV.
> **Source:** Phase VI, Synthesis
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Definition

> **Q:** How does the controllability dimension function as a moral judgment mechanism in social attribution contexts?
> **A:** Perceived controllability of another person's negative condition automatically triggers moral evaluation — reduced sympathy and increased anger — because it generates a judgment that the person is responsible and therefore less deserving of help. The causal description and the moral judgment are produced in a single cognitive act.

> [!flashcard] Seed 6
> **Q:** What does the Nelson-Narens monitoring-control framework contribute to understanding why formative assessment works?
> **A:** It provides the architectural account of how external feedback can fail to influence behavior even when it is accurate. The meta level monitors the object level and regulates it based on monitoring signals. If the meta level's monitoring signals are inaccurate (miscalibrated), control will be miscalibrated even if external information is provided, because the meta level filters external information through its current monitoring model.
> **Source:** Phase II, Nelson-Narens framework; Phase IV, monitoring-control loop
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Process
> **Tags:** #nelson-narens, #monitoring-control, #metacognitive-architecture

> [!flashcard] Seed 6
> **Q:** What is the hedonic adaptation problem in habit maintenance, and what does it imply for reward-based habit formation strategies?
> **A:** The dopamine prediction error system responds to unexpected (not expected) rewards; fully anticipated rewards generate no strengthening signal. As a habit forms and its reward becomes expected, dopaminergic reinforcement diminishes — which is why habits feel unrewarding once established. Implication: reward-based strategies drive early formation but cannot sustain long-term maintenance. The formed habit does not need reward to execute; it needs only the cue.
> **Source:** Phase IV (hedonic adaptation)
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Process
> **Tags:** #hedonic-adaptation, #dopamine, #reward
> *— [[habit-formation-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*

> [!flashcard] Seed 6
> **Q:** Why is positive ability vocabulary ("I'm naturally good at this") also motivationally problematic, according to the Self-Efficacy Calibration Mechanism?
> **A:** Because it encodes efficacy as stable and uncontrollable — just like negative ability vocabulary, only in the positive direction. This produces efficacy fragility: when difficulty arrives (inevitable in academic learning), there is no strategy-and-effort vocabulary available to interpret the experience except as evidence that the original ability attribution was wrong. Positive ability vocabulary reduces strategic investment and creates brittle confidence.
> **Source:** Phase IV, Mechanism 2
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Distinction + Application
> **Tags:** #self-efficacy #vocabulary #growth-mindset

> **Q:** What is the vocabulary granularity principle and why does it matter for SRL?
> **A:** Practitioners with fine-grained vocabulary for their performance states have finer regulatory access than those with coarse-grained vocabulary. For SRL readers: a binary vocabulary ("I get it" / "I don't get it") provides only two motivational states; a tiered vocabulary system (full comprehension, partial comprehension with specific gap identified, structural comprehension gap, strategic re-orientation needed) provides graduated motivational states with specific regulatory implications at each level.

> [!flashcard] Seed 6
> **Q:** What is the "Regulatory Reflexivity Problem"?
>
> **A:** The theoretical challenge that arises from treating motivation as a regulated domain: if the regulatory executive monitors and controls motivation, what motivates the regulatory executive? The resolution is that regulation is bootstrapped from stable dispositional tendencies (trait self-efficacy, general mastery orientation) that are themselves products of developmental history — not a fifth unregulated level, but an initially given motivational basis.
>
> **Source:** Phase VI, Original Synthesis
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Concept

> [!flashcard] Seed 6
> **Q:** What is the primary function of analogy in schema construction, and how does this differ from its standard instructional role?
>
> **A:** In schema construction, analogy functions as *temporary structural scaffolding* — lending the target domain organizational structure from the source domain until native schemas can be constructed. This differs from its standard instructional use (explanation — making unfamiliar familiar by comparison). The structural scaffolding function implies the analogy is eventually discarded as native schemas mature; the explanation function does not imply this.
>
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Distinction

> **Q:** What does the Recht & Leslie (1988) baseball study demonstrate about prior knowledge in reading comprehension?
>
> **A:** It demonstrates that topic-relevant prior knowledge predicts situation model quality more strongly than verbal ability. Low-verbal students with high baseball knowledge comprehended a baseball passage significantly better than high-verbal students with low baseball knowledge. This supports the claim that prior knowledge is constitutive of situation model construction — not merely helpful but necessary for executing the inferential operations that bridge the text base to a coherent world-representation.

> [!flashcard] Seed 6
> **Q:** What distinguishes adaptive inference from defensive inference in the Self-Reaction subsystem?
> **A:** Adaptive inference leads to specific, controllable modifications to future strategy and re-engagement with the material. Defensive inference leads to withdrawal, lowered standards, or avoidance — ostensibly for self-protection — but at the cost of learning and self-efficacy. The diagnostic question: does the inference generate a specific, actionable forward plan? If yes, likely adaptive. If it produces withdrawal without a concrete return plan, likely defensive.
> **Source:** Part IV — Self-Reaction; Part VI — The Feedback Loop
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Distinction

> [!flashcard] Seed 6
> **Q:** What is the attribution dimension most critical for determining whether self-efficacy is updated accurately or defensively after failure?
>
> **A:** The stability dimension. Attributing failure to stable causes (ability: "I'm just not good at this") produces self-efficacy decreases that generalize across future occasions. Attributing failure to unstable causes (insufficient effort, ineffective strategy) allows accurate downward recalibration for the specific strategy without catastrophic generalization to overall capability.
>

> [!flashcard] Seed 6
> **Q:** What is the "calibration-dependency principle" and from what dialectical process did it emerge?
>
> **A:** The calibration-dependency principle states that multi-stage feedback loops are only as adaptive as the accuracy of the signals flowing between their stages. A feedback loop can "run" while producing no genuine adaptation if its internal monitoring signals have become systematically inaccurate. It emerged from the convergence of the empirical challenge (monitoring is inaccurate) and the philosophical challenge (cognitive transparency is limited) — both pointed to the same structural vulnerability in the cycle. It is an original analytical contribution of this report, not yet a standard formulation in the SRL literature.
>
> **Source:** Phase V
>

>
