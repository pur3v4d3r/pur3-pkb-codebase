## Open Threads

> [!open-question] Topics for PKB Expansion
> > [!topic-idea] 1. [[Informal-Logic]] and Argumentation Theory
> > **Connection:** This report addressed formal and informal reasoning patterns but did not deeply explore the field of informal logic — the systematic study of reasoning as it occurs in natural language, including argument mapping, fallacy taxonomy, and argumentation schemes.
> > **Depth potential:** Informal logic provides the practical analytical toolkit that operationalizes many of the CT frameworks discussed. Toulmin's model, Walton's argumentation schemes, and van Eemeren's pragma-dialectical approach each offer distinct contributions.
> > **Priority:** High — directly extends Sections 2-4.
>
> > [!topic-idea] 2. [[Epistemic-Injustice]] and Critical Thinking
> > **Connection:** The report's focus on individual reasoning capacities does not address the social conditions under which critical thinking is enabled or suppressed. Miranda Fricker's concepts of testimonial and hermeneutical injustice reveal how social structures can systematically undermine certain groups' ability to exercise CT.
> > **Depth potential:** Opens the social epistemology dimension of CT; connects to educational equity concerns.
> > **Priority:** High — addresses a significant gap in the individualistic framing.
>
> > [!topic-idea] 3. [[debiasing]] Strategies and Cognitive Intervention Science
> > **Connection:** Section 4 catalogued cognitive biases as CT barriers but did not thoroughly explore what works for mitigating them. The debiasing literature — including Lilienfeld et al.'s taxonomy, consider-the-opposite strategies, and Gigerenzer's ecological rationality critiques — maps directly onto CT pedagogy.
> > **Depth potential:** Provides the bridge between cognitive bias identification and practical CT improvement strategies.
> > **Priority:** High — directly actionable.
>
> > [!topic-idea] 4. [[Argumentation-in-Science-Education]]
> > **Connection:** The generality-specificity debate (Section 7) identified science education as a domain where CT integration has been extensively studied. The argument-driven inquiry model, Erduran's epistemic approaches, and NGSS practice standards provide domain-specific CT case studies.
> > **Depth potential:** Illustrates how CT principles are translated into discipline-specific pedagogy.
> > **Priority:** Medium — strong example for transfer discussion.
>
> > [!topic-idea] 5. [[Digital-Literacy]] and CT in the Information Age
> > **Connection:** Section 8 introduced the digital challenges to CT but could not provide the depth treatment these challenges warrant. Media literacy frameworks, lateral reading strategies, SIFT method, and algorithmic literacy represent a rapidly developing intersection.
> > **Depth potential:** Addresses the most pressing contemporary application of CT; rapidly evolving evidence base.
> > **Priority:** High — urgent practical relevance.
>
> > [!topic-idea] 6. [[emotional-intelligence]] and Critical Thinking
> > **Connection:** The relationship between emotion and reasoning — hinted at in the discussion of motivated reasoning and dispositions — merits deeper exploration. The "emotion as information" perspective, affect heuristic, and the role of emotional regulation in sustaining CT effort all represent underdeveloped connections.
> > **Depth potential:** Addresses the affective dimension of CT that is acknowledged but undertheorized in most frameworks.
> > **Priority:** Medium — growing empirical base.
> *— [[critical-thinking-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*
