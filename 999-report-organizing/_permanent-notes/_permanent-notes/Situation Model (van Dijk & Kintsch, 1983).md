# Situation Model (van Dijk & Kintsch, 1983)

> [!definition] Situation Model (van Dijk & Kintsch, 1983)
> The reader's integrated mental representation of the state of affairs described by a text — the "mental simulation" of the text's referential world, built from textual propositions enriched by inference and prior knowledge beyond what the text explicitly states. Distinguished from the [[textbase]] (propositional representation of explicit text content) and the surface code (verbatim textual form). Situation model construction is the defining criterion of deep reading comprehension.
>
> **Boundary:** A reader can construct an accurate textbase without constructing an adequate situation model — surface paraphrasing is possible without genuine comprehension. The situation model level is where transfer to new problems occurs or fails to occur.
>
> **Historical Development:** The concept was developed in van Dijk & Kintsch's (1983) *Strategies of Discourse Comprehension* as the "knowledge contribution" level of text representation; it was elaborated by Zwaan & Radvansky (1998) into a dimensional model specifying time, space, causality, intentionality, and protagonist.
>
> **See also:** [[situation-model]], [[situation-models-in-text-comprehension]], [[textbase]], [[inferential-bridging]], [[mental-model]]

> [!evidence] Situation Model (van Dijk & Kintsch, 1983)
> A situation model is a multi-dimensional mental representation of the referential content of a text — the entities, events, processes, and situations described — constructed by integrating textual information with prior world knowledge. It is distinguished from the textbase in that it represents the *world the text is about*, not the text itself.
>
> **Boundary:** Situation model construction is not automatic; it requires sufficient prior knowledge, processing resources, and inferential effort. In low prior knowledge conditions or high cognitive load conditions, readers default to textbase-level processing or lower.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Deep motivated reading is functionally equivalent to successful situation model construction sustained across an extended text. Motivation is motivationally significant here because sustained situation model construction is effortful, and effort requires motivational resources.
>

> [!evidence] Situation Model (van Dijk & Kintsch, 1983)
> A [[situation-model]] is the reader's mental representation of the state of affairs described in a text — distinct from the surface form of the text (verbatim wording) and from the propositional text base (the semantic content of individual sentences). A Situation Model integrates textual information with the reader's prior knowledge, inferences, and world understanding to form a rich mental simulation of what the text is *about*. Critical analysis depends on Situation Model construction, not merely text base processing.

> [!evidence] Situation Model (van Dijk & Kintsch, 1983)
> A multidimensional mental simulation of the situation, event sequence, or abstract domain described in a text. The situation model represents what the text is *about* — the referents, causal structures, agents, goals, and mechanisms of the described world — rather than the text's propositional content. It integrates text-derived propositions with prior world knowledge through elaborative inference generation.
>
> **Boundary:** Not identical to prior knowledge, not identical to the text's propositions. A newly constructed, text-specific representation synthesizing both. Two readers with different prior knowledge will construct different situation models from the same text.
>
> **Common Misconception:** That comprehension is demonstrated by ability to paraphrase the text's content. Paraphrase demonstrates textbase access; situation model quality is indexed by the ability to correctly answer inference questions about content the text did not explicitly state.
>
> **Research Status:** Among the most extensively validated constructs in cognitive psychology; supported by reading time, recall, recognition, inference probing, and neuroimaging paradigms.
>
