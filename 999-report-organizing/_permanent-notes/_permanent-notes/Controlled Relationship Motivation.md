# Controlled Relationship Motivation

> [!definition] Controlled Relationship Motivation
> The motivational quality in which a person maintains a close relationship due to external pressure, introjected guilt or obligation, fear of aloneness, or excessive sunk cost reasoning. Controlled motivation is NOT equivalent to dislike of the relationship — a person can be highly satisfied with a relationship while maintaining it for controlled reasons. The psychological cost of controlled motivation accrues through resource depletion and reduced autonomous functioning in other domains.

> [!evidence] Controlled Relationship Motivation
> Motivation for engagement with a close relationship driven by internal or external pressures rather than genuine valuing. Four forms are distinguished: (1) fear-based controlled motivation — remaining to avoid feared consequences of leaving; (2) guilt-driven motivation — continuing engagement because departure would produce intolerable guilt; (3) approval-contingent motivation — engaging to secure or maintain partner approval; (4) self-worth contingent motivation — using the relationship to bolster threatened self-esteem. *Contrasts with*: [[autonomous-motivation]]. *See also*: [[introjection]], [[external-regulation]], [[ego-involvement]]
> *— [[relationship-motivation-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*
