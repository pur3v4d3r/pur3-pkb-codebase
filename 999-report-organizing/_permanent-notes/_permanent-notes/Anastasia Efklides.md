## Additional Material

> [!person] Anastasia Efklides
> Greek psychologist (Aristotle University of Thessaloniki). Developed the most theoretically complete account of metacognitive feelings and their role in self-regulated learning. Her MASRL model represents the most ambitious attempt to integrate cognitive, affective, and motivational dimensions of metacognition within a single theoretical architecture.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by Flavell, Nelson-Narens, and the motivation literature (Nicholls, Dweck). Developed specifically in response to the neglect of phenomenological and affective dimensions in earlier cognitive frameworks.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** The treatment of [[metacognitive-experience|Metacognitive Experience]] and [[metacognitive-feelings|Metacognitive Feelings]] in Phases II and IV draws heavily on Efklides's research program.
> *— [[metacognition-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Anastasia Efklides
> Cognitive and educational psychologist at the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki. Efklides' contributions to metacognition research are particularly focused on metacognitive experiences as a phenomenological component of the metacognitive system — see especially "Metacognition and affect: What can metacognitive experiences tell us about the learning process?" (2006, *Educational Research Review*) and the comprehensive MASRL model (2011).
>
> **Lineage:** Draws on Flavell's metacognitive experiences construct; extends Nelson-Narens in a phenomenological direction; connects to motivational research through SDT and achievement goal frameworks.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the theoretical basis for treating metacognitive feelings as informational and motivational signals, which is essential for understanding why monitoring signals do not automatically translate into adaptive control — see Phase IV.
> *— [[metacognitive-regulation-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Anastasia Efklides
> Cognitive and educational psychologist, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki. Efklides' MASRL model integrates metacognitive experiences — the phenomenological, affectively tinged signals of cognitive processing — as mediating variables between cognition and regulation, and connects metacognitive regulation to motivational theory.
>
> **Lineage:** Extends Flavell's metacognitive experiences construct; connects to Pekrun's Control-Value Theory of achievement emotions; intersects with SDT and achievement goal frameworks.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the theoretical basis for the Calibration-Action Gap's motivational interruption mechanism and for the integration of affect and cognition in Phase IV.
> *— [[metacognitive-regulation-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*
