# Dual-Task Interference (Pashler, 1994; Wickens, 2008)

> [!definition] Dual-Task Interference (Pashler, 1994; Wickens, 2008)
> The degradation in performance on one or both tasks when two tasks are performed concurrently, attributable to competition for shared cognitive resources or structural bottlenecks in central processing. The magnitude of interference depends on the degree to which the tasks overlap in their resource demands — tasks that draw on different processing codes, modalities, or stages may show minimal interference, while tasks competing for the same central executive resources show substantial performance costs.
>
> **Boundary:** Dual-task interference is not inevitable. When one or both tasks are sufficiently automatized — requiring minimal central executive involvement — they can proceed in parallel with little or no measurable cost. The critical variable is not the number of tasks but the degree to which they demand controlled, attentional processing.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This concept determines whether walking while reading constitutes a genuine dual-task situation (with resource competition) or merely the concurrent execution of an automatic process (walking) alongside a controlled process (reading).
>
> **See also:** [[Dual-Task-Paradigm]], [[attention]], [[automaticity]], [[central-executive]], [[Divided-Attention]]
