> [!section-summary] Section 3 Key Takeaways
> 1. Metacognition in reading encompasses both metacognitive *knowledge* (declarative, procedural, and conditional knowledge about reading and cognition) and metacognitive *regulation* (real-time monitoring and control processes). Both are trainable, and both are required for skilled academic reading.
> 2. The Nelson-Narens monitoring-control model reveals that monitoring and control are distinct processes — readers can detect comprehension failures (monitoring) without responding adaptively (control failures). Training must target both.
> 3. Comprehension monitoring is the keystone metacognitive process because all other regulatory strategies depend on it. Its accuracy can be improved through specific training techniques including delayed summarization and self-testing.
> 4. Effective regulation requires strategic flexibility — conditional knowledge of which fix-up strategy applies to which type of comprehension failure — not just the passive deployment of re-reading.
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-and-metacognition-for-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 3 Key Takeaways
> 1. **The generation effect is robust**: Generating information — even from minimal cues — consistently produces superior memory retention compared to passive reading of the same information. This effect is replicated across hundreds of studies and represents one of educational psychology's most reliable empirical findings.
> 2. **Multiple generative strategies share a common mechanism**: The generation effect, testing effect, elaborative interrogation, and self-explanation effects all reflect the same underlying principle — that active mental construction of information, requiring search, selection, and integration with prior knowledge, produces fundamentally more durable and transferable memory traces than passive encoding.
> 3. **Note-making, not note-taking**: The note-taking/note-making distinction illustrates the difference between passive transcription (cognitive) and active transformation (generative). Deep learning favors note-making practices that compel reorganization, paraphrase, and connection rather than verbatim record.
> *— [[generative-learning-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 3 Key Takeaways
> - Metacognitive monitoring relies on accessible cues — especially [[fluency-illusion|processing fluency]] — whose validity for predicting actual cognitive performance is limited and context-dependent, generating systematic biases.
> - The most prevalent biases include overconfidence in comprehension (due to [[the-fluency-trap|fluency effects]]), immediate over-delayed JOL bias (due to study-time rather than retrieval-based monitoring), and domain-specific accuracy limitations (the Dunning-Kruger pattern reveals a structural co-developmental constraint).
> - [[comprehension-monitoring|Comprehension monitoring]] failures — particularly failure to detect resolution gaps, inconsistencies, and the illusion of explanatory depth — are among the most consequential and tractable metacognitive limitations in educational contexts.
> - Monitoring is not a single process but a family of overlapping processes at different levels of explicitness; improvements on one dimension do not automatically generalize.
> *— [[metacognitive-development-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 3 Key Takeaways
> 1. The Nelson-Narens model provides the structural architecture: object-level processes are monitored by the meta-level, which then exercises control over object-level operations through monitoring → meta-level → control signals.
> 2. Metacognitive monitoring is operationalized through EOL, JOL, FOK, and retrospective confidence judgments, each with characteristic calibration errors and practical implications.
> 3. Metacognitive reflection occupies a higher-order meta-meta position: it operates on the monitoring-control system itself, improving the resolution and calibration of that system over time rather than merely using it.
> 4. The monitoring-regulation coupling has systematic failure modes (decoupling, low-resolution monitoring, monitoring delay) that metacognitive reflection can diagnose and address.
> *— [[metacognitive-reflection-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 3 Key Takeaways
> 1. The Nelson-Narens framework organizes metacognition into monitoring (upward information flow from object level to meta level) and control (downward information flow implementing regulatory changes), with all metacognitive phenomena fitting within this architecture.
> 2. Specific monitoring processes — ease of learning judgments, judgments of learning, feelings of knowing, and confidence judgments — each have characteristic biases and accuracy profiles that have direct practical implications for study strategy design.
> 3. The monitoring-control gap — failing to act on correct monitoring information — may be a more common failure mode than monitoring inaccuracy, implicating volitional and motivational dimensions of regulation beyond purely cognitive skill.
> *— [[metacognitive-skills-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

> Autonomous relationship motivation — engaging with connection from genuine valuing and care — is distinguished from controlled forms (fear, guilt, approval-seeking, self-worth contingency) by its stability under challenge and its compatibility with partner autonomy. Controlled relational motivation takes multiple specific forms, each with characteristic psychological signatures and behavioral consequences. Dyadic processes mean that motivational quality in relationships is not simply the sum of two individual motivational profiles but emerges from their interaction — with both supportive spirals (mutual autonomous motivation reinforcing need satisfaction) and undermining spirals (controlled motivation in one partner destabilizing both) as possible outcomes.

> [!section-summary] Section 3 Key Takeaways
> 1. **Schema-driven processing is predictive and top-down.** We do not process familiar material element by element; activated schemas generate expectations and constrain interpretation, making expert processing dramatically more efficient than novice processing.
> 2. **Retrieval is cue-sensitive and context-dependent.** [[encoding-specificity-principle|Encoding specificity]] means the schematic context at encoding constrains how information can be retrieved. A PKB with rich associative link structures functions as a cue-dense retrieval environment that mirrors encoding context.
> 3. **Retrieval practice strengthens schemas; frictionless lookup does not.** A PKB designed exclusively for instant access may be undermining its own schema-building value. Productive difficulty in retrieval serves the schema-strengthening function that passive lookup cannot.
> *— [[schema-externalization-pkb-long-term-memory-foundational-report-2026-04-05_report]]*
