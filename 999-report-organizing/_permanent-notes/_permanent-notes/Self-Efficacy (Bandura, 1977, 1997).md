# Self-Efficacy** *(Bandura, 1977, 1997)*

> [!definition] Self-Efficacy** *(Bandura, 1977, 1997)*
> **Definition:** A person's belief in their capacity to execute the behaviors necessary to produce specific outcomes in a specific domain. Crucially, self-efficacy is *domain-specific* and *task-specific* — high self-efficacy for reading narrative fiction does not transfer to high self-efficacy for reading dense theoretical neuroscience. It is also *calibratable* — well-calibrated self-efficacy (beliefs that accurately reflect actual capability) produces better learning outcomes than either over- or under-estimated self-efficacy.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Self-efficacy for comprehending specific types of complex academic texts must be tracked and protected as part of Forethought design. The scaffolding and template designs in Part IX are explicitly engineered to support accurate, modestly high self-efficacy rather than either inflated confidence or chronic self-doubt.
>
> **Cross-References:** [[self-efficacy]], [[self-efficacy-theory]], [[mastery-experience]], [[albert-bandura]]

> [!evidence] Self-Efficacy** *(Bandura, 1977, 1997)*
> **Definition:** A person's belief in their capacity to execute the behaviors necessary to produce specific outcomes in a specific context. Domain- and task-specific, not a global personality trait. Built through four sources: mastery experiences, vicarious experiences, social persuasion, and physiological/affective state interpretation.
> **Operational Indicators:** Self-efficacy manifests in the level of challenge accepted (high-efficacy learners attempt harder tasks), the persistence shown under difficulty (high-efficacy learners persist longer), and the attributional patterns following failure (high-efficacy learners make controllable attributions).
