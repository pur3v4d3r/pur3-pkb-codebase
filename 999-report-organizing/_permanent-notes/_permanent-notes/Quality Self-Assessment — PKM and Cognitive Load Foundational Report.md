> | **Composite Score** | **8.5/10** | | **PASS** (threshold: 8.0) |
>
> **Identified Limitations:**
> 1. **No empirical PKM-specific evidence.** CLT's application to PKM is principled extrapolation, not validated research. All design recommendations should be treated as evidence-informed hypotheses rather than proven interventions.
> 2. **Framework selectivity.** Of the PKM frameworks, Zettelkasten and PARA receive the most thorough analysis; other significant frameworks (Linking Your Thinking, CommonPlace books, research management tools such as Roam, Logseq, Obsidian) are not examined.
> 3. **Individual difference specification.** The report acknowledges individual differences in WM capacity and prior knowledge but does not provide specific guidance for practitioners with ADHD, dyslexia, or other cognitive profiles that interact with CLT predictions differently.
> 4. **Measurement gap.** The report's recommendations assume practitioners can accurately assess their own cognitive load and expertise stage — assumptions that may not hold without structured metacognitive support.
>
> **Recommendations for Future Revision:**
> - Commission a practitioner's field study: 10–15 PKM practitioners at varying expertise levels complete the Load Phase Audit and implement one protocol change; track outcomes over 3 months.
> - Extend framework analysis to Linking Your Thinking (LYT) methodology and Roam Research's bidirectional link-first architecture.
> - Add a section specifically addressing CLT and PKM for practitioners with ADHD or other attentional differences — high-value underserved topic.
> - Connect to developmental stages research on adult expertise development to strengthen the Three-Stage Model's empirical grounding.
