> The stability dimension is the primary engine through which causal attributions reshape future expectancy. Stable attributions for failure generate expectations of continued failure, creating self-reinforcing cycles exemplified by learned helplessness. Unstable attributions preserve the possibility of different future outcomes, maintaining motivational engagement. The mechanism connects to implicit theories of intelligence (Dweck's entity vs. incremental theories), to self-efficacy formation (Bandura's mastery experience interpretation), and to explanatory style (Seligman's attributional patterns in depression). Stability functions as the temporal horizon of perceived agency — determining not whether one can act, but whether acting matters.

> The phonological loop stores and rehearses sound-based information through a two-subcomponent architecture: a phonological store (approximately 2-second capacity) and an articulatory control process (subvocal rehearsal). Four key behavioral signatures — the phonological similarity effect, the word-length effect, the irrelevant speech effect, and articulatory suppression effects — converge to specify the loop's functional properties and constrain its architecture. The loop is specialized for verbal processing and plays critical roles in language comprehension, foreign vocabulary acquisition, reading, and following verbal instructions. Neuroimaging evidence associates the phonological store with left parietal cortex and the articulatory process with Broca's area, confirming the functional distinctions with neural specificity.

> A chunk is a learned associative unit in long-term memory that is structurally complex in storage but functionally simple in use, allowing one slot in working memory to hold what would otherwise require many. Chunks are organized hierarchically, with higher-order chunks composed of lower-order chunks, and they form not through mere exposure but through attentive engagement with configurations as integrated units. Chunks differ from schemas (which are generalized frameworks) and from automatized procedures (which are compiled action sequences); they are recognition units that schemas and procedures both depend on. The Power Law of Practice describes the empirical signature of the chunk-formation process.
> *— [[chunking-and-expertise-development-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*

> Functional significance is the psychological meaning assigned to external events by experiencing persons, across three primary varieties: informational (competence-affirming), controlling (PLOC-shifting), and amotivating (competence-undermining). The informational/controlling distinction explains the verbal-reward vs. monetary-reward asymmetry. Controlling functional significance can be explicit (reward contingencies) or implicit (surveillance, evaluation pressure, ego-involvement, competitive framing). Amotivating significance operates through a distinct mechanism — helplessness-induction rather than PLOC-shift — requiring different interventions. The concept extends from individual events to the ambient functional character of whole motivational climates.

> Synergistic ecphory, the mechanistic core of the ESP, describes retrieval as a joint product of the stored engram and the retrieval cue — neither alone determines what is remembered. Reviving Semon's concept of ecphory, Tulving formulated a precise account of cue effectiveness: a cue works when its informational content resonates with attributes encoded in the trace. This explains why pre-existing, strong associative relationships fail to produce recall when those associations were not encoded at learning, and why weak-but-encoded relationships succeed. Retrieval is therefore a constructive process, not a readout — the same trace yields different retrieval experiences under different cuing conditions.

> - Implementation intentions work by establishing a cue-behavior link in memory that subsequently operates with three documented properties: enhanced cue detection, accelerated action initiation, and shielding against interference.
