## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technical Detail: The Five Evolutionary Principles of CLT's Cognitive Architecture
> 1. **The Information Store Principle:** [[long-term-memory]] functions as a massive, organized store of information (schemas) analogous to a genome. Learning IS the construction of schemas in LTM.
>
> 2. **The Borrowing and Reorganizing Principle:** Most information in LTM is acquired from other people's LTMs — through imitation, instruction, reading — rather than generated de novo. This is analogous to sexual reproduction transferring genetic information. *The instructional design implication: instruction works primarily by helping learners borrow schemas from experts and reorganize them into the learner's existing knowledge structures.*
>
> 3. **The Randomness as Genesis Principle:** When schemas cannot be borrowed (the information is genuinely novel), the learner must generate it through random generation-and-test — trial and error, exploration, hypothesis testing. This is analogous to genetic mutation. *The implication: problem-solving with novel material is inherently inefficient and imposes maximal element interactivity because the learner has no schemas to chunk the elements.*
>
> 4. **The Narrow Limits of Change Principle:** [[working-memory]] can only process a small amount of novel information at a time. This corresponds to the narrow rate of genetic mutation — large random changes to a complex system are almost always destructive, so change must be incremental. *Element interactivity is the mechanism that translates the narrow limits of change principle into specific predictions about learning outcomes.*
>
> 5. **The Environment Organizing and Linking Principle:** When information has been organized into schemas in LTM, working memory limitations effectively disappear for that information. A schema, regardless of its internal complexity, functions as a single element in working memory. *This is why expertise changes element interactivity: schemas chunk what were previously multiple interacting elements into a single element.*
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*
