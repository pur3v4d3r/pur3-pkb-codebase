# Event Indexing Model (Zwaan, Langston & Graesser, 1995)

> [!definition] Event Indexing Model (Zwaan, Langston & Graesser, 1995)
> The Event Indexing Model proposes that readers construct situation models as multidimensional event spaces, tracking information simultaneously across five situational dimensions: (1) **temporal** — when events occur and how they are ordered; (2) **spatial** — where events occur and how agents and objects are positioned; (3) **causal** — what events causally produce what other events; (4) **intentional** — what goals agents are pursuing and how their actions serve those goals; and (5) **protagonist/entity** — who is present, what their states are, and how their participation changes over the course of the text. Changes in the situation along any dimension signal an event boundary and trigger increased processing effort, indexed empirically by reading time increases at situational transition points.
>
> **Boundary:** The five dimensions were specified for narrative texts. For expository and argumentative texts, interpretive adaptation is required: the "intentional" dimension maps onto authorial argumentative goals and the goals of theorists discussed; the "causal" dimension maps onto explanatory and inferential connections rather than narrative event chains.
>
> **Research Status:** Each dimension has received substantial empirical support through reading time and recognition memory studies. Causal discontinuities produce the largest reading time increases, suggesting the causal dimension is the most cognitively central — consistent with Constructionist Theory's explanation assumption and with the foundational causal network research of Trabasso & van den Broek (1985).
>
