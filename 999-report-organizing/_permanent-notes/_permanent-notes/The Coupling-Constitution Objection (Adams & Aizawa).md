## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Coupling-Constitution Objection (Adams & Aizawa)
> Just because two systems are causally linked does not mean they are parts of the same system. The hammer is causally coupled to the carpenter's arm when she swings it, but we do not conclude that the hammer is part of the carpenter's motor system. Similarly, Otto's notebook is causally coupled to his cognitive processes, but this does not show it is part of his cognitive system. The coupling-constitution inference, Adams and Aizawa argue, is a fallacy. What is needed, and what Clark and Chalmers fail to provide, is a principled account of the **mark of the cognitive** — a criterion distinguishing genuinely cognitive processes from merely causally relevant ones.
> *— [[extended-mind-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*
