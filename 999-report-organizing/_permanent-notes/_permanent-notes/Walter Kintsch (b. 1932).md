## Additional Material

> [!person] Walter Kintsch (b. 1932)
> Austrian-American cognitive psychologist and originator of the Construction-Integration model of discourse comprehension. His decades-long research program at the University of Colorado produced the most comprehensive and empirically validated theory of reading comprehension in cognitive science.
>
> *Core contribution:* The three-level text representation model (surface code → textbase → situation model) and the Construction-Integration computational model that describes how meaning is constructed from text through spreading activation and integration cycles.
>
> *Relationship to other figures:* Extended and formalized insights from Bartlett's schema theory; his situation model concept integrates with Johnson-Laird's mental models; his CI model provides computational grounding for the psychological work of Graesser and Zwaan.
>
> *Key work:* Kintsch (1988); van Dijk & Kintsch (1983)
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-and-metacognition-for-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*

> [!person] Walter Kintsch (b. 1932)
> Cognitive psychologist, University of Colorado at Boulder. Kintsch's Construction-Integration model of discourse comprehension provides the foundational architecture for understanding text representation at multiple levels, distinguishing surface code, textbase, and situation model. This three-level architecture is the theoretical ground on which monitoring-level distinctions are built in this report.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by propositional representation theories; developed the situation model concept with Teun van Dijk; foundational influence on all subsequent reading comprehension research.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** The textbase/situation model distinction in Phase II and the four-layer monitoring architecture in Phase VI derive directly from his theoretical framework.
> *— [[metacognition-and-reading-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Walter Kintsch (b. 1932)
> Austrian-American cognitive psychologist; Professor Emeritus, University of Colorado Boulder. Developed propositional analysis methodology (with van Dijk, 1978), formalized the Construction-Integration model (1988), and produced the comprehensive synthesis *Comprehension: A Paradigm for Cognition* (1998). Among the most influential psycholinguists of the 20th century.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by information processing psychology, cognitive science, and connectionist modeling; influenced Zwaan, van den Broek, and the subsequent generation of discourse psychologists.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** The C-I model is the primary process account of situation model construction throughout this report.
> *— [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29_report]]*
