> [!protocol] Monitoring Accuracy Improvement Protocol
> **Purpose:** To improve the accuracy of metacognitive monitoring during study and knowledge review by providing the monitoring system with more diagnostic cues.
>
> **Steps:**
> 1. **Replace rereading with retrieval practice.** After studying material, close the source and attempt to recall the key concepts, relationships, and mechanisms from memory. The ease or difficulty of retrieval provides more diagnostic monitoring cues than the fluency of rereading.
> 2. **Delay your self-assessment.** Do not judge how well you know material immediately after studying it. Wait at least several minutes (ideally 24 hours) before making a judgment of learning, to allow the misleading cue of current accessibility to dissipate.
> 3. **Generate rather than recognize.** When reviewing notes or flashcards, attempt to produce the answer before viewing it. Generation forces retrieval-based monitoring; recognition allows fluency-based monitoring.
> 4. **Test yourself under exam conditions.** Create practice tests that mimic the retrieval demands of the actual assessment. The monitoring cues generated during practice testing are more diagnostic of exam performance than those generated during passive review.
> 5. **Seek disconfirmation.** After studying, actively look for material that challenges or complicates your understanding. If you can handle the complication, your monitoring signal is confirmed; if you cannot, your monitoring signal needs updating.
> 6. **Track your predictions.** Record your JOLs (predicted recall probability) for specific items, then compare against actual performance. Explicit calibration feedback improves future monitoring accuracy.
> *— [[nelson-narens-metacognitive-control-framework-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
