## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Return-and-Deepen: Attributional Vocabulary After the Challenge Phases
> In Phase II, we defined attributional vocabulary through Weiner's three dimensions (locus, stability, controllability). With the challenge phases now in view, we can see that this three-dimensional framework undersells the constitutive complexity of attributional vocabulary. The *specific vocabulary* used within each dimension carries structural information that the abstract dimension-label conceals. Consider the stability dimension: "I always struggle with dense texts" (highly stable, generalized) vs. "I struggled with this passage" (low stability, specific) vs. "I'm still developing the reading strategies needed for this type of text" (moderate stability but developmental trajectory — the "still" and "developing" encode a trajectory toward mastery). All three encode different stability attributions, but they also encode radically different self-theories, different relationships to [[self-efficacy]], and different activations of the [[forethought-phase]] for future reading tasks. The vocabulary does more motivational work than the abstract dimension can capture.
> *— [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25_report]]*
