## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Expert Debate: The Minimal Guidance Controversy — How Does Element Interactivity Inform the Instructional Guidance Debate?
> **Position A ([[Kirschner,-Sweller-&-Clark|Kirschner, Sweller & Clark]], 2006 — Minimal guidance = minimal learning):** Unguided or minimally guided instructional approaches (discovery learning, problem-based learning, inquiry learning) are ineffective because they impose maximal element interactivity. Without expert guidance (which provides schema-level organization), learners must engage in means-ends search, which creates extraneous element interactivity that overwhelms working memory. Direct instruction provides the schemas that reduce element interactivity.
>
> **Position B (Hmelo-Silver, Duncan & Chinn, 2007 — Guided discovery is not minimal guidance):** Well-designed inquiry and problem-based learning *are* guided — they provide scaffolding that manages element interactivity while preserving learner agency. The Kirschner-Sweller-Clark critique attacks a strawman (unguided discovery) rather than the scaffolded approaches actually used in educational practice.
>
> **What the debate hinges on:** Whether "guidance" is to be defined by the *amount* of element interactivity reduction (the CLT position — more guidance = more interactivity reduction = better for novices) or by the *type* of support (the constructivist position — some support maintains learner agency while managing interactivity, and agency itself promotes schema construction in ways pure interactivity reduction misses).
>
> **Current state:** The debate has evolved beyond its initial 2006-2007 exchange. Most researchers now acknowledge that the question is not "guided vs. unguided" but "how much guidance, of what type, for which learners, on which materials?" — essentially, the expertise × interactivity dynamic from Level 4, but with the added variable of learner agency. The frontier is in specifying how to calibrate guidance to element interactivity while preserving the motivational and metacognitive benefits of learner agency.
>
> **Why the debate matters:** This is the highest-stakes practical application of element interactivity theory. The answer determines how schools, universities, and training programs should be structured.
