> [!person] Philip Winne & Allyson Hadwin
> Simon Fraser University psychologists. Developed the COPES model — the most computationally precise account of metacognitive processing in SRL — emphasizing conditional knowledge, task environment interactions, and the role of metacognitive monitoring as discrepancy detection against task standards.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by Flavell, Brown, and computational cognitive science. Influenced the emerging literature on trace methodologies for studying SRL processes and educational data mining.
> *— [[metacognition-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Philip Winne & Allyson Hadwin
> Educational psychologists at Simon Fraser University. Winne's contributions to metacognition research are particularly focused on the computational modeling of self-regulated learning — see "Studying as self-regulated learning" (Winne, 1995) and "Studying as self-regulated engagement in learning" (Winne & Hadwin, 1998).
>
> **Lineage:** Draws on information-processing models of cognition, Nelson-Narens, and Pintrich; the COPES model is a computational elaboration within the SRL tradition.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the most architecturally elaborate account of metacognitive regulation as a distributed process within the SRL cycle, which informs Phase IV's treatment of the mechanisms through which regulation fails.
> *— [[metacognitive-regulation-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Philip Winne & Allyson Hadwin
> Educational psychologists, Simon Fraser University. Their COPES model (1998) provides the most computationally elaborate account of metacognitive regulation within the SRL tradition, treating regulation as distributed across all phases of learning rather than as a supervisory overlay.
>
> **Lineage:** Draws on information-processing models, Nelson-Narens, and Vygotskian constructivism.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides architectural depth for the understanding of how monitoring and control are embedded within SRL cycles rather than operating as separate control layers.
> *— [[metacognitive-regulation-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Philip Winne & Allyson Hadwin
> Canadian educational psychologists at Simon Fraser University. Developers of the COPES model (Conditions, Operations, Products, Evaluations, Standards) and the most rigorous cognitive-processing account of metacognition in SRL. Their work specifically addresses how learners monitor and regulate their processing during academic tasks.
>
> **Lineage:** Winne builds on Nelson-Narens metacognitive architecture, applied to the academic learning domain. Hadwin's contributions extend the model to account for collaborative and socially shared regulation.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** The COPES model's treatment of resource allocation and cognitive operations directly informs the strategic planning component of the Forethought Protocol.
> *— [[srl-forethought-selfreflection-pkb-report-2026-03-24_report]]*
