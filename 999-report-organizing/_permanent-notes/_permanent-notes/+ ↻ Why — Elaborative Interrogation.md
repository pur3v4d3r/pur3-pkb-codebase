## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] + ↻ Why? — Elaborative Interrogation
> *Why would the quality of beliefs activated before reading determine what kind of learning becomes possible?*
>
> If I accept this claim, I need a causal mechanism. My working hypothesis: beliefs about your capability operate as a *prior* in a Bayesian-ish sense — they shape which interpretations of incoming information feel available to you. If I believe I can't understand dense theory, then when I encounter a confusing paragraph, the interpretation "I don't understand this because it's genuinely hard and requires more careful processing" competes with "I don't understand this because I'm not capable of understanding it." Low self-efficacy priors make the second interpretation more available. That would explain why beliefs constrain the kind of learning that becomes possible — not by changing the text, but by changing what the reader does with difficulty.
> *— [[worked-example-optimal-reading-session_report]]*

> [!evidence] + ↻ Why? — Elaborative Interrogation
> *Why does proximity matter so much for motivation maintenance?*
>
> My hypothesis, constructed before I read the explanation: because the motivational fuel for effortful behavior is feedback. Distal goals provide no feedback for a long time — so the learner is operating on a long feedback delay, which is cognitively and motivationally depleting. Proximal goals provide rapid feedback loops, which generate continuous small mastery experiences — exactly what builds self-efficacy. This also connects to [[deliberate-practice]]: Ericsson emphasizes that deliberate practice requires immediate feedback. Proximal goals create the feedback structure that makes deliberate practice of reading possible.
> *— [[worked-example-optimal-reading-session_report]]*
