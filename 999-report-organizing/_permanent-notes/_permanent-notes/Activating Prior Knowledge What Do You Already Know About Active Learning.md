> [!schema-activation] Activating Prior Knowledge: What Do You Already Know About Active Learning?
> Before engaging with Generative Learning Theory, consider what prior knowledge you already hold that this framework will connect to and extend. If you are familiar with [[constructivism]], you already understand the basic premise that learners construct knowledge rather than receive it. If you have encountered [[levels-of-processing-theory]] by Craik and Lockhart, you know that deeper cognitive processing at encoding yields more durable memory traces. If you have practiced [[elaborative-interrogation]] — asking yourself "why" and "how" questions while studying — you have already engaged in generative processing without necessarily naming it as such.
>
> The guiding question for this report is: **What precisely distinguishes generative cognitive activity from mere mental effort, and under what conditions does that distinction matter for learning?** This question cuts to the heart of one of educational psychology's most productive tensions — between the demonstrable value of cognitive struggle and the equally demonstrated danger of cognitive overload. Your engagement with this report will itself be generative if you actively question, predict, and connect as you read.
>
