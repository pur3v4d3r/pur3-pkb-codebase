> [!methodology-and-sources] Methodology, Epistemic Status, and Intellectual Architecture
> **Traditions Synthesised:**
> This report synthesises three distinct intellectual traditions that rarely speak to each other directly: (1) **Cognitive Science and Educational Psychology** — specifically Cognitive Load Theory, Baddeley's working memory model, and schema theory; (2) **Personal Knowledge Management** — the practices, frameworks, and communities centred on Zettelkasten, PARA, and related approaches; and (3) **Learning Science** — retrieval practice, desirable difficulties, and elaborative encoding research. The synthesis involves interpretive extrapolation: CLT was developed in formal instructional contexts, and its application to self-directed PKM requires reasoned adaptation rather than direct translation.

> **Established Findings vs. Original Contributions:**
> The core CLT findings (worked-example effect, split-attention, expertise-reversal) are robustly replicated. The PKM application of these findings — particularly the PKM Extraneous Load Taxonomy, the Load-Phase Model, the Adaptive Structure Principle, and the Cognitive Priority Principle — represent this report's original theoretical contributions. These contributions are motivated by CLT principles but have not been independently validated. They should be treated as coherent, testable proposals rather than established findings.
>
> **Explicit Limitations:**
> 1. Cognitive load is difficult to measure precisely in authentic PKM contexts remote from tightly controlled laboratory settings. 2. Individual differences in WM capacity, prior knowledge, and metacognitive skill produce substantial variation in CLT predictions. 3. The PKM frameworks evaluated (Zettelkasten, PARA, Progressive Summarization) have been assessed qualitatively through a CLT lens, not empirically.
