> **Q:** What is the structural connection between achievement goal theory and psychological safety in teams?
> **A:** Teams low in psychological safety show behavioral signatures structurally isomorphic to PAV goal dynamics: suppressed error reporting, avoidance of admitting knowledge gaps, reduced interpersonal risk-taking, and surface compliance over genuine engagement. The shared mechanism is self-presentation threat — in both individual learners under PAV goals and team members in low-safety environments, the fear of appearing incompetent activates avoidance regulation that suppresses the exploratory behaviors required for learning and performance. Psychological safety is the team-level equivalent of a mastery-approach motivational climate.
> **Source:** Phase VII, FAR Transfer
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Connection

> **Q:** What is the Calibration-First design principle, and why does it reverse common practice?
> **A:** Calibrate First, Inform Second: generate a calibration event (retrieval attempt, prediction, peer explanation) *before* presenting correct answers or elaborated feedback. This reverses common practice, which delivers information and trusts the monitoring system to register it. The reversal is justified by the MCM: information can only do its work when the monitoring system is accurately tracking the gap the information addresses; a calibration event is what repairs monitoring accuracy.
> **Source:** Phase V, design implications; Appendix Protocol
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Type:** Application
> **Tags:** #calibration-first, #assessment-design, #instructional-design

> **Q:** What does "monitoring vocabulary sensitization" mean, and why does it matter for reading comprehension?
> **A:** The process by which goal vocabulary in the forethought phase tunes the monitoring system to track specific comprehension cues during reading. Mastery-oriented goal vocabulary sensitizes monitoring to understanding cues (noticing non-comprehension, noticing inferential gaps); completion-oriented vocabulary sensitizes monitoring to completion cues (page progress, time). Only understanding-cue monitoring enables effective error detection and repair, so forethought goal vocabulary directly determines comprehension quality.
> **Source:** Phase III, AGT Evidence / Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Process + Connection

> **Q:** What is the difference between effort attribution and effort-strategy attribution in growth-oriented inner speech?
> **A:** Pure effort attribution ("I need to try harder") can produce burnout if effort is already high, because it implies that the gap is quantitative (more of the same effort) rather than qualitative (different approach). Effort-strategy attribution ("I need more effort *and* a better strategy") couples the effort dimension with the strategic-controllability dimension, enabling adaptive persistence rather than fixed-path persistence.
> **Source:** Appendix Lexicon — Effort-Strategy Vocabulary

> **Q:** Describe the tension between Pintrich's motivational regulation framework and SDT.
>
> **A:** Pintrich treats deliberate motivational regulation (self-consequating, interest enhancement) as adaptive. SDT suggests that the highest-quality motivation (integrated regulation) develops through internalization, not deliberate management — and that controlled self-regulation may undermine autonomous motivation over time. The resolution: Pintrich's framework best describes the *transitional zone* of regulatory development, where deliberate regulation is needed; SDT describes the endpoint where autonomous motivation is fully internalized.
>
> **Source:** Phase III, Tension; Phase VI, Synthesis
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Connection

> **Q:** What is a Schema Crystallization Event, and what distinguishes pre- from post-crystallization states?
>
> **A:** A crystallization event is the threshold-crossing consolidation at which accumulated pre-schema knowledge elements rapidly organize into a coherent, retrievable schema. Pre-crystallization: elements are individually accessible but not organized as a unit; processing is slow, effortful, context-dependent. Post-crystallization: the schema activates as a whole; processing is automatic, fluent, context-independent.
>
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Definition

> [!flashcard] Seed 7
> **Q:** What is the Text-Base Trap, and why is it "stable"?
>
> **A:** The Text-Base Trap is a stable cognitive equilibrium in which a reader successfully processes the propositional text base, experiences processing fluency, interprets that fluency as comprehension, and therefore generates no monitoring responses that would initiate the additional operations required for situation model construction. It is stable because it produces no negative feedback signal — the reader feels fine, the text is processing smoothly, and there is no phenomenological marker for the absence of a situation model. It persists until an external comprehension test specifically targeting the situation model reveals the gap.
>
> **Source:** Phase V, Reconstructive Synthesis

> **Type:** Process

> [!flashcard] Seed 7
> **Q:** What is the "expertise trap" in relation to goal orientation, and why does it specifically affect advanced learners?
> **A:** The expertise trap is the pattern where intellectually sophisticated learners who have succeeded academically have internalized a performance orientation — their identity is linked to appearing competent. When encountering genuinely difficult material in private learning, this manifests as avoidance of the most challenging sections, surface processing, and resistance to explicit self-evaluation (which makes deficits visible and thus threatening). Advanced learners are *more* susceptible, not less, because they have more identity investment in appearing competent.
> **Source:** Phase IV, Goal Orientation section
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Application

> [!flashcard] Seed 7
> **Q:** How does the deliberate practice framework (Ericsson) serve as a structural analogue for Zimmerman's SRL cycle's phase-transition mechanism?
>
> **A:** Deliberate practice requires (1) tasks designed to produce informative errors, (2) immediate corrective feedback, and (3) structured reflection that modifies subsequent practice design. This is exactly the non-degenerate SRL cycle: informative performance → quality self-reflection with causal attribution → adaptive inference → modified forethought for next practice episode. The claim "10,000 hours produces expertise" is more precisely "10,000 hours of non-degenerate cycles with high-quality cycle closure."
>
> **Source:** Phase VII, FAR Transfer — Deliberate Practice analogue

> **Type:** Connection

> [!flashcard] Seed 7
> **Q:** How does the Winne & Hadwin COPES model differ from the Zimmerman cyclical model in its account of when self-regulation occurs?
>
> **A:** The Zimmerman model treats regulation as proceeding through sequential phases regardless of task conditions. The COPES model treats regulation as conditional and event-driven: regulatory operations are triggered when specific conditions are met (e.g., a discrepancy between current performance and a standard is detected). This makes COPES more specific about *trigger conditions* — it predicts that regulation fails when the learner's comprehension standards are too coarse to detect genuine failures. COPES is better suited to explaining *when* regulation is triggered; Zimmerman is better suited to describing *what* optimal regulation looks like.
>
> **Source:** Phase IV
>
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
>
> **Type:** Distinction
