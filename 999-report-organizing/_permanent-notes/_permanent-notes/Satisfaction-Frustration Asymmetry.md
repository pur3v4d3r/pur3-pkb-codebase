# Satisfaction-Frustration Asymmetry

> [!definition] Satisfaction-Frustration Asymmetry
> The empirically established principle that need frustration and need absence are not points on the same continuum but qualitatively distinct states with different outcome profiles. Operationalized in the BPNSFS, which uses separate subscales for satisfaction and frustration of each need and shows that frustration predicts ill-being and controlled motivation above and beyond what is predicted by low satisfaction alone.
