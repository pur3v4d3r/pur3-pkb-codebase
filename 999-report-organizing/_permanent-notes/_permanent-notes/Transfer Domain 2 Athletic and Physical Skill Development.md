## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Transfer Domain 2: Athletic and Physical Skill Development
> The relationship between goal language and motivational quality maps directly onto sport psychology's well-documented finding that process goals (focusing on technique execution) outperform outcome goals (focusing on competitive results) for both performance and well-being (Kingston & Hardy, 1997). The *structural principle* is that mastery-oriented, process-focused language activates the same attentional and motivational mechanisms in physical as in intellectual domains. An athlete whose goal is "improve my 5K time by 30 seconds" (outcome) is in a different regulatory state from one whose goal is "develop the capacity to maintain efficient form during the final kilometre when fatigue disrupts my cadence" (process, capability-based). The *boundary condition* is that physical domains provide more immediate and unambiguous performance feedback than intellectual domains, potentially reducing the calibration problem that plagues PKM self-assessment.
>
