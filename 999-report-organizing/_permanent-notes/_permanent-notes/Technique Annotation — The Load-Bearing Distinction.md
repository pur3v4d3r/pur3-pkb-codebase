## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technique: Annotation — The Load-Bearing Distinction
> *Why here: The distinction between adaptive and defensive inference is the conceptual hinge of the entire Self-Reflection Phase. Everything in Zone 3 of the protocol depends on understanding this distinction operationally, not just definitionally.*
>
> **Passage flagged:**
> The report contrasts two learners receiving identical self-judgment outputs:
> - *Learner A (Adaptive):* attributes comprehension failure to "insufficient prior knowledge activation, compounded by strategy lapse" → infers specific adjustments for next session.
> - *Learner B (Defensive):* attributes failure to "this material is too advanced for my current level" → infers withdrawal ("I'll revisit it when I'm more prepared").
>
> **Annotation:** The difference is NOT cognitive — both learners have processed the same information. The difference is **attributional**. Learner A's attribution (controllable, unstable, process-based) licenses adaptive inference. Learner B's attribution (stable, dispositional) licenses defensive inference. The report calls defensive inferences "rational errors" — they feel like good judgment. This is the danger.
> *— [[optimal-reading-session-worked-example-2026-03-25_report]]*
