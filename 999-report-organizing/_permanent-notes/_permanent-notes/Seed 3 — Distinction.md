> [!flashcard] Seed 3 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the difference between assimilation and accommodation?
> **A:** Assimilation bends new experience to fit existing schemas (conserving structure); accommodation bends existing schemas to fit new experience that cannot be assimilated (revising structure). Assimilation preserves; accommodation transforms.
> **Source:** Phase II
> **Difficulty:** Basic

> [!flashcard] Seed 3 — Distinction
> **Q:** In the 2×2 achievement goal framework, what distinguishes mastery-approach from performance-approach orientation?
> **A:** Mastery-approach defines success as developing competence and deepening understanding; performance-approach defines success as demonstrating competence relative to others. Mastery-approach is associated with deep processing and intrinsic interest; performance-approach is associated with surface processing but sometimes positive performance outcomes.

> [!flashcard] Seed 3 — Distinction
> **Q:** What distinguishes a Judgment of Learning (JOL) from a Feeling of Knowing (FOK)?
> **A:** A JOL is made after studying an item, estimating future recall probability. A FOK arises specifically after a retrieval failure, estimating whether the unrecalled item could be recognized or retrieved later. JOLs are post-study prospective; FOKs are post-retrieval-failure prospective.
> **Source:** Phase II; Appendix Section 1
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #metacognition, #judgment-of-learning, #feeling-of-knowing, #monitoring

> [!flashcard] Seed 3 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the difference between "quantity-primary" and "depth-primary" accounts of vocabulary acquisition, and which does this report favour?
>
> **A:** The quantity-primary account holds that encounter frequency is the primary driver — sufficient encounters accumulate into acquisition. The depth-primary account holds that processing depth per encounter is the primary driver — deep elaborative processing produces durable encoding even with few encounters. This report favours depth-primary, based on evidence that context quality (which drives depth) is a stronger predictor than encounter frequency when the two are independently manipulated.
>
> **Source:** Phase III, Conceptual Tensions (Appendix C)
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Distinction
> **Tags:** #vocabulary, #levels-of-processing, #incidental-learning

> [!flashcard] Seed 3 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the difference between receptive and productive vocabulary?
> **A:** Receptive = words understood when encountered in reading or listening. Productive = words actively used in writing or speaking. Receptive vocabulary is always larger (typically 2–4×). Reading builds receptive vocabulary primarily; productive vocabulary requires deliberate generation practice.
> **Source:** Phase II, Conceptual Foundations
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Tags:** #vocabulary, #receptive, #productive, #distinction
> *— [[vocabulary-building-reading-foundational-report-2026-03-28_report]]*
