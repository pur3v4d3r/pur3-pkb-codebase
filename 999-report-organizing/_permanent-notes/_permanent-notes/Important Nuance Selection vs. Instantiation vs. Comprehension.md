## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Important Nuance: Selection vs. Instantiation vs. Comprehension
> These three stages are often conflated in casual treatments. The distinction matters:
> - *Schema selection* is about choosing which schema fits the input domain — choosing RESTAURANT vs. LECTURE vs. JOB INTERVIEW
> - *Schema instantiation* is about binding specific values to the selected schema's slots — which restaurant, which lecturer, what topic
> - *Schema-driven comprehension* is the downstream inference and gap-filling work that occurs once the schema is instantiated
>
> Selection can fail (wrong schema activated, producing systematic misinterpretation of the whole input). Instantiation can be partial (some slots filled, others defaulted). Comprehension can be more or less elaborated depending on the reader's goals and available cognitive resources.
>
> **When the distinction matters:** When diagnosing comprehension failure. A student who misunderstands a passage about "nuclear plant biology" (plants as power plants vs. biological plants) has a *selection* failure. A student who understands the domain but fills EXPERT with "faculty" instead of "practitioner" has an *instantiation* issue. A student who follows along accurately but draws no inferences has a *comprehension elaboration* failure. These require different instructional interventions.
> *— [[schema-and-how-they-work-deep-dive-2026-04-08_report]]*
