## Additional Material

> [!person] Lev Vygotsky (1896 – 1934)
> **Core Contribution:** Developed the theoretical account of inner speech as internalized social dialogue, arguing that private speech is the mechanism through which social-evaluative frameworks become individualized and self-regulatory.
>
> **Role in the Dialectical Arc:** Foundational for both the challenge and reconstruction. The Vygotskian account of inner speech as internalized social dialogue explains *why* vocabulary is constitutive (not merely habitual): the evaluative structures encoded in inner speech vocabulary were formed through social interaction and carry social-evaluative architecture.
>
> **Relationship to Other Figures:** Foundational for all subsequent accounts of self-regulation as internalized social function; directly connected to [[zone-of-proximal-development]] as a model of vocabulary development through supported practice.
>
> **Key Works:** *Thought and Language* (1934/1986); *Mind in Society* (1978)
> *— [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25_report]]*
