# Perceived Competence (White, 1959; Deci & Ryan, 1985)

> [!definition] Perceived Competence (White, 1959; Deci & Ryan, 1985)
> Perceived competence refers to the sense that one is capable of producing desired outcomes and avoiding undesired ones within a given domain — the experience of effectiveness and mastery in interaction with the environment. Within CET, perceived competence is a mediating variable: external events that convey positive information about one's capacities (competence-affirming events) tend to enhance intrinsic motivation, while events that convey negative information (competence-disconfirming events) or that undermine the sense of effectiveness tend to diminish it. Critically, the competence-enhancing effect of positive feedback is contingent upon the contextual frame being autonomy-supportive rather than controlling.
>
> **Boundary:** Perceived competence in the CET framework must be distinguished from general [[self-efficacy]] (Bandura), which concerns confidence in one's capacity to execute specific behaviors toward specific outcomes. Perceived competence is a more diffuse satisfaction of a basic psychological need; self-efficacy is domain-specific and outcome-referenced.
>
> **See also:** [[competence-need]], [[self-efficacy]], [[basic-psychological-needs-theory]], [[perceived-competence]], [[mastery-goal]]
