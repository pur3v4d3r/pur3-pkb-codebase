## Additional Material

> [!person] Isabel Beck (b. 1934)
> American reading researcher and educator, most recently at the University of Pittsburgh. Beck is best known for her work on vocabulary instruction, context quality in naturally occurring text, and text comprehension. Her three-tier vocabulary framework and her context quality research are widely used in reading education.
>
> **Key Works (referenced in this report):** Beck, I.L., McKeown, M.G., & McCaslin, E.S. (1983). "All Contexts Are Not Created Equal." *Elementary School Journal*, 83, 177–181. Beck, I.L., McKeown, M.G., & Kucan, L. (2013). *Bringing Words to Life: Robust Vocabulary Instruction*. Guilford Press.
>
> **Lineage:** Beck's context quality work extended earlier observational research on incidental acquisition by providing a fine-grained coding taxonomy for context types. Her three-tier framework (Tier 1: basic words; Tier 2: general academic vocabulary; Tier 3: domain-specific technical vocabulary) provided a practical framework for instructional prioritization.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Beck's context quality taxonomy is the empirical basis for the analysis in Phase III showing that natural text provides directive contexts for a minority of unfamiliar word encounters — one of the most practically important findings in the report.
> *— [[reading-vocabulary-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
