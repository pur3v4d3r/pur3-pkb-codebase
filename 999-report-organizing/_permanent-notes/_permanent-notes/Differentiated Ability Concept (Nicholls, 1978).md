# Differentiated Ability Concept (Nicholls, 1978)

> [!definition] Differentiated Ability Concept (Nicholls, 1978)
> **Definition:** The cognitive developmental achievement, typically consolidated in middle childhood, by which children come to understand that effort and ability are independent causes of performance outcomes, such that high effort required to succeed can imply lower ability relative to others who succeed with less effort. This understanding is the developmental precondition for ego involvement, as it enables the inference that failure implies low ability.
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The differentiated ability concept is the developmental foundation of the approach-avoidance asymmetry in achievement contexts — it is the cognitive prerequisite for the self-protection dynamic that generates PAV goal adoption.
> **Operational Indicators:** Children who have achieved the differentiated concept characteristically interpret effort as potentially ability-threatening when they succeed; those who have not interpret high effort as evidence of high ability.
> **Etymology/Intellectual Lineage:** Coined by [[john-g.-nicholls]] in a series of developmental studies beginning in 1978; grounded in Piagetian cognitive development theory.
> **Cross-References:** [[achievement-goal-theory]], [[ego-involvement]], [[performance-avoidance-goal]]
