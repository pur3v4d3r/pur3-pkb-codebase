## Reflections

> [!reflection] Phase VI Integration Questions
> 1. If CET is correct that motivation is fundamentally an interpretive achievement, what does this imply for contexts where the individual has limited interpretive agency — for example, under extreme scarcity, institutional coercion, or neurological constraint?
> 2. The "motivational thermodynamics" framing suggests that controlling environments impose a hidden motivational tax. How would you design a research study that could directly measure this tax — that is, quantify the motivational capital depleted by controlling contingencies relative to autonomy-supportive ones?
> 3. CET focuses on how environments affect individual motivation. What is the corresponding account of how individuals can develop the dispositional capacities to preserve intrinsic motivation even in controlling environments? (This connects to [[causality-orientations-theory]] and the [[self-regulated-learning]] literature.)
> *— [[cognitive-evaluation-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*
