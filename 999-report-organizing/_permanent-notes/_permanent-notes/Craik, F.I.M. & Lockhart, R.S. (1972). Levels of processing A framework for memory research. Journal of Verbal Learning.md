## Methodology & Sources

> [!cite] Craik, F.I.M. & Lockhart, R.S. (1972). Levels of processing: A framework for memory research. *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior*, 11, 671–684.
> The foundational paper establishing depth of processing as the primary determinant of memory durability. Proposed that "depth" corresponds to the degree of semantic-elaborative processing — a claim that directly grounds the report's account of why incidental vocabulary encounters produce shallow traces. Required reading for understanding the mechanism-level argument.
>
> **Recommended Sections:** The original processing levels taxonomy; the experimental evidence for depth effects on retention.
>
> **Historical Significance:** Challenged the dominant structural (multi-store) memory models of the 1960s by showing that the *process* of encoding, not the *store* it entered, determined durability.
