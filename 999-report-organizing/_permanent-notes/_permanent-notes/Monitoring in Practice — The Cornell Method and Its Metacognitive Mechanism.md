## Examples

> [!example] Monitoring in Practice — The Cornell Method and Its Metacognitive Mechanism
> The Cornell Note-Taking System, widely but often superficially applied, contains within it a specific metacognitive monitoring structure: the "recitation" phase requires the learner to cover their notes and attempt to recall the main ideas by reading only their cue keywords without seeing the actual notes. This is precisely an implementation of delayed JOL: the retrieval attempt generated by putting a finger over one's notes reveals what has actually been learned versus what is merely present on paper. Students who apply the Cornell method *mechanically* (writing notes but skipping the recitation step) miss the metacognitive mechanism that makes the method valuable; students who understand *why* the recitation step matters are equipped to generalize the principle to contexts without the Cornell scaffold. This is a microcosm of the general principle that effective metacognitive instruction requires not just behavioral protocols but the conceptual understanding of monitoring rationales.
> *— [[metacognitive-skills-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*
