> [!schema-activation] Prior Knowledge Bridge — What You Already Know About Thinking About Thinking
> Before entering this report, activate your existing knowledge. You have almost certainly engaged in metacognitive reflection without naming it as such. When you finish reading a chapter and ask yourself, "Did I actually understand that, or do I just think I did?" — that is metacognitive reflection. When you review a decision and ask yourself, "What was I thinking? What did I miss?" — that is metacognitive reflection. When a student notices, "I always run out of time on exams because I spend too long on easy questions," and changes their strategy — that is metacognitive reflection producing adaptive action.
>
> This report builds from those intuitions toward a systematic, evidence-based account. It connects to your existing knowledge of [[metacognition]] (the broader construct of knowing about and regulating one's own cognition), [[self-regulated-learning]] (how learners orchestrate their cognitive, motivational, and behavioral resources), and [[critical-thinking]] (how reflective evaluation of reasoning processes improves the quality of thought). You may also have encountered [[reflective-practice]] in professional development contexts, or the concept of [[growth-mindset]] — both of which depend on metacognitive reflection as a core mechanism.
>
> **Guiding Question for this report:** *What is the precise nature and mechanism of metacognitive reflection — as distinct from mere self-reflection or metacognitive monitoring — and what do we know about how to cultivate it effectively?*
> *— [[metacognitive-reflection-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*
