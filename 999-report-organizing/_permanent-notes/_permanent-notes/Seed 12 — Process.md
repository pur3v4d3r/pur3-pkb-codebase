## Flashcards

> [!flashcard] Seed 12 — Process
> **Q:** In the PKB context, what distinguishes a note produced through EI (a "synthesis artifact") from a transcription note? Why does this matter?
> **A:** A transcription note records what the source said; a synthesis artifact (EI note) records *why the source's claim is true*, connecting the new proposition to existing PKB nodes through generated causal accounts. The difference is epistemic: synthesis artifacts are integrated into the knowledge graph (with wiki-links to activated prior knowledge nodes), while transcription artifacts are isolated. EI notes add both a memory advantage (networked encoding) and a knowledge graph advantage (new edges between existing nodes).
> **Source:** Phase V, Appendix 6
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #pkb, #elaborative-interrogation, #synthesis

> [!flashcard] Seed 12 — Process
> **Q:** What is the cortical-to-striatal shift in habit formation, and why does it explain the persistence of established habits?
> **A:** With repeated practice, behavioral control shifts from cortical systems (prefrontal cortex, hippocampus) to the dorsal striatum (basal ganglia). The striatal circuit encodes the cue-routine association directly, without requiring prefrontal oversight. Because the striatal circuit is never truly erased — only suppressed by new learning — established habits remain dormant and can be reactivated by exposure to original cue contexts, explaining relapse even after long abstinence.
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #neuroscience, #habit-persistence, #relapse, #cortico-striatal

> [!flashcard] Seed 12 — Process
> **Q:** What is the regulatory reflexivity problem?
> **A:** If metacognitive regulation is itself a cognitive process, it is subject to monitoring and control — which would require a higher-level regulatory process, and so on in an apparent regress. Practically, the regress terminates empirically (at some level, regulation operates without further oversight), but the principled location of this termination is unresolved. The problem has practical manifestations: excessive self-monitoring of regulatory processes can produce regulatory anxiety that impairs rather than aids performance.
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #regulatory-reflexivity-problem, #metacognitive-regulation, #metacognition
