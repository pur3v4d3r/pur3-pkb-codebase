# The Hidden Assumption: Vocabulary-Reading Correlation Reflects Reading Causing Vocabulary Growth

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Hidden Assumption: Vocabulary-Reading Correlation Reflects Reading Causing Vocabulary Growth
> **The assumption**: The strong positive correlation between reading amount and vocabulary size reflects reading as the cause, building vocabulary as the effect.
>
> **Why it's hidden**: The temporal logic seems obvious: you read first, then you learn words. The directionality is assumed to run from input to acquisition.
>
> **What happens if it's wrong**: If vocabulary size partly *enables* reading (as the threshold research confirms it does), then the correlation is partly explained by a reverse path: those with large vocabularies read more because reading is more pleasurable and less effortful for them. The correlation reflects a self-reinforcing system, not a unidirectional cause-effect chain.
>
> **Assessment**: This assumption fails in its unidirectional form. The evidence for bidirectional causality is strong (Cunningham & Stanovich, 1997; Stanovich, 1986). The practical implication: recommending extensive reading as the *primary* vocabulary-building strategy for low-vocabulary readers is analogous to telling someone who can't afford gym equipment to get fit by going to the gym — the strategy works only once the relevant precondition is already in place.
> *— [[vocabulary-building-reading-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-28_report]]*
