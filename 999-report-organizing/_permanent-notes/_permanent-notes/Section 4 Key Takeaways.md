> [!section-summary] Section 4 Key Takeaways
> 1. Academic texts constitute a distinctive genre family characterized by expository structure, conceptual density, specialized vocabulary, and discipline-specific epistemic norms — each of which imposes specific comprehension demands not addressed by general reading skill development.
> 2. Text structure knowledge — awareness of the rhetorical conventions of academic text types — is a form of genre schema that directly facilitates comprehension efficiency and should be explicitly taught rather than assumed.
> 3. Disciplinary literacy goes beyond general reading ability to include knowledge of how claims are made, substantiated, and challenged in a specific academic field — a form of epistemic knowledge with major effects on comprehension depth.
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-and-metacognition-for-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 4 Key Takeaways
> 1. **Strategy taxonomy**: Generative learning strategies include summarization, concept mapping, elaborative questioning, self-explanation, drawing/diagramming, and analogical generation. Each engages all three SOI processes and produces learning benefits beyond passive exposure.
> 2. **Generating beats receiving**: In virtually every studied format, learner-generated representations (summaries, drawings, diagrams, analogies, explanations) produce superior learning compared to learner-received versions of the same representations. The generative act is the mechanism.
> 3. **ICAP hierarchy**: Chi and Wylie's ICAP framework provides a complementary taxonomy organizing activities by generativity level (Interactive > Constructive > Active > Passive), supporting GLT's claim that higher generativity produces better outcomes while specifying the activities that instantiate each level.
> 4. **Analogical generation is undervalued**: Actively generating analogies between new and familiar domains is one of GLT's most powerful but least systematically taught strategies. It requires and trains the capacity to extract structural patterns — a high-level thinking skill with broad transfer value.
> *— [[generative-learning-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 4 Key Takeaways
> - Metacognitive control encompasses initiation, continuation, termination, and re-selection operations that translate monitoring signals into behavioral adjustment.
> - The monitoring-control coupling is a critical but fragile mechanism: monitoring and control can fail independently, and failures at the interface between them (the detection-correction gap, control-without-monitoring, automaticity-induced monitoring suppression) are among the most consequential metacognitive limitations.
> - [[executive-function|Executive function]] provides the enabling architecture for metacognitive control but does not fully determine its quality; conditional knowledge and motivational context are equally important determinants.
> - Motivational architecture systematically shapes how monitoring signals are interpreted and whether control responses are initiated — metacognitive control is not motivationally neutral.
> *— [[metacognitive-development-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

Metacognitive reflection integrates three components: metacognitive knowledge (the knowledge base available for evaluating cognition), metacognitive regulation (the executive processes that implement adaptive responses), and metacognitive experience (the phenomenological signals that motivate and inform reflection).
> 2. Metacognitive knowledge has three sub-types — declarative (what), procedural (how), conditional (when/why) — and can be inaccurate; inaccurate MK actively distorts reflection.

> 4. Metacognitive experiences are both essential and hazardous: they provide the primary phenomenological signals but are susceptible to systematic distortion through fluency illusions.

> [!section-summary] Section 4 Key Takeaways
> 1. Metacognitive knowledge is organized into three subcategories: person knowledge (about self and others as cognitive beings), task knowledge (about how task features influence demands), and strategy knowledge (about when and why strategies are effective).
> 2. Paris's declarative-procedural-conditional taxonomy extends Flavell's framework: declarative knowledge (knowing what), procedural knowledge (knowing how), and conditional knowledge (knowing when and why) must all be developed for metacognitive skill to be fully enacted.
> 3. The strategy knowledge gap — students' systematic overestimation of low-efficacy strategies (rereading, highlighting) and underestimation of high-efficacy strategies (retrieval practice, distributed practice) — is one of the most consequential documented failures of metacognitive knowledge, and one of the most directly addressable through explicit instruction.
> *— [[metacognitive-skills-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 4 Key Takeaways
> Perceived partner responsiveness — comprised of perceived understanding, validation, and caring directed at the authentic self — is RMT's central mechanism. It directly satisfies the relatedness need and creates the relational conditions under which autonomous relationship motivation is developed and maintained. PPR is meaningfully distinct from mere approval, which responds to managed self-presentations rather than to the authentic self. Responsiveness asymmetries within dyads create predictable patterns of motivated distress and relational dysfunction. The disclosure-responsiveness loop provides a dynamic process account of how intimate relationships can either progressively deepen (when the loop is intact) or deteriorate (when it is disrupted).
> *— [[relationship-motivation-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 4 Key Takeaways
> 1. **[[working-memory]] is a limited processing system (~4 active chunks) that schemas expand through compression.** The effective working memory capacity for any domain depends on the richness of available schemas — schemas allow more information to be handled in fewer chunks.
> 2. **[[cognitive-load-theory]] reveals that learning is schema construction under capacity constraints.** A PKB that reduces extraneous cognitive load enables more resources for schema-constructive processing — but only if the user is actually engaged in schema construction, not just retrieval.
> 3. **Optimal PKB design changes with expertise level.** The expertise reversal effect means that PKBs designed to scaffold novices can impede experts. Adaptive design responsive to the user's developing schema sophistication is theoretically required, practically challenging.
> *— [[schema-externalization-pkb-long-term-memory-foundational-report-2026-04-05_report]]*
