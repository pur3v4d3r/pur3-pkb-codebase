## Additional Material

> [!schema-activation] Prior Knowledge Bridge: What You Already Know About Reading and Motivation
> Before entering this report, consider what you already know that bears directly on motivated academic reading. You have direct experience with [[intrinsic-motivation]] and with its collapse under pressure or threat — recall a book you read voraciously versus a textbook you avoided. You likely know something about [[metacognition]] — the experience of realizing mid-paragraph that you have been "reading" without comprehending. You have encountered the concepts of [[growth-mindset]] and [[self-efficacy]], which bear directly on how readers interpret difficulty. You may know Csikszentmihalyi's [[flow-theory]], which describes the absorption state deep reading can produce.
>
> This report will connect these familiar nodes to a richer network. The framework being built here positions motivated academic reading not as a personality trait or a simple habit, but as an *emergent property* — something that arises from the right configuration of cognitive skill, motivational orientation, regulatory flexibility, and environmental structure. The guiding question to carry through the report: **What must be true at the cognitive, motivational, and environmental levels simultaneously for a reader to sustain genuinely engaged deep reading over time, and how do evidence-based strategies address each level?**
>
> Connect this question to your existing understanding of [[self-regulated-learning]] and the relationship between [[metacognition-and-motivation]] — this report is, in part, an application of those models to the specific domain of academic text engagement.
> *— [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*
