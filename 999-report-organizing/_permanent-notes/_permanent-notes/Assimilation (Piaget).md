# Assimilation (Piaget)

> [!definition] Assimilation (Piaget)
> Assimilation is the cognitive process by which new information or experience is incorporated into existing schemas without requiring modification of those schemas. The organism interprets novel input through the lens of already-available cognitive structures — fitting new data into existing categorical frameworks. Assimilation is the conservative movement in Piaget's adaptive pair: it preserves existing structure by interpreting novelty in familiar terms.
>
> **Boundary:** Assimilation does not mean distortion is absent — it often involves distortion, as when a child calls every four-legged animal a "dog." The process functions normally and adaptively within its scope; it becomes problematic only when it persists in the face of anomalies that require accommodation.
>
> **See also:** [[assimilation]], [[accommodation]], [[assimilation-and-accommodation]], [[equilibration]], [[cognitive-disequilibrium]]

> [!evidence] Assimilation (Piaget)
> Assimilation is the cognitive process of interpreting or incorporating new information by fitting it into an existing schema without altering the schema's fundamental structure. When a child who has learned the schema for "dog" encounters a horse for the first time and calls it a "big dog," that is assimilation — the new stimulus is absorbed into an existing framework. Assimilation is cognitively efficient and is the dominant mode of processing familiar or schema-consistent information. **What assimilation is NOT**: it is not passive reception; the incoming information is actively transformed to fit the schema, not merely stored as-is.
> *— [[schema-theory-and-learning-foundational-report-2026-03-06_report]]*

> [!evidence] Assimilation (Piaget)
> The cognitive process of interpreting new information by incorporating it into an existing schema without altering the schema's fundamental structure. Assimilation is the dominant mode of processing schema-consistent information and enables efficient, fluent comprehension. It can produce distortion when new material is forced into an ill-fitting framework. Boundary: assimilation involves active *transformation* of incoming information to fit the schema, not passive storage.
> *— [[schema-theory-and-learning-foundational-report-2026-03-06_report]]*
