## Open Threads

> [!open-question] Expansion Topics
> This treatment generates several high-value expansion opportunities:
>
> 1. > [!topic-idea] **[[Domain-Specific Critical Thinking Standards]]**
>    Deep exploration of how critical thinking criteria vary by domain (scientific, legal, historical, financial reasoning) and strategies for recognizing domain-specific vs universal standards. Would provide practical "field guides" for critical thinking in specific professional contexts.
>
> 2. > [!topic-idea] **[[Metacognitive Intervention Design]]**
>    Systematic frameworks for designing personal or educational interventions targeting specific metacognitive deficiencies (e.g., improving monitoring accuracy, calibrating confidence, enhancing strategic flexibility). Would operationalize the MAI diagnostic framework into improvement protocols.
>
> 3. > [!topic-idea] **[[Cognitive Bias Mitigation Protocols]]**
>    Comprehensive catalog of cognitive biases with specific detection signatures and correction algorithms—moving beyond awareness ("confirmation bias exists") to operational intervention ("when you notice X, deploy Y corrective procedure").
>
> 4. > [!topic-idea] **[[Transfer-Enabling Pedagogical Frameworks]]**
>    Detailed implementation of Halpern's transfer model and the Infusion approach for educational design—how to structure learning experiences that build transferable critical thinking rather than domain-locked skills.
>
> 5. > [!topic-idea] **[[Epistemic Humility and Intellectual Virtue]]**
>    The dispositional foundations underlying critical thinking deployment—cultivating intellectual character traits (curiosity, open-mindedness, intellectual honesty, fair-mindedness) that motivate effortful reasoning even when cognitive ease is available.
>
> 6. > [!topic-idea] **[[Real-Time Reasoning Quality Monitoring Systems]]**
>    Technology-assisted metacognitive monitoring—using decision journals, argument mapping software, calibration training apps, and other external scaffolds to enhance natural metacognitive limitations.

> [!open-question] Expansion Topics
> > [!topic-idea]
> > **Topic**: Oaksford & Chater's Probabilistic Mental Models
> > **Report Type**: First Principles
> > **Description**: A first-principles inquiry into whether the probabilistic rational analysis framework constitutes a genuinely distinct theory of reasoning or is better understood as a formal implementation of the axioms identified in this report. The investigation would audit the assumption that Bayesian inference is the appropriate normative standard for everyday reasoning and derive what the probabilistic account requires vs. assumes.
> > **Core Concepts**: Rational analysis methodology, prior probabilities in reasoning, Bayesian inference as normativity, the probability heuristics model
> > **Related Topics**: Reasoning norms and naturalism, philosophy of normativity, ecological rationality
>
> > [!topic-idea]
> > **Topic**: The Format of Mental Representation — Empirical and Philosophical Boundaries
> > **Report Type**: Foundational
> > **Description**: A comprehensive landscape map of the analogical-vs-propositional representation debate: its history from the imagery debates of the 1970s through embodied cognition and predictive processing accounts. Would survey the neuroimaging evidence, computational modeling approaches, and philosophical arguments for and against the format distinction that Assumption 1 in this report found insufficiently established.
> > **Core Concepts**: Dual coding theory, perceptual symbol systems (Barsalou), format underdetermination, Pylyshyn's tacit knowledge objection, predictive coding
> > **Related Topics**: Cognitive neuroscience of representation, embodied cognition, philosophy of mind
>
> > [!topic-idea]
> > **Topic**: The Pragmatic Calibration of Everyday Reasoning
> > **Report Type**: First Principles
> > **Description**: A first-principles inquiry into the "pragmatic construction" insight derived in Phase IV — the hypothesis that what looks like deductive failure is often adaptive probabilistic calibration. Would audit the assumption that laboratory deductive tasks measure the same cognitive processes as everyday reasoning, derive what an adequate account of everyday reasoning must explain, and reconstruct from axioms what it means for reasoning to be "good enough."
> > **Core Concepts**: Ecological validity, naturalistic decision making, fast and frugal heuristics (Gigerenzer), bounded rationality, normativity in cognition
> > **Related Topics**: Heuristics and biases tradition, dual process theory, philosophy of rationality
>
> > [!topic-idea]
> > **Topic**: Dual Process Theory
> > **Report Type**: First Principles
> > **Description**: Following from the dissolution of the rule/model dichotomy in this report, a first-principles inquiry into whether System 1/System 2 is itself a theoretically grounded distinction or another artifact of a particular experimental tradition. Would audit the type-1/type-2 binary, the normative/descriptive conflation in dual process research, and reconstruct what evidence actually requires.
> > **Core Concepts**: System 1/System 2, autonomous vs. controlled processes, default-interventionist models, parallel competitive models
> > **Related Topics**: Cognitive control, working memory, metacognition
>
> > [!topic-idea]
> > **Topic**: Reasoning Interventions — What the Evidence Actually Supports
> > **Report Type**: Foundational
> > **Description**: A comprehensive survey of empirically validated interventions for improving reasoning, designed to be read alongside this report. Would inventory training studies, educational interventions, and environmental design approaches, assessing which are format-specific (require assuming mental models or mental logic) and which are format-agnostic (work regardless of the representational debate).
> > **Core Concepts**: Argument mapping, counterexample training, debiasing, informal logic instruction, decision support systems
> > **Related Topics**: Educational psychology, behavioral economics, human factors

> [!open-question] Expansion Topics
> > [!topic-idea] 1. [[self-directed-learning|Self-Directed Learning]] and Pragmatist Foundations
> > - **Connection:** Dewey's concept of growth as the ongoing expansion of inquiry capacity provides the deepest philosophical justification for self-directed learning. The pragmatist learner is inherently self-directing because genuine inquiry cannot be externally mandated.
> > - **Depth Potential:** A foundational report connecting SDL research (Knowles, Tough, Candy) to its largely unacknowledged pragmatist philosophical roots would fill a significant gap in the PKB.
> > - **Knowledge Graph Role:** Bridges [[pragmatism]] → [[self-directed-learning]] → [[heutagogy]] → [[lifelong-learning]]
> > - **Priority:** High
>
> > [!topic-idea] 2. [[Inquiry-Based-Learning-—-Empirical-Evidence-and-Design-Principles|Inquiry-Based Learning — Empirical Evidence and Design Principles]]
> > - **Connection:** IBL is the most direct pedagogical descendant of pragmatist philosophy. A dedicated report would systematically examine the empirical evidence for its effectiveness, the conditions under which it succeeds and fails, and its relationship to [[cognitive-load-theory|cognitive load theory]].
> > - **Depth Potential:** The tension between discovery learning and direct instruction is one of the most contested issues in educational research. A foundational treatment would map the complete evidence landscape.
> > - **Knowledge Graph Role:** Bridges [[pragmatism]] → [[inquiry-based-learning]] → [[cognitive-load-theory]] → [[scaffolding]]
> > - **Priority:** High
>
> > [!topic-idea] 3. [[embodied-cognition|Embodied Cognition]] and Its Pragmatist Roots
> > - **Connection:** The 4E cognition research program represents, in important respects, the cognitive-scientific elaboration of Dewey's organism-environment transaction thesis. A dedicated report would trace this lineage and assess its implications for learning theory.
> > - **Depth Potential:** Embodied cognition is one of the most active research fronts in cognitive science; connecting it explicitly to its pragmatist ancestry would generate productive cross-fertilization.
> > - **Knowledge Graph Role:** Bridges [[pragmatism]] → [[embodied-cognition]] → [[4e-cognition]] → [[Enactive-Cognition]] → [[ecological-psychology]]
> > - **Priority:** Medium
>
> > [!topic-idea] 4. [[reflective-practice|Reflective Practice]] in Professional Education
> > - **Connection:** Schon's reflective practitioner model is the most influential application of Dewey's reflective thinking to professional development. A dedicated report would examine its theoretical foundations, empirical evidence, implementation in various professions, and critiques.
> > - **Depth Potential:** Directly practical for the PKB's interest in metacognition, self-regulation, and lifelong learning.
> > - **Knowledge Graph Role:** Bridges [[Dewey's-Reflective-Thinking]] → [[reflective-practice]] → [[double-loop-learning]] → [[self-directed-learning]]
> > - **Priority:** Medium
>
> > [!topic-idea] 5. [[productive-failure|Productive Failure]] and [[desirable-difficulties|Desirable Difficulties]] — The Pragmatist Case for Struggle
> > - **Connection:** Pragmatism's insistence that genuine learning begins with genuine difficulty provides the philosophical framework for understanding why pedagogical techniques that initially produce failure (Kapur's productive failure, Bjork's desirable difficulties) produce superior long-term learning.
> > - **Depth Potential:** Would integrate findings from cognitive psychology, motivation research, and philosophy of education into a unified framework.
> > - **Knowledge Graph Role:** Bridges [[pragmatism]] → [[productive-failure]] → [[desirable-difficulties]] → [[cognitive-load-theory]] → [[schema-construction]]
> > - **Priority:** Medium
>
> > [!topic-idea] 6. [[democratic-self-governance|Democratic Education]] and Pragmatist Citizenship
> > - **Connection:** Dewey's *Democracy and Education* argues that democracy is not merely a form of government but a mode of associated living organized around shared inquiry. A dedicated report would examine the relationship between pragmatist pedagogy and democratic citizenship, with attention to contemporary challenges of epistemic fragmentation and [[political-polarization|political polarization]].
> > - **Depth Potential:** Highly relevant to current discussions of civic education, media literacy, and the epistemic foundations of democratic participation.
> > - **Knowledge Graph Role:** Bridges [[pragmatism]] → [[democratic-self-governance]] → [[critical-thinking-in-education]] → [[epistemic-citizenship]] → [[deliberative-democracy]]
> > - **Priority:** Exploratory
