# Split-Attention Effect

> [!definition] Split-Attention Effect
> A CLT-derived design principle: when learners must mentally integrate two physically or temporally separated sources of information that are logically interdependent (e.g., a diagram and its text description), cognitive resources are consumed by the integration process rather than by schema formation. Integrating the sources in the instructional design eliminates this extraneous load. The split-attention effect is one of the best-replicated effects in CLT research and survives dialectical scrutiny robustly.

> [!evidence] Split-Attention Effect
> The CLT-derived finding that instructional materials that require learners to mentally integrate spatially or temporally separated information impose extraneous cognitive load. The mental integration of split sources requires holding partial information in working memory while searching for and retrieving the related source, consuming working memory resources that could otherwise be devoted to schema construction. Eliminated by physically integrating related information in the presentation (e.g., labeling diagrams directly rather than providing separate legends).
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-06_report]]*

> [!evidence] Split Attention Effect
> The [[split-attention-effect]] occurs when learners must mentally integrate two or more sources of mutually referring information that are physically or temporally separated. The cognitive effort of mentally integrating these separated sources constitutes extraneous load that does not contribute to learning. Eliminating the split by physically integrating the information sources reduces extraneous load and improves learning.
