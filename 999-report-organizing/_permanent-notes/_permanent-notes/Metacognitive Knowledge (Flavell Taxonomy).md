# Metacognitive Knowledge (Flavell Taxonomy)

> [!definition] Metacognitive Knowledge (Flavell Taxonomy)
> Metacognitive knowledge consists of one's accumulated beliefs and information about cognitive phenomena, organized into three subcategories: (a) *person knowledge* — beliefs about oneself and others as cognitive beings, including stable characteristics, variable characteristics, and universals of human cognition; (b) *task knowledge* — understanding of how features of cognitive tasks influence their difficulty and the demands they place on cognitive resources; and (c) *strategy knowledge* — knowledge about the effectiveness, applicability conditions, and limitations of various cognitive strategies.
>
> **Boundary:** Metacognitive knowledge is relatively stable and constitutes a long-term memory representation; it is distinct from metacognitive experiences, which are transient online events, and from metacognitive regulation, which is the executive deployment of these knowledge structures to manage ongoing cognition.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The Flavell taxonomy provides the framework for Section 4's analysis of how metacognitive knowledge is structured, acquired, updated, and deployed — and why deficits at this level produce characteristic learning failures.
>
