> [!section-summary] Level 4 Summary
> At the substructural level, we saw element interactivity's components. At the dynamics level, we now see how those components produce observable behavior. The master dynamic is the expertise × interactivity interaction: instructional interventions succeed by reducing element interactivity for novices and fail by adding extraneous interactivity for experts. Every named CLT effect is a specific instantiation of this dynamic in a specific instructional context. The effects form a continuous space, not a categorical list. The faded worked example strategy is the paradigmatic dynamic application, tracking element interactivity reduction to schema development. The next level will examine where these dynamics break down — the edge cases where element interactivity's explanatory power encounters its limits.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 4 Summary
> At the previous level, [[Element-Interactivity]] was a learner-relative count. At THIS level, the count becomes *time-varying* through the schema-construction loop — high-interactivity material is processed effortfully now in order to construct schemas that will reduce its element count later, freeing working-memory capacity for material previously out of reach. The dynamics transform CLT from a static container theory into a dynamical systems account of cognitive growth, and they generate the central design problem of modern instructional theory: navigating the optimal trajectory through element-interactivity space. The next level will examine where this elegant story breaks down — at the edge cases that complicate the loop and that have forced the modern theory to reorganize itself in significant ways.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-element-interactivity-deep-dive-2026-04-20]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 4 Summary
> At substructure level, we catalogued the individual cues. At the DYNAMICS level, we've traced how monitoring accuracy propagates through the control loop. The discrepancy reduction model and the region of proximal learning model specify how monitoring outputs drive study-time allocation. Calibration errors affect the total amount of study; resolution errors affect its distribution. The feedback dynamics are critically important: self-correcting loops (with outcome feedback) attenuate errors over time, while self-amplifying loops (without feedback) trap learners in persistent overconfidence. Strategy selection compounds the problem — overconfident monitoring degrades not just study quantity but study quality. The dynamics are nonlinear, with per-item errors compounding across cycles.
>
> **The next level zooms into where these dynamics break down catastrophically:** the systematic failure modes and edge cases.

> [!section-summary] Level 4 Summary
> Need satisfaction and regulatory style are not in a one-way causal relationship but in a continuous reciprocal loop, mediated by the [[functional-significance-informational-controlling-amotivating|functional significance]] interpretation of moment-to-moment events. Each regulatory position constructs the interpretive conditions that tend to sustain it, producing self-stabilizing continuum dynamics that operate across multiple timescales (moment-to-moment, weeks-to-months, years-to-decades). Reversibility of integration is the predictable consequence of this dynamic — integration is sustained equilibrium, not permanent achievement. Skilled application of SDT requires attentiveness to the recipient's interpretive register, which no behavior checklist can substitute for. State and trait need satisfaction operate at different timescales and predict different outcomes.
>
> Level 5 will move to edge cases — configurations where the standard understanding breaks down and where the deepest insights of the framework live.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-internalization-continuum-deep-dive-2026-04-21]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 4 Summary
> At mechanism level, walking appeared to impose a small CE cost. At dynamics level, we now see this is only half the story. Walking simultaneously **modulates the arousal environment** in which reading occurs, through the catecholamine inverted-U, in a way that is beneficial at light intensities and potentially harmful at high intensities. [[transient-hypofrontality]] is not a concern at walking intensities. The net effect depends on the **interaction between task difficulty and exercise intensity**: net positive for moderate-difficulty material at light walking intensity, net negative for high-difficulty material that pushes WM to capacity. The temporal dynamics suggest an optimal protocol involving **cycled walking and sitting periods** to capture both arousal benefits and full CE capacity.
> *— [[walking-academic-reading-working-memory-deep-dive-2026-04-13_report]]*
