## Additional Material

> [!schema-activation] Activating Prior Knowledge: Bridging What You Know
> Before entering this report, consider these connections to frames you likely already carry:
>
> If you are familiar with [[self-determination-theory]], you know its foundational claim: human beings have three innate psychological needs — for [[autonomy-need|autonomy]], [[competence-need|competence]], and [[relatedness-need|relatedness]] — and that the degree to which environments satisfy or frustrate these needs determines the quality of motivation, engagement, and well-being. COT extends this framework by asking: *beyond environmental conditions, what dispositional factors determine whether a person perceives and responds to any given environment as autonomy-supporting or controlling?*
>
> If you've encountered [[locus-of-control]] (Rotter, 1966), you know the distinction between internal and external causal attribution. COT shares conceptual terrain but moves beyond a binary internal/external dimension to a tripartite model that includes the functionally distinct impersonal orientation.
>
> If you've worked with [[achievement-goal-theory]], you're familiar with the idea that goal orientations (mastery vs. performance) shape how students engage with learning. COT operates at a deeper motivational level — it describes the *dispositional filter* through which goal contents and environmental features get interpreted.
>
> **Guiding question for this report:** How does a person's causality orientation function as a motivational affordance system — a lens that selectively amplifies some environmental signals and suppresses others, thereby shaping not just what people do but why they do it and how they feel about doing it?
> *— [[causality-orientations-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*
