# Generation Effect

> [!definition] Generation Effect
> The well-replicated finding that items generated by the learner (from partial cues, incomplete words, or guided prompts) are better remembered than items passively received. The generation effect operationalizes deep, elaborative processing by definition — generating requires construction of the correct response, which demands semantic engagement. The generation effect is one of the strongest empirical demonstrations of the LOP prediction that active, meaning-constructive processing outperforms passive receipt of the same information.
>
> **Boundary:** The generation effect is strongest when generation requires semantic construction (completing meaningful words) and weaker when generation is purely arbitrary (selecting from random letter combinations). The cognitive operation during generation, not generation per se, drives the effect.
>
> **See also:** [[generation-effect]], [[deep-processing]], [[retrieval-practice]], [[elaborative-rehearsal]]

> The empirical finding that information generated by a learner (through production, completion, prediction, or reformulation) is encoded more deeply and retained more durably than information received passively, even when the informational content is equivalent. The effect is robust across materials, populations, and retention intervals.
