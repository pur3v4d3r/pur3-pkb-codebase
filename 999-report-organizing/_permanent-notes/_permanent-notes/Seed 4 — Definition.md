> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Definition
> **Q:** What is introjected regulation in SDT, what is its characteristic inner speech vocabulary, and why is it problematic despite being internal?
> **A:** Introjected regulation is motivation driven by internally administered approval/shame contingencies; characterized by *should, must, have to, ought to* inner speech constructions. It is problematic despite being internal because it produces controlled (non-autonomous) motivation, generating pressure and conditional self-worth rather than genuine endorsement — with the same cognitive and motivational costs as external control (reduced deep processing, lower well-being, reduced intrinsic motivation).
> **Source:** Phase III
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #sdt #introjection #regulatory-language #autonomous-motivation

> **Q:** What is the Zone of Proximal Development?
> **A:** The ZPD is the distance between what a learner can accomplish independently and what the learner can accomplish with guidance from a more capable partner. It represents the zone where learning and development are actively occurring.

> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Definition
> **Q:** What is an implementation intention, and how does it differ from a motivational intention?
> **A:** An implementation intention is an if-then plan specifying *when, where, and how* a behavior will be performed ("If X, then Y"). It differs from a motivational intention ("I intend to do X") by addressing the intention-action gap rather than the motivation-intention gap. It creates prospective memory automation, not higher motivation.
> **Source:** Phase III
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Tags:** #implementation-intentions, #habit-formation, #self-regulated-learning
