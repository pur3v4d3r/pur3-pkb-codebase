## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Clinical Reasoning and Diagnostic Practice
> Clinicians — physicians, psychologists, therapists — engage in a regulatory cycle structurally isomorphic to the SRL model. **Forethought:** Before a patient encounter, the clinician reviews available information, generates differential diagnoses, and plans the examination or session. **Performance:** During the encounter, the clinician monitors for confirmatory and disconfirmatory evidence, adjusting the diagnostic hypothesis in real time. **Reflection:** After the encounter, the clinician reviews the outcome, evaluates the accuracy of the initial hypothesis, and adjusts future practice accordingly. Clinical decision support systems function as metacognitive scaffolds — they prompt consideration of possibilities the clinician might overlook, monitor for cognitive biases (anchoring, confirmation bias), and structure post-encounter review. The Developmental Arc applies directly: novice clinicians require comprehensive scaffolding; experienced clinicians require scaffolding only for unusual presentations; experts scaffold their own reasoning.
>
> **Structural principle:** Externalised monitoring reduces the impact of cognitive biases under the cognitive load of real-time decision-making.
