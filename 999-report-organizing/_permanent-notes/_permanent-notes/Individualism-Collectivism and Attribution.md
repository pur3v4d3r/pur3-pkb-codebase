# Individualism-Collectivism and Attribution

> [!definition] Individualism-Collectivism and Attribution
> **Individualistic** cultural frameworks — common in North American and Western European contexts — conceptualize the person as a discrete, autonomous agent whose behavior primarily reflects internal properties (traits, abilities, intentions, values). This cultural self-model primes dispositional attribution: when a person acts in a particular way, the natural causal search targets the person's internal properties. **Collectivistic** cultural frameworks — more common in East Asian, South Asian, and many African contexts — conceptualize the person as fundamentally embedded in relational, hierarchical, and situational contexts. This cultural self-model primes situational attribution: behavior is more naturally explained by reference to the relational and situational forces acting on the person. The individual-level FAE is thus not a universal architectural feature of human causal reasoning but a culturally amplified default setting.
