# Region of Proximal Learning (Metcalfe, 2002)

> [!definition] Region of Proximal Learning (Metcalfe, 2002)
> The region of proximal learning hypothesis proposes that learners optimally allocate study time to items of intermediate difficulty — items that are learnable with reasonable effort but not yet mastered. This hypothesis refines the Nelson–Narrens control framework by specifying that effective control involves not merely knowing what one does not know but estimating the expected learning gain from further effort.
>
> **Boundary:** The region of proximal learning operates as a normative model of optimal control; actual learner behaviour may deviate from this optimum under conditions of time pressure, motivation deficit, or monitoring failure.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This hypothesis provides the most empirically grounded account of how the control channel implements adaptive study-time allocation.
>
> **See also:** [[Janet-Metcalfe]], [[self-regulated-learning]], [[zone-of-proximal-development]]
