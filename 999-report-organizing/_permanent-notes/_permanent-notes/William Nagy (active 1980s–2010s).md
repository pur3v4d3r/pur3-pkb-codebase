## Additional Material

> [!person] William Nagy (active 1980s–2010s)
> American educational psychologist, formerly at Seattle Pacific University, best known for his large-scale empirical studies on incidental vocabulary acquisition from reading. Nagy's 1985 studies with Herman and Anderson provided the first systematic rate estimates for incidental acquisition and shaped the subsequent two decades of vocabulary research.
>
> **Key Works (referenced in this report):** Nagy, W.E., Herman, P., & Anderson, R.C. (1985). "Learning Words from Context." *Reading Research Quarterly*, 20, 233–253. Nagy, W.E., & Anderson, R.C. (1984). "How Many Words Are There in Printed School English?" *Reading Research Quarterly*, 19, 304–330.
>
> **Lineage:** Nagy worked within the Anderson-Freebody tradition of vocabulary research and created the empirical foundation that Stanovich's Matthew Effect and Nation's coverage threshold would subsequently theorize. His work on morphological families in English vocabulary remains foundational.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Nagy's acquisition rate estimates (5–15% per encounter in natural reading) are the primary empirical data around which Phases III and IV are organized — the finding that creates the puzzle the Cognitive Slack Model resolves.
> *— [[reading-vocabulary-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
