# Self-Regulated Learning (Pintrich, 2000; Zimmerman, 2002)

> [!definition] Self-Regulated Learning (Pintrich, 2000; Zimmerman, 2002)
> Self-regulated learning refers to the active, constructive process by which learners set goals for their learning, monitor their progress, regulate their cognitive strategies and motivational states in response to monitoring feedback, and reflect on outcomes to guide future learning attempts. SRL is distinguished from mere intelligent behavior by its cyclical, metacognitive, and motivational character: it requires not merely doing things that facilitate learning, but monitoring whether those things are working and adapting on the basis of that monitoring.
>
> **Boundary:** SRL models describe a normatively ideal learning cycle but document wide variation in how systematically individuals execute it. Many learners are "locally regulated" (applying regulation in isolated strategies without a coherent meta-level system) rather than "globally regulated" (operating a consistent meta-level practice that oversees the whole learning enterprise). The SRL literature shows that global regulation is learnable but requires explicit training and motivational support.
>
