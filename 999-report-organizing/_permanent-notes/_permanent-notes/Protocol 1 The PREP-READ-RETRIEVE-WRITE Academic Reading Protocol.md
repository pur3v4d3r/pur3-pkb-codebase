> [!protocol] Protocol 1: The PREP-READ-RETRIEVE-WRITE Academic Reading Protocol
> A four-phase protocol for academic texts optimizing generative processing, metacognitive monitoring, and long-term retention:
>
> **Phase 1 — PREP (5-10 min before reading):**
> - Structural preview: examine title, abstract, headings, topic sentences, conclusion
> - Prior knowledge activation: write 3-5 sentences on what you already know about the topic
> - Set comprehension goals: formulate 3 specific questions you want this text to answer
> - Activate appropriate reading mode: this is discursive reading, not narrative reading
>
> **Phase 2 — READ (calibrated to text difficulty):**
> - Deploy section-by-section reading: complete one major section before moving to next
> - Self-explain at causal mechanism points: explain "why" to yourself before reading further
> - Annotate comprehension failures explicitly: mark passages where monitoring signals failure
> - Apply elaborative interrogation to key claims: "Why is this true? What evidence supports it?"
>
> **Phase 3 — RETRIEVE (10-15 min after reading each major section):**
> - Close the text and retrieve everything recalled about the section
> - Write your retrieval (even just notes) to check against text
> - Return to text specifically for items not recalled or recalled inaccurately
> - Answer your three comprehension goal questions
>
> **Phase 4 — WRITE (15-30 min after completing the full text):**
> - Write a synthesis paragraph integrating the text's central argument with prior knowledge
> - Write one question the text raises that it does not answer
> - Write one connection to other texts, ideas, or experiences
> - Note any comprehension failures that remain unresolved and require follow-up
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-and-metacognition-for-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*
