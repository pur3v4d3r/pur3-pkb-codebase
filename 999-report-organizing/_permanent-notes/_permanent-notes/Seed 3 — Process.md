> [!flashcard] Seed 3 — Process
> **Q:** Describe the attribution-efficacy cascade step-by-step, from the trigger event (comprehension difficulty) to the comprehension outcome.
> **A:** (1) Comprehension difficulty triggers automatic attribution search; (2) inner speech vocabulary determines the attribution made; (3) attribution is processed on stability and controllability dimensions; (4) stable/uncontrollable attribution reduces efficacy; unstable/controllable maintains it; (5) reduced efficacy reduces effort and depth of processing; (6) comprehension outcome feeds back into the next attribution cycle.
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Advanced

> [!flashcard] Seed 3 — Process
> **Q:** What is the Comprehension Gateway Mechanism, and what are its three structural components?
>
> **A:** The Comprehension Gateway Mechanism is the process by which EI produces comprehension benefits: a Register 2 "why" question makes textbase-level retrieval insufficient, forcing situation model construction. Its three components are: (1) Demand-Exceeds-Textbase Structure — the question cannot be answered from the text; (2) Integration Anchor Requirement — construction requires connecting text propositions to prior knowledge schemas; (3) Construction Premium — generating the connection produces richer encoding than receiving it.
>

> [!flashcard] Seed 3 — Process
> **Q:** Describe the chunking mechanism as it operates in basal ganglia habit encoding.
> **A:** With repeated practice, the basal ganglia consolidate a sequence of individually controlled steps into a single executable chunk, bracketed by start and stop signals. The chunk fires as a unit in response to the triggering cue, removing the need for step-by-step deliberate control. This is the neural substrate of habit automaticity.

> [!flashcard] Seed 3 — Process
> **Q:** In the Nelson-Narens monitoring-control model, what is the direction of information flow for monitoring vs. control?
> **A:** Monitoring is upward flow: information about object-level cognitive processes becomes available to the meta-level (allowing metacognitive judgments). Control is downward flow: meta-level evaluations generate commands that alter object-level processes (triggering strategy switches, pacing adjustments, effort allocation).

> [!flashcard] Seed 3 — Process
> **Q:** Describe the four cognitive operations involved in context-inference vocabulary acquisition. What can go wrong at each step?
>
> **A:** (1) *Lexical gap detection*: recognizing that a word is unfamiliar — can fail if the reader is too focused on decoding to notice. (2) *Context consolidation*: collecting syntactic, semantic, and topic cues — fails when surrounding words are also unfamiliar (below coverage threshold). (3) *Hypothesis generation and evaluation*: forming a candidate meaning and checking it — fails when context is misdirective (supports an incorrect inference). (4) *Provisional storage*: encoding a preliminary form-meaning mapping — fails when no elaborative processing occurs, producing only a faint trace.
>
> **Source:** Phase IV, Context-Inference Mechanism section
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Type:** Process
> **Tags:** #vocabulary-acquisition, #context-inference, #working-memory, #reading
> *— [[reading-vocabulary-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
