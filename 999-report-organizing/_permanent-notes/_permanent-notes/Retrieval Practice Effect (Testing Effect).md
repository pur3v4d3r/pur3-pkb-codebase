# Retrieval Practice Effect (Testing Effect)

> [!definition] Retrieval Practice Effect (Testing Effect)
> The robust finding that attempting to retrieve information from memory produces substantially better long-term retention than re-studying the same material for equivalent time, even when initial retrieval is difficult or incomplete. The benefit persists and often increases over longer retention intervals. Mechanisms proposed include strengthening of retrieval routes, elaborative encoding during effortful retrieval, and error-correction processes engaged by retrieval failure. A core instance of the broader principle of desirable difficulties. Practically, this means that low-stakes frequent testing is a learning strategy, not merely an assessment strategy.
