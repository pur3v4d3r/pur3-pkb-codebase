# Mastery Grammar (formative assessment literature)

> [!definition] Mastery Grammar (formative assessment literature)
> A linguistic register for evaluative prompts, self-talk, and feedback that frames evaluation in terms of growth, understanding, and capability development rather than judgment, comparison, or performance adequacy. Questions in mastery grammar ask "what did you learn?" and "where is your growing edge?" rather than "how well did you do?" and "was that good enough?" Mastery grammar activates mastery-approach goal orientation through repeated use.
>
> **Boundary:** Mastery grammar is not euphemistic or imprecise. It does not avoid honest evaluation of performance — it reframes the evaluative purpose from judgment to diagnosis. "Here is precisely where my understanding fell short, and here is what I will do about it" is mastery grammar. "I didn't do well enough" is not — because it produces no actionable next step.
>
