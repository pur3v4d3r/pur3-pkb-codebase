## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] WEIRD Sample Limitations
> The majority of laboratory research on deductive reasoning has been conducted with Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic (WEIRD) populations, typically undergraduates at North American or European universities. Cross-cultural research, including foundational work by [[Luria]] on reasoning in non-literate populations, suggests that performance on formal deductive tasks is substantially shaped by schooling and cultural exposure to formal argumentation. Educators should be cautious about treating Western laboratory norms as universal developmental endpoints.
> *— [[deductive-reasoning-learning-foundational-report-2026-03-10_report]]*

> [!evidence] WEIRD-Sample Limitations
> The preponderance of research on feedback, autonomy, and motivation has been conducted with Western, highly educated populations, primarily in formal schooling contexts. Cross-cultural replication of SDT effects has been reasonably successful — competence and autonomy support appear to be universal needs — but the specific linguistic and relational forms through which autonomy-supportive feedback is delivered vary substantially across cultural contexts. Practitioners working in non-WEIRD contexts should treat these principles as frameworks for contextual adaptation rather than universal scripts.
> *— [[feedback-design-autonomy-mastery-foundational-report-2026-03-10_report]]*
