# Transfer of Learning

> [!definition] Transfer of Learning
> The application of knowledge or skills acquired in one context to a new, different context. Transfer is the ultimate goal of CT instruction but the most persistent challenge: CT skills learned in one setting frequently fail to transfer without explicit instruction in the principles underlying the skills, practice across varied contexts, and metacognitive awareness of when to apply CT.

> [!evidence] Transfer of Learning
> **Term:** Transfer of Learning (Perkins & Salomon, 1989; Barnett & Ceci, 2002)
> **Definition:** The application of knowledge, skills, or strategies learned in one context to a different context. Near transfer involves similar contexts; far transfer involves structurally different contexts. Critical thinking transfer is the application of reasoning skills across domain boundaries.
> **Boundary conditions:** Far transfer does not occur automatically — it requires explicit "mindful abstraction" and structural mapping. Domain knowledge constrains transfer possibilities.
> **Report significance:** Central to the generality-specificity debate (Section 7) and the Far Transfer section.
> **See also:** [[transfer-of-learning]], [[analogical-reasoning]], [[self-directed-learning]]

It is particularly relevant to reasoning because one of the central debates in [[educational-psychology|Educational Psychology]] concerns whether reasoning skills transfer: can training in deductive reasoning in a logic class improve reasoning in history or science? The evidence, as we will see, is more qualified than optimists once hoped.

> [!evidence] Transfer of Learning
> The application of knowledge learned in one context to different, often novel contexts. *Near transfer* involves applying knowledge to cases structurally similar to training cases; *far transfer* involves applying knowledge across structural distances that require genuine reorganisation. Far transfer is widely regarded as the most robust marker of deep understanding, because it requires that knowledge be organised in terms of underlying structure rather than surface features.
> *— [[what-does-it-mean-to-truley-understand-something-2026-03-10_report]]*

> [!evidence] Transfer of Learning
> **Definition**: The application of knowledge or skills acquired in one context to a new, different context. Distinguished as *near transfer* (application to highly similar contexts), *far transfer* (application to structurally similar but superficially different contexts), and *far-far* or *analogical* transfer (application of abstract structural principles to distant domains). Research consistently shows that [[far-transfer]] requires explicit encoding of the abstract principle, not just repeated practice in the original domain.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance**: The FAR Transfer architecture in Phase VI depends on the abstract principle extraction described here.
>
> **Cross-References**: [[far-transfer]], [[transfer-appropriate-processing]], [[encoding-specificity]]
