# Temporal Marker Vocabulary

> [!definition] **Temporal Marker Vocabulary** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Definition:** The specific temporal words and grammatical structures that encode the stability dimension of attributions: "always" and "never" (high stability, fixed); "so far," "until now," "not yet" (moderate stability, developmental trajectory); "today," "in this session," "with this type of text" (low stability, situationally bounded). Temporal marker vocabulary is among the highest-leverage vocabulary categories for SRL because it directly encodes the fixed/growth mindset distinction at the grammatical level.
> 
> **Cross-References:** [[attribution-theory]], [[growth-mindset]], [[fixed-mindset]], [[incremental-theory-of-intelligence]]

**A:** (1) Locus: "I haven't yet built the skill" (internal, self-agentive). (2) Stability: "so far in this area, I've struggled — not yet mastered" ("so far" encodes unstable). (3) Controllability: "I need a better strategy for this type of text" (controllable — strategy is modifiable).
**Source:** Phase II; Appendix Lexicon
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Type:** Application
**Tags:** #attribution #weiner #vocabulary #srl

> [!flashcard] **Card 3** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 3
> **A:** **Q:** Why does the attribution retraining literature constitute evidence for the constitutive (rather than expressive) account of vocabulary?
**A:** Because in attribution retraining, vocabulary change (practicing "I haven't mastered this yet") precedes and produces the underlying belief change — not the reverse. If vocabulary were purely expressive, you would need the underlying belief to change first. The timing evidence favors constitutive causality.
**Source:** Phase III Empirical Challenge
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Type:** Connection

> [!flashcard] **Card 4** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 4
> **A:** **Q:** What is the regulatory language spectrum and how does it map onto SDT's internalization continuum?
**A:** Introjected regulatory language ("I have to or I'll feel bad") → Identified ("I want to because this matters for my goals") → Integrated ("this is what I do as a learner"). Corresponds to SDT's introjected → identified → integrated regulation. The vocabulary quality (pressure-based vs. value-based vs. self-defining) both reflects and partially constitutes the regulatory style.
**Source:** Phase II; Appendix Lexicon — Regulatory Language Spectrum
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Type:** Connection
**Tags:** #sdt #regulatory-language #inner-speech #autonomy

> [!flashcard] **Card 5** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 5
> **A:** **Q:** What does Vygotsky's account of inner speech predict about the source of a reader's inner evaluative vocabulary?
**A:** Because inner speech is internalized social dialogue, the evaluative vocabulary of a reader's inner speech reflects the evaluative language used in the social environments (teachers, parents, texts, peers) they inhabited during reading development. Negative inner self-talk is not self-generated — it is the internalized residue of social evaluations. This means vocabulary change requires re-internalization: extended contact with new vocabulary in self-relevant, need-satisfying contexts.
**Source:** Phase IV; Alternative Framework 1
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Type:** Process
**Tags:** #vygotsky #inner-speech #internalization #srl

> [!flashcard] **Card 6** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 6
> **A:** **Q:** What is the vocabulary granularity principle and why does it matter for SRL?
**A:** Practitioners with fine-grained vocabulary for their performance states have finer regulatory access than those with coarse-grained vocabulary. For SRL readers: a binary vocabulary ("I get it" / "I don't get it") provides only two motivational states; a tiered vocabulary system (full comprehension, partial comprehension with specific gap identified, structural comprehension gap, strategic re-orientation needed) provides graduated motivational states with specific regulatory implications at each level.
**Source:** Phase VI FAR Transfer; Phase IV
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Type:** Process
**Tags:** #vocabulary-granularity #comprehension-monitoring #srl #metacognition

> [!flashcard] **Card 7** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 7
> **A:** **Q:** What is the difference between effort attribution and effort-strategy attribution in growth-oriented inner speech?
**A:** Pure effort attribution ("I need to try harder") can produce burnout if effort is already high, because it implies that the gap is quantitative (more of the same effort) rather than qualitative (different approach). Effort-strategy attribution ("I need more effort *and* a better strategy") couples the effort dimension with the strategic-controllability dimension, enabling adaptive persistence rather than fixed-path persistence.
**Source:** Appendix Lexicon — Effort-Strategy Vocabulary
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Type:** Distinction
**Tags:** #attribution #effort #strategy #inner-speech

> [!flashcard] **Card 8** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 8
> **A:** **Q:** How does goal framing vocabulary differ between completion-oriented and transformation-oriented formulations, and what motivational orientation does each tend to activate?
**A:** Completion-oriented: "I'll read this chapter" / "I'll get through this section" — frames success as an event-boundary and can support performance-avoidance orientation. Transformation-oriented: "I'll understand the core argument well enough to apply it" — frames success as a comprehension quality and activates mastery-approach orientation. The vocabulary literally constructs the success criterion and the motivational lens under which reading proceeds.
**Source:** Phase IV Alternative Framework 2; Phase II Perspectival Framework
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Type:** Application
**Tags:** #goal-framing #vocabulary #achievement-goal-theory #srl

> [!flashcard] **Card 9** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 9
> **A:** **Q:** Why do the forethought and self-reflection phases of Zimmerman's SRL model qualify as "primarily vocabulary events" according to the reconstruction?
**A:** Because all the key processes in these phases — goal setting, self-motivation beliefs, strategic planning (forethought) and self-evaluation, attributional reasoning, adaptive inferences (self-reflection) — occur in inner speech. And because inner speech vocabulary is constitutive of (not merely expressive of) the motivational outcomes of these processes, the specific vocabulary deployed in each phase directly constitutes the motivational architecture of the subsequent phase. Vocabulary development is therefore SRL practice, not merely support for SRL practice.
**Source:** Phase V Reconstructive Synthesis
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Type:** Connection
**Tags:** #zimmerman #srl-phases #vocabulary #constitutive

> [!flashcard] **Card 10** *(from [[inner-speech-vocabulary-motivation-self-efficacy-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 10
> **A:** **Q:** What are temporal marker words, and why are they high-leverage in attributional vocabulary for SRL?
**A:** Temporal markers encode the stability dimension of attributions at the grammatical level: "always/never" = stable/fixed; "so far / not yet / until now" = developmental trajectory (unstable, growth-oriented); "today / in this session / with this type of text" = situationally bounded (low stability, specificity-preserving). They are high-leverage because they directly encode the fixed/growth distinction in a single word — "I can't do this" vs. "I can't yet do this" differ only in the temporal marker "yet," but carry radically different motivational architectures.
**Source:** Appendix Lexicon — Temporal Marker Vocabulary; Phase III
**Difficulty:** Basic
**Type:** Definition
**Tags:** #temporal-markers #attribution #vocabulary #growth-mindset

## References

- **Vygotsky, L. S. (1986). *Thought and Language* (revised edition). MIT Press. (Original work published 1934)**: The foundational account of inner speech as internalized social dialogue. Essential for understanding why vocabulary carries evaluative architecture. Chapters 6-7 on inner speech are directly relevant to this report's reconstructed account.
- **Alderson-Day, B., & Fernyhough, C. (2015). Inner speech: Development, cognitive functions, phenomenology, and neurobiology. *Psychological Bulletin, 141*(5), 931–965.**: Comprehensive review of contemporary inner speech research. Particularly valuable for the section on inner speech's dialogic properties and motivational valence. Supports the Vygotskian account with neuroscientific evidence.
- **Weiner, B. (1985). An attributional theory of achievement motivation and emotion. *Psychological Review, 92*(4), 548–573.**: The primary paper establishing the three-dimensional model of attribution and its motivational consequences. Central to Phase III's empirical challenge. The paper's treatment of affect (pride, shame, guilt) as consequences of attributional dimensions is especially relevant to the vocabulary-emotion connection.
- **Dweck, C. S. (1975). The role of expectations and attributions in the alleviation of learned helplessness. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 31*(4), 674–685.**: Classic attribution retraining study. The temporal structure of this intervention — vocabulary practice preceding belief change — is the primary empirical support for the constitutive account.
- **Bandura, A. (1997). *Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control*. W.H. Freeman.**: Comprehensive treatment of self-efficacy theory. The chapter on verbal persuasion as an efficacy source is directly relevant to the inner speech vocabulary mechanism. Chapter 3's treatment of the four efficacy sources should be read alongside this report's reconstruction.
- **Elliot, A. J., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (1996). Approach and avoidance achievement goals and intrinsic motivation. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 70*(3), 461–475.**: Distinguishes approach vs. avoidance orientations within mastery and performance goal frameworks. Directly relevant to the goal framing vocabulary analysis in Phase IV.
- **Ames, C. (1992). Classrooms: Goals, structures, and student motivation. *Journal of Educational Psychology, 84*(3), 261–271.**: Establishes the [[motivational-climate]] research base, showing how evaluative vocabulary in classroom settings is internalized by students and shapes self-evaluative inner speech. The Vygotskian mechanism in an educational context.
- **Zimmerman, B. J. (2002). Becoming a self-regulated learner: An overview. *Theory Into Practice, 41*(2), 64–70.**: Accessible overview of the three-phase cyclical model. The forethought and self-reflection phases described here are the primary sites where inner speech vocabulary operates as motivational architecture.
- **Dweck, C. S. (2006). *Mindset: The New Psychology of Success*. Random House.**: Accessible account of fixed vs. growth mindset. The vocabulary contrast between entity-theory and incremental-theory inner speech is the practical application of the constitutive vocabulary principle.
- **Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000). Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being. *American Psychologist, 55*(1), 68–78.**: Core SDT paper. The regulatory styles section provides the theoretical foundation for the regulatory language spectrum developed in Phase II.
- **Halpern, D. F. (1998). Teaching critical thinking for transfer across domains. *American Psychologist, 53*(4), 449–455.**: The four-component transfer model informing the FAR Transfer architecture of this report. Also models the vocabulary development approach to transfer explicitly.
