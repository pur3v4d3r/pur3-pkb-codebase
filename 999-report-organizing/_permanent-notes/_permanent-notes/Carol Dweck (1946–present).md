## Additional Material

> [!person] Carol Dweck (1946–present)
> American psychologist, Stanford University. Developer of the implicit theories of intelligence framework (entity vs. incremental theories) and populariser of the growth mindset concept. Dweck's research demonstrated that beliefs about the malleability of intelligence function as upstream constraints on the stability dimension, determining whether difficulty is attributed to stable inability or unstable, remediable factors.
>
> **Relationship to other figures:** Extended Weiner's stability dimension by identifying the meta-beliefs that calibrate it. Connected to Seligman through the helpless vs. mastery-oriented response patterns.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!person] Carol Dweck (1946–present)
> **Core Contribution:** Developed the [[growth-mindset|implicit theories of intelligence]] framework (1986, 1999) and the mastery/helpless response pattern research, providing the psychological substrate for understanding why goal orientation in the Forethought Phase determines the type of inference generated in Self-Reflection.
> **Relationship to Other Figures:** Research program overlaps with and complements Nicholls' achievement goal theory; extends Weiner's attribution research by identifying the incremental/entity theory of intelligence as the upstream variable that predicts attribution type.
> **Key Works:** *Self-Theories: Their Role in Motivation, Personality, and Development* (1999), "Motivational Processes Affecting Learning" (1986, *American Psychologist*).
> **Relevance to This Analysis:** The mastery/defensive inference distinction in Phase IV and the language architecture principles in Phase V draw on Dweck's research on mastery-oriented versus helpless response patterns.
