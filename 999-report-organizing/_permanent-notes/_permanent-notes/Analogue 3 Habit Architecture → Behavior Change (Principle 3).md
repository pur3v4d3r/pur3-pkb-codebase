## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Analogue 3: Habit Architecture → Behavior Change (Principle 3)
> Pintrich's context regulation principle maps directly to [[james-clear]]'s habit architecture framing and the broader behavior change literature's shift from willpower-based models (regulate the agent) to environment design models (regulate the context). The structural analogue: if motivational regulation is costly and often fails (as the evidence in Phase III suggests), then the most robust intervention is to make high-quality behavior the *path of least resistance* by environmental restructuring. This is not a novel observation in the behavior change literature, but Pintrich's framework provides the theoretical mechanism: by shifting the regulatory burden from motivated deliberate control (Phase 3 control strategies) to proactive context structuring (Phase 1 context forethought), the total regulatory cost is reduced.
> *— [[pintrichs-framework-srl-focused-analysis-2026-03-23_report]]*
