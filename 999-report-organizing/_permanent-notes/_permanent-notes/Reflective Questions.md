> [!reflection] Reflective Questions
> 1. If working memory limitation is genuinely constitutive of expertise development rather than merely an obstacle to it, what would be lost if the constraint were artificially removed?
> 2. How does the chess paradigm's finding — that expert memory advantage disappears for random arrangements — constrain what can and cannot be claimed about the nature of expert knowledge?

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions
> 1. If expertise involves the progressive automation of knowledge structures, does this mean that experts are, in some sense, *less conscious* of their domain than competent intermediates? What are the implications of this for expert self-assessment?
> 2. The Dreyfus model suggests that rule-following characterizes novice performance while intuitive recognition characterizes expert performance. Does this mean that explicit rules and principles become *irrelevant* at higher levels of expertise, or do they serve a different function?

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions

> 2. The expertise reversal effect suggests that optimal instruction must be continuously recalibrated to the learner's developing knowledge. What practical implications does this have for educational systems that deliver standardized instruction to heterogeneous groups?
