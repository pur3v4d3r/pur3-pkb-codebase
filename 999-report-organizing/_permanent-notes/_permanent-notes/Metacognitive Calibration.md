# Metacognitive Calibration

> [!definition] Metacognitive Calibration
> **Definition:** The accuracy of a learner's metacognitive monitoring — the degree to which their judgments of learning (JOLs), feelings of knowing (FOKs), and confidence ratings correspond to their actual knowledge state. High calibration = monitoring accuracy closely tracks performance. Miscalibration = systematic over- or under-estimation of one's own knowledge. **Boundary condition:** calibration is domain-specific; a student can be well-calibrated in one subject and poorly calibrated in another.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This is the core construct of the MCM. If monitoring accuracy predicts the degree to which formative feedback produces learning gains, the MCM has explanatory power the ITM lacks.
>
> **Cross-References:** [[metacognitive-accuracy]], [[feeling-of-knowing]], [[monitoring-gap]], [[nelson-narens-model]]

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Calibration
> [[metacognitive-calibration|Metacognitive calibration]] refers to the accuracy of metacognitive monitoring — the alignment between a person's confidence in their knowledge or performance and their objective knowledge state. A perfectly calibrated learner who rates their confidence at 70% on a set of items would answer exactly 70% of them correctly. Calibration is measured through indices like the Brier score, the absolute calibration index (|confidence − accuracy|), and resolution measures. Calibration is domain-specific and trainable, but generally poor in the absence of deliberate calibration practice.
>
> **Boundary:** Calibration is not the same as overall accuracy or ability. A highly knowledgeable person can be poorly calibrated (consistently overconfident), and a less knowledgeable person can be well-calibrated (accurately aware of what they do and do not know). The metacognitive virtue is not *knowing a lot* but *knowing what you know*.
>

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Calibration
> The degree of alignment between a learner's metacognitive judgments (predictions of performance, assessments of comprehension, estimates of mastery) and actual cognitive states or performance outcomes. Accurate calibration enables appropriate regulatory responses; miscalibration — particularly overconfident calibration — generates false positive monitoring signals that suppress necessary strategy deployment. Calibration is improvable through retrieval practice, delayed judgments of learning, and explicit instruction in the sources of processing-fluency illusions.
>
> **Research Status:** Calibration accuracy is the single metacognitive parameter with the most direct and robust relationship to learning outcomes in experimental studies. The finding that calibration predicts performance beyond raw cognitive ability has been replicated across numerous domains and populations.
>

> Metacognitive calibration is the degree of correspondence between metacognitive judgments (confidence ratings, JOLs, FOKs) and actual performance outcomes (correct answers, demonstrated recall). Perfect calibration means that a person who says they are 70% confident is correct 70% of the time; overconfidence means actual accuracy is systematically below expressed confidence; underconfidence means the reverse. [[metacognitive-calibration]] is both a dependent variable in metacognitive research (measuring monitoring accuracy) and a target of metacognitive intervention (improving the correspondence between reflective self-assessments and actual performance serves both epistemic and motivational functions).
