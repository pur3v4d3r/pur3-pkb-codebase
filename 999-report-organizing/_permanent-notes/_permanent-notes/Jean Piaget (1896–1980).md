> [!person] Jean Piaget (1896–1980)
> Swiss developmental psychologist and founder of genetic epistemology. Trained as a biologist, Piaget shifted to psychology to investigate the development of knowledge — how a person moves from the rudimentary knowing of infancy to the formal logical capacities of adulthood. His clinical method and decades of systematic research generated the framework of developmental stages and the assimilation-accommodation-equilibration model that constitutes one of the two foundations of constructivist learning theory.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by Kant's epistemology, Baldwin's developmental psychology, and biological adaptation theory. Later influenced by cybernetics and systems theory. Influenced virtually all subsequent constructivist theorists, including Bruner, Inhelder, Case, and (via productive disagreement) Vygotsky.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** The Piagetian framework provides the core mechanism of cognitive constructivism — assimilation, accommodation, equilibration, and schema theory — that constitutes Phase II's conceptual foundation.
> *— [[constructivist-learning-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Jean Piaget (1896–1980)
> **Position:** Swiss psychologist and genetic epistemologist; foundational theorist of cognitive constructivism
> **Core contribution:** The assimilation-accommodation-equilibration model of cognitive development; the identification of four developmental stages; the concept of schemas as units of knowledge; the clinical method for investigating children's reasoning.
> **Intellectual lineage:** Influenced by Kant (active cognition), Bergson (vitalism), James Mark Baldwin (developmental epistemology). Influenced von Glasersfeld (radical constructivism), Bruner (process of education), Ausubel (meaningful learning theory), Inhelder (formal operations research).
> **Key works:** *The Language and Thought of the Child* (1923); *The Construction of Reality in the Child* (1937); *The Origins of Intelligence in Children* (1952); *Genetic Epistemology* (1970)
> *See also:* [[jean-piaget]], [[genetic-epistemology]], [[assimilation-and-accommodation]]

> [!person] Jean Piaget (1896–1980)
> Swiss developmental psychologist whose structural-developmental theory provided the foundational account of schemas in developmental psychology. Piaget's concepts of *assimilation* (incorporating new experience into existing schemas), *accommodation* (modifying schemas to fit new experience), and *equilibration* (the self-regulatory process driving developmental change) map directly onto the accretion-restructuring distinction in cognitive schema theory. See also: [[jean-piaget]], [[Piaget]], [[assimilation]], [[accommodation]].
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
