## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Performance-Approach Goal Controversy
> The core of the controversy can be stated precisely. Harackiewicz et al. (1997, 2002) found that PAP goals predicted course grades better than MAP goals in introductory psychology, suggesting that normative competition is motivationally potent under conditions of evaluative pressure. Midgley et al. (2001) challenged these findings on methodological and theoretical grounds, arguing that (1) MAP goals predict more important long-term outcomes than PAP goals even when the reverse is true for near-term grades, and (2) the finding reflects assessment artifacts (grades reward relative standing, so performance goals activate relevant strategies) rather than genuine motivational superiority. A subsequent meta-analysis (Hulleman et al., 2010) partially resolved the controversy by demonstrating that the PAP-performance association is stronger under conditions of evaluative threat, high-stakes testing, and ability-focused classroom environments — precisely the conditions that activate normative comparison.
