> [!person] Ann L. Brown (1943–1999)
> UC Berkeley educational psychologist. Distinguished metacognitive knowledge-of-cognition from regulation-of-cognition (planning, monitoring, evaluating), bridging metacognition theory with educational practice. Her work on reciprocal teaching demonstrated that metacognitive skills can be explicitly taught.
>
> **Relationship to other figures:** Built on Flavell's conceptual foundations; influenced Zimmerman's SRL model; her regulation taxonomy anticipated the executive function parallels central to this report.
> **Key works:** *Knowing when, where, and how to remember* (1978); *Metacognition, executive control, SRL, and other more mysterious mechanisms* (1987).
> *— [[metacognition-and-pkm-pkb-foundational-report-2026-04-12_report]]*

> [!person] Ann L. Brown (1943–1999)
> Developmental and educational psychologist, University of California, Berkeley. Brown's contributions to metacognitive reading research were twin-tracked: empirical (demonstrating comprehension monitoring deficits in poor readers and younger students) and pedagogical (co-developing Reciprocal Teaching with Palincsar). Her synthesis of Flavell's framework with Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development produced the social-constructivist metacognitive pedagogy that reciprocal teaching instantiates.
>
> **Lineage:** Student of the Flavellian tradition; collaborator with [[Anneliese-Palincsar|Anneliese Palincsar]] on instructional applications; influenced by Vygotsky via the Zone of Proximal Development.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Central to Phases III and V; her distinction between monitoring (evaluation) and regulation (fix-up) anchors the mechanistic treatment in Phase IV.
> *— [[metacognition-and-reading-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Ann L. Brown (1943–1999)
> Educational psychologist at University of California, Berkeley. Applied metacognitive theory to reading comprehension and produced foundational research on children's comprehension monitoring failures. Developed reciprocal teaching — the most empirically validated metacognitive instruction method. Distinguished between metacognition "in the hot sense" (online regulatory processing) and "in the cold sense" (propositional knowledge about cognition), a distinction anticipating later monitoring/knowledge debates.
>
> **Lineage:** Deeply influenced by Flavell and by the Soviet sociocultural tradition (Vygotsky). Influenced all subsequent applied metacognition research in educational settings.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Brown's distinction between cold metacognitive knowledge and hot metacognitive regulation, and her applied research on comprehension monitoring, inform the mechanisms discussion in Phase IV.
> *— [[metacognition-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Ann L. Brown (1943–1999)
> Ann Brown, working primarily at the University of Illinois and UC Berkeley, was the central figure in translating metacognitive theory from developmental description to instructional application. Her research on reading comprehension, deliberate memory, and the development of strategic metacognition in school-age children established the developmental pattern of metacognitive naïveté to strategic competence. With Annemarie Palincsar, she developed the reciprocal teaching instructional model, which remains one of the most extensively validated metacognitive instruction approaches. Her broader theoretical work on learning as a self-regulatory activity was enormously influential in educational psychology through the 1980s and 1990s.
> *— [[metacognitive-development-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

> [!person] Ann L. Brown (1943–1999)
> A developmental and educational psychologist at the University of California, Berkeley, Brown made foundational contributions to the metacognitive knowledge literature through her studies of reading comprehension, memory development, and cognitive strategy instruction. Her distinction between knowledge *of* cognition and regulation *of* cognition has been as influential as Flavell's taxonomy, providing the conceptual framework that most educational interventions have used. Her work on reciprocal teaching — a pedagogical approach for scaffolded metacognitive strategy instruction — is among the most replicated educational interventions in the literature.
>
> **Lineage:** A contemporary and occasional collaborator of [[john-h.-flavell|Flavell]]; influenced by Soviet activity theory and Vygotskian accounts of mediated learning.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the knowledge-regulation distinction (Phase III.2) that is central to the report's argument about the Knowledge-Activation Gap.
> *— [[metacognitive-knowledge-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Ann L. Brown (1943–1999)
> Ann Brown was a developmental psychologist at the University of California, Berkeley, whose foundational research in the 1970s and 1980s established the developmental trajectory of metacognitive skills in children. Her concepts of metacognitive inertia, metacognitive production deficiency, and the scaffolded development of comprehension monitoring strategies shaped both developmental theory and educational practice. Her collaborative development of reciprocal teaching (with Palincsar) produced one of the most empirically validated educational interventions for metacognitive reading skills training.
> **Key works:** Baker & Brown (1984), *Metacognitive Skills and Reading*; Palincsar & Brown (1984), *Reciprocal Teaching of Comprehension-Fostering and Comprehension-Monitoring Activities*.
> **Relationship to other figures:** Flavell's developmental orientation and metacognitive inertia concept were direct influences; Brown's educational applications informed Zimmerman's SRL framework and Hattie's synthesis.
> *— [[metacognitive-skills-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*

> [!person] Ann L. Brown (1943–1999)
> [[ann-brown|Ann Brown]], Professor at UC Berkeley, independently developed an approach to metacognition in children that emphasized the regulatory, strategic dimension — how children learn to plan, monitor, and evaluate their own cognitive activities. Her distinction between knowledge about cognition and regulation of cognition paralleled and complemented the monitoring-control distinction in the Nelson-Narens framework.
>
> **Core contribution:** Established the knowledge-about/regulation-of distinction; pioneered research on metacognition in children; developed reciprocal teaching.
> **Relationship to other figures:** Worked in parallel with Flavell on developmental metacognition; her regulatory emphasis anticipated Nelson and Narens' control channel.
> **Key works:** Brown (1978, 1987).
> *— [[nelson-narens-metacognitive-control-framework-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
