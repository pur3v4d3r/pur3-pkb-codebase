# Organismic Integration Theory (OIT)

> [!definition] Organismic Integration Theory (OIT)
> A mini-theory within SDT describing the internalization and integration of extrinsic motivation. Provides the motivational continuum (external → introjected → identified → integrated → intrinsic) within which COT orientations are contextually located: control orientation activates external and introjected motivation; autonomy orientation activates identified, integrated, and intrinsic motivation; impersonal orientation activates amotivation.

> SDT's mini-theory explaining how extrinsic motivations are internalized across a continuum from external regulation to integration. When applied to relationship motivation, OIT's internalization continuum maps onto the autonomous-controlled motivation distinction: fully autonomous relationship motivation represents integrated regulation (the value of the relationship has been fully incorporated into the person's identity), while controlled relationship motivation represents introjected or externally regulated engagement.

> [!evidence] Organismic Integration Theory (OIT)
> The SDT mini-theory describing the internalisation continuum — the process through which external regulations are progressively taken in, transformed, and (ideally) integrated into the person's self-concept and value system. OIT proposes a qualitatively ordered sequence: external regulation → introjected regulation → identified regulation → integrated regulation → intrinsic motivation. The theory specifies the social conditions (rationale provision, perspective acknowledgment, minimal pressure) that facilitate healthy progression toward integrated and identified regulation for activities that may not be inherently interesting. Failure of healthy internalisation leaves the person in external or introjected regulation — compliant but fragile.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*
