# Relationship Motivation Theory (RMT)

> [!definition] Relationship Motivation Theory (RMT)
> The seventh mini-theory of Self-Determination Theory, developed by Deci and Ryan, which examines how the basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness operate within close personal relationships and how the need-support or need-thwarting character of a relationship determines both its quality and its effects on participants' broader well-being. RMT does NOT reduce to the claim that good relationships are beneficial; it specifies the motivational mechanisms that distinguish beneficial from harmful relationships.

> [!evidence] Relationship Motivation Theory (RMT)
> The sixth mini-theory within [[self-determination-theory]], formally articulated by Richard Ryan and Edward Deci, that extends SDT's motivational analysis to the domain of close relationships. RMT proposes that close relationships can be objects of motivation (not merely motivational contexts), that relational motivation exists on a continuum from fully autonomous to fully controlled, and that the quality of this relational motivation is a significant independent predictor of relational and individual well-being outcomes. *Boundary*: RMT analyzes motivation for close relationship engagement, not motivation for specific relational behaviors (which is addressed by other SDT applications). *See also*: [[basic-psychological-needs-theory]], [[organismic-integration-theory]], [[self-determination-theory]]
