# Conditional Metacognitive Knowledge

> [!definition] Conditional Metacognitive Knowledge
> Conditional metacognitive knowledge governs the *application conditions* of cognitive strategies — the when and why of strategy use. It encompasses knowing that retrieval practice is most beneficial for material that will need to be recalled in new contexts (not merely recognized), that analogical scaffolding is most powerful when learners have already developed partial understanding of the target domain, and that elaborative interrogation ("why?" and "how?" questioning) is most efficacious for material with causal and explanatory relations rather than arbitrary associations.
>
> **Boundary:** Conditional metacognitive knowledge is the most difficult component to develop and the most difficult to assess, because it requires not just strategy knowledge but the ability to read task demands, match them to strategy profiles, and override habitual strategies when conditions change. Its absence produces what we might call metacognitive rigidity — the deployment of a small fixed repertoire of strategies regardless of task demands.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Conditional knowledge is the gateway to far transfer of metacognitive skill. When students develop conditional knowledge, they can export metacognitive strategies to new domains without explicitly being taught domain-specific applications.
>
