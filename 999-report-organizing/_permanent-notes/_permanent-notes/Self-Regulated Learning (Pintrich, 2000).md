# Self-Regulated Learning (Pintrich, 2000)

> [!definition] Self-Regulated Learning (Pintrich, 2000)
> **Definition:** An active, constructive process by which learners set goals for their learning and then attempt to monitor, regulate, and control their cognition, motivation, affect, behavior, and context — guided and constrained by their goals and the contextual features of the environment. Self-regulation is not a fixed learner trait but a context-sensitive, goal-directed activity that can vary across tasks and settings.
>
> **Boundary conditions:** SRL is distinguished from automatic processing (habit execution without deliberate monitoring), from reactive coping (adjusting to disruption without proactive goal orientation), and from compliance (following external regulation without internalized goal structures). SRL requires that the learner both monitors their own regulatory processes and controls them in the service of learning goals.
