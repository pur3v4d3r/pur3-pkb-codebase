## Methodology & Sources

> [!methodology-and-sources] ⚙️ **The Process of Assent: Stoic Cognitive Control
> Understanding the Stoic theory of assent reveals the practical core of their psychology. When an impression arises in consciousness, we face a choice point—we can either *assent* to the impression (accept it as true and allow it to guide action), *dissent* from it (reject it as false), or *suspend judgment* (withhold commitment either way). This moment of assent is where human freedom and responsibility lie for the Stoics. We cannot control what impressions arise (these are determined by our sensory apparatus and past conditioning), but we *can* control whether we assent to them, reject them, or investigate them further. This is the psychological mechanism behind the famous Stoic claim that "we are disturbed not by things, but by the judgments we form about them." An external event triggers an impression ("this is terrible"), but we become emotionally disturbed only if we *assent* to that evaluative judgment. By learning to **pause** before assenting—by inserting conscious deliberation into what normally operates automatically—we gain the power to choose our responses and maintain rational control even in difficult circumstances.
