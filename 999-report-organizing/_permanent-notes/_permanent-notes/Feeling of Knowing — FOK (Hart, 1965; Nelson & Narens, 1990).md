# Feeling of Knowing — FOK (Hart, 1965; Nelson & Narens, 1990)

> [!definition] Feeling of Knowing — FOK (Hart, 1965; Nelson & Narens, 1990)
> [[Feeling-of-Knowing-—-FOK|Feelings of Knowing]] are metacognitive judgments made after a retrieval failure, reflecting the meta-level's assessment of whether the currently unrecalled information is nevertheless stored in memory and will be recognized if encountered again. FOK judgments operate in the gap between retrieval failure and potential recognition — a gap that, under a single-level account of memory, should not yield informative metacognitive signals, since the retrieval process has already failed. The fact that FOK judgments carry above-chance predictive validity for subsequent recognition performance demonstrates that the meta-level has access to information about the object-level's state that goes beyond what the object-level can currently deliver through direct retrieval.
>
> **Boundary:** FOK judgments are post-retrieval-failure judgments; they are definitionally distinct from JOLs (which are pre-retrieval) and from Retrospective Confidence Judgments (which follow successful retrieval).
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The FOK is the metacognitive judgment that most directly demands a two-level account, because it demonstrates that the meta-level can assess the object-level's state even when the object-level's primary output channel (retrieval) has failed.
>
> **See also:** [[Metacognition-and-Memory-Monitoring]], [[metacognitive-experience]], [[metacognitive-experiences]]
