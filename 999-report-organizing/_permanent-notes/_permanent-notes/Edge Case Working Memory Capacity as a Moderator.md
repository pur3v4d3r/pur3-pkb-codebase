# Edge Case: Working Memory Capacity as a Moderator

> [!definition] Edge Case: Working Memory Capacity as a Moderator
> *Definition pending — derived from 2 source report(s).*

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Edge Case: Working Memory Capacity as a Moderator
> **The case:** [[working-memory-capacity]] (WMC) varies substantially across individuals (by approximately 2:1 between 25th and 75th percentile on standard complex span tasks). This variation doesn't just scale performance — it qualitatively changes the walking-reading interaction.
>
> **What standard understanding predicts:** The general model predicts that walking's dual-task cost is small because CE resource competition is minimal. This should hold for everyone.
>
> **What actually happens:** WMC mediates the dual-task cost **non-linearly:**
> - **High-WMC individuals:** Have substantial CE headroom. Can absorb the small CE cost of walking-task-maintenance without meaningful impact on reading. For these individuals, the general model's prediction holds well — walking cost is negligible and arousal benefit dominates. These readers may genuinely be *better* at walking-while-reading than sitting-reading, especially for moderate-difficulty material.
> - **Low-WMC individuals:** Have limited CE headroom. Even the small CE cost of walking-task-maintenance may push their total demand past capacity for moderately challenging material — a threshold that high-WMC individuals don't reach until material is much more difficult. For these individuals, the general model's prediction may systematically overestimate the benefit of walking.
> - **Near-WMC-ceiling processing:** When reading material pushes any individual close to their personal WM capacity limit, the dual-task cost of walking becomes **disproportionately impactful**. This is because WM operates with a "catastrophic cliff" near capacity — performance degrades gradually as demand approaches capacity, then collapses rapidly when demand exceeds capacity. Walking's small cost, added to near-capacity reading demands, can push readers past this cliff.
>
> **Why this matters:** The general model's prediction of "small, manageable dual-task cost" is accurate **on average** but may systematically mislead low-WMC individuals or any individual reading material near their personal difficulty ceiling.
>
> **Implications:** WMC functions as a boundary condition for the walking-reading practice. Readers with high WMC have a wider "safe zone" for walking-while-reading; readers with low WMC have a narrower one. Knowing your own WMC (which can be roughly calibrated via tasks like operation span or reading span) is relevant to optimizing the practice.
