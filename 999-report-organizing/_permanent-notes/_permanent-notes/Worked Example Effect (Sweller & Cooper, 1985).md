# Worked Example Effect (Sweller & Cooper, 1985)

> [!definition] Worked Example Effect (Sweller & Cooper, 1985)
> The finding that novice learners acquire skills and knowledge more effectively when studying fully solved examples than when solving equivalent problems independently. Explained by CLT as a reduction in the extraneous load imposed by means-ends analysis during goal-directed problem solving. The effect is moderated by expertise (expertise reversal effect) and by example quality (poorly formatted examples may reintroduce extraneous load).

> [!evidence] Worked Example Effect (Sweller & Cooper, 1985)
> The empirical finding that studying worked-out solutions (complete solution steps with explanations) produces superior learning outcomes compared to solving equivalent problems independently, particularly for novice learners. The effect is explained by the elimination of cognitively expensive means-ends analysis during problem solving.
>
> **Boundary:** The effect reverses for advanced learners (expertise reversal effect) and is most pronounced in well-structured domains with clear solution procedures.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The founding empirical result of CLT and its most practically significant design principle.
