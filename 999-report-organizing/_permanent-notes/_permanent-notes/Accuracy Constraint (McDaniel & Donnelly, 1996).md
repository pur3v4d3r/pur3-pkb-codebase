# Accuracy Constraint (McDaniel & Donnelly, 1996)

> [!definition] Accuracy Constraint (McDaniel & Donnelly, 1996)
> The principle that elaborative interrogation benefits depend on the accuracy of the generated why-answers. Inaccurate elaborations, when generated with confidence, create interference: the incorrect causal account may be encoded alongside or instead of the target fact, producing distorted memory rather than enhanced retention.
>
> **Boundary:** The accuracy constraint does not require that every generated answer be textbook-perfect; it requires that answers not be systematically misleading. A partially accurate why-answer (correct in mechanism, approximate in magnitude) typically still produces encoding benefits.
>
> **Research Status:** Well-supported in the core experimental literature (McDaniel & Donnelly, 1996; Reder & Anderson, 1982). The instructional implication — that accuracy verification should follow EI execution — is theoretically well-motivated but understudied operationally.
>
> **See also:** [[metacognitive-accuracy]], [[comprehension-monitoring]], [[calibration]], [[fluency-illusion]], [[metacognitive-knowledge]]
