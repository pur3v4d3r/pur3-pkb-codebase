## Examples

> [!example] Goal Language Comparison: The Same Objective, Multiple Vocabularies
> Consider a reader who intends to understand Weiner's three attribution dimensions while reading this report. Here are five different ways to formulate that goal, each encoding a different motivational vocabulary:
>
> 1. *"I need to memorize the three dimensions so I can use them."* — **Performance-approach, controlled, surface.** The goal is reproduction and instrumental use; the operative word is *need* (mild introjection); the criterion is recall.
>
> 2. *"I should be able to understand this by the end — it's not supposed to be that hard."* — **Performance-avoidance, introjected.** The goal is competence demonstration; the criterion is normative sufficiency; failure will generate shame.
>
> 3. *"I want to genuinely understand how locus, stability, and controllability each differentially affect motivation — not just as labels but as mechanisms."* — **Mastery-approach, identified, deep.** The goal is mechanistic understanding; the criterion is self-set and substantive; failure generates curiosity, not shame.
>
> 4. *"I'm building schema for a framework I'll apply to my own inner speech. I want to understand this well enough to recognize attribution patterns in real-time."* — **Mastery-approach, integrated, applied.** The goal is connected to identity and practical application; the criterion is transfer.
>
> 5. *"How does this work? I want to follow the argument."* — **Intrinsic.** No explicit goal construction; engagement is pure curiosity-driven; the criterion is felt comprehension.
> *— [[attribution-self-efficacy-inner-speech-vocabulary-foundational-report-2026-03-25_report]]*
