# Shame vs. Guilt in Attribution Theory

> [!definition] Shame vs. Guilt in Attribution Theory
> **Shame**: Negative affect arising from failure attributed to stable, internal, uncontrollable causes (inherent ability limitations). Produces identity-level self-condemnation; associated with withdrawal and avoidance. Motivationally destructive in achievement contexts.
>
> **Guilt**: Negative affect arising from failure attributed to controllable, internal causes (insufficient effort, poor strategy). Produces behavior-level self-criticism; associated with corrective action and renewed engagement. Motivationally constructive in achievement contexts despite being aversive.
>
> This distinction has immediate clinical implications: depression frequently involves a pattern of shame — attributing persistent negative outcomes to permanent, global, internal inadequacies — rather than guilt. Attribution retraining for depression attempts to shift the attribution profile from the shame-generating structure toward more differentiated, controllable, unstable attributions.
