## Reflections

> [!reflection] Motivational Self-Inventory
> 1. Read this self-reflection prompt and notice your immediate internal response: "This reading session did not meet the process goals I set at the start." Is your first response one of curiosity (what happened? what can I learn from this?), self-criticism (I should have done better), or minimisation (the goals were too ambitious)? Your characteristic response is the motivational signature of your current self-reflection practice.
> 2. The distinction between descriptive and evaluative self-feedback is cognitively available but emotionally challenging: honest description of gaps requires a secure enough relationship with self-evaluation to not need defensive distortion. What would it mean — practically — to develop that security? What conditions support it in your learning practice?
> 3. How does your PKB itself function as a motivational architecture? Does the experience of adding a new, well-formed permanent note feel like a genuine competence experience? What features of your current PKB workflow most reliably produce the sense of genuine learning rather than mere information accumulation?
