## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Asymmetry Thesis as a General Principle for Environment Design
> The satisfaction-frustration asymmetry suggests a design principle that generalizes beyond BPNT: the *first priority* in any motivational environment design should not be maximizing positive conditions but *identifying and eliminating active frustrations*. A learning environment that scores highly on autonomy-supportive practices but simultaneously includes features that actively frustrate the competence need (e.g., normative comparisons that make failure publicly visible, evaluations tied to social status) will show worse outcomes than a less autonomy-supportive environment that has eliminated active competence frustrations. The reason, the asymmetry tells us, is that active frustrations activate avoidance-oriented defensive systems that are not simply the low end of an approach-motivation continuum — they are a different motivational register altogether. The implication: audit for frustrations before optimizing for satisfactions.
> *— [[basic-psychological-needs-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-13_report]]*
