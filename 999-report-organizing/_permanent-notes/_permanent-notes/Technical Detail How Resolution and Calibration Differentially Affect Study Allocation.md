## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technical Detail: How Resolution and Calibration Differentially Affect Study Allocation
> The distinction between calibration and resolution (established at Level 1) becomes consequential at the dynamics level because the two dimensions of accuracy affect study allocation through different mechanisms:
>
> <span style='color: #9E6CD3;'>**Calibration errors**</span> affect the **total amount** of study time allocated. An overconfident learner perceives globally smaller discrepancies and allocates less total study time than warranted. The error is uniform — all items get less attention than they should, proportionally.
>
> <span style='color: #9E6CD3;'>**Resolution errors**</span> affect the **distribution** of study time across items. A learner with poor resolution cannot discriminate well-learned from poorly-learned items, so their study allocation is quasi-random with respect to actual need. They may spend equal time on items they already know and items they don't, or they may follow a default strategy (study from front to back) rather than a need-based strategy.
>
> **The asymmetry of impact:** Resolution errors are typically more damaging than calibration errors of comparable magnitude, because a learner with good resolution but poor calibration can at least direct their (under-allocated) time to the right items, while a learner with poor resolution directs even well-allocated time to the wrong items.
>
> **Precision:** This analysis applies to self-paced study situations. In time-constrained situations (e.g., timed exams), calibration errors dominate because the question shifts from "how much to study each item" to "should I attempt this item at all."
> **Dependencies:** Requires Level 1 calibration-resolution distinction and Level 3 cue reliability analysis.
> *— [[metacognitive-monitoring-accuracy-calibration-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*
