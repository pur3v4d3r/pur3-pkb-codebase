## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Pintrich's Integrative SRL Framework — Paul Pintrich (2000–2004)
> **Core claim**: Self-regulated learning is a process by which learners actively and constructively set goals for their learning and attempt to monitor, regulate, and control their cognition, motivation, behaviour, and context, guided by and constrained by their goals and the contextual features of the environment. Unlike frameworks that treat motivation and cognition separately, Pintrich explicitly theorises their joint operation across four regulatory areas (cognition, motivation/affect, behaviour, context) and four regulatory phases (forethought/planning, monitoring, control, reflection/evaluation).
>
> **How it works**: The framework maps sixteen cells in a 4×4 matrix. Learners regulate their *cognition* through metacognitive planning (setting learning goals, activating prior knowledge) and monitoring (comprehension checking), control (applying cognitive and metacognitive strategies), and evaluation (judging learning outcomes). They regulate their *motivation/affect* through goal adoption (choosing mastery or performance orientations), monitoring (awareness of anxiety, boredom, interest), control (effort management, self-efficacy maintenance), and evaluation (attributional processes). They regulate their *behaviour* through time management, effort regulation, and help-seeking. They regulate their *context* through environment management and task selection. The [[mslq]] (Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire), developed largely by Pintrich, operationalises these components.
>
> **Distinctive contribution**: Pintrich's unique contribution is the systematic treatment of *motivational self-regulation* — the idea that learners not only regulate their cognition but actively regulate their motivational states, and that motivation regulation is as important as cognitive regulation for learning quality. This is the conceptual bridge point between SDT and SRL: Pintrich's "motivation/affect" regulatory area acknowledges that goal adoption, anxiety management, and interest generation are things learners *do*, not just things that happen to them. This anticipates the SDT-SRL interface without fully theorising it.
>
> **Evidence base**: Extensive research with the [[mslq]] across secondary and higher education contexts, spanning cultures and subject domains. Pintrich's work on goal orientation and strategy use demonstrated that mastery goal orientation predicts deep processing strategies, while performance-avoidance orientation predicts surface strategies and disengagement. The four-area framework has been validated through factor-analytic and path-modelling studies.
>
> **When it shines**: Pintrich's framework is most useful for comprehensive assessment of learning quality across multiple regulatory dimensions simultaneously — it is the "full panel diagnostic" of SRL frameworks, identifying whether difficulties lie in cognitive, motivational, behavioural, or contextual regulatory areas.
>
> **Known limitations**: The framework's comprehensiveness is also its limitation — at sixteen cells, it risks becoming a taxonomy rather than a theory, cataloguing what happens without explaining the causal dynamics. The motivational theory embedded within it is largely drawn from [[achievement-goal-theory]] and expectancy-value theory, without a systematic account of need satisfaction or internalization dynamics.
