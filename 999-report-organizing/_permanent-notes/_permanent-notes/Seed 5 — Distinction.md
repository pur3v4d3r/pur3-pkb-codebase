> [!flashcard] Seed 5 — Distinction
> **Q:** How do mastery-approach and performance-avoidance goal orientations differentially interpret the same comprehension difficulty during reading?
> **A:** Mastery-approach: difficulty is information about what remains to be learned ("what do I need to understand here?"). Performance-avoidance: difficulty is potential evidence of inadequate competence ("if I don't understand this, it means I'm not smart enough") — triggers withdrawal or surface processing to minimize the shame risk.
> **Source:** Phase III
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate

> **Q:** How does scaffolding differ from the ZPD?
> **A:** The ZPD is a theoretical space (the zone of emerging competence between independent and assisted performance); scaffolding is an instructional intervention for working within that space — contingent, calibrated support that is progressively withdrawn as competence develops.

> [!flashcard] Seed 5 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the Dual Register Diagnostic, and how is a Register 1 question distinguished from a Register 2 question?
>
> **A:** The Dual Register Diagnostic classifies EI questions by their answer level: Register 1 questions can be answered by someone who knows only the text (textbase-satisfiable); Register 2 questions require world knowledge not in the text (situation model-requiring). The diagnostic test is: "Could a reader with only this text, and no other knowledge, answer this question?" If yes: Register 1. If no: Register 2. Register 1 questions do not function as comprehension gateways and produce weak EI effects.
>
> **Source:** Phase IV, Section 4.2
>
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
>

>

> [!flashcard] Seed 5 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the difference between metacognitive monitoring failure and motivational disengagement in the context of reading comprehension deficits?
> **A:** Monitoring failure = the reader genuinely does not detect comprehension breakdown (cognitive deficit in the detection mechanism). Motivational disengagement = the reader detects failure but suppresses regulatory response (motivationally driven suppression, often to protect self-efficacy). These two pathways require different instructional responses.
> **Source:** Phase VII, Section 3 (Tensions)
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #metacognition, #motivation, #comprehension-monitoring

> [!flashcard] Seed 5 — Distinction
> **Q:** What are metacognitive experiences (Efklides), and how do they differ from metacognitive knowledge?
> **A:** Metacognitive experiences are online, phenomenological, affective-cognitive states that arise during task processing (feelings of knowing, difficulty, confidence, familiarity). They are situationally specific, affectively toned, and generated by heuristic processes (fluency, familiarity). Metacognitive knowledge is stored, stable, propositional, and retrievable. Metacognitive experiences serve as the primary triggers for regulatory responses and as the interface through which stored knowledge is engaged with ongoing activity.
> **Source:** Phase II.3, Phase III.4
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #metacognitive-experience #metacognitive-feelings #Efklides #metacognitive-knowledge
