> [!section-summary] Section 7 Takeaways
> 1. Integrated, content-embedded instruction — teaching vocabulary, schema activation, inference generation, and metacognitive monitoring as a coordinated system within disciplinary content — consistently outperforms isolated skills instruction.
> 2. Reciprocal teaching's dialogic strategy-role structure makes visible the metacognitive processes of expert readers, and its consistent effect sizes make it one of the most evidence-based reading interventions available.
> 3. Long-run academic reading development requires both strategic investment (explicit instruction, scaffolded practice) and motivational-ecological factors (reading volume, engagement, identity) — neither alone is sufficient.
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 7 Takeaways
> 1. Expert reading depends on continuous metacognitive monitoring that operates at the meta-level, tracking comprehension quality and initiating adaptive responses (fix-up strategies) when failures are detected. Less-skilled readers' primary deficit is often metacognitive rather than cognitive: they do not monitor accurately enough to detect comprehension failures before false confidence sets in.
> 2. Zimmerman's cyclical SRL model provides a practical architecture for reading self-regulation: forethought phase (goal-setting, strategic planning, motivational alignment), performance phase (strategy deployment, comprehension monitoring, fix-up), and self-reflection phase (self-evaluation, attribution, adaptive planning). Each phase requires deliberate skill development.
> 3. Calibration — the alignment of subjective confidence with actual comprehension — is the metacognitive property most directly protective against fluency-illusion-driven study inefficiency. Retrieval practice is simultaneously the most effective calibration training method and the most effective comprehension-strengthening strategy.
> *— [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 7 Takeaways
> 1. Prior knowledge activation is not a motivational warm-up but a cognitive prerequisite — it establishes the schema context within which new information can be meaningfully organized. Effective activation requires specifically eliciting the schemas that new content will instantiate.
> 2. The worked example effect is fundamentally a schema construction effect: worked examples provide explicit organized structure that novices can use to induce the relevant schemas, whereas unaided problem-solving in the absence of schemas produces cognitive overload with minimal learning.
> 3. Conceptual change instruction must create cognitive conflict that makes schema inadequacy vivid before scaffolding schema restructuring — straightforward informational correction is routinely ineffective against embedded misconceptions.
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 7 Takeaways
> 1. The [[nelson-narens-model]] frames metacognition as a monitoring-control system, with monitoring (object → meta level) providing information about cognitive state and control (meta → object level) adjusting cognitive operations in response — both components necessary for effective self-regulation.
> 2. The key monitoring operations — [[judgment-of-learning]], [[feeling-of-knowing]], comprehension monitoring, and calibration — are systematically distorted by the fluency illusion, causing most readers to systematically overestimate their comprehension and terminate study too early.
> 3. A PKB can function as external metacognitive infrastructure, externalizing and persisting the monitoring data that fallible internal metacognition provides transiently — converting the reading system into a genuine monitoring-and-control system operating across time.
> *— [[strategic-reading-systems-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*
