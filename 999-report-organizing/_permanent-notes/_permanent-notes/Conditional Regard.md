# Conditional Regard

> [!definition] Conditional Regard
> **[[conditional-regard]]** — A socialization pattern in which parental (or institutional) approval, love, and acceptance are contingent on meeting specific behavioral or achievement standards. GCT identifies conditional regard as a primary developmental origin of extrinsic aspiration formation — children learn that their worth depends on external indicators of success, and they internalize extrinsic goal contents as central life aspirations. See Sections 3, 8.
> **See also:** [[Unconditional-Positive-Regard]], [[introjected-regulation]], [[Parenting-Styles]]

> [!evidence] Conditional Regard
> The provision of regard, warmth, and love that is contingent on specific performance, compliance, or behavioral criteria rather than unconditionally available to the person as a whole. Conditional regard as a parenting and relational practice is theoretically implicated in the development of introjected regulation and self-worth contingency — the person learns to pursue regard-contingent behaviors as a means of securing the love that should be unconditionally available. *Contrasts with*: Unconditional positive regard [Rogers]. *See also*: [[introjection]], [[Self-Worth-Contingency]], [[contingent-self-esteem]]

> [!evidence] Conditional Regard
> Teacher or parent warmth that is contingent on student performance or compliance — love and approval are available when the student succeeds but withheld when the student fails or resists. A documented form of relatedness thwarting that produces introjected regulation (students internalize the contingency — "I am valuable when I perform") rather than identified or integrated regulation. Associated with high anxiety, fragile self-esteem, and vulnerability to collapse of motivation under failure conditions.

> [!evidence] Conditional Regard
> A parenting or pedagogical practice in which warmth, approval, or affection is made contingent on the child/student performing in desired ways — provided when performance is satisfactory, withheld when it is not. SDT predicts that conditional regard produces introjected regulation (the child works to maintain love and avoid its withdrawal rather than from genuine self-endorsement) and is associated with lower authentic self-esteem, higher contingent self-worth, and more fragile, ego-contingent functioning. It is contrasted with **unconditional regard** — maintaining warmth and care as a baseline, independent of performance, while still providing honest feedback about performance quality.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*

> [!evidence] Conditional Regard
> A controlling socialization technique in which the regard of the socializing agent (parent, teacher, partner) is made contingent on the recipient performing or not performing specified behaviors. Empirically associated with the development of introjected regulation, contingent self-worth, and resistance to subsequent movement up the internalization continuum. The most thoroughly characterized mechanism by which Edge Case 1 (controlled internalization) is produced.
