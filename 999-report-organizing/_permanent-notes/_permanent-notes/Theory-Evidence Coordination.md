# Theory-Evidence Coordination

> [!definition] Theory-Evidence Coordination
> The cognitive capacity to separately evaluate one's existing theoretical commitments and the evidence bearing on them, assigning evidential weight based on the evidence's properties rather than its fit with prior beliefs. Identified by Deanna Kuhn as a central component of scientific reasoning and one of the most difficult to develop. Learners without this capacity systematically distort evidence interpretation to confirm existing theories, undermining productive inductive reasoning.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Theory-Evidence Coordination
> One of the most important and under-developed inductive reasoning skills is the ability to distinguish between a hypothesis and the evidence bearing on it, and to evaluate evidence independently of one's current theoretical commitments. This capacity — called *theory-evidence coordination* by Deanna Kuhn — develops slowly through adolescence and is not fully mature even in many adults. Its development is a legitimate educational goal that requires explicit cultivation.
> *— [[inductive-reasoning-foundational-report-2026-03-06_report]]*
