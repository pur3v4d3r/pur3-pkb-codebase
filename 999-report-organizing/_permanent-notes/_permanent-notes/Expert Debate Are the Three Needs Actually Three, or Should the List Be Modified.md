## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Expert Debate: Are the Three Needs Actually Three, or Should the List Be Modified?
> **Position A (mainstream SDT):** Three needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness) constitute the complete and minimal set of basic psychological needs whose satisfaction is required for sustained well-being and successful internalization.
> **Position B (need-list-revisionists):** Additional needs, particularly **beneficence** (the need to feel that one's actions contribute to others' well-being), **novelty** (the need for variety and exploration), or some form of **physical safety/security**, may also qualify as basic in the SDT sense and should be added to the list.
> **What the debate hinges on:** Whether the candidate additional needs do specific developmental work in the internalization mechanism that the existing three do not already cover, or whether they can be analyzed as derivative or as adjacent to the basic-needs framework without being part of it.
> **Current state:** The mainstream three-need position remains dominant but is not unchallenged. Beneficence has the most empirical support among the candidates, with [[Frank Martela|Martela]] and others producing programmatic work suggesting that satisfaction of beneficence makes contributions to well-being not fully mediated by autonomy, competence, and relatedness.
> **Why the debate matters:** Resolution would either confirm the parsimony of the standard three-need framework or extend the substrate of internalization in ways that would require corresponding extensions to the OIT mechanism.
