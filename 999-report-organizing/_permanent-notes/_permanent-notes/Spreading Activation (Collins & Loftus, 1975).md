# Spreading Activation (Collins & Loftus, 1975)

> [!definition] Spreading Activation (Collins & Loftus, 1975)
> The mechanism by which activation of one concept node in a semantic network propagates along associative links to related nodes, with the strength of propagation determined by associative weight. This mechanism accounts for priming effects, retrieval dynamics, and the associative character of human memory.
>
> **Boundary:** Spreading activation is a model of retrieval dynamics, not a complete model of memory. It does not account for strategic retrieval processes, contextual constraints, or inhibitory mechanisms.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Provides the cognitive-scientific justification for link-based over folder-based PKM organization (Section 3). The link supremacy thesis rests on this mechanism.
>
> **See also:** [[spreading-activation]], [[Associative-Learning]], [[Knowledge-Graph]]

> [!evidence] Spreading Activation (Collins & Loftus, 1975)
> [[spreading-activation]] is a cognitive mechanism in which the activation of a concept node in semantic/associative memory propagates along associative links to neighboring nodes, partially activating them in proportion to link strength. Spreading activation is the mechanism underlying priming effects, associative recall, and the fluency of expert retrieval in familiar domains. Activation spreads in parallel across multiple pathways simultaneously, reaching remote associations through successive hops along the network.
>
> **Boundary:** Spreading activation is not deliberate or conscious search. It is an automatic, parallel process that pre-activates associated concepts before conscious access occurs. The practitioner is not aware of it happening; they experience only its output: certain concepts come to mind more readily than others, and unexpected connections surface during thinking.
>

No PKB implements this automatically; the PKB provides the structural substrate for the practitioner to manually simulate it.
