> The emotional consequences of causal attribution are not peripheral to the motivational system; they constitute its affective engine. Each dimension generates a distinctive family of emotions: locus → pride/shame, stability → hope/hopelessness, controllability → anger/pity/guilt/gratitude. These attribution-dependent emotions are temporally layered, emerge only after causal processing, and function as mediators between dimensional classification and behavioural response. The guilt-shame asymmetry is particularly consequential: guilt (controllable cause) motivates repair and re-engagement, while shame (uncontrollable cause) motivates withdrawal and disengagement. The controllability dimension additionally functions as a moral-inference bridge, transforming causal explanations into evaluations of character and desert.

> The visuospatial sketchpad maintains temporary visual and spatial representations, with evidence suggesting internal differentiation into a visual cache (object features) and inner scribe (spatial/movement information) that parallels the phonological loop's architecture. Neuropsychological dissociations between visual and spatial working memory, combined with neuroimaging connecting visual working memory to perceptual cortex, support a model in which the sketchpad operates through the controlled re-activation of visual perceptual machinery. The sketchpad underpins navigation, imagery, spatial reasoning, reading comprehension (situation models), and mathematics learning. Its connection to Paivio's dual coding theory provides the working memory mechanism for Mayer's cognitive theory of multimedia learning.

> CET generates a differentiated taxonomy of reward contingencies with specific motivational predictions. Task-non-contingent unexpected rewards: no undermining effect. Engagement-contingent and completion-contingent rewards: reliable undermining via controlling mechanism. Performance-contingent rewards: smaller, contextually modifiable undermining effect combining informational and controlling aspects. Verbal rewards: enhancement effect via informational-competence pathway. The overjustification effect is the headline empirical prediction: expected tangible rewards for intrinsically interesting activities undermine subsequent intrinsic motivation by providing external over-justification. The Cameron-Pierce vs. Deci-Koestner-Ryan meta-analytic debate illustrates that theoretical precision in contingency-type distinctions is methodologically essential.

> Radical constructivism (Glasersfeld) takes the epistemological implications of Piaget's constructivism to their limit, arguing that knowledge is not a representation of mind-independent reality but a set of cognitively viable constructions — a position that generates both profound insights and genuine philosophical difficulties. Bruner's cognitive constructivism develops the spiral curriculum and discovery learning as practical instructional realizations of constructivist principles, and his later narrative constructivism extends the framework to meaning-making as storytelling. Social constructionism (Berger, Luckmann) extends constructivist reasoning from individual cognition to social knowledge systems, with implications for curriculum and power.
