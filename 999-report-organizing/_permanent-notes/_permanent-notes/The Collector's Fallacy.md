## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Collector's Fallacy
> The PKM community has a name for the pathology that the fluency illusion produces in knowledge systems: the **[[Collector's Fallacy]]**, coined by Christian Tietze. It is the mistaken belief that gathering information is equivalent to learning it—that a well-stocked note-taking system constitutes a well-developed understanding. The irony is that the more *efficient* a PKM tool is at capturing information, the more aggressively it may foster this fallacy. Speed of collection and depth of understanding are not only uncorrelated; in many cases, they are inversely related, because the friction of slower, effortful processing is precisely what drives encoding into long-term memory.
> *— [[cog-sci-pkm-first-priciples-and-socratic-metacognition-and-its-role-in-pkm_report]]*

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Collector's Fallacy
> A persistent trap in PKM practice is what Christian Tietze termed the "Collector's Fallacy" — the mistaken belief that collecting information is equivalent to learning it. Cognitive science explains precisely why this belief is false: collection involves shallow encoding (classifying, tagging, filing), while learning requires deep encoding (elaborating, connecting, generating). A vault filled with highlighted passages and clipped articles may *feel* like a knowledge base, but if those materials were processed only at the surface level, they remain cognitively inert — present in the system but absent from the learner's retrievable knowledge.
> *— [[pkb-pkm-planning-and-cognitive-science-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*
