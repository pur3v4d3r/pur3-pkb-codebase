# Inner Speech (Vygotsky, 1934/1986; Fernyhough, 2016)

> [!definition] Inner Speech (Vygotsky, 1934/1986; Fernyhough, 2016)
> The internalized, self-directed form of language that functions as a cognitive and self-regulatory tool; characterized by condensed, predicative structure in which the subject is typically omitted. Distinct from silent reading subvocalization (phonological activation for word recognition) and from mind-wandering.
>
> **Boundary:** Inner speech is not equivalent to "thinking in words" in the broad sense; it specifically refers to the self-directed regulatory dialogue that accompanies deliberate cognitive work. Unstructured verbal association or unbidden thought is not inner speech in Vygotsky's technical sense.
>
> **Operational Indicator:** Evaluative commentary, planning language, monitoring statements, and self-efficacy appraisals during deliberate cognitive activity.
>
> **See also:** [[vygotsky]], [[metacognition]], [[self-regulation]], [[stream-of-consciousness]]
