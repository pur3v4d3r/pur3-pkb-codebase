# Active Word-Noticing Disposition (adapted from Schmidt's Noticing Hypothesis, 1990)

> [!definition] Active Word-Noticing Disposition (adapted from Schmidt's Noticing Hypothesis, 1990)
> **Definition:** A metacognitive stance toward reading in which the reader deliberately maintains attention to unfamiliar lexical items, attempts semantic inference from context, flags items for follow-up study, and monitors for vocabulary gaps rather than tolerating them as comprehension noise. Distinguished from passive reading by the deliberate allocation of attentional resources to word-level processing alongside passage-level comprehension. Not natural or automatic; a learnable disposition that requires explicit metacognitive development.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Identified in the reconstructive synthesis as the key variable mediating reading's vocabulary acquisition efficiency. The same reading volume produces substantially different vocabulary outcomes depending on whether the active word-noticing disposition is present.
>
> **Cross-References:** [[metacognitive-monitoring]], [[comprehension-monitoring]], [[self-regulated-learning]]
