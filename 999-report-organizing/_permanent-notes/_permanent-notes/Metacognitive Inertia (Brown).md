# Metacognitive Inertia (Brown)

> [!definition] Metacognitive Inertia (Brown)
> Metacognitive inertia refers to the developmental state in which cognitive strategies and metacognitive knowledge exist in a learner's repertoire but are not spontaneously deployed in appropriate contexts without explicit prompting or scaffolding. Young children may know, when asked, that they should check their work or verify their understanding, but they do not spontaneously perform these regulatory behaviors without adult prompting. Metacognitive development, on this account, involves the progressive internalization and automatization of regulatory behaviors that were initially triggered only by external scaffolding.
>
> **Boundary:** Metacognitive inertia is not a fixed trait but a developmental stage characteristic. With appropriate instruction (explicit strategy teaching, modeling, Socratic questioning, peer dialogue), children can develop more spontaneous metacognitive deployment at younger ages than naive maturationist accounts would predict. The degree of inertia is domain-dependent; children can show more developed metacognitive regulation in personally meaningful and familiar domains than in academic contexts.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Understanding metacognitive inertia provides the developmental rationale for explicit strategy instruction: without explicit teaching, many learners remain in states of metacognitive inertia not because they lack the capacity but because the capacity has never been activated and scaffolded to the point of spontaneous internalization.
>

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Inertia (Brown)
> The developmental state in which cognitive strategies and metacognitive knowledge exist in a learner's repertoire but are not spontaneously deployed in appropriate contexts without explicit prompting. Young children are paradigmatically metacognitively inert: they can follow a strategy when prompted but do not initiate it without external cueing. Overcoming metacognitive inertia — the developmental achievement of spontaneous, self-initiated strategy deployment — is the primary mechanism by which explicit strategy instruction produces lasting metacognitive improvement.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Metacognitive inertia explains why strategy inventories (knowing strategies) and strategy use (deploying strategies) are dissociated in developing learners, and why metacognitive training that produces gains on trained tasks often fails to transfer to novel tasks: the conditional knowledge required to trigger spontaneous deployment has not been built.
>
