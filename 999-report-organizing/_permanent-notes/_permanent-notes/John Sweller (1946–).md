## Additional Material

> [!person] John Sweller (1946–)
> Emeritus Professor, University of New South Wales, Australia. Developed Cognitive Load Theory beginning in 1988, originating the framework from research on problem-solving and the means-ends analysis strategy. Sweller's evolutionary psychology framing (biologically primary vs. secondary knowledge) provided CLT with its theoretical unity beyond empirical findings. His 2010 reconceptualization of germane load represented significant self-correction. CLT's application to instructional design is his primary legacy.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-and-pkm-foundational-report-2026-04-12_report]]*

> [!person] John Sweller (1946–)
> Professor Emeritus of Educational Psychology, University of New South Wales, Australia. Originator of Cognitive Load Theory (1988). Sweller's foundational contribution was recognizing that the information-processing constraints identified by cognitive psychology had direct and systematic implications for instructional design. His subsequent work elaborated the tripartite load taxonomy, identified the major CLT effects, and led the 2010 reconceptualization of germane load.
>
> **Core contributions to this report:** CLT's founding framework, element interactivity construct, effect principles, germane load reconceptualization.
>
> **Key works referenced:** Sweller (1988), Sweller & Chandler (1991, 1992), Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas (1998), Sweller (2010).
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!person] John Sweller (1946–)
> *University of New South Wales, Australia*
> Creator of [[cognitive-load-theory]] (1988), the framework that explains why any secondary task can degrade learning from complex material by consuming finite working memory resources.

> **Relationship to others:** Built directly on Baddeley's WM model and Miller's capacity limits; extended by Paas, van Merriënboer, and Kalyuga.
