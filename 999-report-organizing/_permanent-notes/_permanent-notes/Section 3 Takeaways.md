> [!section-summary] Section 3 Takeaways
> 1. Vocabulary knowledge is the strongest predictor of academic reading comprehension for readers who have mastered decoding — the critical vocabulary layer is Tier 2 general academic vocabulary, not just domain-specific technical terms.
> 2. Achieving 98% vocabulary coverage of academic texts requires approximately 5,000–8,000 general word families plus 2,000–5,000 specialised academic families — far more than everyday reading develops.
> 3. Explicit vocabulary instruction embedded within content learning is more effective than isolated word-list study because it builds the deep, contextuelle word knowledge that supports situation model construction.
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 3 Takeaways
> 1. Reading motivation is irreducibly multidimensional; effective intervention requires targeting specific motivational components (self-efficacy, interest, value, cost) rather than generic "motivation enhancement."
> 2. Interest in academic reading can be actively generated rather than passively awaited through deliberate text selection and epistemic stance cultivation — treating interest as a strategy, not just a personality trait.
> 3. Expectancy-value framing reveals multiple leverage points for reading motivation intervention, particularly utility value and cost reduction, which are often more tractable than intrinsic value for assigned academic content.
> *— [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 3 Takeaways
> 1. Schemas operate through five core mechanisms — activation, instantiation, variable binding, default value assignment, and interactive (top-down/bottom-up) processing — each contributing distinct cognitive functions.
> 2. Default value assignment explains both the efficiency of schema-based comprehension and a large class of systematic memory errors — default inferences are frequently misremembered as explicitly stated.
> 3. The effort after meaning describes the cognitive system's drive toward schematic coherence, which is responsible both for fast comprehension and for systematic distortion of schema-inconsistent information.
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 3 Takeaways
> 1. The [[generation-effect]] — robust across decades of research — demonstrates that self-generated information is encoded more deeply and retained more durably than passively received information, via mechanisms of effortful processing, distinctive encoding, and prediction-error signals.
> 2. Generation strategies for academic reading include pre-reading prediction, generative titling, look-away reformulation, and blanking-and-completion techniques, all of which convert passive reception into active construction.
> 3. A PKB built around generative note-making rather than note-taking treats every note as a self-authored knowledge artifact — the primary encoding act of the reading session — and is thus a genuine implementation of the generation principle at the system level.
> *— [[strategic-reading-systems-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*
