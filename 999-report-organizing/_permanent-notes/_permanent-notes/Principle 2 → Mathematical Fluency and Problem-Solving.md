## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Principle 2 → Mathematical Fluency and Problem-Solving
> Mathematical problem-solving requires fluent access to procedural knowledge (how to execute operations) and conceptual understanding (what the operations mean and when to apply them). These two capabilities are mutually enabling: conceptual understanding makes procedures meaningful and easier to remember; procedural fluency frees working memory for conceptual reasoning. Students who enter a course with deficits in both face the mathematical analogue of the vocabulary-comprehension spiral: every attempt to understand a new concept is hampered by the cognitive load of managing unfluent procedures; every attempt to practise procedures is hampered by the absence of conceptual anchors. The same intervention logic applies: targeting both simultaneously, with fluency-building support that temporarily compensates for the bootstrapping gap, is more effective than targeting either alone.
> *— [[vocabulary-acquisition-reading-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
