# Generation Effect (Slamecka & Graf, 1978)

> [!definition] Generation Effect (Slamecka & Graf, 1978)
> The generation effect refers to the superior memory for self-produced information compared to information that is merely read. The mechanism operates at the level of encoding: generating an item requires active semantic processing (retrieving knowledge, formulating associations, applying rules) that deepens the memory trace beyond what reading the same item produces.
>
> **Boundary:** The generation effect requires that the generated material be substantially correct. Generating incorrect information (errors of commission) can, under certain conditions, produce interference. Effect magnitude is largest when generation requires semantic—rather than purely orthographic—processing.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** For academic reading, the generation effect means that pausing at section boundaries to generate summaries, questions, or predictions produces substantially better retention than reading through without such pauses—even if the generated content is incomplete or imperfect.
>
> **See also:** [[the-generation-effect]], [[generation-effect]], [[generative-learning]], [[generative-learning-theory]]

> [!evidence] Generation Effect (Slamecka & Graf, 1978)
> The empirical finding that information actively generated by the learner — produced in response to a partial cue or constructive prompt — is remembered significantly better than the same information passively received through reading.
>
> **Boundary:** The generation effect does not apply when the generation task is so difficult that it prevents acquisition of the target information. The optimal generation difficulty is within the learner's reach — effortful but achievable.
>
> **Operational Indicator:** The generation effect is leveraged when the reader pauses to produce their own explanation, paraphrase, or causal account rather than reading the text's explanation passively.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The generation effect provides the empirical basis for the Generation Constraint — the claim that situation models require active construction and cannot be built through passive reception.
>
> **See also:** [[the-generation-effect]], [[self-explanation-effect]], [[retrieval-practice]], [[Testing-Effect]]

> [!evidence] Generation Effect (Slamecka & Graf, 1978)
> The robust finding that actively generating information (through recall, paraphrasing, elaboration, or transformation) produces better memory than passively receiving the same information. Effect sizes typically range from d = 0.40 to d = 0.87.
>
> **Boundary:** Requires successful generation — generating errors can be counterproductive. Most powerful when generation involves effortful retrieval or transformation.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The decisive factor in the walking-reading question: active PKB engagement invokes the generation effect while walking-reading does not.
>
