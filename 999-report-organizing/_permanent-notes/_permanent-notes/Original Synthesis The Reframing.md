## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Original Synthesis: The Reframing
> The deepest speculative insight from this analysis is a reframing of the walking reader's practice: **walking-while-reading is not a single behavior to be evaluated; it is one phase of a two-phase cognitive workflow.** The walking phase serves as prime (arousal regulation, first-pass exposure, material triage). The sitting phase serves as process (deep comprehension, integration, externalization). Evaluating walking-while-reading in isolation — "is it good or bad?" — misses the point. The right question is: **how should the two phases be designed, sequenced, and transitioned to maximize the combined workflow's cognitive yield?**
>
> This reframing transforms the walking reader's dilemma ("should I keep walking or make myself sit?") into a design question ("how should I allocate walking and sitting across my reading session to optimize comprehension and PKB integration?"). The answer involves neither abandoning walking (which would sacrifice a genuine arousal benefit and potentially worsen reading through restlessness) nor clinging to pure walking-reading (which sacrifices deep-processing capacity and note-making). It involves **intentional cycling** between a priming phase and a processing phase, with the transition cued by metacognitive awareness of difficulty changes.
> *— [[walking-academic-reading-working-memory-deep-dive-2026-04-13_report]]*
