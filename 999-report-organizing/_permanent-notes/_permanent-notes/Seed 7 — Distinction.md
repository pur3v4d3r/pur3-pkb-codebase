> [!flashcard] Seed 7 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the key distinction between constructivism as epistemology and constructivism as pedagogy?
> **A:** Constructivism as epistemology describes HOW learning occurs (knowledge is constructed, not received). Constructivism as pedagogy makes claims about HOW instruction should proceed. The inference from the epistemological claim to minimally-guided instruction is a separate theoretical step — not logically entailed — and has been extensively empirically contested.
> **Source:** Phase III, V
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #constructivism, #pedagogy, #epistemology, #critical-thinking

> [!flashcard] Seed 7 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is regulatory poverty, and how does it differ from monitoring inaccuracy?
> **A:** Regulatory poverty is the condition in which accurate monitoring of a performance discrepancy fails to produce adaptive control because the learner lacks alternative strategies to deploy — the strategic repertoire is insufficient for corrective response. It is distinct from monitoring inaccuracy (where the detection itself is wrong) and motivational interruption (where the strategy is available but motivationally suppressed).
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #regulatory-poverty, #calibration-action-gap, #metacognitive-regulation, #metacognitive-strategy

> [!flashcard] Seed 7 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the difference between vocabulary breadth and vocabulary depth, and which should a reader prioritize first?
>
> **A:** *Vocabulary breadth*: the number of word families for which a reader has at least recognition-level knowledge — knowing a word exists and has an approximate meaning. *Vocabulary depth*: the richness and precision of representation for known words — including collocations, register, morphological family, and precise semantic content. Priority depends on coverage level: breadth should be prioritized first (until 98% coverage of target genres is achieved) because coverage-level effects are threshold-gated. Once breadth reaches the 98% threshold, depth becomes more valuable — precise knowledge enables more accurate inference, better production, and richer comprehension.
>
> **Source:** Phase V, Depth-Breadth Trade-off; Appendix C
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Type:** Distinction
> **Tags:** #vocabulary-depth, #vocabulary-breadth, #coverage-threshold, #reading-strategy

> [!flashcard] Seed 7 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the "partial knowledge problem" and why is it a metacognitive challenge rather than merely a knowledge gap?
>
> **A:** Partial knowledge refers to a lexical knowledge state between zero knowledge and full productive mastery — the reader recognises the word and has a vague sense of its meaning but cannot define it precisely or use it correctly in novel contexts. It is a *metacognitive* challenge because the fluency of recognising the orthographic form generates a familiarity signal that the monitoring system misinterprets as "I know this word." This prevents the reader from detecting the gap and triggering the inferential or consultative behaviours that would convert partial knowledge to full knowledge. The fluency signal and the knowledge signal are experientially indistinguishable.
>
> **Source:** Phase IV
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Distinction
> **Tags:** #metacognition, #vocabulary, #shallow-processing, #calibration
> *— [[vocabulary-acquisition-reading-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*
