## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Orientations as Interpersonal Cascades
> Beyond their effects on how individuals process motivational information, causality orientations influence the motivational climate they create for others. Research by Koestner, Bernieri, and Zuckerman (1992) found that control-oriented individuals were rated as more controlling in their interactions with others — they tended to structure interactions with more directive, evaluative, and pressure-laden communication. Autonomy-oriented individuals, conversely, tended to create more autonomy-supportive conditions for their interaction partners. This interpersonal transmission means that orientations have cascading effects: the control-oriented manager creates a more controlling environment for subordinates, whose motivational functioning shifts toward more controlled forms, increasing their likelihood of developing control-oriented patterns over time. The self-reinforcing cycle is not merely intrapersonal — it operates interpersonally as well, spreading through social systems in ways that give causality orientations a kind of motivational ecology.
> *— [[causality-orientations-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-13_report]]*
