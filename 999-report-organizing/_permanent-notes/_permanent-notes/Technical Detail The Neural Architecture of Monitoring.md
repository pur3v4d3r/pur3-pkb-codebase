## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technical Detail: The Neural Architecture of Monitoring
> Fleming and Dolan (2012) proposed a hierarchical model where:
>
> 1. **Sensory/memory cortex** generates the object-level representation (the percept, the memory trace, the comprehension state)
> 2. **Lateral prefrontal cortex** evaluates the quality of the object-level representation — a first-order monitoring process
> 3. **Anterior prefrontal cortex** generates the meta-level judgment by integrating information about Object-level state quality with contextual expectations — this is the metacognitive judgment proper
>
> Crucially, monitoring ACCURACY depends on the **fidelity of the signal** from sensory/memory cortex to aPFC. If the signal is noisy (low signal-to-noise ratio), the monitoring judgment will be poorly calibrated even if the monitoring circuit itself is intact. This maps elegantly onto the SDT framework: discriminability ($d'$) reflects the quality of the signal reaching the monitoring circuit.
>
> Fleming, Weil, Nagy, Dolan & Rees (2010) found that individual differences in metacognitive sensitivity (measured as SDT $d'$ for confidence judgments) correlated with gray matter volume in anterior prefrontal cortex. This suggests a structural neuroanatomical basis for individual differences in monitoring accuracy.
>
> **Precision:** The hierarchical model is schematic. The actual neural circuits are distributed and involve interaction between PFC, anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), insula, and parietal cortex.
> **Dependencies:** Requires neuroanatomy basics; see [[prefrontal-cortex]] and materials on [[cognitive-architecture|cognitive architecture]].
