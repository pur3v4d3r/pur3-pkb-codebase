> [!open-question] Expansion Topics for PKB Development
> > [!topic-idea] [[Conceptual-Change-Theory]] — Schema Restructuring in Education
> > *Connection:* Conceptual change theory provides the most direct empirical research program on schema restructuring in educational contexts — exactly the most cognitively demanding and most educationally important form of schema modification.
> > *Depth Potential:* The Vosniadou, Chi, and diSessa branches of conceptual change theory each offer distinct frameworks (framework theory, ontological categories, p-prims respectively) that extend and challenge schema-theoretic accounts.
> > *PKB Role:* Central bridge between Schema Theory and learning science applications; prerequisite for understanding why knowledge-rich curriculum matters and why misconceptions are so persistent.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[predictive-processing]] — A Neural Implementation of Schemas?
> > *Connection:* Predictive processing (Clark, Friston) proposes that the brain is a hierarchical prediction machine that generates top-down predictions and updates on prediction error — a neural implementation story with significant functional overlap with schema theory's top-down processing account.
> > *Depth Potential:* Exploring the mapping between predictive processing accounts and schema theory may provide the neural implementation story that classical (symbolic) schema theory lacked.
> > *PKB Role:* High-priority bridge note connecting Schema Theory to computational neuroscience and philosophy of mind branches of the PKB.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[Early-Maladaptive-Schemas]] — Clinical Applications of Schema Theory
> > *Connection:* Young's Schema-Focused Therapy is the most developed clinical application of schema theory, applying the core theoretical framework (schemas as overlearned, largely automatic interpretive frameworks that resist modification) to personality-level psychological difficulty.
> > *Depth Potential:* Rich clinical literature with strong empirical support (for personality disorders, chronic depression) and deep theoretical grounding in schema theory and attachment theory.
> > *PKB Role:* Connects Schema Theory to clinical psychology, mental health, and self-knowledge branches of the PKB.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[spreading-activation]] — Neural Substrate of Schema Activation
> > *Connection:* Spreading activation is the mechanism at the network level by which schema activation occurs — activation spreads from initial cues through associative links to related nodes, with schemas representing clusters of activated nodes.
> > *Depth Potential:* Connections to Collins and Quillian's (1969) semantic network model, Collins and Loftus (1975) spreading activation theory, and contemporary connectionist implementations.
> > *PKB Role:* Mechanism-level foundation note for understanding how schemas are retrieved; connects to semantic memory, neural network modeling, and priming effect branches.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[schema-automation]] — From Controlled to Automatic Processing
> > *Connection:* Schema automation — the transition from effortful controlled processing to rapid automatic processing through practice — is one of the most practically important aspects of schema acquisition and the primary mechanism of expertise efficiency.
> > *Depth Potential:* Connections to Logan's instance theory, Schneider and Shiffrin's controlled vs. automatic processing distinction, and deliberate practice research.
> > *PKB Role:* Essential mechanism-level note connecting Schema Theory to skill acquisition, expertise development, and cognitive load reduction branches of the PKB.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[dual-coding-theory]] — Integrating Verbal and Imagistic Schemas
> > *Connection:* Paivio's Dual Coding Theory proposes that knowledge is encoded in both verbal (symbolic) and imagistic (analogical) systems — a framework that extends schema theory to include non-propositional, imagistic schemas and their distinct properties.
> > *Depth Potential:* Rich connections to mental imagery research, multimedia learning theory (Mayer), and embodied cognition; important for understanding how schemas interact with visual-spatial processing.
> > *PKB Role:* Bridge note connecting Schema Theory to multimedia learning, visual-spatial cognition, and memory encoding branches.
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
