## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technical Detail: The Operational Markers of External Regulation
> Empirically, external regulation is identified by a constellation of features that researchers in the SDT tradition have learned to detect through self-report instruments such as the SRQ (Self-Regulation Questionnaire) family and through behavioral observation. Key markers include:
>
> - **Reasons given for the behavior** appeal exclusively to external consequences: "because I'll be punished if I don't," "because I'll get a reward," "because someone is watching."
> - **Behavioral persistence** is contingent on the salience of the external contingency; the behavior decays rapidly when monitoring is withdrawn or when the contingency is removed.
> - **Affective accompaniment** is typically negative or neutral; the behavior is performed without engagement, often with visible reluctance.
> - **Locus of causality** (in [[deci-and-ryan|Heider's terms, adopted by SDT]]) is perceived as external — the person experiences the behavior as caused by something outside themselves rather than chosen by themselves.
>
> **Precision:** This is the operational specification used in mainstream SDT measurement. Boundary cases (e.g., where external contingency has been internalized as introjection) require finer instruments to distinguish.
> **Dependencies:** Assumes familiarity with the [[deci-and-ryan|perceived locus of causality]] construct and with self-report measurement methodology.
