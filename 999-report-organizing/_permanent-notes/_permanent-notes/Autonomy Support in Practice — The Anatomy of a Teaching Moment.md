## Examples

> [!example] Autonomy Support in Practice — The Anatomy of a Teaching Moment
> Consider two teachers presenting the same mathematics lesson. Teacher A announces: "Today you will complete problems 1-20. You have 40 minutes. This will be graded." Teacher B says: "Today's problems connect to the modeling project you've been developing. I've selected these because they build the tools you'll need for next week's design challenge. You can work through them in any order, and I'd suggest starting with whichever area you found most difficult last time." Both teachers assign the same problems. The difference is in the functional significance of the event: Teacher A's framing emphasizes external control (deadlines, grades); Teacher B's framing provides rationale (connection to valued goals), acknowledges perspective (starting point flexibility), and satisfies competence (calibrated scaffolding). The predicted outcomes — deeper processing, greater persistence, better transfer for Teacher B's students — follow from CET and OIT's mechanisms without requiring any new theoretical apparatus.
