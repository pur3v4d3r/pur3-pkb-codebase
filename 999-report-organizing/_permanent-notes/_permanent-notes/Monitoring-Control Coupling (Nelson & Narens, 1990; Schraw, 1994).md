# Monitoring-Control Coupling (Nelson & Narens, 1990; Schraw, 1994)

> [!definition] Monitoring-Control Coupling (Nelson & Narens, 1990; Schraw, 1994)
> Monitoring-control coupling refers to the degree of functional integration between the monitoring and control components of metacognitive regulation — specifically, the extent to which monitoring signals reliably trigger appropriate regulatory responses. High coupling characterizes expert metacognitive regulation: accurate monitoring rapidly and consistently produces adaptive control adjustments. Low coupling characterizes regulatory failure even in the presence of adequate monitoring capacity: detection of difficulty does not translate into corrective action.
>
> **Boundary:** Coupling is not a single binary state but a gradient variable sensitive to task demands, motivational context, and cognitive load. The same learner may show high coupling in a familiar domain and low coupling in a novel domain.
>
> **Common Misconception:** Low coupling is frequently attributed to monitoring failure (inaccurate detection), when in practice it may reflect adequate monitoring with absent or blocked control — the Calibration-Action Gap mechanism described in Phase IV.
>
