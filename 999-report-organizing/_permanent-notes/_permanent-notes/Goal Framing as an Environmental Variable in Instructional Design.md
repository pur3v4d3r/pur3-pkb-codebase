## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Goal Framing as an Environmental Variable in Instructional Design
> The experimental evidence from Vansteenkiste and colleagues has a profound implication that has not been sufficiently absorbed by instructional design practice: the *language* used to justify and frame learning activities functions as a motivational environment variable, activating either intrinsic or extrinsic goal processing in learners *regardless of their pre-existing dispositions*. This means that instructional designers are, in effect, continuously making goal content choices through their rationale framing — and they are doing so largely inadvertently. Most contemporary learning objectives are framed in extrinsic terms ("upon completing this module, you will be able to demonstrate X on the assessment"). This framing may be systematically undermining the kind of deep processing and intrinsic motivation that leads to durable learning. The implication is not merely theoretical — it constitutes a specific, testable design intervention.
> *— [[goal-contents-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-13_report]]*
