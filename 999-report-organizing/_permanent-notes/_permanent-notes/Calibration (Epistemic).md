# Calibration (Epistemic)

> [!definition] Calibration (Epistemic)
> The degree to which an agent's confidence in their beliefs matches the frequency with which such beliefs are true. A well-calibrated agent who expresses 70% confidence in a class of claims is right approximately 70% of the time. Overconfidence — the prevalent human tendency — is the systematic practical violation of fallibilist norms. Calibration research connects epistemology to cognitive psychology and decision science.

> [!evidence] Calibration (Epistemic)
> The degree of alignment between a person's confidence in their beliefs or judgments and the actual accuracy of those beliefs or judgments. Well-calibrated thinkers are approximately as confident as their evidence and reasoning warrant — neither systematically overconfident nor underconfident. Metacognitive monitoring is a major determinant of calibration quality; poor metacognitive accuracy produces systematic miscalibration.
> *— [[metacognition-and-critical-thinking-foundational-report-2026-03-05_report]]*
