## Examples

> [!example] The Continuum Made Concrete: A Single Behavior Across Five Architectures
> Consider the behavior of a graduate student writing a dissertation chapter:
>
> - **External:** She writes only because her advisor has set a deadline and the consequences of missing it would be severe; if she could avoid the work without consequence, she would.
> - **Introjected:** She writes because if she does not, she will feel like a failure, a fraud, an unworthy person; she writes to silence the internal accuser whose voice will not let her rest.
> - **Identified:** She writes because she has decided that completing the doctorate matters for the career she wants and the contribution she wants to make; the writing is hard, but the value it serves is one she has examined and endorsed.
> - **Integrated:** She writes because the doctorate, the career, the contribution, and the kind of person she is becoming have all been thought through together, and the writing takes its place in an integrated identity in which trade-offs with other parts of her life have already been considered and resolved.
> - **Intrinsic:** She writes because she finds the activity of working out the argument, of choosing the words, of seeing the chapter take shape, deeply engaging in itself; she would write whether or not it were required.
>
> The behavior — typing words into a document — is identical across all five. The regulation is categorically different. And the predictions about persistence, well-being, and quality of work that follow from the difference are some of the most empirically robust findings in the SDT literature.
