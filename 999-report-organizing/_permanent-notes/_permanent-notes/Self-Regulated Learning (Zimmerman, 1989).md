# Self-Regulated Learning (Zimmerman, 1989)

> [!definition] Self-Regulated Learning (Zimmerman, 1989)
> The active, cyclical process by which learners set goals, select and deploy learning strategies, monitor their own performance, and adjust their approach based on feedback. SRL is cyclical — each learning episode feeds back into subsequent forethought and planning. It is distinguished from externally regulated learning by the locus of control: the learner, rather than a teacher or curriculum, drives the regulatory process.

> [!evidence] Self-Regulated Learning (Zimmerman, 1989)
> A cyclical, goal-directed learning process in which the learner proactively regulates their own cognitive, behavioral, and motivational resources. The cycle comprises forethought (goal-setting, self-efficacy appraisal, planning), performance (strategy deployment, self-monitoring), and self-reflection (self-evaluation, attribution, affect, adaptation). SRL is both a model of what effective learners do and a framework for designing instructional interventions.
> *— [[metacognition-motivation-foundational-report-2026-03-05_report]]*
