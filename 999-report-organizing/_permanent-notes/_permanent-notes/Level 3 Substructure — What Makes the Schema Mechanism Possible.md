# Level 3: Substructure — What Makes the Schema Mechanism Possible

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Level 3: Substructure — What Makes the Schema Mechanism Possible
> **Zoom progression:** At Level 2, we saw schemas as constraint-satisfaction engines: they activate top-down predictions, generate inferences, fill defaults, and constrain comprehension. Level 3 asks: *what is the representational architecture that implements these mechanisms?* What are schemas made of — not at the computational level (what they do) but at the representational and implementational level (how they are structured and potentially instantiated in neural tissue)?
> **What you'll see at this level:** The connectionist architecture underlying schema-like behavior; distributed representations; schema hierarchies and embedded sub-schemas; the distinction between symbolic and subsymbolic schema accounts; the relationship between schemas and propositional networks; and the critical issue of how schemas are organized in memory ([[long-term-memory]]) without explicit boundaries.
> **Specialist value:** The substructural question is where schema theory connects to both computational modeling and neuroscience. Understanding it is prerequisite to evaluating competing theoretical accounts of schemas (symbolic vs. connectionist vs. predictive processing) and to understanding why schema construction is cognitively difficult.
> *— [[schema-and-how-they-work-deep-dive-2026-04-08_report]]*
