## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Calibration Problem as a Translational Bottleneck Between Monitoring and Regulation
> A way of understanding why calibration error is so resistant to correction is to recognize that the monitoring system was not designed (in either the evolutionary or the developmental sense) to deliver absolute accuracy; it was designed to support real-time decisions about whether to continue or intervene. For these decisions, sensitivity is more important than calibration — what matters is whether the reader can distinguish a passage that needs more work from one that does not, not whether the absolute confidence level is correctly mapped to probability of being correct. Evolution and development optimized for a use case (relative comparison) that does not require what the calibration paradigm tests for (absolute mapping). This means calibration error is not a *bug* in the monitoring system; it is a *feature* of a system optimized for a different purpose. The implication for instruction is that calibration cannot be fixed by training the monitoring system to be more accurate in its absolute outputs; it must be worked around by introducing external checks (testing, explanation, application) that supply the absolute information the monitoring system cannot generate. (Original integration; consistent with the calibration literature but framed in this way to clarify why the standard intervention pattern — train monitoring, expect calibration to follow — has produced disappointing results.)
