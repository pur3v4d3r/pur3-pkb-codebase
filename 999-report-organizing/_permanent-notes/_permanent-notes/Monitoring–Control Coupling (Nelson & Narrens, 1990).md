# Monitoring–Control Coupling (Nelson & Narrens, 1990)

> [!definition] Monitoring–Control Coupling (Nelson & Narrens, 1990)
> [[monitoring-control-coupling|Monitoring–control coupling]] refers to the degree to which metacognitive monitoring information is effectively translated into metacognitive control actions. Tight coupling means that accurate monitoring leads to adaptive control (e.g., allocating more study time to items judged as poorly learned). [[monitoring-regulation-decoupling|Decoupling]] occurs when monitoring information is available but is not acted upon, or when control actions do not correspond to monitoring signals.
>
> **Boundary:** Coupling is a relational property of the monitoring–control system, not a property of either channel in isolation.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The coupling concept explains why monitoring accuracy alone is insufficient for effective self-regulation — the control channel must respond appropriately to monitoring signals.
>
> **See also:** [[monitoring-control-coupling]], [[monitoring-regulation-coupling]], [[monitoring-regulation-decoupling]]
