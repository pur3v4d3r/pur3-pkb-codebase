# Declarative, Procedural, and Conditional Metacognitive Knowledge

> [!definition] **Declarative, Procedural, and Conditional Metacognitive Knowledge** *(from [[srl-forethought-self-reflection-pkb-focused-analysis-2026-03-24]])*
> **Declarative Metacognitive Knowledge:** What one knows *about* cognition — knowledge about oneself as a learner ("I tend to skim when tired and miss important qualifications"), about task demands ("dense theoretical texts require slower reading and more monitoring than narrative texts"), and about strategies ("elaborative interrogation produces deeper understanding than passive re-reading").
> 
> **Procedural Metacognitive Knowledge:** Knowledge of *how* to implement cognitive strategies — knowing not just that elaborative interrogation exists, but how to conduct it effectively during actual reading.
> 
> **Conditional Metacognitive Knowledge:** Knowledge of *when* and *why* to use specific strategies — understanding that elaborative interrogation is most valuable during initial processing of new frameworks, while spacing and retrieval practice are more valuable for consolidating already-understood material.
> 
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The development of all three types is required for sophisticated Forethought and Self-Reflection. Declarative knowledge enables accurate self-assessment; procedural knowledge enables effective strategy execution; conditional knowledge enables optimal strategy selection during Forethought planning.
> 
> **Cross-References:** [[declarative-metacognitive-knowledge]], [[conditional-metacognitive-knowledge]], [[Declarative,-Procedural,-and-Conditional-Metacognitive-Knowledge]]

Without active monitoring during the Performance Phase — comprehension checkpoints, confusion flagging, goal tracking — the Self-Reflection Phase has only subjective impressions to evaluate, which are distorted by the Fluency Illusion. Accurate self-reflection requires real-time monitoring data.
**Source:** Part VII — Metacognitive Monitoring
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Type:** Connection
**Tags:** #metacognitive-monitoring #self-reflection #comprehension-monitoring

> [!flashcard] **Card 9** *(from [[srl-forethought-self-reflection-pkb-focused-analysis-2026-03-24]])*
> **Q:** Seed 9
> **A:** **Q:** What are the three types of metacognitive knowledge according to Flavell, and what role does each play in the Forethought/Self-Reflection cycle?
**A:** Declarative (knowing about yourself, tasks, strategies — used in Forethought to select appropriate strategies and in Self-Reflection to recognize what went wrong), Procedural (knowing how to implement strategies — used in the Performance Phase), and Conditional (knowing when and why to use strategies — used in both Forethought to select and in Self-Reflection to identify which strategy should have been used instead).
**Source:** Part VII — Metacognitive Awareness
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Type:** Connection
**Tags:** #metacognition #flavell #forethought #self-reflection

> [!flashcard] **Card 10** *(from [[srl-forethought-self-reflection-pkb-focused-analysis-2026-03-24]])*
> **Q:** Seed 10
> **A:** **Q:** What is the Cold Reconstruction protocol and what problem does it solve?
**A:** The Cold Reconstruction protocol involves writing everything you can remember about a text immediately after reading, before consulting any notes, and then comparing this reconstruction to the actual text. It solves the Fluency Illusion problem: because recognition is contaminated by familiarity (making comprehension feel higher than it is), only retrieval-based testing — like reconstruction from memory — produces an accurate assessment of what was genuinely understood.
**Source:** Part V — Zone 1
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Type:** Application

> [!flashcard] **Card 11** *(from [[srl-forethought-self-reflection-pkb-focused-analysis-2026-03-24]])*
> **Q:** Seed 11
> **A:** **Q:** What is the theoretical distinction between goal proximity and goal type in the Forethought Phase?
**A:** Goal proximity refers to the temporal distance between goal and achievement (proximal = achievable today; distal = long-term). Goal type refers to the evaluative orientation (mastery — focused on learning; performance — focused on demonstrating competence). Both are independent dimensions: a proximal goal can be mastery-oriented or performance-oriented. For PKB reading practice, the optimal combination is proximal + mastery-oriented, which provides both direction (outcome goal) and self-efficacy protection (process goal) simultaneously.
**Source:** Part II — Subsystem 1a
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Type:** Distinction
**Tags:** #goal-setting #goal-proximity #goal-type #forethought

> [!flashcard] **Card 12** *(from [[srl-forethought-self-reflection-pkb-focused-analysis-2026-03-24]])*
> **Q:** Seed 12
> **A:** **Q:** Explain the five feedback channels through which Self-Reflection generates the next Forethought Phase.
**A:** (1) Self-Efficacy Recalibration — updated confidence based on accurate self-assessment; (2) Prior Knowledge Update — new understanding from the session that enriches the PKB reconnaissance in the next Forethought; (3) Strategic Knowledge Refinement — information about which strategies worked/failed; (4) Goal Standard Recalibration — whether goal difficulty was appropriate; (5) Motivational Trajectory Maintenance — whether adaptive or defensive inferences were drawn.
**Source:** Part VI — The Five Feedback Channels
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Type:** Process

> [!flashcard] **Card 13** *(from [[srl-forethought-self-reflection-pkb-focused-analysis-2026-03-24]])*
> **Q:** Seed 13
> **A:** **Q:** What does it mean for the SRL cycle to function as a "self-improving algorithm," and what determines whether it does?
**A:** The SRL cycle is self-improving when each cycle generates better inputs for the next — calibrated self-efficacy, richer prior knowledge, refined strategic knowledge, more accurate goal standards, sustained adaptive motivation. This is determined primarily by the quality of the Self-Reflection Phase: when it produces accurate self-evaluation, controllable attributions, and adaptive inferences, each cycle's output enriches the next cycle's input. When Self-Reflection is skipped or produces defensive inferences, cycles remain episodically isolated and do not compound.
**Source:** Part VI — Synthesis
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Type:** Synthesis
**Tags:** #srl-cycle #self-reflection #compounding #deliberate-practice

> [!flashcard] **Card 14** *(from [[srl-forethought-self-reflection-pkb-focused-analysis-2026-03-24]])*
> **Q:** Seed 14
> **A:** **Q:** What is the "Early Implementation Dip" and how should a PKB learner respond to it?
**A:** The Early Implementation Dip is the initial performance degradation that occurs when learners begin implementing explicit self-regulatory protocols — because the cognitive overhead of the new practices competes with resources available for content processing. It is normal, temporary, and expected. The correct response is to persist with process-goal evaluation standards (counting success by protocol execution, not comprehension outcome), scaffold the protocol by implementing one zone at a time, and explicitly expect the dip rather than interpreting it as evidence that the protocol is wrong.
**Source:** Part VIII — Protecting Self-Determination
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Type:** Application
**Tags:** #self-efficacy #intrinsic-motivation #implementation

> [!flashcard] **Card 15** *(from [[srl-forethought-self-reflection-pkb-focused-analysis-2026-03-24]])*
> **Q:** Seed 15
> **A:** **Q:** How does the Motivational Beliefs subsystem "corrupt" the Task Analysis subsystem when self-efficacy is low?
**A:** Low self-efficacy operates as a filter on goal-setting: learners with low self-efficacy for a specific reading task set more superficial goals (because they expect to fail at deeper ones) and select less demanding strategies — both invisibly and often below conscious awareness. This means that motivational impairments in Subsystem 2 produce systematically less ambitious goals and less effective strategy choices in Subsystem 1, creating a self-limiting dynamic that reinforces itself across cycles.
**Source:** Part II — The Hidden Corruption Mechanism

**Type:** Synthesis

Classrooms: Goals, structures, and student motivation. *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 84(3), 261–271.**: The defining paper for the mastery/performance goal orientation distinction as applied to educational settings. Grounds the language bank in Template 4 and the motivational architecture in Part VIII. Directly applicable to the design of self-directed learning environments.

Motivational processes affecting learning. *American Psychologist*, 41(10), 1040–1048.**: Dweck's foundational articulation of entity vs. incremental theories of intelligence and their relationship to achievement goal orientations.
