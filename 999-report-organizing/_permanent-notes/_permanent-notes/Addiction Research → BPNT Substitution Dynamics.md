## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Addiction Research → BPNT: Substitution Dynamics
> The compulsive, rigidity-producing pattern of substitute need satisfaction maps closely onto mechanisms described in addiction research. Koob and Volkow's (2010) allostatic model of addiction proposes that addictive substances initially activate reward circuits in a way that mimics genuine motivational satisfaction, but chronic use establishes a new baseline that requires continued substance use merely to avoid withdrawal — a condition they call *allostatic load*. The structural parallel to BPNT's substitute satisfaction is striking: activities that substitute for genuine need satisfaction may initially provide relief from need deprivation, but chronic reliance on them creates a motivational architecture that requires continuous maintenance and that progressively crowds out genuine need-satisfying activities. This is speculative as a direct mechanistic parallel, but it suggests that the "dark side" patterns documented in BPNT may involve neurobiological substrates similar to those studied in addiction, a connection that has received insufficient theoretical attention.
> *— [[basic-psychological-needs-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-13_report]]*
