## Additional Material

> [!section-summary] Section 8 Takeaways
> 1. Motivated academic reading is an emergent property of four interacting systems: cognitive capacity, processing strategies, motivational orientation, and metacognitive monitoring. Each can be a limiting factor regardless of the others' development.
> 2. The virtuous cycle (comprehension → competence satisfaction → motivation → continued reading → deeper comprehension) can only operate when all four systems meet minimum threshold levels. The vicious cycle operates when the cognitive or motivational system is below threshold, requiring deliberate identification and targeted intervention at the weakest link.
> 3. The developmental trajectory has five ordered stages, each addressing prerequisite conditions for the next. Adults returning to academic reading or entering new domains begin at Stage 1 regardless of their general reading sophistication, because domain-specific cognitive prerequisites must be built before domain-specific strategies and motivational architectures can operate.
> *— [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 8 Takeaways
> 1. Schema Theory and Cognitive Load Theory are deeply complementary frameworks — CLT provides the resource-constraint account of why schema construction and automation are demanding; Schema Theory provides the representational account of what is being built and what is being automated.
> 2. The tension between Schema Theory's symbolic architecture and connectionist/PDP models has not been resolved; the practical resolution has been to treat schema language as a functional description level that does not commit to a particular neural implementation.
> 3. Challenges from situated, embodied, and dynamic cognition programs are genuine — schemas are not decontextualized, static structures but are shaped by situational, cultural, and bodily factors. Classical schema theory remains predictively powerful, but its scope and the conditionality of its predictions must be understood in light of these situated perspectives.
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 8 Takeaways
> 1. [[self-determination-theory]] provides the motivational architecture for sustainable reading engagement: autonomous motivation (identified, integrated, intrinsic) predicts consistent strategy use and long-term engagement more reliably than effort-based or disciplinary models.
> 2. The three [[basic-psychological-needs]] — autonomy (self-chosen activity), competence (genuine mastery growth), and relatedness (connection to community and purpose) — are design constraints for any reading system intended to be used consistently over years rather than abandoned under pressure.
> 3. A PKB designed around SDT principles builds a *reading life* — a self-sustaining cycle of autonomous engagement, competence development, and growing intellectual identity — rather than merely an efficient individual study session protocol.
> *— [[strategic-reading-systems-foundational-report-2026-04-10_report]]*
