> [!flashcard] Seed 9 — Connection
> **Q:** How does elaborative interrogation connect to the desirable difficulties framework, and what specifically is "desirable" about EI's difficulty?
>
> **A:** EI is a desirable difficulty (Bjork, 1994) because the cognitive demand it imposes is precisely the demand of situation model construction — the load that produces durable, transferable understanding. But EI's difficulty is not generically desirable; it is specifically targeted: the question format structurally blocks the textbase shortcut and requires exactly the kind of cognitive work (integration of text propositions with prior schemas) that produces rich encoding. The difficulty is desirable because it *is* the mechanism of the benefit — not an unfortunate cost of a useful technique but the productive struggle of construction itself.
>
> **Source:** Phase IV, Section 4.4
>

> [!flashcard] Seed 9 — Connection
> **Q:** How does the identity-based habit framework connect to SDT's concept of integrated regulation?
> **A:** Both describe behaviors experienced as self-authoring rather than externally compelled. Integrated regulation in SDT is the level at which a behavior is fully congruent with the self-concept and experienced as genuinely volitional. Identity-based habit architecture proposes that sustainable habits require identity-level commitment — essentially integrated regulation — rather than mere motivational intention. The frameworks converge on self-concept coherence as the substrate of durable motivation.

> [!flashcard] Seed 9 — Connection
> **Q:** How does Levels of Processing Theory explain why deep vocabulary learning strategies produce better retention than rote definition memorization?
> **A:** LOP holds that depth of cognitive processing at encoding predicts durability and accessibility of the memory trace. Rote definition memorization engages shallow phonological/structural processing. Elaborative encoding (generating examples, connecting to prior knowledge, noting collocations) engages deep semantic processing, producing richer, more retrievable lexical representations.
> **Source:** Phase IV, Mechanisms
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
