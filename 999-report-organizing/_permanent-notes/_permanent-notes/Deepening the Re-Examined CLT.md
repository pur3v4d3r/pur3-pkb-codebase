## Open Threads

> [!open-question] Deepening the Re-Examined CLT
> > [!topic-idea] [[expertise-reversal-effect]]
> > The expertise reversal effect is the empirical fulcrum on which the reconstructed CLT's expertise-indexed prescriptions rest, but it deserves a dedicated dialectical re-examination in its own right: the effect is well-established in laboratory settings with well-defined tasks, but its precise boundary conditions (how much expertise? which task types? what learning timescales?) remain incompletely mapped. The reconstructed CLT would become substantially more actionable if the expertise reversal effect were examined with the same multi-perspectival scrutiny applied here to CLT itself. Priority: **High**.
>
> > [!topic-idea] [[working-memory-capacity]]
> > The WMC homogeneity assumption identified as failing in Phase III makes individual WMC differences a high-priority expansion topic. A focused examination of how WMC varies, what it predicts, and how WMC variation should modulate instructional prescriptions would provide the personalization framework that the reconstructed CLT requires but cannot currently supply. Of particular interest: the relationship between WMC and schema-mediated expertise — do WMC differences shrink as domain expertise grows? Priority: **High**.
> >
> > [!topic-idea] [[Metacognitive-Monitoring-Under-Cognitive-Load-—-When-Monitoring-is-Most-Needed-a]]
> > The bidirectional relationship between cognitive load and metacognitive monitoring warrants dedicated examination. High load degrades monitoring capacity exactly when monitoring is most needed; poor monitoring allows load to escalate unchecked. This vicious cycle has direct implications for self-regulated learning under challenging conditions and for the design of instructional environments that support both cognitive efficiency and metacognitive skill development. Priority: **Medium-High**.
> >
> > [!topic-idea] [[cognitive-theory-of-multimedia-learning]]
> > Mayer's CTML represents the most systematic extension of CLT into the design of multimedia instructional materials. The same dialectical pressure applied here to CLT would be productively applied to CTML: does CTML's twelve-principle framework inherit CLT's germane load problem? Does the expertise reversal effect apply to multimedia design in the same way it applies to conventional instruction? Does CTML account for motivational variables? A dialectical re-examination of CTML would complete the picture begun here. Priority: **Medium**.
> >
> > [!topic-idea] [[self-regulated-learning]]
> > The critical lens challenge — that CLT neglects motivational and affective variables — points directly toward the SRL integration that the reconstructed CLT needs. A synthesis examination focused specifically on how SRL frameworks (Zimmerman, Winne, Pintrich) model the motivational architecture that CLT lacks, and whether a genuine CLT-SRL integration is theoretically coherent, would represent a major advancement over both frameworks as currently specified. Priority: **Medium**.
> >
> > [!topic-idea] [[toward-an-expertise-indexed-instructional-design-principle]]
> > The reconstructed CLT in Phase V moves toward an expertise-indexed prescriptive framework but does not fully specify it. A dedicated note developing the principle that all instructional design prescriptions must specify the expertise range for which they apply — including worked examples, scaffolding, feedback density, problem variability, and assessment format — would provide the practical design guidance the reconstructed CLT promises but cannot itself deliver. Priority: **High**.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-22_report]]*
