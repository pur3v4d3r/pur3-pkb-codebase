# Self-Determination Theory (SDT)

> [!definition] Self-Determination Theory (SDT)
> [[self-determination-theory]] is a macro-theory of human motivation developed by [[edward-deci|Edward L. Deci]] and [[richard-ryan|Richard M. Ryan]] that distinguishes between different *types* of motivation along a continuum from amotivation through controlled (extrinsic) forms to fully [[autonomous-motivation|autonomous]] (intrinsic) forms, and posits that the quality of motivation depends on the satisfaction of three [[basic-psychological-needs-theory|basic psychological needs]]: autonomy, competence, and relatedness.
>
> **Boundary:** SDT is not a theory of motivation quantity (how much motivation) but of motivation quality (what kind). The common misconception that SDT simply endorses "intrinsic motivation good, extrinsic motivation bad" fundamentally misrepresents the theory's nuanced position on internalisation.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** SDT provides the theoretical mechanism explaining *why* goal orientation matters — mastery goals tend to satisfy basic psychological needs and thereby foster autonomous motivation, whereas performance goals, particularly under controlling conditions, tend to frustrate those needs.
>
> **See also:** [[basic-psychological-needs-theory]], [[autonomous-motivation]], [[controlled-motivation]], [[intrinsic-motivation]], [[organismic-integration-theory]]

> [!evidence] Self-Determination Theory (SDT)
> A macro-theory of human motivation, personality, and well-being developed by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan. SDT comprises six integrated mini-theories: [[cognitive-evaluation-theory]] (how external events affect intrinsic motivation), [[organismic-integration-theory]] (the internalization continuum), [[causality-orientations-theory]] (individual differences in motivational orientation), [[basic-psychological-needs|Basic Psychological Needs Theory]] (the three needs), [[relationship-motivation-theory]] (needs in close relationships), and [[goal-content-theory]] (intrinsic vs. extrinsic goal contents). SDT contrasts with both behavioral theories (which focus on external rewards and punishments) and purely cognitive theories (which focus on beliefs and expectations), arguing that the *quality* of motivation — autonomous vs. controlled — is more important than motivational *quantity*.

> [!evidence] Self-Determination Theory (SDT)
> A macro-theory of human motivation, development, and well-being developed by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan at the University of Rochester beginning in the 1970s. SDT proposes that human beings have three universal basic psychological needs — autonomy, competence, and relatedness — whose satisfaction is necessary for flourishing, and that social contexts affect motivation quality and well-being through their facilitation or frustration of these needs. SDT consists of six mini-theories: Cognitive Evaluation Theory (CET), Organismic Integration Theory (OIT), Basic Psychological Needs Theory (BPNT), Causality Orientations Theory (COT), Goal Contents Theory (GCT), and Relationship Motivation Theory (RMT). **See also**: [[self-determination-theory]], [[basic-psychological-needs-theory]], [[edward-deci]], [[richard-ryan]]
