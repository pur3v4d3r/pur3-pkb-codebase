## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technical Detail: Temporal Dynamics of the Arousal Benefit
> The catecholamine and arousal benefits of walking have a temporal profile that affects their interaction with reading:
>
> **Onset (0-5 minutes):** Catecholamine levels begin rising. Heart rate increases. The arousal benefit is developing but not yet at maximum. There may be a brief transition period where the dual-task cost is present but the arousal benefit has not yet fully materialized — this predicts a brief initial performance dip.
>
> **Plateau (5-30 minutes):** Catecholamine levels reach a steady state appropriate to walking intensity. Arousal benefit is at maximum. This is the window where walking-while-reading is most likely to be net beneficial (for material within appropriate difficulty range).
>
> **Extended duration (30-90+ minutes):** Physiological adaptation occurs. Catecholamine release stabilizes or slightly declines as the body adapts to the sustained light activity. Muscular fatigue, though mild, may begin to shift some postural control from subcortical to cortical involvement. Mental fatigue from the reading task itself accumulates. The arousal benefit may diminish while dual-task costs remain stable or slightly increase.
>
> **Post-cessation (stopping walking):** There is a well-documented **post-exercise cognitive enhancement** window lasting 15-60 minutes, during which catecholamine levels remain elevated above resting baseline and executive function remains enhanced. This has important practical implications: walking for 20 minutes and then sitting down to read may capture the arousal benefit WITHOUT the dual-task cost — a potentially optimal protocol (addressed in Level 7).
