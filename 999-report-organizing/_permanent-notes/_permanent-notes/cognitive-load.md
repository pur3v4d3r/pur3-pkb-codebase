# Cognitive Load

> [!definition] **Cognitive Load** *(from [[cognitive-load-theory-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-22]])*
> The total demand placed on working memory by a learning task at any given moment. Originally conceived as a single unitary construct, later differentiated into three types. Critical boundary condition: cognitive load is a property of the interaction between a learner and a task, not a property of the task alone — the same task imposes different loads on different learners, and on the same learner at different stages of expertise development. The boundary condition that "load" is learner-relative is frequently forgotten in practice.

The primary source for the CLT-desirable difficulties contradiction examined in Phase III.
- **Paas, F., & van Merriënboer, J. J. G. (1993). The efficiency of instructional conditions: An approach to combine mental effort and performance measures. *Human Factors, 35*(4), 737-743.**: Introduced the subjective mental effort scale as a proxy measure for cognitive load. Critical for understanding both CLT's measurement methodology and its limitations — subjective mental effort ratings capture total load experience but cannot distinguish load types. Supports the measurement independence problem analysis.
- **Daneman, M., & Carpenter, P. A. (1980). Individual differences in working memory and reading. *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 19*(4), 450-466.**: A landmark paper demonstrating that individual differences in working memory capacity predict reading comprehension and other complex cognitive skills. Supports the WMC homogeneity assumption critique and the argument that CLT needs to account for learner-level differences in WMC.
- **Kornell, N., & Bjork, R. A. (2008). Learning concepts and categories: Is spacing the "enemy of induction"? *Psychological Science, 19*(6), 585-592.**: Demonstrates the interleaving effect: interleaved practice of concept categories produces worse acquisition performance but better long-term category learning than blocked practice. A clean empirical demonstration of the CLT-desirable difficulties tension. Supports Phase III's practical challenge section.
- **Mayer, R. E. (2009). *Multimedia Learning* (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press.**: Systematic presentation of the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning (CTML) as a CLT extension to multimedia environments. Provides the twelve design principles for multimedia instruction. Important for understanding both what CLT's applied tradition has achieved and where the same dialectical pressure would reveal similar vulnerabilities in the CTML framework.
- **van Merriënboer, J. J. G., & Sweller, J. (2005). Cognitive load theory and complex learning: Recent developments and future directions. *Educational Psychology Review, 17*(2), 147-177.**: Addresses CLT's extension to complex, real-world learning tasks. Acknowledges some of the limitations identified in this re-examination — including the difficulty of applying CLT to naturalistic environments and the challenges of measuring load in complex tasks. Supports both the practical challenge analysis and the reconstructive synthesis.
