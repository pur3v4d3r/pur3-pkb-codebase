## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Metacognitive Failure Taxonomy: A Unified Framework
> The literature on metacognitive failures can be unified within a single coherent taxonomy organized by which layer of the Metacognitive Skill Stack has failed:
>
> **Level 1 — Knowledge Failures:** Incorrect or absent metacognitive knowledge. Includes: entity theory beliefs about intelligence (false person knowledge), false beliefs about study strategy effectiveness (false strategy knowledge), miscalibrated assessment of task demands (false task knowledge). Treatment: direct knowledge correction through instruction and evidence exposure.
>
> **Level 2 — Monitoring Failures:** Monitoring generates incorrect or absent signals. Includes: illusion of knowing (false positive — monitoring signals comprehension that is absent), Dunning-Kruger effect (false high confidence from absent comparative monitoring capability), metacognitive blindness (absent signal — no monitoring of a blind zone). Treatment: external calibration standards (testing, expert feedback, peer review).
>
> **Level 3 — Regulation Failures:** Monitoring is accurate but regulation fails to respond appropriately. Includes: strategy inertia (monitoring signals strategy failure but regulation fails to switch strategies), metacognitive disengagement (monitoring signals need for effort but regulation responds with withdrawal), hindsight bias (retroactive distortion of monitoring memories prevents calibration learning). Treatment: regulation training, attribution reframing, growth mindset intervention.
>
> **Level 4 — Integration Failures:** Knowledge, monitoring, and regulation are individually functional but fail to integrate. Includes: the monitoring-control gap (JOL accuracy does not predict study allocation), performance-calibration decoupling (accurate JOLs coexist with poor test performance). Treatment: explicit practice at integrating layers across complete learning cycles.
>
> This taxonomy provides clearer diagnostic guidance than the undifferentiated concept of "poor metacognition" — it identifies which layer requires intervention.
> *— [[metacognitive-skills-foundational-report-2026-04-09_report]]*
