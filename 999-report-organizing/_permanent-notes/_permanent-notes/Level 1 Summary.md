> [!section-summary] Level 1 Summary
> At the surface, Cognitive Load Theory appears to be a tripartite model (intrinsic, extraneous, germane load) that generates a suite of instructional effects. Element interactivity appears as a background concept — the factor that determines intrinsic load, treated as a fixed property of learning materials. The three load types are treated as ontologically distinct, additively combining categories. This is the version of CLT most instructional designers encounter. The next level will reveal that this surface is misleading: element interactivity is not a background concept but the *entire explanatory engine* of the theory, and the three load types are not distinct categories but different perspectives on a single mechanism.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 1 Summary
> At surface level, [[Cognitive-Load-Theory]] appears to be a classification scheme — three additive sources of load, three corresponding instructional design strategies, one capacity-limited container they fill. This picture is durable, useful, and as far as it goes, valid. But it conceals the construct that the modern theory has come to rest on, and it cannot answer the *why* questions that any specialist must ask. The next level will zoom past the taxonomy to the mechanism that generates intrinsic load in the first place: [[Element-Interactivity]].
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-element-interactivity-deep-dive-2026-04-20]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 1 Summary
> At the surface level, metacognitive monitoring accuracy presents as the empirically measurable gap between what people think they know and what they actually know. This gap is systematic (overconfidence of 10-20%), asymmetric (larger at high confidence), and consequential (it directly degrades [[self-regulated-learning]] by corrupting the information on which regulatory decisions are based). The gap has two orthogonal dimensions — absolute calibration and relative resolution — which have different causes, consequences, and remedies. Resolution (the ability to discriminate known from unknown) is often more practically important than calibration (the absolute accuracy of the confidence level).
>
> **The next level zooms into the mechanism:** How is the monitoring signal generated in the first place? What information does the monitoring system actually use?
> *— [[metacognitive-monitoring-accuracy-calibration-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 1 Summary
> At surface level, internalization appears as a transformation in the regulatory locus of behavior — from external prompting to self-initiation. Careful observation reveals that this transformation is non-uniform across behaviors, that absence of external pressure underdetermines the regulatory style, that maintenance can be driven by internal coercion as readily as by autonomous endorsement, that the developmental trajectory is nonlinear and reversible, and that the phenomenological signature of "doing this because I want to" tracks the underlying regulation more reliably than any objective feature of the situation. The surface is real, important, and insufficient. Each of these surface puzzles will receive its mechanistic explanation at the next level of zoom.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-internalization-continuum-deep-dive-2026-04-21]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 1 Summary
> At surface level, walking-while-reading appears to be a multitasking problem — two tasks competing for the same cognitive resources. The folk experience suggests walking helps, while the standard dual-task prediction says it should hurt. The paradox between these two perspectives is real and cannot be resolved without examining the specific architecture of working memory resource allocation. The next level will zoom into that architecture.
> *— [[walking-academic-reading-working-memory-deep-dive-2026-04-13_report]]*
