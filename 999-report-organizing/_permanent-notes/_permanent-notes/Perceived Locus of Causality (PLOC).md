# Perceived Locus of Causality (PLOC)

> [!definition] Perceived Locus of Causality (PLOC)
> [**Perceived-Locus-of-Causality-Definition**:: The subjective psychological sense of whether one's behavior originates from oneself (internal PLOC) or from external pressures, contingencies, or demands (external PLOC). In CET, a shift toward internal PLOC is associated with increased intrinsic motivation and psychological well-being; a shift toward external PLOC is associated with decreased intrinsic motivation. PLOC is a dynamic, situationally sensitive experience, distinct from Rotter's trait-level Locus of Control construct.]

> [!evidence] Perceived Locus of Causality (PLOC)
> [**PLOC-Definition**:: The perceived origin of one's behavior — whether the person experiences themselves as the initiator and author of their actions (internal PLOC) or as responding to external pressures, contingencies, and controls (external PLOC). Adapted by Deci and Ryan from deCharms's (1968) origin-pawn concept. PLOC is CET's primary mediator of reward effects on intrinsic motivation: events that shift PLOC externally undermine intrinsic motivation; events that maintain or enhance internal PLOC support it. Critically distinct from Rotter's (1966) Locus of Control, which concerns beliefs about outcome contingencies rather than experienced behavioral causation.]

> [!evidence] Perceived Locus of Causality (PLOC)
> The phenomenological dimension underlying the regulatory continuum — the degree to which the individual experiences the initiation of their behavior as coming from within themselves (internal PLOC) or from outside (external PLOC). [[Perceived-Locus-of-Causality-(PLOC)]] is not identical with actual causal locus but refers to the phenomenological experience of agency. Greater internal PLOC is associated with more volition, spontaneity, and authentic expression; greater external PLOC with experience of compulsion, pressure, and inauthenticity.

> The learner's subjective experience of the origin of their behavior — whether they feel that their engagement arises from their own choices and values (internal PLOC) or from external demands and pressures (external PLOC). PLOC is the central mechanism through which autonomy support affects motivational quality: autonomy-supportive environments shift PLOC toward internal, while controlling environments shift it toward external.

> [!evidence] Perceived Locus of Causality (PLOC)
> A construct developed by [[Richard deCharms]] and central to SDT: the extent to which a person experiences the source of their behaviour as internal (self-initiated, volitional) versus external (controlled by environmental forces, pressure, or obligation). An internal PLOC is characteristic of autonomous motivation; an external PLOC is characteristic of controlled motivation. PLOC is not a fixed trait but is dynamically influenced by features of the immediate social environment — surveillance, controlling rewards, threats, and evaluative pressure all shift PLOC externally, while rationale provision, choice, and acknowledgment of internal states shift it internally.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-11_report]]*

> The degree to which a person perceives themselves as the origin (internal PLOC) or the pawn (external PLOC) of their own behavior. DeCharms (1968) introduced the origin-pawn distinction; Deci and Ryan building on this proposed that PLOC is a key mediating variable through which environments affect motivation quality.
