## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Attribution as Cause vs. Attribution as Symptom
> **Position A (Weiner's model):** Causal attributions are the proximate cognitive cause of differential emotional and motivational outcomes. Change the attribution, change the motivation.
>
> **Position B (Mood-congruence view):** Prior emotional states and motivational dispositions are the primary determinants of causal attributions — people in negative affective states make more external, stable attributions; failure-expecting students make stable attributions for failure not because of logical inference but as an expression of a broader negative self-concept.
>
> **Current Evidence State:** Bidirectional. Experimental studies can demonstrate both directions of influence. The most parsimonious account is a reciprocal causal model in which attributions and emotions mutually constitute each other over time — but this account is more complex and less therapeutically tractable than the unidirectional cognitive-primacy model.
>
> **Why This Matters:** If attributions are partly symptoms rather than causes, cognitive attribution retraining may have limited efficacy without addressing the underlying emotional and self-concept substrate. This is consistent with the clinical literature showing that attribution interventions have larger effects in early-stage helplessness than in established depressive episodes.
>
> **This Report's Stance:** The two-stage intervention architecture proposed in Phase VI partially dissolves this tension by treating the attribution-emotion relationship as bidirectional — the intervention targets both the cognitive attribution AND the emotional reorganization simultaneously.
> *— [[attribution-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-24_report]]*
