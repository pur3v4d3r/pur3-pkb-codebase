## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Transfer Domain 4: Software Engineering and Technical Learning
> The technology industry's pervasive use of sprint goals, story points, and velocity metrics instantiates the very metric seduction pattern this report warns against. The *structural principle* is that when learning goals (mastering a new framework, understanding a system's architecture) are expressed as production goals ("close 8 tickets this sprint"), the metric displaces the understanding it was meant to proxy. The motivational grammar framework applies directly: "Develop sufficient understanding of the authentication subsystem to independently diagnose and resolve token-refresh failures" differs from "Complete the auth ticket backlog by end of sprint" in ways that goal-setting theory, SDT, and AGT all predict will produce different learning trajectories. The *boundary condition* is that engineering contexts impose genuine production deadlines that create irreducible tension between mastery-oriented learning and output-oriented delivery — the specificity-autonomy dialectic in its most acute organisational form.
>
