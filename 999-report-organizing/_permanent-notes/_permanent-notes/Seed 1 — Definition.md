> [!flashcard] Seed 1 — Definition
> **Q:** What is elaborative interrogation, and what distinguishes it from simply re-reading or questioning a text?
> **A:** EI is a generative learning strategy in which the learner answers "why is this true?" questions about specific factual propositions, drawing on prior knowledge to produce causal-explanatory accounts. It is distinguished by its generative character (answers must be produced, not read), its why-format constraint (causal-explanatory, not just reflective), and its prior knowledge activation requirement.

> **Difficulty:** Basic

> **Q:** What is the operational definition of a habit, and how does it differ from a frequently performed behavior?
> **A:** A habit is a context-triggered automatic behavioral response that executes without deliberate goal activation. It differs from mere frequency: the defining feature is cue-triggered automaticity, not recurrence. A behavior performed frequently but always in response to deliberate intent is not a habit.

> **Q:** What is metacognitive knowledge, and how does it differ from metacognitive regulation?
> **A:** Metacognitive knowledge is stored, consciously accessible beliefs about cognitive processes, strategies, and states — the *content* of the metacognitive system residing in long-term memory. Metacognitive regulation refers to the executive processes (planning, monitoring, evaluating) that govern cognitive activity in real time. Knowledge provides the content from which regulation draws; regulation is the operational use of that content.

> [!flashcard] Seed 1 — Definition
> **Q:** What is the Coverage Threshold in vocabulary acquisition, and why does it matter?
>
> **A:** The Coverage Threshold (Nation & Waring, 1997) is the proportion of running words in a text that a reader must already know before incidental vocabulary acquisition operates efficiently — approximately 98%. Below this level, working memory is fully consumed by comprehension demands, leaving no cognitive slack for vocabulary inference. Above it, the reader has capacity to notice and infer unfamiliar words. It matters because it sets the prerequisite for reading-based vocabulary growth: readers below threshold cannot bootstrap their vocabulary efficiently through reading.
>
> **Source:** Phase II, Coverage Threshold Hypothesis; Phase IV, Cognitive Slack Model
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Definition
> **Tags:** #vocabulary-acquisition, #coverage-threshold, #reading, #working-memory
> *— [[reading-vocabulary-focused-analysis-2026-03-28_report]]*

> [!flashcard] Seed 1 — Definition
> **Q:** What is the difference between a textbase and a situation model?
>
> **A:** A textbase is a propositional representation of *what the text said* — the ideas expressed in the text, stripped of their exact wording. A situation model is a representation of *what the text is about* — the state of affairs, system, or argument described, integrated with prior knowledge. The textbase represents the text; the situation model represents the world the text describes.
>
> **Source:** Phase II, Three-Level Architecture
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Type:** Distinction
> **Tags:** #situation-model #textbase #reading-comprehension

> [!flashcard] Seed 1 — Definition
> **Q:** What does the vocabulary threshold hypothesis claim, and what approximate coverage ratio does it specify?
>
> **A:** The vocabulary threshold hypothesis claims that fluent, unassisted text comprehension requires knowing a minimum proportion of the running words — approximately 95–98% coverage. Below this threshold, comprehension degrades because too many unknowns prevent the construction of a coherent situation model. This corresponds to knowing the most frequent ~8,000–9,000 word families for typical academic English.
>
> **Source:** Phase II & Phase III
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Type:** Definition
