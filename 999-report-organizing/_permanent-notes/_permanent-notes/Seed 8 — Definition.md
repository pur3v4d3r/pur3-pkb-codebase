> [!flashcard] Seed 8 — Definition
> **Q:** What is the verbal persuasion source of self-efficacy, and why does self-directed inner speech function through the same mechanism?
> **A:** Verbal persuasion is one of Bandura's four sources of self-efficacy: evaluative statements from credible others that influence capability beliefs. Self-directed inner speech functions through the same mechanism because the individual is simultaneously the source and the recipient of efficacy-relevant evaluations; the individual's credibility with themselves (based on their performance history) makes inner speech a potent efficacy input — both positive ("I've handled this kind of material before") and negative ("I can never follow this level of argument").
> **Source:** Phase II
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate

> [!flashcard] Seed 8 — Definition
> **Q:** What is the Demand-Architecture Isomorphism, and what does it predict about the relationship between task design and learning depth?
>
> **A:** The Demand-Architecture Isomorphism is the principle that the level of cognitive architecture engaged in processing is isomorphic to the level required to satisfy the task's demand structure. It predicts that: the level of understanding produced by a learning activity is determined by the minimum representational level needed to answer the task, not by the learner's subjective effort. A textbase-satisfiable question produces textbase encoding regardless of effort; a situation-model-requiring question forces situation model encoding. This implies that the most important instructional design decision is not "how difficult" but "what level of architecture does answering this demand?"
>

> [!flashcard] Seed 8 — Definition
> **Q:** What distinguishes formative assessment from summative assessment, and why is formative assessment the appropriate model for PKM?
> **A:** Formative assessment is designed to inform and improve ongoing learning (assessment *for* learning); summative assessment certifies or ranks attainment (assessment *of* learning). Formative assessment's three functions — making criteria transparent, providing evidence of current standing, and indicating gap-closing actions — align with PKM's purpose of continuous knowledge development rather than terminal certification.

> [!flashcard] Seed 8 — Definition
> **Q:** What is pseudometacognition, and how is it distinguishable from genuine metacognitive knowledge?
> **A:** Pseudometacognition is metacognitive-appearing knowledge or behavior that does not reflect genuine awareness of one's actual cognitive processes or accurately calibrated monitoring. It is characterized by context-insensitivity (same narrative regardless of task/performance variation) and failure to generate adaptive regulatory responses when monitoring signals indicate need. Distinguished from genuine metacognitive knowledge by: insensitivity to performance feedback, failure to update under disconfirming evidence, and absence of corresponding regulatory improvement.
> **Source:** Phase V.3
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #pseudometacognition #metacognitive-knowledge #calibration #monitoring
