## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Edge Case: The Dunning-Kruger Effect as a Monitoring Accuracy Phenomenon
> **The case:** Low-performing individuals show dramatically worse calibration than high-performing individuals — they not only perform poorly but are most overconfident about their poor performance. High performers show slight underconfidence.
> **What standard understanding predicts:** The cue-utilization framework predicts that calibration will be imperfect across the performance spectrum, but does not by itself predict the dramatic asymmetry of the [[dunning-kruger-effect]].
> **What actually happens:** Kruger and Dunning (1999) documented that bottom-quartile performers estimated their performance at approximately the 62nd percentile — a massive overestimation. Top-quartile performers underestimated slightly.
> **Why this matters:** The Dunning-Kruger effect reveals a specific structural vulnerability: **the skills needed to produce correct answers are the same skills needed to recognize correct answers.** A person who lacks expertise in a domain lacks the very knowledge that would allow them to detect their own errors. This is [[pseudometacognition|meta-ignorance]] — not just ignorance but ignorance of one's own ignorance.
> **Implications:** Monitoring accuracy is not a general cognitive skill that operates independently of domain knowledge. It is partially constituted by domain knowledge. This means monitoring accuracy cannot be trained in a domain-general way — it must be developed within each content domain.
> *— [[metacognitive-monitoring-accuracy-calibration-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*
