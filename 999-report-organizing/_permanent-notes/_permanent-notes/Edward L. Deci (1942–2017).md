## Additional Material

> [!person] Edward L. Deci (1942–2017)
> Co-creator of Self-Determination Theory and co-developer of Causality Orientations Theory. Deci's early experimental work on the effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation (Deci, 1971) provided the empirical foundation for what would become Cognitive Evaluation Theory, and eventually the broader SDT framework. With Ryan, he developed COT's three-orientation taxonomy and the GCOS instrument. Deci's theoretical emphasis was consistently on the organismic tendency toward self-determination as a fundamental feature of human nature.
> *— [[causality-orientations-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*

> [!person] Edward L. Deci (1942–2017)
> University of Rochester. Co-founder of SDT alongside Richard Ryan. Conducted the seminal 1971 puzzle experiments demonstrating the undermining effect of external rewards on intrinsic motivation. Co-authored the foundational texts *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior* (1985) and *Self-Determination Theory: Basic Psychological Needs in Motivation, Development, and Wellness* (2017). Deci's contribution was primarily empirical and theoretical — he insisted on grounding the organismic commitment in experimental evidence.
>
> **Relationship to other figures:** Lifelong collaboration with Ryan; influenced by White's effectance motivation and deCharms' personal causation; positioned SDT against Skinner's behaviorist framework.
>
> **Key works:** Deci (1971), Deci & Ryan (1985, 2000, 2017).
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
