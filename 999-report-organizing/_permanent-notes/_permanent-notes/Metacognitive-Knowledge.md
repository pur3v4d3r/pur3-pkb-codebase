> [!evidence] Metacognitive Knowledge
> The declarative and conditional beliefs a person holds about cognition — their own and cognition in general. Subdivided into person knowledge (beliefs about oneself and others as cognitive agents), task knowledge (understanding of how task characteristics affect cognitive demands), and strategy knowledge (awareness of cognitive strategies, their costs, benefits, and conditions of applicability). Strategy knowledge is the most instructionally tractable component.
> *— [[metacognition-and-critical-thinking-foundational-report-2026-03-05_report]]*

> [!evidence] Metacognitive-Knowledge
> Flavell's (1979) construct distinguishing three types of knowledge about cognition: declarative metacognitive knowledge (knowing what — about persons, tasks, and strategies), procedural metacognitive knowledge (knowing how — how to deploy strategies), and conditional metacognitive knowledge (knowing when and why — under what conditions each strategy is effective).
