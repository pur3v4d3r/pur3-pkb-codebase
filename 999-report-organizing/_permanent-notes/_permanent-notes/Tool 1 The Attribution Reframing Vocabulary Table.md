## Practical Implications

> [!example] Tool 1: The Attribution Reframing Vocabulary Table
> The following vocabulary table provides direct attributional substitutes along the stability and controllability dimensions — the two dimensions most consequential for motivational outcomes (expectancy and effort mobilization, respectively).
>
> | Destructive Pattern | Dimension Encoded | Constructive Substitute | Dimension Encoded |
> |--------------------|--------------------|------------------------|-------------------|
> | "I can't do this" | Internal, stable, uncontrollable | "I don't have the right approach yet" | Internal, unstable, controllable |
> | "I'm just not good at this kind of reading" | Internal, stable, uncontrollable | "My strategy for this text type needs development" | Internal, unstable, controllable |
> | "This is too hard for me" | Internal, stable, uncontrollable | "This is at the edge of my current comprehension — exactly where growth happens" | Internal, unstable, controllable |
> | "I don't have the background for this" | External/Internal, stable, uncontrollable | "I need to develop specific background — here's what's missing" | Internal, unstable, controllable |
> | "I understand this easily" (ability praise) | Internal, stable, uncontrollable | "My current approach is working well for this material" | Internal, unstable, controllable |
>
> Note on the table: the constructive substitutes are not merely positive reframings — they are dimensional reassignments. The critical move is always toward unstable and controllable, not simply toward internal or external.
> *— [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25_report]]*
