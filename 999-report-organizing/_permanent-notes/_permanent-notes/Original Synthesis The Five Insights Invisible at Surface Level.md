## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Original Synthesis: The Five Insights Invisible at Surface Level
> 1. **Walking is almost free.** The dual-task cost of hyper-habituated indoor walking on [[central-executive]] resources is genuinely small — perhaps 3-8% of CE capacity. This is invisible at surface level, where "multitasking is bad" suggests massive interference.
>
> 2. **Sitting is not free.** Sitting during reading imposes its own cognitive costs — restlessness suppression, under-arousal, and potentially faster [[attention|attentional]] fatigue. The comparison is not "walking cost vs. zero cost" but "walking cost vs. sitting cost." At surface level, sitting looks like the neutral baseline; at depth, it isn't.
>
> 3. **Arousal and dual-task cost are independent dimensions.** Walking simultaneously increases arousal (beneficial) and imposes dual-task cost (detrimental). These are additive, not canceling. The net effect depends on where the reader sits on the catecholamine curve and how much CE load the material demands. This independence is invisible at any single level of analysis.
>
> 4. **The temporal structure creates an exploitable arbitrage.** The post-exercise catecholamine window means the arousal benefit of walking persists after walking stops — but the dual-task cost vanishes immediately when walking ends. This temporal asymmetry creates a window (walking cessation + 15-60 minutes) where the reader gets the benefit without the cost. This is the design principle behind the hybrid protocol, and it is invisible without Level 4's temporal dynamics analysis.
>
> 5. **The fluency-comprehension dissociation is the deepest risk.** Walking maintains reading fluency (page-turning, eye-movement, decoding speed) while potentially degrading comprehension for high-difficulty material. Because fluency feels like understanding, the walker-reader may systematically overestimate comprehension. This metacognitive blind spot is the single most important practical insight and is invisible without Level 5's edge case analysis.
> *— [[walking-academic-reading-working-memory-deep-dive-2026-04-13_report]]*
