> [!person] Lev Vygotsky (1896–1934)
> Soviet developmental psychologist whose sociohistorical theory of mind places language — and specifically its internalization as inner speech — at the center of higher cognitive functions and self-regulation. Although Vygotsky died at 37, his work (especially *Thought and Language*, 1934/1986) has had enormous influence on developmental and educational psychology. Key works: *Thought and Language* (1934/1986); *Mind in Society* (1978).
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by Marx's sociocultural theory and opposed to Piaget's individualistic account of cognitive development; profound influence on educational psychology (Bruner, Rogoff, Wertsch), reading research (inner speech in reading comprehension), and self-regulation research (Berk, Winsler, Fernyhough).
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the theoretical foundation for understanding inner speech as a regulatory (not merely expressive) medium — the basis for claiming that vocabulary can be *constitutive* of motivational cognition rather than merely reflecting it.

> [!person] Lev Vygotsky (1896–1934)
> Soviet psychologist whose work on the cultural and social mediation of cognition represents the second major pillar of constructivist learning theory. Vygotsky died at 37, leaving a body of work that was suppressed in the Soviet Union for decades and became fully available to Western scholars only in the 1970s–1980s. His concepts of the ZPD, internalization, and cultural tools have had transformative impact on cognitive development research, educational psychology, and instructional design.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by Marxist historical-materialist philosophy, Saussurean linguistics, and Spinoza. Engaged critically with Piaget (whom he reviewed at length in *Thought and Language*). Influenced Bruner, Wertsch, Rogoff, and the entire social constructivist and sociocultural traditions.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Vygotsky's framework provides the social-constructivist extension of the core model — the ZPD, scaffolding, and internalization mechanisms discussed in Phases II and IV.
> *— [[constructivist-learning-theory-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Lev Vygotsky (1896–1934)
> **Position:** Soviet psychologist; founder of social constructivism and cultural-historical psychology
> **Core contribution:** The zone of proximal development; the social origins of higher mental functions; language as the primary tool of thought; the internalization of social processes as individual cognitive capacities.
> **Intellectual lineage:** Influenced by Marx (dialectical materialism), Engels, Spinoza, Piaget (against whom he argued). Influenced Wood, Bruner & Ross (scaffolding), Lave & Wenger (situated learning), Wertsch (mediated action), Engeström (activity theory).
> **Key works:** *Thought and Language* (1934/1962); *Mind in Society* (1978); *The Collected Works of L. S. Vygotsky* (6 vols.)

> [!person] Lev Vygotsky (1896–1934)
> Soviet developmental psychologist. Originated the zone of proximal development and scaffolding concepts (though the term "scaffolding" was coined by Wood, Bruner, and Ross in 1976). His developmental framework — in which learning occurs in the gap between what a learner can do independently and what they can do with support — provides the theoretical basis for the scaffolded fading and Developmental Arc Model presented in Section 7. Key work: "Mind in Society" (1978).
> **Relationship to other figures:** Foundational influence on all subsequent scaffolding research; Zimmerman's developmental perspective on SRL draws indirectly on Vygotskian principles.
