> [!person] Barry Zimmerman (1942–2020)
> American educational psychologist at the City University of New York whose cyclical model of self-regulated learning became one of the most influential frameworks in the SRL literature. Zimmerman's contribution was to specify the *phases* and *sub-processes* of SRL with sufficient precision to guide both research and intervention. His work is the primary SRL framework employed in Section 3.
>
> **Key works:** Zimmerman (1989, 2000, 2002).
>
> **Relationship:** Complementary to Pintrich's (2000) framework, which emphasises the *areas* of regulation; parallel to Winne and Hadwin's (1998) information-processing approach to SRL.
> *— [[goal-setting-and-assessment-in-pkm-foundational-report-2026-04-15_report]]*

> [!person] Barry Zimmerman (1942–2020)
> Developer of the tripartite [[zimmerman-srl-model|SRL model]] (forethought → performance → self-reflection) and leading theorist of self-regulation as a learnable skill. His work demonstrated that expert self-regulators differ from novices not in cognitive ability but in the quality of their regulatory processes. Key works: "Self-Regulated Learning and Academic Achievement" (1990, 2000). Relationship: his SRL cycle provides the process architecture within which Locke-Latham's goal mechanisms and Deci-Ryan's motivational dynamics operate.
> *— [[goal-setting-and-assessment-language-in-pkm-foundational-report-2026-04-15_report]]*

His work made metacognition central to the SRL literature and enabled extensive applied research on self-regulated learning development.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by Bandura (social cognitive theory) and Flavell. Influenced virtually all applied SRL research, including PKB and self-directed learning applications.

> [!person] Barry Zimmerman (1942–2020)
> **Core Contribution:** Developer of the cyclical model of self-regulated learning (1989–2002), grounding SRL in [[albert-bandura|Bandura's]] social cognitive theory and providing the most operationally precise account of the three-phase cycle. Zimmerman distinguished the Forethought, Performance, and Self-Reflection Phases with sufficient specificity to generate both empirical research programs and practical interventions.
> **Relationship to Other Figures:** Built on Bandura's self-efficacy theory; worked alongside Pintrich in the development of the SRL field; influenced the development of metacognitive scaffolding research. His model integrates more motivational detail than earlier cognitive SRL models.
> **Key Works:** *Self-Regulation of Learning and Performance* (1994), "Attaining Self-Regulation" in *Handbook of Self-Regulation* (2000), "Becoming a Self-Regulated Learner" (2002, *Theory into Practice*).
> **Relevance to This Analysis:** The entire structural framework of this report — the Forethought and Self-Reflection phases, their sub-components, and the cyclical architecture — is Zimmerman's original formulation.
> *— [[srl-forethought-self-reflection-pkb-focused-analysis-2026-03-24 (1)_report]]*

> [!person] Barry Zimmerman (1942–2020)
> American educational psychologist at City University of New York and Harvard. Developer of the cyclical model of self-regulated learning, the most influential operationalisation of SRL in educational psychology. His research program moved SRL from abstract theoretical concept to measurable, trainable competency. Key works: *Developing Self-Fulfilling Cycles of Academic Regulation* (1999), *Becoming a Self-Regulated Learner* (2002), and his collaborative studies with Kitsantas on goal-setting and self-reflection.
>
> **Lineage:** Zimmerman built on [[albert-bandura|Bandura's]] social cognitive theory, Vygotsky's zone of proximal development, and Winne's information processing approaches to metacognition. His students include numerous researchers now prominent in self-regulation and educational psychology.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the three-phase cyclical model on which the entire Forethought-Reflection architecture is built.
> *— [[srl-forethought-selfreflection-pkb-report-2026-03-24_report]]*
