## Open Threads

> [!open-question] What is the Natural Ecological Validity of Inner Speech Vocabulary Research?
> **The Question:** Nearly all research on inner speech in academic contexts uses verbal protocols (think-aloud) or retrospective self-report, which may not capture the condensed, predicative character of natural inner speech. Is the vocabulary that appears in verbal protocols representative of the vocabulary that actually operates during reading?
>
> **What We Know:** Fernyhough and colleagues have documented that inner speech in naturalistic settings (experience sampling methodology) often takes non-verbal or partially verbal forms — visual imagery, emotional tone, fragmentary language — rather than full propositions. The rich attribution and efficacy language in this report may correspond more to *potential* inner speech (what could be verbalized if prompted) than to *occurring* inner speech.
>
> **What We Don't Know:** Whether natural inner speech during academic reading is predominantly verbal (and thus vocabulary-modifiable), whether the non-verbal components carry the same motivational content as their verbal counterparts, and whether deliberate verbal practice generalizes to condensed inner speech.
>
> **Research Direction:** Experience-sampling studies of inner speech during authentic academic reading, combined with concurrent comprehension measures, would provide important data.
>
