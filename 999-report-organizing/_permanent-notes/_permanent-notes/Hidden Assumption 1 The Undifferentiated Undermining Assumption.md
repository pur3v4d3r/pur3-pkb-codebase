## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Hidden Assumption 1: The Undifferentiated Undermining Assumption
> **The assumption**: SDT's standard account, particularly as disseminated in popular summaries and policy documents, assumes that external rewards represent a categorical threat to intrinsic motivation for engaging activities — that the undermining effect is a reliable, general phenomenon that practitioners should design around.
>
> **Why it's hidden**: The nuanced moderator-conditional version of the claim is found in careful readings of the technical literature. The categorical version is what gets transmitted through educational policy documents, management consulting materials, and graduate course summaries. The gap between the technical claim and the disseminated claim is wide, and the technical authors are not always clear that the categorical version is an overstatement.
>
> **What happens if it's wrong**: If the undermining effect is substantially moderator-dependent, then anti-reward policies based on the categorical claim may be eliminating useful extrinsic scaffolding in contexts where it would not undermine intrinsic motivation — and may be generating unnecessary performance anxiety in contexts where informational contingent rewards would support competence without threatening autonomy.
>
> **Assessment**: The assumption fails. The moderator-conditional version is better supported. Undermining is real for specific conditions; it is not a categorical law. The reconstruction will need to specify when rewards threaten and when they scaffold.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-dialectical-re-examination-2026-03-19_report]]*
