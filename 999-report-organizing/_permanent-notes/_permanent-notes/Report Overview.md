> This report traces SDT's intellectual origins in [[humanistic-psychology]] and early reward research, then systematically develops its core architecture: the tripartite structure of [[basic-psychological-needs]] (autonomy, competence, and relatedness), the regulatory continuum from [[external-regulation]] to [[integrated-regulation]], and the concept of [[autonomy-support]] as the environmental condition that enables optimal functioning. The six primary mini-theories — [[cognitive-evaluation-theory]], [[organismic-integration-theory]], [[basic-psychological-needs-theory]], [[causality-orientations-theory]], [[goal-contents-theory]], and [[relationship-motivation-theory]] — are examined as specialized lenses that together constitute an integrated theoretical architecture. The report then evaluates the empirical foundation, cross-cultural research, and major critiques including those arising from the [[Replication-Crisis-in-Psychology]], before developing an original synthesis that reconceives SDT as a motivational vulnerability-and-resilience framework for human flourishing. The analysis concludes with extensive far-transfer applications in education, organizational management, healthcare, and the design of AI systems that preserve human autonomy.
>
