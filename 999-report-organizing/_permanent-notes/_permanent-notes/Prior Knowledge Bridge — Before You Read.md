## Additional Material

> [!schema-activation] Prior Knowledge Bridge — Before You Read
> This report assumes familiarity with basic cognitive psychology and some experience with personal knowledge management tools. Before proceeding, bring to mind what you already understand about these domains:
>
> **From cognitive science:** You likely know that human memory is imperfect and that we struggle to hold much information in mind simultaneously. You may have encountered [[Miller's-Magical-Number-Seven]] — the famous (if somewhat oversimplified) claim that working memory holds about seven items. You may also have some intuition about how expertise feels different from novice learning: experts seem to "just know" things without effortful retrieval. These intuitions are the soil in which CLT grows.
>
> **From PKM practice:** If you use [[zettelkasten]], [[obsidian]], [[anki]], or any systematic note-taking workflow, you have direct experience with the cognitive phenomena this report explains. The relief of capturing a complex idea into a note, the frustration of trying to read and take notes simultaneously, the satisfaction of rediscovering a linked idea months later — these are CLT in action, even if you have never used the framework's vocabulary.
>
> **Connecting threads:** The report traces a path from [[Baddeley's-Working-Memory-Model]] → [[intrinsic-cognitive-load]], [[extraneous-cognitive-load]], and [[germane-cognitive-load]] → instructional effects that follow from load theory → [[cognitive-offloading]] as the mechanism linking CLT to PKM → specific design principles. See also [[cog-sci-report-metacognition-as-central-integrating-mechanism-for-pkm-2025111306]] and [[Cognitive-Load-Measurement-and-Self-Monitoring-in-PKM-Practice]] for adjacent treatments.
>
> **Guiding question:** As you read, ask yourself: *For any given PKM practice I use or am considering, which type of cognitive load does it increase, and which does it reduce? Is that trade-off justified by the learning outcome it enables?*
>
> *Calibrated for advanced reader.* This report does not hedge foundational claims. Where the science is well-established, it is presented as such. Where debates persist, they are flagged explicitly.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-and-pkm-foundational-report-2026-04-12_report]]*
