# Correspondent Inference Theory

> [!definition] Correspondent Inference Theory
> **Correspondent inference theory** (Jones & Davis, 1965) is a model of person perception that addresses the conditions under which observers make **correspondent inferences** — inferring that a person's observed behavior reflects a stable, personal disposition. The theory holds that observers make dispositional attributions most readily when: the behavior produces distinctive (unique, non-redundant) effects; the behavior is socially non-common (uncommon); and the behavior is non-role-prescribed (not required by the social position). Low social desirability of the behavior also amplifies dispositional attribution. The theory systematized the conditions under which the observer's inference "this behavior reflects this person's character" is cognitively licensed.
