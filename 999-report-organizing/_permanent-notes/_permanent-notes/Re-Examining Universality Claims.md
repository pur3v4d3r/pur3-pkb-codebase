## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] Re-Examining Universality Claims
> The cross-cultural challenge to SDT and COT is deeper than it might first appear. It is not merely a question of whether the GCOS is a good measurement instrument across cultures — it is a question about whether the foundational construct, autonomy as experienced through individual self-determination, has the same psychological architecture across the human developmental range. What I find most intellectually honest in this debate is that the cross-cultural evidence does not cleanly falsify SDT's universality claim but complicates it substantially. The evidence consistently shows that autonomy need satisfaction predicts well-being across cultures — suggesting genuine universality in the functional role of autonomy. But the pathways through which autonomy need is expressed, the social forms that support it, and the individual-difference structure of orientations toward it may be meaningfully more variable across cultures than early COT formulations acknowledged. The project of a genuinely cross-cultural COT — one that preserves the theory's core insights while radically de-centering its Western assumptions about individuals, autonomy, and development — remains substantially incomplete.
> *— [[causality-orientations-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-01_report]]*
