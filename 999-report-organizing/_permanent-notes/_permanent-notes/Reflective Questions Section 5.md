## Reflections

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions: Section 5
> 1. Review your current academic reading practices honestly. What proportion of your strategy use falls into "high utility" (retrieval practice, elaborative interrogation, self-explanation) versus "low utility" (re-reading, passive highlighting) categories? What would it take to shift this distribution?
> 2. Self-explanation requires approaching difficult passages as opportunities for explanation rather than obstacles to avoid. How do you currently respond to passages you find opaque? What would a "self-explanation habit" look like in practice in your reading?
> 3. The claim that strategy selection is the master skill implies that the most important thing to develop may not be mastery of any single strategy but fluency in selecting across a repertoire. How would you design a reading practice that develops this meta-strategic flexibility?
> *— [[developing-reading-comprehension-and-metacognition-for-academic-texts-foundational-report-2026-04-08_report]]*
