## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Credibility Premium of Internal Verbal Persuasion
> Research on source credibility in persuasion establishes that perceived expertise and objectivity are the primary determinants of persuasive impact. Inner speech carries a credibility premium in the domain of self-assessment because we experience it as unmediated access to our own cognitive state, not as an attempt to influence us. This means that negative inner speech — "I'm struggling because I'm not intelligent enough for this material" — may have *higher* persuasive impact on self-efficacy than the same message delivered externally, because it is perceived as an objective self-report rather than a motivated evaluation. The implication is significant: correcting inner speech vocabulary matters more, not less, than correcting externally delivered feedback.
> *— [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25_report]]*
