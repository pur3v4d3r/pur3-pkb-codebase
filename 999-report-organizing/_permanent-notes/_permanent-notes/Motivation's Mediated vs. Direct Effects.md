## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Motivation's Mediated vs. Direct Effects
> The accumulated MSLQ literature suggests motivation's effects on academic performance are substantially (though not completely) mediated by cognitive strategy use and metacognitive regulation. Studies by Pintrich, Wolters, and colleagues consistently show that self-efficacy and mastery goal orientation predict strategy use, which in turn predicts performance. Direct paths from motivational variables to performance, controlling for strategy use, are typically smaller and less consistent. This pattern does not undermine the theoretical importance of motivation in SRL — but it raises a pointed question about whether the mechanism is motivational *regulation* (as Pintrich claims) or motivational *activation* (motivation enables strategy use without the learner actively managing motivation).
> *— [[pintrichs-framework-srl-focused-analysis-2026-03-23_report]]*
