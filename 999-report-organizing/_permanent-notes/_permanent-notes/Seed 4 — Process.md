> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Process
> **Q:** Describe the Nelson-Narens monitoring-control architecture as applied to reading.
> **A:** Object level = direct text processing (decoding, parsing, semantic integration). Meta level = metacognitive monitoring of object-level processes. Monitoring flows upward: represents state of comprehension. Control flows downward: adjusts object-level strategy in response. Effective comprehension monitoring requires: (1) accurate monitoring signals, (2) reliable monitoring-control coupling, (3) appropriate control responses.
> **Source:** Phase II, Section 2.3
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #nelson-narens, #metacognitive-monitoring, #reading

> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Process
> **Q:** What is the Fluency Illusion and why does it impair metacognitive monitoring accuracy?
> **A:** The Fluency Illusion is the misattribution of processing fluency (ease of processing) as evidence of deep learning or comprehension. It impairs monitoring accuracy because fluency is a non-diagnostic cue for long-term retention — re-reading produces fluency without improving retention, but the learner's JOLs become inflated because the monitoring system uses fluency as its primary cue.
> **Source:** Phase IV; Appendix Section 1
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #metacognition, #fluency-illusion, #monitoring, #calibration
> *— [[metacognition-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Process
> **Q:** What is the monitoring-control hierarchy in the Nelson-Narens model, and what role does metacognitive knowledge play in it?
> **A:** The model distinguishes an object level (where cognitive work occurs) and a meta-level (where processes are represented and managed). Monitoring flows upward (object → meta: the meta-level receives information about current processing). Control flows downward (meta → object: the meta-level modifies processing). Metacognitive knowledge provides the meta-level's representational content: the standards for monitoring and the inventory of control responses.
> **Source:** Phase III.3
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #Nelson-Narens #monitoring #control #metacognitive-knowledge
> *— [[metacognitive-knowledge-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!flashcard] Seed 4 — Process
> **Q:** What is the coverage threshold and what vocabulary size achieves it for general English text?
> **A:** The coverage threshold is the proportion of running words a reader must know to sustain comprehension and incidental acquisition — approximately 98% (1 unknown word per 50). Achieving 98% coverage of general English text requires knowledge of approximately 8,000–9,000 word families.
> **Source:** Phase II, Conceptual Foundations
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #vocabulary, #coverage-threshold, #nation, #reading
