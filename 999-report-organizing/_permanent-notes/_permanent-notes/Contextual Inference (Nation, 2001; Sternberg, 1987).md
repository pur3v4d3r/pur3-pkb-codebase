# Contextual Inference (Nation, 2001; Sternberg, 1987)

> [!definition] Contextual Inference (Nation, 2001; Sternberg, 1987)
> The cognitive process by which a reader constructs a provisional meaning for an unfamiliar word by attending to surrounding words, sentence structure, discourse structure, and prior world knowledge. Contextual inference is the primary mechanism of incidental vocabulary acquisition in reading; it is selective (not all readers attempt it for all unknown words), fallible (contextual cues can be misleading), and cumulative (repeated partial inferences across multiple encounters eventually converge on accurate meaning).
>
> **Boundary:** Contextual inference does not always produce accurate word meanings. High-ambiguity contexts, words with multiple meanings, or words used metaphorically can generate misleading inferences. The accuracy of contextual inference depends heavily on the quality of the context surrounding the unknown word.
>
> **Operational Indicator:** A reader engaging in contextual inference pauses at an unknown word, re-reads the surrounding text, identifies semantic and syntactic cues, and formulates a provisional meaning before continuing. Readers who skip over unknown words are not engaging in contextual inference.
>
> **See also:** [[incidental-learning|Incidental Learning]], [[schema-theory|Schema Theory]], [[elaborative-inference|Elaborative Inference]], [[situation-models-in-text-comprehension|Situation Models in Text Comprehension]]
