## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Application Domain 1: Organisational Knowledge Management
> The same principles that govern individual PKM effectiveness — the Extraneous-First principle, the Trust Condition quality standard, the Retrieval-as-Computation principle — apply to organisational knowledge systems (wikis, knowledge bases, documentation systems). Organisations consistently create knowledge archives that fail the Trust Condition: documentation is outdated, inconsistently formatted, impossible to navigate without tribal knowledge about where things are hidden. The boundary conditions identified in Section 7 also apply: some tacit knowledge that lives in experienced practitioners cannot be extracted into documentation, and attempting to do so can create a false confidence in the completeness of the documented system. Organisations that understand the cognitive offloading framework can design knowledge management infrastructure that genuinely distributes expertise rather than archiving it into inaccessibility.
>
> **Structural principle transferred:** The [[extended-mind-thesis]] coupling conditions (reliability, trust, prior endorsement) define quality standards for any cognitive extension, individual or organisational.
>
> **See also:** [[distributed-cognition]], [[Organisational-Learning]]
