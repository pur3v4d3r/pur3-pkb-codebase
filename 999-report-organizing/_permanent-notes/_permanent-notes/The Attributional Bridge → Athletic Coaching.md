## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] The Attributional Bridge → Athletic Coaching
> In athletic contexts, the interpreter between a performance outcome and an athlete's confidence update is the coach's (or athlete's own) causal account of what happened. A missed free throw attributed to "you weren't focused" (controllable, effort) versus "you're not a clutch player" (uncontrollable, ability) will produce dramatically different self-efficacy trajectories — and different training investments — through the identical mechanism as the academic SRL cycle. The structural isomorphism is complete: athletic self-regulation also has Forethought (pre-game preparation and confidence), Performance (execution and real-time monitoring), and Self-Reflection (post-game review and causal attribution) phases. The asymmetric ratchet is visible in sports slumps: athletes in slumps typically receive more encouragement but progressively withdraw from the strategic resources (play selection, risk-taking) that would break the cycle, because the slump has depleted the Forethought Phase self-efficacy that would sanction those resources.
> *— [[zimmerman-srl-focused-analysis-2026-03-22_report]]*
