## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application Bridge: Feedback System Design in Organizations
> In organizational feedback systems — performance reviews, 360-degree assessments, developmental feedback programmes — the approach-avoidance asymmetry predicts that feedback systems structured around avoiding failure (don't miss targets, don't fall below benchmark) will activate PAV-like regulatory dynamics in recipients, suppressing genuine learning from feedback and increasing defensive processing. Adaptive alternatives: structure feedback explicitly around learning and growth targets (approach); use criterion-referenced rather than comparative standards; separate developmental feedback from evaluative judgment; create low-stakes practice contexts where failure-signal processing is decoupled from reward and punishment consequences. The key adaptation from the educational domain: the *stakes decoupling* that enables mastery-approach orientation in educational contexts is achievable in organizational settings through structured developmental feedback distinct from formal performance evaluation.
> *— [[achievement-goal-theory-focused-analysis-2026-03-24_report]]*
