# Actor-Observer Asymmetry

> [!definition] Actor-Observer Asymmetry
> The **actor-observer asymmetry** (Jones & Nisbett, 1971) is the tendency for actors — people performing a behavior — to attribute their own actions to situational factors, and for observers of the identical behavior to attribute it to the actor's dispositions. The asymmetry is theorized to reflect differential attentional focus: actors attend to the situational context from the inside; observers attend to the person who is perceptually salient against the situational background. Contemporary meta-analyses have identified this asymmetry as more reliable for negative behaviors and less universal than originally claimed, with emotional involvement and cultural context moderating the effect.
