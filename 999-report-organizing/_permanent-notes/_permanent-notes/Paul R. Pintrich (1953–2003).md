## Additional Material

> [!person] Paul R. Pintrich (1953–2003)
> **Core Contribution:** Developed the most comprehensive theoretical framework for SRL in the literature, unifying motivational and cognitive components through the 4×4 matrix architecture. Created the Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire (MSLQ), the most widely used instrument in SRL research. Authored over 100 articles and book chapters before his death in 2003.
>
> **Relationship to Other Figures/Frameworks:** Trained in motivation theory and drew heavily from [[Social-Cognitive-Theory-—-Bandura|Bandura's social cognitive theory]]. Extended Zimmerman's work by adding explicit motivational and contextual areas. Contributed alongside Carol Dweck and Andrew Elliot to the development of [[achievement-goal-theory|achievement goal theory]]. His motivational science perspective was explicitly integrative — attempting to bridge motivational, cognitive, and contextual explanations.
>
> **Key Works:** *A motivational science perspective on the role of student motivation in learning and teaching* (2003, Journal of Educational Psychology); *A conceptual framework for assessing motivation and SRL in college students* (2004); *MSLQ Manual* with Smith, Garcia, & McKeachie (1991).
>
> **Relevance to This Analysis:** Every phase and area in the 4×4 matrix is traceable to Pintrich's theoretical papers. The identification of motivational and context regulation as first-class regulated domains is his signature contribution.
> *— [[pintrichs-framework-srl-focused-analysis-2026-03-23_report]]*
