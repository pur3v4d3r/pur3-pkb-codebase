> [!section-summary] Level 3 Summary
> At the mechanism level, element interactivity appeared as a clear explanatory engine. At the substructural level, we now see its internal components: elements that are expertise-relative, not fixed; interactivity that is defined by the simultaneous processing requirement; the isolated-interacting elements distinction that generates a key instructional effect; the counting problem that prevents objective quantification; the topological structure of interactions (linear, hierarchical, network) that may independently affect difficulty; and the fundamental insight that elements are functional processing units, not ontological primitives. The concept remains powerful but is revealed to be inherently learner-relative and resistant to context-independent measurement — a feature that Level 6 (Frontier) will engage as CLT's central unresolved problem. The next level will examine how this substructure produces observable behavior: the specific instructional effects that CLT predicts and explains.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 3 Summary
> At the previous level, [[Element-Interactivity]] was a count of mutually constraining elements. At THIS level, both the *element* and the *interaction* turn out to be more contested constructs than the surface formulations suggest — elements are learner-relative units of representation that correspond to chunks in long-term memory, interactivity is a graded relational property that operates at multiple levels of representation simultaneously, and the resulting construct is a *family* rather than a single quantity. The ambiguity is productive but creates measurement problems. The next level will trace how this learner-relative element count *changes over time* as expertise develops, and will reveal the developmental dynamics that the static account cannot capture.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-element-interactivity-deep-dive-2026-04-20]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 3 Summary
> At mechanism level, we saw that monitoring uses proxy cues. At the SUBSTRUCTURE level, we've now examined the individual cues and their reliability profiles: processing fluency (dominant but multi-sourced and variable in validity), familiarity (valid for recognition, misleading for recall), accessibility (high validity via partial retrieval), and extrinsic cues (time, repetitions, effort — generally low validity). The cues combine through salience-driven weighting rather than formal computation. The critical finding is that the monitoring system's accuracy is determined largely by which cues are salient, which in turn depends on the learning conditions. Retrieval fluency is the highest-validity cue, which explains why paradigms that elicit retrieval (delayed JOLs, practice testing) produce the best monitoring accuracy.
>
> **The next level zooms into dynamics:** How does monitoring accuracy, once established, propagate through the regulatory control loop?
> *— [[metacognitive-monitoring-accuracy-calibration-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 3 Summary
> Beneath the regulatory architecture of Level 2 lies a substrate of three basic psychological needs — [[autonomy-need|autonomy]], [[competence-need|competence]], [[relatedness-need|relatedness]] — whose satisfaction is necessary for internalization to proceed and whose frustration produces predictable patterns of motivational degradation. Each need does specific developmental work in the continuum (relatedness for the initial taking-in, autonomy for the move from introjection to identification, competence for the stabilization of identified regulation against difficulty), and the three are not substitutable. Need frustration is functionally distinct from need deprivation, with different downstream signatures. Heterogeneity of internalization within a person reflects local conditions of need support around specific behaviors rather than any global property of the person.
>
> Level 4 will open the dynamics — the reciprocal feedback by which need satisfaction and motivational quality produce one another over time, and the timescales on which this dynamic operates.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-internalization-continuum-deep-dive-2026-04-21]]*

> [!section-summary] Level 3 Summary
> At mechanism level, we identified the Central Executive as the site of resource competition. At substructure level, we now see the precise neural implementation: the competition is specifically at the **dorsolateral prefrontal cortex** level, with all other neural systems architecturally distinct. Locomotion's cortical engagement is minimal for habituated walking (primarily subcortical Level 1-3 control), but can spike at **turning points, terrain changes, and moments of high reading difficulty** (prioritization stopping). The phonological loop and visuospatial sketchpad are loaded by different tasks (reading and walking respectively) with minimal cross-talk. The episodic buffer is probably unaffected or slightly enhanced. The substructural picture is one of remarkably clean resource separation with a narrow prefrontal bottleneck.
> *— [[walking-academic-reading-working-memory-deep-dive-2026-04-13_report]]*
