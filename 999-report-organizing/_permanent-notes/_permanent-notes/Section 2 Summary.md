> Weiner's three-dimensional taxonomy — locus, stability, and controllability — transforms the infinite variety of specific causal attributions into a systematic architecture with predictable emotional and motivational consequences. The locus dimension determines self-referent affects (pride, shame); the stability dimension determines future expectancy (hope, hopelessness); the controllability dimension determines social-moral affects (anger, pity, guilt, gratitude). This taxonomy integrates with earlier models (Kelley's covariation model, Jones and Davis's correspondent inference theory) by providing the *output* architecture that those models lack — specifying not just how attributions are formed, but what they *do* once formed.

> [!section-summary] Section 2 Summary
> Working memory as a theoretical construct emerged from the inadequacies of the Atkinson-Shiffrin multi-store model, which treated short-term memory as a single unitary buffer that was both the mandatory gateway for long-term learning and the general workspace for complex cognition. Evidence from neuropsychological dissociations, individual differences research, and Baddeley & Hitch's own dual-task experiments revealed that this single-buffer view was empirically inadequate. The 1974 multicomponent model proposed a central executive coordinating two specialized slave systems (phonological loop and visuospatial sketchpad), with a fourth component — the episodic buffer — added in 2000. The dual-task methodology became the primary empirical tool for dissecting component functions and establishing the independence and specialization of working memory subsystems.
> *— [[baddeley-model-of-working-memory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*

> [!section-summary] Section 2 Summary
> The chunking account of expertise emerged from the convergence of three lines of work: de Groot's discovery that grandmasters do not search more deeply than masters but begin search from a better starting point; Chase and Simon's demonstration that this advantage results from the encoding of meaningful positions into larger memory units; and Miller's theoretical framework that distinguished the *number* of items in immediate memory (which is fixed) from the *information content* of each item (which can vary). Together these established that expertise resides in the contents of long-term memory rather than in any general cognitive enhancement, and that those contents act on incoming material by changing what counts as a single unit.

> [!section-summary] Section 2 Summary
> CET's theoretical architecture rests on two mediating constructs: Perceived Locus of Causality (PLOC), which tracks the felt sense of self-causation vs. external control, and Perceived Competence, which tracks the felt sense of mastery and effectiveness. External events exercise their motivational influence through these mediators — those that shift PLOC externally undermine intrinsic motivation via the controlling mechanism, those that diminish perceived competence undermine it via the amotivating mechanism, and those that affirm competence within autonomy-supportive contexts can enhance intrinsic motivation. The asymmetry between monetary and verbal rewards is explicable within this architecture: the former tends to be controlling, the latter informational.

> Piaget's cognitive constructivism provides the foundational mechanisms of individual knowledge construction: schemas as the structural units, assimilation and accommodation as the dual processes of incorporating and restructuring experience, and equilibration as the self-regulating drive toward increasingly coherent cognitive organization. Contemporary developmental psychology has substantially revised Piaget's timeline and questioned the sharp boundaries of his stages, but the core insights — active construction, qualitative cognitive reorganization, the necessity of supported challenge — remain foundational to learning science.
