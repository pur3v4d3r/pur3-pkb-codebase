## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Transfer Domain 2: Organisational Knowledge Management
> **Structural Principle from CLT/PKM:** The PKM trade-off between extraneous load reduction (simple, navigable structure) and germane load production (synthesis requirements, mandatory connection identification) applies to any organisational system that manages collective knowledge — corporate wikis, research group note systems, project knowledge bases, onboarding documentation.
>
> **Application in Organisational KM:** The failure modes identified in Section 5 (taxonomy proliferation, synthesis debt, link abandonment, decision proliferation, trust deficit) are endemic in enterprise knowledge management systems, often at far greater scale. The principle that trust deficit is the most pernicious failure mode takes on particular significance in collaborative systems where multiple contributors' inconsistent practices accumulate into an unreliable corpus that no individual actor has motivation or authority to fix. The minimum viable structure principle suggests that organisation-level KM systems should err toward fewer categories with clear definitions over comprehensive taxonomies with ambiguous boundaries — a counterintuitive prescription for organisations that instinctively build comprehensive taxonomies as a first design move.
>
> **Boundary Condition:** Organisational KM adds a social coordination dimension absent from individual PKM. Structures that are individually suboptimal but collectively legible (a standard folder hierarchy that everyone follows consistently) may outperform individually optimal structures that coordination costs prevent from being applied consistently. The minimum structure principle requires modification: minimum viable shared structure is not the same as minimum individual structure.
>
