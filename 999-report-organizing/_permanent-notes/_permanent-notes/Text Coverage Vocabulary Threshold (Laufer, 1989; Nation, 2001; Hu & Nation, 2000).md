# Text Coverage / Vocabulary Threshold (Laufer, 1989; Nation, 2001; Hu & Nation, 2000)

> [!definition] Text Coverage / Vocabulary Threshold (Laufer, 1989; Nation, 2001; Hu & Nation, 2000)
> **Definition:** The proportion of running words in a text that a reader already knows. The *vocabulary threshold* (or coverage threshold) is the level of text coverage — approximately 95–98% — below which comprehension breaks down sufficiently to prevent the contextual inference on which incidental vocabulary acquisition depends. At 95% coverage, approximately 1 in 20 words is unknown; at 98%, 1 in 50. For English fiction, 98% coverage corresponds to approximately 8,000–9,000 word families; for academic text, substantially more.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The single most important empirical finding in the dialectical challenge — the threshold creates a paradox for the reading-primary hypothesis and forces the conditioned reconstruction.
>
> **Operational Indicators:** Calculated by identifying all word tokens in a text and determining what proportion fall within a given frequency band (using resources like Nation's BNC/COCA word lists).
>
> **Cross-References:** [[reading-fluency]], [[working-memory-in-reading]], [[cognitive-load-theory]]
