> [!flashcard] Seed 2
> **Q:** What is the "differentiated ability concept" and why is it the developmental precondition for performance-avoidance goal adoption?
> **A:** The developmental achievement (typically in middle childhood) of understanding that effort and ability are independent causes of performance. Once differentiated, high effort required to succeed can imply low ability. This makes failure under normative comparison identity-threatening — because failure implies low fixed ability — creating the conditions for PAV goal adoption.
> **Source:** Phase II, Nicholls Foundation
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Process

> **Q:** What is the emotion-mediation claim in Weiner's mature model, and why does it matter for intervention design?
> **A:** The claim is that the path from attribution to behavior runs through specific discrete emotions generated by dimensional classification — not directly from attribution to behavior. It matters because interventions that change cognitive attributions without producing corresponding affective change fail to propagate through to behavioral change.

> [!flashcard] Seed 2
> **Q:** How does the fluency illusion generate metacognitive miscalibration in typical study conditions?
> **A:** Re-reading generates high processing fluency (familiarity + recognition ease), which the monitoring system interprets as competence. This inflates Feeling-of-Knowing judgments without corresponding increases in retrievable knowledge. Students arrive at formative assessment events with monitoring systems that over-report their own competence because their study method was fluency-generating rather than calibration-testing.
> **Source:** Phase II, Fluency Illusion definition; Phase IV, mechanism analysis

> **Type:** Process

> **Q:** Why is ability vocabulary described as "the primary motivational toxin" in the context of academic reading?

> [!flashcard] Seed 2
> **Q:** What is the difference between accommodation and restructuring in Schema Theory?
>
> **A:** Accommodation modifies an existing schema to incorporate information that cannot be directly assimilated — it *adjusts* structure. Restructuring creates a new schema where none existed — it *builds* structure. Accommodation requires a relevant schema to exist and be modified; restructuring does not. Accommodation handles degree novelty; restructuring handles categorical novelty.
>
> **Source:** Phase II
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Distinction

> [!flashcard] Seed 2
> **Q**: What is the difference between a schema as a "data structure" and a schema as a "soft attractor"?
> **A**: A data structure is discrete, pre-formed, and retrieved from storage; an attractor is a dynamically stable pattern that the cognitive system settles into given sufficient contextual matching with prior regularities. The attractor account explains context-sensitivity (attractors shift with context), cultural embeddedness (attractors form on cultural experience distribution), and the construction problem (attractors emerge from statistical learning, not prior schemas).
> **Source**: Phase V, Reconstructive Synthesis
> **Difficulty**: Advanced
> **Type**: Distinction

> [!flashcard] Seed 2
> **Q:** What are the five dimensions of the Event Indexing Model, and why do they matter for reading?
>
> **A:** Temporal (when), spatial (where), causal (why), intentional (what agents are trying to achieve), and entity (who and their current state). Skilled readers continuously monitor all five dimensions as they read, updating their situation model when any shifts. The dimensions matter because situation model failure often maps to failure in a *specific* dimension — naming the dimension of confusion allows targeted intervention rather than indiscriminate re-reading.
>

> [!flashcard] Seed 2
> **Q:** Why is the comprehension criterion the structural counter to the fluency illusion?
> **A:** Because the comprehension criterion requires *cognitive production* (recall, explanation, example generation) to satisfy — which cannot be faked by recognition fluency. The fluency illusion operates through phenomenological ease of processing, which can feel like understanding without being it. A production-based criterion forces the learner to generate rather than recognize, revealing genuine comprehension status.
> **Source:** Phase IV, Goal Setting mechanism
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Type:** Process

> [!flashcard] Seed 2
> **Q:** What distinguishes an adaptive inference from a defensive inference in Zimmerman's Self-Reflection Phase?
>
> **A:** Adaptive inferences attribute performance outcomes to controllable, internal factors (strategy choice, effort) and produce specific strategy modifications for the next cycle. Defensive inferences attribute outcomes to uncontrollable or external factors (ability, task difficulty, luck) and produce no meaningful cycle modification, protecting self-esteem at the cost of adaptive updating.
>
> **Source:** Phase II & Phase IV, Adaptive/Defensive Inference Analysis

> [!flashcard] Seed 2
> **Q:** What is the "tight-loop assumption" in Zimmerman's model, and what is the primary evidence that challenges it?
>
> **A:** The tight-loop assumption is the implicit architectural claim that monitoring during the Performance Phase reliably flows into the Self-Reflection Phase's evaluation, which reliably modifies subsequent Forethought. It is challenged primarily by process-tracing studies (e.g., Azevedo) showing that learners frequently monitor without regulating — they notice comprehension failures without changing strategy — and by metacognitive calibration research showing that monitoring signals are systematically inaccurate in novices.
>
