## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Restructuring Resistance Problem
> Schema restructuring is not only cognitively demanding — it is actively resisted. Existing schemas are deeply embedded in associative networks; they support efficient processing across many domains, not just the domain where they are inadequate. Restructuring one schema can propagate destabilization throughout the network. Furthermore, existing schemas are often tied to identity and social belonging (e.g., political schemas, religious schemas). The cognitive mechanisms that prevent maladaptive schema oscillation — the stability that makes schemas useful — are the same mechanisms that create conceptual change resistance. This explains why genuine deep learning is difficult and why instructional design must create not just informational encounters but *experiences* that make schema inadequacy concrete and personally salient.
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
