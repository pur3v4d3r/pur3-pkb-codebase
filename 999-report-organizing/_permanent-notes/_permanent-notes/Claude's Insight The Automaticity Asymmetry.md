## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] Claude's Insight: The Automaticity Asymmetry
> Here is an insight that emerges from the mechanism level: the dual-task cost in walking-while-reading is **asymmetric** in a way that favors the walking reader. Walking becomes more automatic with practice and route familiarity — meaning the dual-task cost *decreases over time* as the walking component becomes more automated. Reading, by contrast, cannot meaningfully become more automatic for *novel* academic content (it requires controlled processing almost by definition, since the content is new). This means the system naturally optimizes itself by **reducing the cost of the non-reading task** while leaving the reading task unconstrained by automaticity limits. A reader who has spent months walking the same indoor route has a lower dual-task cost than a reader who just started walking while reading yesterday.
> *— [[walking-academic-reading-working-memory-deep-dive-2026-04-13_report]]*
