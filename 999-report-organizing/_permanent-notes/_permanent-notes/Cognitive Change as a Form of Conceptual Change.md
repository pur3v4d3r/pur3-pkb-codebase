## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Cognitive Change as a Form of Conceptual Change
> The parallels between therapeutic cognitive change and educational conceptual change are not merely analogical — they reflect a common underlying cognitive architecture. Both clinical schemas and naive conceptual frameworks share the features that make change difficult: they are implicit (operating below conscious awareness), they have high explanatory value within their domain (they "work" in the sense of generating predictions that are often confirmed), they are emotionally invested, and they are self-confirming (they create the experiences that appear to validate them). This means that the strategies effective in [[Conceptual-Change-Theory|conceptual change]] in educational contexts — creating cognitive conflict, providing intelligible and plausible alternatives, requiring active engagement with disconfirmatory evidence — are structurally similar to those used in cognitive-behavioral therapy.
> *— [[clinical-psychology-foundational-report-2026-03-19_report]]*
