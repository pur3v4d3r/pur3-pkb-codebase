# Motivational Lexicon** (Original Synthesis — this report)

> [!definition] Motivational Lexicon** (Original Synthesis — this report)
> **Definition:** The structured vocabulary repertoire that constitutes a learner's inner speech architecture for self-regulated academic engagement — specifically the vocabulary available for attributional narration, regulatory self-direction, self-efficacy evaluation, and goal construction. Distinct from domain vocabulary (vocabulary for content understanding) and strategy vocabulary (vocabulary for cognitive operations). The Motivational Lexicon is the set of words available for narrating, evaluating, and directing the motivational experience of learning itself.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The central construct of this report's original synthesis. Functions as the integrating concept linking Attribution Theory, SDT, Self-Efficacy Theory, and AGT through their shared implication that vocabulary determines motivational structure.
>
> **Operational Indicators:** Observable through the language used in think-aloud protocols, journal entries, and reflection responses; measurable through vocabulary richness in attributional, regulatory, and evaluative registers.
>
> **Etymology/Intellectual Lineage:** Synthesized in this report from Weiner's attribution dimensionality, Bandura's verbal persuasion source, SDT's regulatory language research, and the metacognitive monitoring literature.
>
> **Cross-References:** [[attribution-theory]], [[self-efficacy]], [[metacognitive-regulation]], [[internalization-continuum]], [[self-regulated-learning]]
