## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Important Nuance: Computation vs. Measurement
> Computational formalization of element interactivity is not the same as *measuring* element interactivity in a learner's brain. A computational model would tell you the *objective* element interactivity of materials for a learner with a given schema set. It would not tell you the *subjective* experience of load or the *actual* neural processing. These are complementary research programs: the formal model specifies what the brain *must* accomplish; the neuroscience program investigates *how* the brain accomplishes it.
>
> - **When the distinction matters:** When designing adaptive instruction — the formal model tells you what to adapt *to*; the neuroscience tells you what to *observe*.
> - **When it doesn't:** When making broad instructional design decisions (high vs. low interactivity materials), the formal model alone suffices.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*
