# Socratic Method

> [!definition] Socratic Method
> A dialogic pedagogical technique in which the teacher poses a sequence of open, probing questions designed to surface the implicit assumptions in a learner's beliefs, reveal internal contradictions, and motivate the reconstruction of understanding on firmer evidential and logical grounds. Named for Socrates, whose practice is documented in Plato's dialogues.

> [!evidence] Socratic Method
> **Term:** Socratic Method ([[socrates]], via Plato, ~399 BCE)
> **Definition:** A method of inquiry through structured dialogue in which the questioner elicits contradictions within the interlocutor's beliefs, demonstrating the limits of their knowledge and prompting deeper examination. Characterised by elenchus (cross-examination) and aporia (productive puzzlement).
> **Boundary conditions:** Requires a discourse community willing to engage; can be experienced as aggressive in cultures that value face-saving; effectiveness depends on the skill of the questioner.
> **Report significance:** Identified as the philosophical origin of critical thinking in Section 1.
> **See also:** [[socratic-questioning]], [[Socratic-Seminar]], [[Dialogue]], [[Philosophical-Inquiry]]
