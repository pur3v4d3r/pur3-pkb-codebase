# Intrinsic Motivation (Deci & Ryan, 1985)

> [!definition] Intrinsic Motivation (Deci & Ryan, 1985)
> Motivation that arises from the inherent interest, enjoyment, or satisfaction of an activity itself, independent of separable outcomes. An intrinsically motivated person engages in an activity because it is experienced as freely chosen, interesting, and inherently rewarding. The prototypical markers are: voluntary initiation, persistence in the absence of external prompts, positive affect during engagement, and free-choice resumption after an interruption. Intrinsic motivation is NOT the same as liking an activity — one can like (be positively valenced toward) an activity one is extrinsically motivated to perform. The critical property is the perceived source of regulation: intrinsic motivation is internally regulated; extrinsic motivation is regulated by anticipated consequences outside the activity itself.

> [!evidence] Intrinsic Motivation (Deci & Ryan, 1985)
> The disposition to engage in an activity for its own sake — because the activity is inherently interesting, enjoyable, or satisfying. Intrinsically motivated behavior is autonomous in the fullest sense: it does not require external incentives, threats, or justifications. The regulation of the behavior is fully *internal* to the self. A learner who reads a history book out of genuine fascination with how historical events unfolded is intrinsically motivated; one who reads the same book because it is required for an exam they fear failing is not.
> *— [[metacognition-motivation-foundational-report-2026-03-05_report]]*

> [!evidence] Intrinsic Motivation (Deci & Ryan, 1985)
> Engagement with an activity for its inherent interest and satisfaction, without reliance on external contingencies. Operationally identified by persistence in the absence of rewards and free-choice engagement when alternatives are available. Distinguishable from *identified* extrinsic motivation (which feels similarly autonomous but is ultimately instrumental) by the absence of any separable goal beyond the activity itself.
