> [!person] Gregory Schraw
> Educational psychologist; developed the Schraw-Moshman bipartite metacognitive framework and the Metacognitive Awareness Inventory (MAI), the most widely used self-report measure of metacognition in educational research. His theoretical contribution clarified the separability of knowledge-of-cognition from regulation-of-cognition as empirically distinct constructs with different developmental trajectories and instructional implications.
>
> **Lineage:** Works within the Flavellian tradition; extended by Paris's declarative/procedural/conditional taxonomy; influenced by information-processing theories of self-regulation.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** The Schraw-Moshman framework anchors the conceptual foundations in Phase II and the theoretical landscape in Phase III.
> *— [[metacognition-and-reading-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Gregory Schraw
> Educational psychologist (University of Nevada, Las Vegas). Developed the Metacognitive Awareness Inventory with Rayne Dennison (1994), integrating Flavell's knowledge taxonomy with Brown's regulatory framework. Authored numerous empirical and theoretical papers on metacognition in academic learning contexts, with particular attention to the role of metacognitive monitoring in reading comprehension and problem-solving.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by Flavell, Brown, and Zimmerman. Applied a psychometric tradition absent from earlier theoretical accounts.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** The Knowledge of Cognition / Regulation of Cognition distinction that structures Phase II derives primarily from Schraw and Dennison (1994).
> *— [[metacognition-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Gregory Schraw
> Educational psychologist (UNLV). Developed the Knowledge of Cognition / Regulation of Cognition decomposition (1994) and the Metacognitive Awareness Inventory with Dennison. Made metacognition empirically tractable for large-scale educational research by providing a reliable, validated self-report instrument.
>
> **Lineage:** Influenced by Flavell, Brown, and the educational psychology tradition. Influenced all subsequent MAI-based research on metacognition and academic achievement.
> *— [[metacognition-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!person] Gregory Schraw
> Educational psychologist at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas (later University of Nevada, Las Vegas College of Education). Schraw's contributions include the theoretical synthesis (with Moshman, 1995), the Metacognitive Awareness Inventory (with Dennison, 1994), and extensive empirical work on metacognitive monitoring, calibration, and the effects of metacognitive training on academic performance.
>
> **Lineage:** Draws on Flavell, Brown, and Nelson-Narens; integrated into the SRL literature through connections to Pintrich and Zimmerman.
>
> **Relationship to Report:** Provides the taxonomic framework (planning/monitoring/evaluating) adopted throughout Phase II and Phase IV, and the empirical evidence base for the educational significance of metacognitive regulation training.
> *— [[metacognitive-regulation-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*
