# Regulatory Mode (Approach vs. Avoidance)

> [!definition] Regulatory Mode (Approach vs. Avoidance)
> **Definition:** A fundamental dimension of motivated behavior distinguishing whether behavioral regulation is oriented toward achieving a desired positive outcome (approach) or toward preventing an undesired negative outcome (avoidance). NOT equivalent to the distinction between positive and negative affect — both approach and avoidance can involve positive or negative affect; the distinction is regulatory, not affective.
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This is the primary analytic construct in this report. The regulatory mode primacy thesis claims that this dimension is more theoretically fundamental than the mastery-performance distinction for predicting motivational consequences in achievement contexts.
> **Operational Indicators:** Approach regulation produces broader attentional scope, higher risk tolerance, and more adaptive failure responses. Avoidance regulation produces narrowed attentional scope, lower risk tolerance, and threat-dominated failure responses.
> **Etymology/Intellectual Lineage:** Theorized across multiple frameworks: Gray's BAS/BIS neurobiological model, Higgins' regulatory focus theory, Carver and Scheier's control theory, and Elliot's achievement goal expansion.
> **Cross-References:** [[2×2-Framework]], [[goal-framing]], [[mastery-approach-goal]], [[performance-avoidance-goal]]
