# Overjustification Effect

> [!definition] Overjustification Effect
> The specific psychological phenomenon in which adding an external justification (reward) to a behaviour that is already intrinsically motivated reduces the person's subsequent intrinsic interest in that behaviour. The term "overjustification" refers to the idea that the external reason provides more justification than is needed — and in the process, changes the person's interpretation of why they were performing the activity in the first place. Originally named by Lepper, Greene, and Nisbett (1973); theoretically explained by CET's PLOC mechanism.

> [!evidence] Overjustification Effect
> [**Overjustification-Effect-Definition**:: The phenomenon whereby introducing an expected external reward for an activity that is already intrinsically motivating reduces subsequent intrinsic motivation for that activity when the reward is no longer available. Originally demonstrated by Lepper, Greene, and Nisbett (1973) and named by analogy to the "insufficient justification" effect in cognitive dissonance theory. The mechanism in CET terms is that the external reward shifts PLOC from internal (doing the activity for its own sake) to external (doing the activity for the reward), and when the reward is removed, the original internal PLOC does not automatically recover.]

> [!evidence] Overjustification Effect
> The empirical finding that providing extrinsic rewards for an activity that was already intrinsically motivated tends to undermine subsequent intrinsic motivation for that activity. First demonstrated experimentally by Deci (1971) and Lepper, Greene & Nisbett (1973). The underlying mechanism is a PLOC shift: the introduction of the reward causes the person to perceive the activity as being done *for* the reward rather than for its inherent interest. **See also**: [[overjustification-effect]], [[cognitive-evaluation-theory]], [[intrinsic-motivation]]
