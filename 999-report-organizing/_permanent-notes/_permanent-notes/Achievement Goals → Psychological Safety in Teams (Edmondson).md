## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] Achievement Goals → Psychological Safety in Teams (Edmondson)
> Amy Edmondson's research on [[psychological-safety|psychological safety]] in teams maps onto the PAV dynamics identified in this analysis with near-perfect structural isomorphism. Teams low in psychological safety show the same behavioral signatures as PAV-oriented learners: suppressed help-seeking, avoidance of admitting errors, surface compliance over genuine engagement, and reduced willingness to take interpersonal risks. The mechanism is structurally identical: the self-presentation concern — not appearing incompetent to the group — activates avoidance regulation that suppresses the very behaviors (transparent error reporting, asking questions, proposing novel approaches) that make teams learn and perform effectively. The structural analogue is precise: psychological safety is the team-level equivalent of a mastery-approach motivational climate. The application bridge runs both ways: achievement goal research on climate design can inform psychological safety interventions, and psychological safety research (which has a rich intervention literature) can inform motivational climate design in educational contexts.
