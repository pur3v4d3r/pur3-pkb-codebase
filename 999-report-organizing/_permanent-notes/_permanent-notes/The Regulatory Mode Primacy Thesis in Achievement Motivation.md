## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Regulatory Mode Primacy Thesis in Achievement Motivation
> The analysis in this report supports what I will call the *regulatory mode primacy thesis* in achievement motivation: the approach-avoidance distinction is the primary axis of motivational architecture in competence-based achievement contexts, and the mastery-performance distinction is the secondary axis that modulates the consequences of that primary regulation. This thesis has three components. First, approach and avoidance regulatory modes activate fundamentally different cognitive processing profiles — differing in attentional scope, information integration style, error tolerance, and help-seeking behavior — and these profiles are not symmetric under environmental pressure. Second, the standard by which competence is defined (mastery vs. performance) modulates the stability and cost of these profiles rather than generating them. Third, the practical implication is that motivational climate interventions should prioritize reducing avoidance-mode activation before optimizing the competence-definition standard — because an environment structured to support mastery goals but that still activates avoidance regulation through high-stakes normative evaluation will generate PAP and PAV goal adoption, not MAP adoption.
