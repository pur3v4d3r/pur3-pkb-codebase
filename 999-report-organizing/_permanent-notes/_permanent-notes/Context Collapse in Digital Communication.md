# Context Collapse in Digital Communication

> [!definition] Context Collapse in Digital Communication
> *Context collapse* — a concept developed by danah boyd in social media research — describes the situation in digital communication where messages intended for specific audiences are instead visible to radically heterogeneous, unanticipated audiences simultaneously. The professional posting their political views on LinkedIn, the person whose private message is screenshotted and shared publicly, the speaker addressing one audience whose words are distributed to another: all experience context collapse. Managing context collapse requires either highly general communication (accessible and innocuous to all possible audiences) or careful channel selection — neither of which fully recovers the communicative richness of targeted, contextually calibrated face-to-face communication.
