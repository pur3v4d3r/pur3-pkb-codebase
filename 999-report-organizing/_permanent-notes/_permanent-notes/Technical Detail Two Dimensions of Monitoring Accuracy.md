## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technical Detail: Two Dimensions of Monitoring Accuracy
> [[calibration]] in the metacognition literature is assessed along two orthogonal dimensions that are frequently conflated in casual discussion but must be distinguished:
>
> 1. **Calibration (absolute accuracy):** The correspondence between mean confidence and mean performance. If a learner gives items a 70% confidence rating and gets 70% correct, they are perfectly calibrated in the absolute sense. Calibration is measured via calibration curves and the Brier score decomposition.
>
> 2. **Resolution (relative accuracy):** The ability to discriminate between items one knows and items one does not know, regardless of the absolute level of confidence. A learner has good resolution if they assign higher confidence to items they get right and lower confidence to items they get wrong — even if their absolute confidence level is consistently too high. Resolution is measured via gamma correlations, the area under the ROC curve, or da' from signal detection theory.
>
> **Precision:** This distinction is exact, not approximate. Calibration and resolution are mathematically independent — a learner can be well-calibrated with poor resolution, or poorly calibrated with excellent resolution.
> **Dependencies:** Requires understanding of basic psychometric concepts.
> *— [[metacognitive-monitoring-accuracy-calibration-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*
