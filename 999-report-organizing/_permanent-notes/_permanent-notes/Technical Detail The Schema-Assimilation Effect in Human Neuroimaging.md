## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Technical Detail: The Schema-Assimilation Effect in Human Neuroimaging
> Van Kesteren et al. (2012) identified a neural dissociation between schema-congruent and schema-incongruent encoding:
>
> - **Schema-congruent information:** Showed engagement of medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), a neocortical region associated with schema storage, with reduced hippocampal activation. Interpretation: congruent information is assimilated directly to neocortical schemas, bypassing heavy hippocampal encoding.
>
> - **Schema-incongruent information:** Showed greater hippocampal activation and relatively reduced mPFC engagement. Interpretation: incongruent information requires hippocampal episodic encoding because it cannot be directly assimilated to neocortical schemas.
>
> **Clinical implication:** This dissociation predicts that hippocampal damage (as in amnesia) should disrupt learning of schema-incongruent information more than schema-congruent information. Evidence from amnesic patients partially supports this: amnesic patients can show surprisingly good learning of schema-consistent facts while showing impaired learning of schema-inconsistent facts.
>
> **The prior knowledge facilitation effect:** This is the neural basis for the pedagogical principle that prior knowledge facilitates learning: schema availability reduces the hippocampal encoding demand for new information, making schema-congruent learning faster and less resource-intensive. Educational interventions that activate prior schemas before new content should, on this account, produce measurable differences in mPFC/hippocampal engagement and better long-term retention.
> *— [[schema-and-how-they-work-deep-dive-2026-04-08_report]]*
