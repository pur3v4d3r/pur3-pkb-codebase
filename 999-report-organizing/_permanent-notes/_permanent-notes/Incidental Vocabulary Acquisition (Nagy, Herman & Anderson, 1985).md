# Incidental Vocabulary Acquisition (Nagy, Herman & Anderson, 1985)

> [!definition] Incidental Vocabulary Acquisition (Nagy, Herman & Anderson, 1985)
> The learning of word meanings as a by-product of reading for comprehension, without any explicit intention to learn vocabulary. The term distinguishes this mode from intentional vocabulary learning (direct study) and from implicit learning (unconscious statistical tracking). Incidental acquisition from reading is neither fully intentional nor fully implicit — it typically involves conscious noticing of an unknown word, but the learning goal is comprehension rather than memorization.
>
> **Boundary:** "Incidental" does not mean "effortless" or "automatic." Incidental acquisition requires noticing, inferencing, and some degree of processing that goes beyond simple word recognition. The term refers to the *goal state* (comprehension, not vocabulary building) rather than the *process* (which involves active cognitive work).
>
> **Operational Indicator:** A reader who finishes a novel having learned three new words without having looked any of them up has engaged in incidental acquisition. A reader who stops at each unfamiliar word and records it for later review is transitioning toward intentional acquisition.
>

> [!evidence] Incidental Vocabulary Acquisition (Nagy, Herman & Anderson, 1985)
> The learning of word meanings as a by-product of reading for comprehension, without explicit intention to learn vocabulary. Distinguished from intentional acquisition (deliberate word study) by its goal state — comprehension rather than memorization — and from implicit learning by the conscious awareness of unfamiliar words. Research consistently finds acquisition rates of 5–15% per unfamiliar word encounter in natural reading, dependent on context quality.
>
> **Boundary:** "Incidental" describes the learning goal, not the cognitive process — incidental acquisition still requires conscious noticing and active inference. Unconscious statistical learning of word patterns is a separate mechanism not covered by this term.

> **Operational Indicator:** A reader finishing a novel who can now recognize and approximately define three words they previously did not know, without having deliberately studied any words.
