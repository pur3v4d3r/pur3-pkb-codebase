## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] The Schema Coherence Drive as a Learning Challenge
> The effort after meaning represents a fundamental challenge for education. Learners do not come to new material as blank slates — they come with existing schemas that generate strong interpretive expectations. When new information is genuinely inconsistent with existing schemas (as is often the case in conceptual change contexts — e.g., learning that the earth moves around the sun when one's everyday physics schema predicts the sun moves across the sky), the cognitive system's default response is not schema revision but assimilative distortion: the new information is interpreted in terms that make it consistent with the existing schema, often by fundamentally misunderstanding what is being taught. This is why conceptual change instruction must begin by creating genuine *cognitive conflict* — making the inadequacy of existing schemas viscerally apparent — before scaffolding the construction of more adequate schemas.
> *— [[schema-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
