> [!flashcard] Seed 14 — Connection
> **Q:** How does constructivism connect to self-regulated learning?
> **A:** If knowledge must be actively constructed by the learner rather than received, then the capacity to monitor, evaluate, and regulate one's own cognitive processes (self-regulated learning) is not supplementary but architecturally necessary for the learning process itself. The self-regulating learner can recognize when their current schema is inadequate (metacognitive monitoring), generating the productive disequilibrium that drives accommodation.
> **Source:** Phase V
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #constructivism, #self-regulated-learning, #metacognition, #connection

> [!flashcard] Seed 14 — Connection
> **Q:** How does [[Zimmerman's-Cyclical-SRL-Model]] connect to habit formation in the PKB framework?
> **A:** Habits function as automated volitional scaffolding in the SRL cycle: habitualized study routines execute in the performance phase even during periods of low motivation, because the contextual cue triggers the behavior without requiring goal activation. This frees metacognitive resources for genuine monitoring and strategy adjustment rather than procedural management. Habit formation is the process by which volitional strategies become unnecessary.

> [!flashcard] Seed 14 — Connection
> **Q:** How does the elaborative interrogation strategy operationalize the depth-of-processing principle in reading?
> **A:** Depth-of-processing principle: semantic/elaborative processing produces more durable encoding than surface processing. Elaborative interrogation operationalizes deep processing by requiring the reader to generate *why/how* explanations for text propositions, thereby enforcing semantic elaboration and prior-knowledge integration. The strategy converts passive proposition reception into active inferential construction — the functional equivalent of forced deep processing.
> **Source:** Phase III, Section 3.5; Phase VII, Lexicon
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #elaborative-interrogation, #levels-of-processing, #reading

> [!flashcard] Seed 14 — Connection
> **Q:** How does Ann Brown's production deficiency concept ground the distinction between metacognitive knowledge and metacognitive regulation?
> **A:** Production deficiency refers to the failure to spontaneously deploy strategies that the learner possesses and can use when instructed. It demonstrates that knowing a strategy (metacognitive knowledge) is insufficient for using it without explicit instruction (metacognitive regulation). Children displaying production deficiency have the strategic knowledge but lack the regulatory initiation — the self-monitoring and self-cueing — required for autonomous deployment. This is the empirical foundation for treating regulation as distinct from knowledge.
> **Source:** Phase III
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #ann-brown, #metacognitive-knowledge, #metacognitive-regulation, #self-regulated-learning
> *— [[metacognitive-regulation-foundational-report-2026-03-31_report]]*

> [!flashcard] Seed 14 — Connection
> **Q:** How does the SRL feedback loop function as a metacognitive calibration training system over time?
> **A:** Each cycle: Forethought specifies monitoring targets (process goals), performance monitoring generates data, self-reflection evaluates monitoring signals against actual performance outcomes (the calibration check). When monitoring signals mismatch performance outcomes (felt understanding ≠ generative test performance), that discrepancy is calibration training. Over many cycles, monitoring signals become more accurate predictors of actual performance — meta-level model quality improves.
> **Source:** Phase IV-V
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #metacognition, #calibration, #srl, #self-regulated-learning
> *— [[srl-forethought-selfreflection-pkb-report-2026-03-24_report]]*
