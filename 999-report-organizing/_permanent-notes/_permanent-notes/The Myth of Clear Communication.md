## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Myth of "Clear" Communication
> A culturally pervasive but empirically problematic assumption holds that communication failures are primarily caused by lack of clarity — that if one simply "said what one meant" more directly, problems would resolve. While clarity matters enormously (see Section 2), most significant communication failures occur not at the level of verbal clarity but at the level of relational framing, schema mismatch, emotional activation, or cognitive bias. Improving one's vocabulary and precision without attending to these deeper dynamics produces marginally better outcomes at best.
> *— [[how-to-communicate-effectively-foundational-report-2026-04-03_report]]*
