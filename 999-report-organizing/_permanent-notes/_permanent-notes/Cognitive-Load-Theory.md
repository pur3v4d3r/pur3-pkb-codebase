# Cognitive-Load-Theory

> [!definition] Cognitive-Load-Theory
> An instructional design theory proposing that learning is optimized when instructional procedures align with the limitations of human cognitive architecture — specifically the narrow processing capacity of working memory and the role of schema acquisition in long-term memory.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Cognitive-Load-Theory
> An instructional design theory proposing that learning is optimized when instructional procedures align with the limitations of human cognitive architecture — specifically the narrow processing capacity of working memory and the role of schema acquisition in long-term memory.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-deep-dive-2026-04-12_report]]*

> [!evidence] Cognitive Load Theory
> [[cognitive-load-theory|Cognitive load theory]] is a theory of instructional design grounded in the architecture of human cognition — specifically, the limited capacity of [[working-memory|working memory]] and the effectively unlimited capacity of [[long-term-memory|long-term memory]]. The theory distinguishes three types of cognitive load imposed by any learning task: [[intrinsic-cognitive-load|intrinsic load]] (arising from the inherent complexity of the material and the learner's [[prior-knowledge|prior knowledge]]), [[extraneous-cognitive-load|extraneous load]] (arising from poor instructional or system design that does not contribute to learning), and [[germane-cognitive-load|germane load]] (arising from cognitive processes directly contributing to [[schema-construction|schema construction]] and [[schema-automation|automation]]). Effective design minimizes extraneous load and redirects freed capacity toward germane processing.
> *— [[pkb-pkm-cognitive-strategies-for-learning-and-pkb-architecture-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*
