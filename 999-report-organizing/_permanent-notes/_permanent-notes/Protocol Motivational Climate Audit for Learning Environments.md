> [!protocol] Protocol: Motivational Climate Audit for Learning Environments
> **Context:** Use when evaluating or designing any learning environment (course, programme, workshop, training) for its goal-activating structure.
>
> **Steps:**
> 1. **Evaluate the evaluation structure.** Is grading criterion-referenced (absolute standard) or norm-referenced (relative standing)? Norm-referenced structures activate PAP and PAV goal orientations.
> 2. **Assess comparison salience.** Are student performances made public or easily visible to peers? Public ranking and leaderboard-style feedback elevate normative comparison salience, activating performance goal orientations.
> 3. **Examine failure consequences.** Are mistakes and errors treated as learning information (low stakes, informative feedback) or performance deficits (high stakes, evaluative feedback)? High-stakes error consequences activate avoidance regulation.
> 4. **Check for mastery cues.** Does the environment explicitly communicate that improvement over time is valued as much or more than absolute performance level? Are progress narratives (you have improved in X) part of the feedback vocabulary?
> 5. **Assess help-seeking signals.** Is asking for help, admitting confusion, or revealing knowledge gaps culturally safe in this environment? Environments where such behaviors are stigmatized will generate PAV-like processing even without explicit competitive evaluation.
>
> **Success Criteria:** A mastery-structured climate scores: criterion-referenced evaluation, low comparison salience, low-stakes error treatment, explicit mastery cues, and psychologically safe help-seeking.
>
> **Common Pitfalls:** Well-intentioned "effort praise" without structural changes often fails because the evaluative structure (norm-referenced grading, public ranking) continues to activate performance goal orientations regardless of the interpersonal feedback style.
