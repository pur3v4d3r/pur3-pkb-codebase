## Methodology & Sources

> [!methodology-and-sources] Methodology Note: How This Report Was Constructed
> **Evidence integration:** This report synthesizes empirical research, theoretical frameworks, and cross-domain applications. Where empirical evidence is cited, I have aimed to identify the original sources and note the population/context of the research. Where cross-domain applications are made (e.g., applying Hattie-Timperley feedback taxonomy to interpersonal communication), I have noted these as interpretive extensions rather than empirically established facts.
>
> **Source limitations:** The claims in this report draw primarily from English-language, peer-reviewed psychology and communication research — predominantly from WEIRD contexts. Claims about human communication universality should be read within this context. Cross-cultural variation in communication norms and practices is both real and important, and a more thorough treatment (beyond this report's scope) would require engagement with cross-cultural communication research.
>
> **Theoretical tensions:** Several active theoretical debates in the relevant literature (basic vs. constructionist emotion theory; deliberate practice applicability to open vs. closed skills; growth mindset intervention effects) were noted in the body text with honest assessment of where the evidence stands, rather than artificially resolving them in either direction.
>
> **Annotation Methodology:**
> This report employs three annotation types: (1) inline claim annotations (`[!annotation]`) appearing immediately after significant claims, providing source basis, confidence level, and alternatives considered; (2) section-level epistemic status markers (`[!epistemic-status]`) providing overall evidence assessment for the section; (3) extended reasoning traces (`[!reasoning-trace]`) for complex multi-step analytical moves.
>
> **Confidence Scale:**
> - **5/5:** Established consensus with strong empirical support
> - **4/5:** Well-supported with minor caveats or boundary conditions
> - **3/5:** Supported but with meaningful counter-evidence or methodological concerns
> - **2/5:** Plausible interpretation with limited or conflicting evidence
> - **1/5:** Speculative — original to this report or weakly supported
>
> **Limitations of the annotation approach:**
> - Confidence ratings are subjective assessments, not quantitative measurements
> - The annotation author (Claude) and the claim author are the same entity, limiting independence of the epistemic assessment
> - The practice of annotation may introduce epistemic conservatism (systematically lower ratings to appear appropriately humble) or over-qualification
> - Confidence ratings are context-dependent: the same claim may warrant higher confidence cited for one purpose and lower for another
