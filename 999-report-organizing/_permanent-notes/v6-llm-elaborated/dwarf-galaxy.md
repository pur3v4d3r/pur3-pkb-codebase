# Dwarf Galaxy

> [!definition] **Dwarf Galaxy**
> A Dwarf Galaxy is a low-mass galaxy characterized by stellar masses typically below ~10⁹ M☉ and includes various morphological types such as dwarf elliptical, dwarf spheroidal, dwarf irregular, and ultra-faint dwarfs. It falls under the broader concept of Galaxy Classification but excludes larger galaxies like spiral or elliptical ones.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> The concept of Dwarf Galaxies excludes larger galaxies such as spiral or elliptical galaxies. It should not be confused with other galaxy classifications based on morphology alone without considering mass.

## Core Explanation

Dwarf Galaxies are small celestial systems that play a crucial role in understanding galaxy formation and evolution within the framework of cosmological models, particularly ΛCDM (Lambda Cold Dark Matter). These galaxies often serve as satellites to larger galaxies like the Milky Way, with our own galaxy hosting approximately 60 known dwarf satellite galaxies. Their low mass-to-light ratios make them highly dark-matter dominated, a characteristic that sets them apart from other types of galaxies and makes them invaluable for studying dark matter.

The prevalence of Dwarf Galaxies around larger galaxies suggests they are remnants of the early universe's hierarchical structure formation process, where smaller structures merge to form larger ones. This hierarchical assembly is a cornerstone of ΛCDM theory, which predicts that many more dwarf galaxies should exist than we observe, leading to the missing satellite problem.

Observations and theoretical models have shown that Dwarf Galaxies are not only dark matter-dominated but also exhibit significant variations in their morphologies and star formation histories. These differences provide insights into how environmental factors such as tidal interactions with larger host galaxies influence galaxy evolution.

## Mechanism

The dark matter content of Dwarf Galaxies is inferred through stellar velocity dispersion measurements, which assume virial equilibrium and isotropy in the distribution of stars within these systems. However, this assumption can be problematic for dwarf galaxies that are undergoing tidal disruption due to gravitational interactions with larger host galaxies. Such disruptions can lead to overestimates of dark matter mass if not properly accounted for.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Testing Cosmological Models**
> Dwarf Galaxies serve as critical test beds for cosmological models like ΛCDM. The missing satellite problem, where the number of observed dwarf galaxies is significantly lower than predicted by theory, challenges our understanding of dark matter and galaxy formation processes. Addressing this issue could refine or necessitate modifications to current cosmological frameworks.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Understanding Dark Matter**
> The high mass-to-light ratios in Dwarf Galaxies highlight their importance for studying dark matter properties. By analyzing the dynamics of stars within these galaxies, researchers can infer the distribution and nature of dark matter, contributing to our broader understanding of this elusive component of the universe.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Dwarf Elliptical vs Dwarf Irregular**
> Dwarf ellipticals are compact systems with smooth, nearly featureless light distributions and little ongoing star formation. In contrast, dwarf irregulars exhibit chaotic structures and significant star-forming activity. These differences reflect distinct evolutionary paths influenced by their internal dynamics and external interactions.

## Key Figures

- **Sandra Moore Faber** — Her work on the Faber-Jackson relation, which links galaxy luminosity to stellar velocity dispersion, has been instrumental in understanding the properties of Dwarf Galaxies and their dark matter content.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How do tidal disruptions affect inferred dark matter content?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Detailed simulations and observational studies that account for tidal interactions could provide insights into how these processes influence the dynamics of stars in Dwarf Galaxies, thereby refining estimates of their dark matter content.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What are the implications if the missing satellite problem persists?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Further observations and theoretical advancements that either confirm or refute the predictions of ΛCDM regarding dwarf galaxy populations would help resolve this issue, potentially leading to new paradigms in cosmology.

## Synthesis

Understanding Dwarf Galaxies is crucial for advancing our knowledge in astrophysics and cosmology. Their dark matter dominance and prevalence as satellite galaxies make them ideal laboratories for testing fundamental theories about galaxy formation and the nature of dark matter. Resolving questions surrounding their properties and distribution will not only enhance our comprehension of these small-scale structures but also provide critical insights into the larger framework of cosmic evolution.
