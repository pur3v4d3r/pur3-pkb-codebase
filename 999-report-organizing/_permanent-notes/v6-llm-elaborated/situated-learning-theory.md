# Situated Learning Theory

> [!definition] **Situated Learning Theory**
> Situated Learning Theory, developed by Jean Lave and Etienne Wenger, posits that learning is fundamentally a process of becoming a participant in a community of practice — acquiring identity, tools, and skill through legitimate peripheral participation in authentic activity — rather than the abstract acquisition of decontextualised knowledge. It falls under [[learning-theories]], emphasizing the social and practical aspects of learning within communities.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This theory excludes the notion of isolated or decontextualized learning, focusing instead on the social and practical aspects of learning within communities.

## Core Explanation

Situated Learning Theory suggests that learning is not a solitary or isolated process but one deeply embedded in the context of community and practice. Through legitimate peripheral participation, learners gradually integrate into a community of practice by observing and eventually engaging with its members in meaningful activities. This integration allows learners to acquire knowledge and skills that are relevant and transferable within the specific context of the community.

In practice, this means that learning occurs not just through direct instruction but also through observation, imitation, and gradual participation in the tasks and routines of a community. For instance, in a medical training program, new doctors learn by shadowing experienced practitioners, observing their procedures, and eventually performing simpler tasks under supervision before taking on more complex responsibilities.

The theory draws its roots from sociocultural theories that emphasize the importance of social interaction and shared practices in learning. It challenges traditional views of learning as an individual cognitive process, instead highlighting how knowledge is constructed through participation in a community's activities and interactions. This perspective underscores the role of context and practice in shaping understanding and skill acquisition.

Empirical evidence supports this theory, particularly in fields like education and training where learners often struggle to apply classroom knowledge in real-world settings. Studies have shown that learners who participate in authentic, situated learning experiences are more likely to retain and transfer their knowledge effectively compared to those who learn through decontextualized methods.

## Mechanism

Legitimate peripheral participation is a key mechanism within Situated Learning Theory. It involves learners starting at the periphery of a community's activities, observing and gradually moving towards full participation. This process allows learners to understand the norms, values, and practices of the community before becoming active participants.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, Situated Learning Theory suggests that learning materials should be embedded in authentic contexts. For example, a language course could include real-life scenarios where students practice the target language in meaningful situations rather than through abstract exercises.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Corporate training**
> For corporate training programs, this theory implies that on-the-job training and mentorship are more effective than traditional classroom instruction. Employees can learn by observing experienced colleagues and gradually taking on responsibilities within their roles.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Higher education**
> In higher education, Situated Learning Theory supports the integration of experiential learning opportunities such as internships, fieldwork, and collaborative projects. These activities allow students to apply theoretical knowledge in practical settings, enhancing their understanding and retention.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> While Situated Learning Theory focuses on the intrinsic load of learning within a community's context, other theories like cognitive load theory emphasize extraneous load or unnecessary complexity in instructional design. Understanding these differences helps educators balance between authentic, situated experiences and structured, explicit instruction.

## Key Figures

- **Jean Lave** — Co-originator of Situated Learning Theory, Jean Lave conducted seminal research on how people learn in everyday contexts, emphasizing the importance of social interaction and practice.
- **Etienne Wenger** — Co-originator of Situated Learning Theory, Etienne Wenger developed the concept of communities of practice, highlighting how learning occurs through participation and shared activities within these groups.

## Open Questions

> How can formal education better integrate the principles of Situated Learning Theory?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Further research on hybrid models that combine situated practice with explicit instruction could provide insights into effective integration strategies.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What are the long-term effects of learning in a community of practice?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Longitudinal studies tracking learners' development over extended periods would help understand the lasting impacts of situated learning experiences.

## Synthesis

Situated Learning Theory matters because it challenges traditional views of learning as an isolated, cognitive process and instead emphasizes the social and practical aspects of knowledge acquisition. By integrating this theory into educational practices, we can create more effective and meaningful learning environments that prepare learners for real-world challenges.

This concept also intersects with other theories like Communities of Practice and Distributed Cognition, enriching our understanding of how learning occurs in different contexts. Understanding these connections helps educators design more holistic and contextually rich learning experiences.
