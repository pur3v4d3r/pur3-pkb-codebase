# Near Transfer

> [!definition] **Near Transfer**
> Near Transfer is the application of learned knowledge or skill to a new task that shares substantial surface and structural features with the original training context, as opposed to far transfer which occurs between dissimilar contexts; it falls under [[Cognitive Architecture]].

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It excludes tasks that are completely different from the original learning context (far transfer) and focuses on tasks that share similar surface-level characteristics but may differ in underlying principles.

## Core Explanation

Near Transfer plays a crucial role in practical skill acquisition by allowing learners to apply previously learned skills to new but similar tasks. This phenomenon is robust and ubiquitous in skill learning, making it an essential component of effective educational practices.

The foundational mechanism behind Near Transfer involves the automation of procedural knowledge through extensive practice, enabling learners to perform tasks with minimal cognitive effort. This automation allows for efficient application of learned skills to new contexts that share similar surface features, such as solving a problem in one domain and applying it to another with slight modifications.

Theoretical roots of Near Transfer can be traced back to the work of John Sweller, who highlighted its importance in his research on cognitive load theory. Sweller's findings underscore the role of automation in facilitating Near Transfer, emphasizing that highly automated skills are more likely to transfer effectively to new but similar tasks.

Empirical evidence supports the reliability and ubiquity of Near Transfer. Studies have consistently shown that learners who practice a task extensively tend to perform better on related tasks, even if those tasks differ slightly from their original training context.

## Mechanism

Near Transfer operates through the automation of procedural knowledge, where learners develop a deep understanding and fluency in performing specific tasks. This process involves breaking down complex skills into smaller, more manageable components that can be practiced until they become automatic. As these skills are automated, they can be readily applied to new but similar tasks without requiring significant cognitive effort.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> Understanding Near Transfer is crucial for instructional designers who aim to create curricula that facilitate skill transfer. By focusing on the automation of procedural knowledge, educators can design lessons that encourage learners to practice tasks until they become automatic, thereby enhancing their ability to apply these skills in new but similar contexts.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Curriculum development**
> In curriculum development, Near Transfer informs the creation of a coherent and cumulative learning path. By structuring courses to build on previously learned skills, educators can ensure that students are well-prepared to tackle increasingly complex tasks that share surface features with their initial training.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Skill acquisition**
> For learners aiming to acquire new skills, Near Transfer provides a practical framework for understanding how practice and repetition lead to the automation of procedural knowledge. By recognizing this process, learners can focus on deliberate practice that enhances their ability to transfer skills across similar tasks.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> Near Transfer is often associated with intrinsic load, which refers to the inherent difficulty of a task. In contrast, extraneous load involves unnecessary cognitive demands that do not contribute to learning. Understanding this distinction helps educators design tasks that minimize extraneous load and maximize the automation of procedural knowledge for effective Near Transfer.

## Key Figures

- **John Sweller** — Sweller is recognized as an originator of cognitive load theory, which has significantly contributed to our understanding of Near Transfer. His research highlighted the importance of automation in procedural knowledge for facilitating effective skill transfer.

## Synthesis

Understanding Near Transfer is crucial for effective learning and teaching strategies because it provides a clear framework for how previously learned skills can be applied to new but similar tasks. By focusing on the automation of procedural knowledge, educators can design curricula that facilitate skill transfer across various domains. This concept also intersects with other related concepts like Far Transfer, Worked Examples, and Transfer-Appropriate Processing, highlighting its importance in cognitive architecture.

The robust and ubiquitous nature of Near Transfer underscores its practical value in educational settings. By leveraging this phenomenon, educators can create more effective learning experiences that prepare students to apply their skills across a range of contexts.
