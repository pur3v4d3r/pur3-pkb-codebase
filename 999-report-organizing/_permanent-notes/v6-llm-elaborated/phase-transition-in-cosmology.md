# Phase Transition In Cosmology

> [!definition] **Phase Transition In Cosmology**
> A Phase Transition In Cosmology signifies a shift in the ground state of quantum fields within the early universe as it cooled through critical temperatures, often marked by spontaneous symmetry breaking. This concept is confined to cosmological contexts and does not encompass phase transitions observed in everyday materials. It falls under Early-Universe Phenomena.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes phase transitions that occur outside cosmological contexts and focuses on those relevant to early-universe phenomena. It should not be confused with thermodynamic phase transitions observed in everyday materials.

## Core Explanation

Cosmological phase transitions are pivotal events that shaped the early universe's evolution. These transitions occur when quantum fields, which permeate all of space, shift from one ground state to another as the temperature drops below a critical point. This process is often accompanied by spontaneous symmetry breaking, where the symmetrical properties of the initial state are lost in the new phase. For instance, the QCD chiral / confinement transition at approximately 150 MeV and the electroweak transition around 160 GeV represent significant milestones in this evolutionary timeline.

The theoretical underpinnings of these transitions draw from quantum field theory and particle physics, providing a framework to understand how symmetries are broken and restored as the universe cools. The dynamics of such transitions can lead to observable phenomena, including relic gravitational waves that could be detected by future instruments like LISA. These waves serve as echoes of the violent processes that occurred during phase transitions in the early universe.

Empirical evidence for cosmological phase transitions is indirect but compelling. Observations of cosmic microwave background radiation and large-scale structure formation provide clues about the conditions prevailing shortly after the Big Bang, suggesting a series of phase transitions that could have left their mark on the cosmos. Theoretical models predict specific signatures in these observations, making them valuable tools to probe the nature of early-universe physics.

## Mechanism

During a cosmological phase transition, quantum fields undergo spontaneous symmetry breaking as they cool through critical temperatures. This process can be first-order or crossover, depending on the dynamics and energy scales involved. In a first-order transition, there is a discontinuous change in field values, leading to the formation of bubbles where the new phase nucleates. These bubbles expand rapidly, creating shock waves that generate gravitational radiation detectable today as relic gravitational waves.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Gravitational Wave Detection**
> Cosmological phase transitions are a promising source of gravitational waves in the millihertz band. These waves, relics from early-universe events like first-order phase transitions, could be detected by future space-based observatories such as LISA. The detection of these waves would provide direct evidence for cosmological phase transitions and offer insights into the conditions prevailing shortly after the Big Bang.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Electroweak Baryogenesis**
> The electroweak phase transition is a critical period in early-universe history, potentially serving as a site for baryogenesis—the process by which matter came to dominate antimatter. However, recent studies indicate that the Standard Model predicts a smooth crossover rather than a first-order transition, complicating efforts to explain baryon asymmetry within this framework. Beyond-Standard-Model physics is required to recover scenarios where electroweak phase transitions could support baryogenesis.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **First-order vs Crossover Phase Transitions**
> The distinction between first-order and crossover phase transitions in cosmology is crucial for understanding their implications. First-order transitions involve a discontinuous change in field values, leading to the formation of bubbles where new phases nucleate. These transitions can generate relic gravitational waves detectable today. In contrast, crossover transitions are smooth changes without such abrupt shifts, making them less likely to produce observable relics.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What mechanisms underlie the generation of relic gravitational waves from cosmological phase transitions?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Detailed theoretical models and observational data from gravitational wave detectors like LISA could resolve this question by providing evidence for or against specific scenarios.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How can beyond-Standard Model physics address the issue of a smooth crossover in electroweak transitions for baryogenesis?
>
> *What would resolve it:* New theoretical frameworks and experimental results from particle accelerators could shed light on alternative mechanisms that support first-order phase transitions, enabling baryogenesis.

## Synthesis

Understanding cosmological phase transitions is essential for unraveling the mysteries of early-universe physics. These events not only shaped the cosmic landscape but also hold clues to fundamental questions about matter and energy. By studying these transitions, scientists can test theories of particle physics at extreme energies and conditions, potentially revealing new insights into the origins of cosmic structure and the asymmetry between matter and antimatter.
