# Cyclic Universe Model

> [!definition] **Cyclic Universe Model**
> The Cyclic Universe Model posits an endless series of expansion and contraction cycles for the universe, each cycle beginning with a Big Bang-like event followed by a Big Bounce or brane collision that avoids singularities. This model contrasts with non-cyclical models like the steady-state theory by addressing cosmological questions through repeated cycles rather than proposing a static or one-time creation scenario. It falls under [[Cosmological Models]].

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This model excludes singularities at the end of each cycle and contrasts with non-cyclical models like the steady-state theory. It should not be confused with oscillating models that do not address the 'before' question through cycles.

## Core Explanation

The Cyclic Universe Model offers an intriguing alternative to the Big Bang theory, suggesting that our universe has experienced countless cycles of expansion and contraction without ever reaching a singular state at any point in time. Each cycle begins with a rapid expansion phase akin to the Big Bang but concludes not in a catastrophic collapse into a singularity, as some models predict, but rather in a 'Big Bounce' or brane collision that initiates another round of cosmic expansion.

The theoretical attractiveness of this model lies in its ability to address fundamental cosmological questions such as the horizon and flatness problems without invoking inflation. Instead, it proposes a mechanism where slow contraction phases naturally lead to conditions conducive for solving these issues, thereby providing an alternative explanation for the uniformity and geometry observed in our universe.

The concept of cyclic universes has roots in ancient philosophical speculations about eternal recurrence but gained modern scientific traction with the development of string theory and brane cosmology. These frameworks allow for the possibility of multiple dimensions and interactions between them, which can explain how a Big Bounce or brane collision might occur without violating known physical laws.

While the Cyclic Universe Model is theoretically compelling, its empirical grounding remains an open question. Observational evidence that could distinguish it from other models, such as predictions about primordial gravitational waves, has yet to be conclusively tested.

The Cyclic Universe Model's theoretical framework is deeply intertwined with string theory and brane cosmology, which propose that our universe exists as a three-dimensional 'brane' floating in higher-dimensional space. This multidimensional perspective allows for the possibility of collisions between different branes, each collision potentially initiating a new cycle of cosmic expansion. Such an event would not only reset the universe but also erase any evidence of previous cycles, making it challenging to detect past events directly through observational astronomy.

## Mechanism

In each cycle of the Cyclic Universe Model, the universe expands rapidly after a Big Bang-like event until it reaches a point where contraction begins. This phase is characterized by slow contraction that avoids singularities and instead leads to conditions ripe for another expansion phase. The role of primordial gravitational waves becomes crucial here as they are predicted to be essentially absent in most cyclic models due to the nature of these cycles, distinguishing them from inflationary predictions.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Cosmological Observations**
> The Cyclic Universe Model has significant implications for cosmological observations, particularly regarding gravitational waves. Unlike inflationary models that predict a specific pattern of these waves, cyclic models often predict their absence or very low levels during the slow contraction phase. This distinction could be crucial in distinguishing between different cosmological scenarios through future observational data.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Cyclic vs Non-cyclical Models**
> The Cyclic Universe Model fundamentally differs from non-cyclical models like the steady-state theory by proposing an endless series of cosmic cycles rather than a static or one-time creation event. This distinction is crucial as it addresses questions about the universe's history and future in a cyclical framework, offering alternative explanations for observed phenomena.

> [!key-distinction] **Big Bang Theory vs Cyclic Universe**
> While the Big Bang theory posits a singular origin of our universe followed by continuous expansion, the Cyclic Universe Model suggests an eternal series of cosmic cycles. This difference is significant as it alters how we interpret cosmological observations and predict future states of the universe.

> [!key-distinction] **Top-Down vs Bottom-Up Processing in Cyclic Universe Models**
> In cosmology, top-down processing involves starting with a broad theoretical framework and deriving specific predictions from it. The Cyclic Universe Model exemplifies this approach by proposing brane collisions as the mechanism for cosmic cycles. In contrast, bottom-up approaches start with observational data to build theories. This distinction is crucial because top-down models like cyclic cosmology can make unique predictions that are testable through future observations.

## Common Misconceptions

<!-- enhancement-pass:1 (2026-05-14) -->

> [!warning] **Misconception** — People think the Cyclic Universe Model predicts an infinite series of Big Bangs and Big Crunches.
>
> This misconception arises from a misunderstanding of how cycles in cyclic cosmology are initiated. Unlike traditional oscillating models, each cycle begins with a 'Big Bounce' or brane collision that avoids singularities. This mechanism ensures that the universe does not collapse into a singularity but instead transitions smoothly to another expansion phase.

## Key Figures
