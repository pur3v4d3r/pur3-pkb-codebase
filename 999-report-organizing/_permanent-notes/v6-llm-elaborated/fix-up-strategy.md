# Fix-Up Strategy

> [!definition] **Fix-Up Strategy**
> A fix-up strategy is a specific type of metacognitive action employed by readers to address comprehension issues, such as rereading or looking up information. It falls under [[Cognitive Architecture]], focusing on the reactive measures taken during reading to restore understanding.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes anticipatory strategies and planning techniques used before reading begins. It also does not encompass the broader cognitive architecture but focuses on the reactive measures taken during reading.

## Core Explanation

Fix-up strategies are essential for maintaining and restoring comprehension while reading. These strategies include actions like rereading a passage, returning to earlier text (look-back), continuing to read ahead (look-ahead), consulting external sources (looking up), self-questioning, summarizing, and meta-pausing. Each of these actions is designed to address specific breakdowns in understanding by leveraging the reader's working memory to process and integrate new information.

The effectiveness of fix-up strategies depends on both the repertoire available to the reader and their conditional knowledge about when each strategy fits best. For instance, rereading might be used when a sentence or paragraph is unclear, while looking up definitions can help resolve vocabulary issues. These strategies are reactive in nature, meaning they are deployed in response to detected comprehension breakdowns, as opposed to anticipatory or planning strategies that occur before reading begins.

The theoretical roots of fix-up strategies lie in cognitive science and metacognition. They are part of the broader set of reciprocal teaching techniques, which aim to enhance reading comprehension through active engagement with text. The concept is grounded in empirical research, particularly studies on working memory and how it influences our ability to process new information while maintaining existing knowledge.

Historically, fix-up strategies have been studied since the 1980s, with John Sweller's work being a foundational contribution. His research highlighted the importance of these strategies in addressing comprehension issues, emphasizing that effective deployment requires both possession of the repertoire and conditional knowledge about when each strategy is most appropriate.

## Mechanism

Fix-up strategies operate by leveraging working memory to process and integrate new information. When a reader encounters an unclear passage, they might reread it or look back at earlier text for context. This process involves temporarily storing the new information in working memory while integrating it with existing knowledge. External sources can also be consulted through looking up, which helps resolve specific vocabulary or concept issues.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, incorporating fix-up strategies into reading materials and activities can significantly enhance comprehension. For example, providing opportunities for students to reread challenging passages or look up unfamiliar terms can help them better understand the material. This approach ensures that learners have the tools they need to address their own comprehension breakdowns, leading to more effective learning outcomes.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Reading interventions**
> In reading interventions for struggling readers, fix-up strategies can be explicitly taught and practiced. By guiding students through these strategies, educators can help them develop the skills needed to independently address their own comprehension issues. This not only improves immediate understanding but also builds long-term reading proficiency.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Technology integration**
> The use of technology in education can both enhance and impede fix-up strategies. Digital tools like interactive glossaries or embedded summaries can facilitate look-up and summarization, making it easier for students to address comprehension issues. However, over-reliance on technology might reduce the need for these skills, potentially weakening their ability to engage with text independently.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> Fix-up strategies are reactive and address intrinsic load by directly addressing comprehension breakdowns. In contrast, extraneous load is managed through planning and anticipatory strategies that occur before reading begins. For example, while rereading (a fix-up strategy) addresses current confusion, pre-reading activities like setting goals or predicting content manage potential future difficulties.

## Key Figures

- **John Sweller** — John Sweller is recognized as the originator of the concept in his 1988 work. His research highlighted the importance of fix-up strategies in addressing comprehension issues, emphasizing their role in leveraging working memory to process and integrate new information.

## Synthesis

Fix-up strategies are crucial for maintaining and restoring comprehension during reading. By addressing specific breakdowns in understanding, these strategies enhance overall reading proficiency and contribute to the broader field of cognitive architecture. Their practical applications in instructional design and reading interventions underscore their importance in education. Moreover, ongoing research into how technology can both support and challenge fix-up strategies highlights the dynamic nature of this concept within the evolving landscape of digital learning.
