# Conditional Regard

> [!definition] **Conditional Regard**
> Conditional Regard refers to a socialization technique where an individual's regard or acceptance by others depends on their behavior, often leading to introjected regulation and resistance to internalization. It falls under Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that individuals have three basic psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes other forms of socialization that do not involve conditional regard, such as unconditional positive regard. It should not be confused with intrinsic motivation or autonomous regulation.

## Core Explanation

Conditional Regard is a controlling socialization technique where the regard of others is contingent on an individual's behavior. This mechanism can be observed in various settings such as parenting, teaching, or romantic relationships. For instance, a parent might praise their child only when they achieve good grades, thereby making the child's self-worth dependent on external validation.

The core meaning of Conditional Regard lies in its ability to shape internalization processes within SDT. When individuals experience conditional regard, they may develop introjected regulation, where their motivation is driven by fear of negative consequences or desire for positive reinforcement rather than intrinsic interest or personal values. This can lead to resistance when the external conditions change.

Theoretical roots of Conditional Regard are deeply embedded in SDT, which emphasizes the importance of autonomy, competence, and relatedness. When these needs are not met through conditional regard, individuals may experience frustration and a sense of powerlessness, leading to maladaptive behaviors or resistance to further internalization.

Empirical evidence supports the role of Conditional Regard in producing controlled internalization. For example, studies have shown that children who experience conditional regard from their parents tend to develop introjected regulation, which can hinder their autonomous motivation and well-being.

## Mechanism

The process by which Conditional Regard influences behavior involves several stages. Initially, individuals learn that their self-worth is tied to specific behaviors. Over time, this leads to introjected regulation, where the individual's internalized standards are driven by fear of negative consequences or desire for positive reinforcement rather than personal values.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In digital and AI-mediated learning environments, understanding Conditional Regard can inform instructional strategies that promote autonomous motivation. For example, designing adaptive systems that provide feedback based on intrinsic interest rather than external rewards can help students develop a genuine passion for learning.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Controlling teaching styles**
> Teachers who use controlling teaching styles often rely on Conditional Regard to manage classroom behavior. However, this approach may backfire by fostering introjected regulation and resistance among students. By shifting towards more autonomy-supportive practices, teachers can create a supportive environment that encourages intrinsic motivation.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Conditional Regard vs Autonomous Regulation**
> Conditional Regard is distinct from autonomous regulation in that the former relies on external contingencies for behavior control, while the latter involves internalized standards driven by personal values and interests. Understanding this distinction helps educators design interventions that promote intrinsic motivation rather than controlled internalization.

## Synthesis

Conditional Regard is a critical concept in educational psychology, as it highlights the potential pitfalls of external control in shaping internalization processes. By understanding how Conditional Regard operates, educators can design interventions that promote autonomous motivation and support the development of intrinsic values and interests.

The broader implications of Conditional Regard extend beyond education into various domains such as parenting, workplace management, and therapeutic practices. Recognizing its role in fostering or hindering autonomous regulation is essential for creating supportive environments that nurture personal growth and well-being.
