# Supervenience

> [!definition] **Supervenience**
> Supervenience is an asymmetric dependence relation where higher-level properties are fixed by lower-level base properties such that no difference in the higher level can occur without a corresponding difference in the lower level. It falls under [[Physicalism]], serving as a tool for stating non-reductive forms of physicalism, but it does not imply reduction or explanation; it only describes covariation between levels of properties.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> The supervenience relation does not imply reduction, explanation, or causation; it only describes covariation between levels of properties.

## Core Explanation

Supervenience is a philosophical concept that captures the idea that higher-level properties are dependent on lower-level base properties. For instance, mental states like pain supervene on physical brain states because any change in one's experience of pain must be accompanied by a corresponding change in their brain activity. This relationship does not imply that mental states can be reduced to or explained by physical states; rather, it asserts only that the higher-level properties are determined by lower-level ones.

The concept of supervenience operates in practice through various scenarios where higher-level properties are seen as emergent from lower-level base properties without necessitating a reductionist stance. For example, in the context of moral philosophy, ethical principles might supervene on social and biological facts, meaning that any change in ethics must be accompanied by changes at the level of social or biological conditions.

Theoretical roots of supervenience can be traced back to discussions about physicalism, where it is used to articulate a non-reductive form of this philosophical position. Physicalism holds that all phenomena are ultimately reducible to physical facts, but supervenience allows for higher-level properties like consciousness or morality without committing to such reduction.

Empirically, the concept has been applied in various fields, including cognitive science and ethics. In cognitive science, it helps explain how mental states can arise from brain activity without requiring a detailed reductionist account of each mental state.

## Mechanism

Supervenience operates through a covariational mechanism where higher-level properties are determined by lower-level base properties. This means that any change in the higher level must be accompanied by a corresponding change in the lower level, but it does not imply causation or explanation of why these changes occur.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, understanding supervenience can help educators ensure that learning outcomes (higher-level properties) are aligned with the underlying cognitive processes and environmental factors (lower-level base properties). This ensures that changes in teaching methods or materials will lead to observable differences in student performance.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Moral philosophy**
> In moral philosophy, supervenience helps articulate how ethical principles can emerge from social and biological facts without reducing morality to these lower-level bases. This allows for a nuanced discussion of moral responsibility and the impact of societal structures on individual behavior.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Supervenience vs Reduction**
> While reductionism posits that higher-level properties can be fully explained by lower-level base properties, supervenience does not imply this. Instead, it only asserts a covariational relationship where changes in the higher level are accompanied by corresponding changes in the lower level.

> [!key-distinction] **Supervenience vs Explanation**
> Supervenience is often mistakenly equated with explanation or reduction, but it does not provide any causal mechanism. It merely states that higher-level properties covary with lower-level base properties without explaining why this relationship exists.

## Key Figures

- **Jaegwon Kim** — Kim is a key figure in the development of supervenience theory, particularly through his exclusion-argument literature which highlights that supervenience alone does not secure mental causation against physical pre-emption.

## Synthesis

Supervenience matters because it provides a nuanced framework for understanding the relationship between higher-level and lower-level properties without committing to reductionism. It is particularly valuable in fields like physicalism, where it allows for non-reductive forms of explanation. By highlighting covariation rather than causation or explanation, supervenience opens up new avenues for philosophical inquiry into issues such as mental causation and the mind-body problem.

The concept also has practical implications across various disciplines, from cognitive science to ethics, by helping researchers and practitioners align higher-level phenomena with their underlying bases. This alignment is crucial for developing effective theories and interventions in these fields.
