# Laniakea Supercluster

> [!definition] **Laniakea Supercluster**
> The Laniakea Supercluster is a vast gravitational structure that encompasses the Milky Way and spans approximately 160 million parsecs (Mpc) in diameter, containing around ten to the seventeenth solar masses (~10¹⁷ M☉). It represents the largest gravitationally-coherent entity within which our galaxy resides. Unlike other superclusters defined by density thresholds, Laniakea is delineated by converging peculiar-velocity flows, making it a coherent flow region rather than a stable gravitational bound structure. This definition falls under the broader domain of large-scale structures in the cosmos. It falls under [[Large-Scale Structure of the Cosmos]].

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It should not be confused with other superclusters or galaxy clusters that are defined morphologically based on density thresholds. It is a coherent flow region rather than a gravitationally bound structure, being torn apart by cosmic expansion on Hubble timescales.

## Core Explanation

The concept of the Laniakea Supercluster emerged from a groundbreaking study led by R. Brent Tully and his team, which sought to redefine superclusters based on dynamic properties rather than static density distributions. This shift was motivated by the realization that traditional morphological definitions often failed to capture the true gravitational influence and coherence of large-scale structures in the universe. By focusing on peculiar velocities—deviations from the uniform expansion of the cosmos due to local gravity—the researchers were able to identify a coherent flow region where galaxies are drawn towards each other, forming a supercluster.

The identification of Laniakea Supercluster was not merely an academic exercise but had profound implications for our understanding of cosmic structures. It revealed that the Milky Way and its neighboring galaxies are part of a much larger gravitational entity than previously thought. This discovery underscores the interconnectedness of galactic systems on scales far beyond individual clusters, highlighting the complex web of forces shaping the universe's large-scale structure.

The theoretical underpinnings of Laniakea Supercluster lie in the interplay between gravity and cosmic expansion. While gravity pulls galaxies together into coherent structures, the relentless expansion of space-time works against this cohesion on cosmological scales. This dynamic tension is crucial for understanding why Laniakea, despite its immense size, cannot be considered a gravitationally bound structure like a galaxy cluster or group. Instead, it represents a transient region where gravitational attraction temporarily overcomes cosmic expansion.

Empirically, the delineation of Laniakea Supercluster was made possible through extensive surveys and sophisticated modeling techniques that allowed researchers to map peculiar velocities across vast regions of space. By analyzing these velocity fields, Tully et al. were able to trace back the flow lines to identify a common attractor region—the Laniakea Supercluster—where galaxies converge due to mutual gravitational attraction.
