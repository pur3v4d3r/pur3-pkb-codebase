# Parker Solar Probe

> [!definition] **Parker Solar Probe**
> The Parker Solar Probe is a NASA mission launched in August 2018 to study the Sun's corona and origins of the solar wind by making progressively closer approaches to the Sun, becoming the closest human-made object to the Sun. It does not cover general information about other space missions or broader concepts in solar physics unless directly related to its findings. This mission falls under Solar In-Situ Exploration Missions.

## Core Explanation

The Parker Solar Probe's primary objective is to study the Sun's corona and the origins of the solar wind, phenomena that have been challenging to understand due to their complexity and the difficulty in obtaining direct measurements. By approaching within a distance of about 9.86 solar radii from the Sun’s surface, the probe has provided unprecedented insights into these processes.

One of the key findings from the Parker Solar Probe is the discovery of 'switchbacks'—sudden reversals of the solar magnetic field—which are now recognized as ubiquitous features in young solar wind. These switchbacks provide critical constraints on coronal heating and wind-acceleration mechanisms, which were previously inaccessible to remote observation.

The probe's data has also revealed that these switchbacks are not entirely new phenomena but rather re-discovered features similar to those observed by the Helios missions in the 1970s. This highlights the importance of careful interpretation of in-situ measurements and underscores the need for a nuanced understanding of solar wind dynamics.

The mission's success is underpinned by its ability to navigate extreme conditions near the Sun, collecting data on solar wind and magnetic field reversals that are crucial for advancing our knowledge of space weather and protecting Earth from solar events.

## Mechanism

To collect data on solar wind and magnetic field reversals, the Parker Solar Probe employs a suite of instruments designed to withstand extreme temperatures and radiation. These include sensors capable of measuring plasma properties, magnetic fields, and energetic particles. The probe's trajectory is carefully planned to pass through regions where these phenomena are most pronounced, allowing for detailed in-situ measurements.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Space Weather Prediction**
> Understanding the mechanisms behind switchbacks and coronal heating can significantly improve space weather predictions. By incorporating insights from the Parker Solar Probe into predictive models, scientists can better forecast solar events that could affect satellite operations, communications systems, and power grids on Earth.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Solar Storm Mitigation**
> The data collected by the Parker Solar Probe helps in developing strategies to mitigate the effects of solar storms. By identifying patterns and triggers for these storms, space agencies can issue more accurate warnings and take preventive measures to protect critical infrastructure from potential damage.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Switchbacks as a Feature of Young Solar Wind vs. Older Observations**
> The Parker Solar Probe's findings on switchbacks highlight the importance of distinguishing between features observed in young solar wind and those seen in older data from missions like Helios. While both sets of observations are valuable, they require careful interpretation to avoid overstating the novelty of recent discoveries.
