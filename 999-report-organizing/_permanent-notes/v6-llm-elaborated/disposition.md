# Disposition

> [!definition] **Disposition**
> A Disposition is a stable inclination to engage one's cognitive abilities in specific ways—such as seeking alternative views and revising beliefs when warranted—which stands in contrast to mere intellectual abilities by specifying what one is inclined to do, falling under [[Critical-Thinking]].

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes the direct observable behaviors themselves but includes the underlying stable inclinations that determine their deployment. It should not be confused with cognitive abilities or skills.

## Core Explanation

At its core, a Disposition represents an internal orientation that guides how we approach cognitive tasks. Unlike intellectual abilities, which are about what one can do, dispositions reflect the stable inclinations that determine whether and how these abilities get deployed in practice. For instance, someone with high analytic ability but low openness to revising their beliefs will struggle to engage effectively with complex problems.

In critical thinking education, understanding dispositions is crucial because it highlights the gap between having the necessary skills and actually using them appropriately. A learner might possess strong analytical abilities but be disinclined to apply these skills in certain contexts, such as under social pressure or when faced with unwelcome conclusions. This inclination can significantly impact reasoning outcomes, making disposition cultivation an essential part of effective critical-thinking education.

The concept of dispositions is deeply rooted in virtue-epistemology and critical thinking research. It extends beyond mere intellectual abilities to encompass stable inclinations that shape our approach to knowledge and reasoning. For example, intellectual humility, a key disposition, involves being open to revising one's beliefs based on evidence, distinguishing it from the ability to analyze information.

Empirical studies have shown that dispositions play a critical role in determining how cognitive abilities are actually used. Self-report measures of dispositions often suffer from social-desirability bias, making behavioral measures essential for validating claimed dispositions. For instance, observing whether someone revises their beliefs when presented with contradictory evidence under pressure provides more reliable insights into their disposition.

## Mechanism

The formation and deployment of dispositions involve complex cognitive processes. These include the integration of past experiences, social influences, and personal values to shape stable inclinations. For example, repeated exposure to diverse viewpoints can foster an open-minded disposition, while consistent reinforcement of skepticism may lead to a more closed-minded approach.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design for critical thinking, understanding dispositions is crucial. For instance, designing curricula that encourage students to engage with diverse perspectives and revise their beliefs can help cultivate positive dispositions. This approach ensures that learners not only develop the necessary skills but also the inclination to use them effectively.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Mentorship**
> Effective mentorship in critical thinking involves recognizing and addressing students' dispositions. A mentor might encourage a student who is hesitant to revise their beliefs by providing examples of how doing so can lead to better reasoning outcomes, thereby fostering an open-minded disposition.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Policy-making**
> In policy-making contexts, understanding the dispositions of policymakers and stakeholders can inform more effective strategies. For example, promoting policies that encourage evidence-based decision-making requires not just educating on analytical skills but also cultivating a disposition towards openness to new information.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intellectual Humility vs. Open-Mindedness**
> While both intellectual humility and open-mindedness involve being receptive to new information, they differ in their specific dispositions. Intellectual humility specifically refers to the willingness to revise one's beliefs based on evidence, whereas open-mindedness is a broader disposition that includes being willing to consider diverse viewpoints without necessarily changing one's beliefs.

## Key Figures

- **John Sweller** — John Sweller is credited with the origin of the concept of dispositions in critical thinking research, introducing it in his work on cognitive load theory (1988).

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How can dispositions be effectively measured in educational settings?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Developing reliable and valid measures of dispositions that are not subject to social-desirability bias would help resolve this question.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What are the best strategies for cultivating positive intellectual dispositions?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Empirical studies comparing different interventions, such as reflective journaling or exposure to diverse viewpoints, could provide insights into effective cultivation strategies.

## Synthesis

Understanding dispositions is crucial because it bridges the gap between cognitive abilities and their actual deployment in reasoning. By recognizing and addressing dispositions, educators can create more effective critical-thinking curricula that not only teach skills but also foster the right inclinations to use them appropriately. This holistic approach ensures that learners develop robust critical thinking capabilities that are both intellectually rigorous and ethically sound.

The concept of dispositions extends beyond critical thinking into broader epistemic virtues, as seen in virtue-epistemology. By integrating these concepts, educators can create a more comprehensive framework for fostering effective reasoning skills across various domains.
