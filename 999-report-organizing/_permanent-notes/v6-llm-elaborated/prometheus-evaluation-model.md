## Core Explanation

Prometheus was developed to address a critical gap in natural language generation (NLG) evaluation: the need for an efficient, cost-effective, and reliable method to assess NLG output quality without relying on proprietary or expensive models. By fine-tuning a smaller model on high-quality evaluation feedback from larger general-purpose models like GPT-4, Prometheus demonstrates that evaluation capability can be transferred efficiently through distillation on specific training data.

The core mechanism of Prometheus involves using rubric-based prompts to generate quality scores and rationales for NLG outputs. These rubrics are designed to capture the nuances of human judgment in evaluating text quality across various criteria such as coherence, relevance, informativeness, and helpfulness. This approach allows Prometheus to produce evaluations that closely match those from larger models but at a fraction of the cost.

Prometheus's training process is crucial for its effectiveness. It leverages a large dataset of evaluation feedback generated by GPT-4 in the form of (input, response, rubric criteria, score, feedback) tuples. This data enables Prometheus to learn how to produce quality scores and rationales that are comparable to those from GPT-4 on tasks well-represented in its training set.

The theoretical underpinning of Prometheus lies in the idea that evaluation capability can be efficiently transferred through fine-tuning on specific evaluation data, rather than being strictly dependent on model scale. This challenges the notion that only large models can perform high-quality evaluations and opens up new possibilities for cost-effective NLG assessment.

Prometheus's development reflects a broader trend in machine learning towards more efficient and targeted model usage. As computational resources become increasingly expensive, there is growing interest in techniques that can achieve high performance with smaller models. ## Mechanism

Prometheus operates by receiving a generated text along with a rubric-based prompt as input. The rubric specifies evaluation criteria such as coherence, relevance, informativeness, and helpfulness. Prometheus then generates a quality score and an evaluation rationale based on these criteria. This process is designed to be comparable in quality to evaluations produced by larger models like GPT-4 but at significantly lower cost.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Evaluation-specific fine-tuning vs general-purpose LLMs**
> Prometheus is specifically fine-tuned on evaluation data, making it highly effective for assessing the quality of NLG outputs based on predefined criteria. In contrast, general-purpose LLMs are trained on a wide variety of tasks and may not perform as well in specialized evaluation contexts. This distinction highlights Prometheus's tailored approach to evaluation, which can produce more reliable scores than general-purpose models when used within its training distribution.

This reflective process allows evaluators and content creators to understand the reasoning behind quality assessments, facilitating more informed decision-making.
