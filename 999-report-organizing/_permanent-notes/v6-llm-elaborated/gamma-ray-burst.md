# Gamma Ray Burst

> [!definition] **Gamma Ray Burst**
> A Gamma Ray Burst (GRB) is a brief and intense flash of gamma-rays originating from cosmological distances, categorized into long-duration (>2 seconds) and short-duration (<2 seconds) classes based on their duration distribution. These bursts are not to be confused with other transient high-energy phenomena such as hypernovae or kilonovae, which have distinct characteristics. It falls under the broader category of Transient High-Energy Phenomena.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes other transient high-energy phenomena not associated with the specific characteristics of GRBs such as hypernovae or kilonovae. It should not be confused with less energetic cosmic events like supernovae.

## Core Explanation

Gamma Ray Bursts (GRBs) represent some of the most energetic and luminous events in the universe, with peak luminosities that can exceed those of entire galaxies. These bursts are typically observed as brief flashes of gamma-rays lasting from milliseconds to thousands of seconds. The extreme brightness of GRBs is due to the beaming effect of relativistic jets, which focus their energy into narrow cones, making them appear much brighter to observers within these beams than they would if viewed isotropically.

The mechanism behind GRBs involves either the collapse of massive stars (collapsars) or the merger of compact objects such as neutron stars. In both cases, a relativistic jet is formed and directed along the axis of rotation, leading to an intense burst of gamma-rays when it intersects with Earth's line of sight. This beaming effect means that only a small fraction of the total energy emitted by these events is actually observed, making accurate measurements of their true energetics challenging.

Understanding GRBs requires integrating insights from both theoretical astrophysics and observational astronomy. Theoretical models predict that the jets are powered by the rotational energy of newly formed black holes or neutron stars, while observations provide critical constraints on the physical conditions within these jets. This interplay between theory and observation has led to significant advances in our understanding of GRBs over recent decades.

The study of GRBs is crucial for several reasons. Firstly, they serve as powerful probes into the early universe due to their extreme luminosity, allowing astronomers to detect them at cosmological distances where other types of light are too faint or redshifted to be observed. Secondly, GRBs provide a unique window into the processes that occur during the formation and evolution of black holes and neutron stars.

## Mechanism

The mechanism behind long-duration GRBs is thought to involve the collapse of massive stars (collapsars) into black holes or highly magnetized neutron stars. During this process, a jet of matter is ejected at relativistic speeds along the rotational axis of the progenitor star. This jet interacts with surrounding material, producing gamma-rays through processes such as synchrotron radiation and inverse Compton scattering.

Short-duration GRBs are believed to result from the merger of two compact objects, typically neutron stars or a black hole-neutron star pair. The collision leads to the formation of a new, more massive object (often a black hole) surrounded by an accretion disk. As this material spirals inward and is ejected in jets perpendicular to the orbital plane, it generates intense gamma-ray emission.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Cosmological Probes**
> GRBs serve as powerful probes into the early universe due to their extreme luminosity. By studying GRBs at high redshifts, astronomers can gather information about the intergalactic medium and the star formation history of distant galaxies. This is particularly useful for understanding conditions in the first billion years after the Big Bang.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Black Hole Formation**
> GRBs provide a unique opportunity to study black hole formation, especially when they are associated with long-duration bursts from collapsars. Observations of these events can reveal details about the mass and spin of newly formed black holes, which is crucial for testing theories of general relativity in extreme conditions.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Long-Duration vs Short-Duration GRBs**
> The distinction between long-duration (≳2 seconds) and short-duration (≲2 seconds) GRBs is based on their duration distribution. Long-duration GRBs are typically associated with the collapse of massive stars into black holes or highly magnetized neutron stars, while short-duration GRBs are believed to result from the merger of two compact objects such as neutron stars.

## Key Figures

- **Stan Woosley** — Stan Woosley is a key figure in the study of Gamma Ray Bursts, particularly for his work on collapsar models that explain long-duration GRBs. His theoretical contributions have been instrumental in understanding how massive stars can collapse to form black holes and produce relativistic jets.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What are the true energetics of Gamma Ray Bursts when corrected for jet opening angles?
>
> *What would resolve it:* High-resolution observations that accurately measure the jet opening angle would provide a more precise estimate of the total energy output from GRBs.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How do different types of GRBs (long-duration vs short-duration) form and what distinguishes their mechanisms?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Further observational studies, especially those that capture the full range of electromagnetic emissions from these events, could help clarify the differences in formation mechanisms between long- and short-duration GRBs.

## Synthesis

Understanding Gamma Ray Bursts is crucial for advancing our knowledge of high-energy astrophysical phenomena. By studying GRBs, astronomers gain insights into extreme conditions involving black holes, neutron stars, and relativistic jets. These events also serve as powerful probes into the early universe, providing a unique window into its earliest epochs.
