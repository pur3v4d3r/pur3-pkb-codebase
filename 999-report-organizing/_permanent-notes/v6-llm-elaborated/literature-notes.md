# Literature Notes

> [!definition] **Literature Notes**
> Literature Notes are restatements of source material in one's own words, indexed to the original source, serving as a staging area for ideas that may become permanent atomic notes. This concept excludes summaries and highlights; it focuses on the act of reformulation, which is why copy-pasted highlights cannot substitute for them even when they look superficially complete. It falls under [[zettelkasten-workflow]].

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes summaries and highlights; it focuses on the act of reformulation. It should not be confused with fleeting or evergreen notes.

## Core Explanation

Literature Notes are a crucial component of the Zettelkasten workflow, designed to force the reader to engage deeply with source material by restating it in their own words. This process ensures that the information is not merely absorbed but truly understood and integrated into one's knowledge base. The act of reformulation itself is a form of cognitive processing that enhances comprehension and retention.

In practice, creating Literature Notes involves reading a passage from a source text, then writing down a restatement in your own words. This step is critical because it requires the reader to actively think about the content, identify key points, and articulate them clearly. The indexed nature of these notes allows for easy reference back to the original sources, facilitating further exploration and synthesis.

Theoretical roots of Literature Notes can be traced back to cognitive psychology, particularly the concept of intrinsic vs. extraneous load. Intrinsic load refers to the inherent difficulty of a task, while extraneous load is the unnecessary mental effort required by the presentation or format of information. By forcing reformulation, Literature Notes reduce extraneous load and enhance intrinsic load, making the learning process more effective.

Historically, the concept was popularized in 1988 by John Sweller, who emphasized the importance of deep processing over shallow processing in cognitive load theory. This approach aligns with the Zettelkasten method, which advocates for continuous and incremental knowledge construction through active engagement with sources.

## Mechanism

The creation of Literature Notes involves several steps: first, reading a passage from a source text; second, restating it in one's own words; third, indexing the note to the original source. This process ensures that each note is both a summary and an active engagement with the material.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, Literature Notes can significantly enhance student comprehension by forcing them to engage deeply with course materials. By restating information in their own words, students not only remember the content better but also develop a deeper understanding of complex concepts.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Academic research**
> For researchers, Literature Notes serve as a valuable tool for synthesizing and integrating diverse sources into a coherent body of knowledge. This process helps in identifying gaps in existing literature and formulating new hypotheses or insights.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Professional development**
> In professional settings, Literature Notes can be used to document learning experiences and reflect on best practices. By restating key points from training materials or industry reports, professionals can better retain the information and apply it effectively in their work.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Literature Notes vs Fleeting Notes**
> Fleeting notes are temporary records of ideas that do not require reformulation. In contrast, Literature Notes involve restating source material in one's own words, making them a more robust form of engagement with the content.

> [!key-distinction] **Literature Notes vs Evergreen Notes**
> Evergreen notes focus on key points and insights from sources, whereas Literature Notes aim to understand the source material thoroughly. This distinction is crucial because it highlights the different purposes: evergreen notes are for quick reference, while Literature Notes are for deep understanding.
