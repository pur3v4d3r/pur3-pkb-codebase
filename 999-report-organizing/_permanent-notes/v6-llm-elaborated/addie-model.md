# ADDIE Model

> [!definition] **ADDIE Model**
> The ADDIE Model is a widely-used framework for instructional design, organizing development into five phases: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation. It falls under [[Instructional-Systems-Design]], providing cross-organizational teams with shared vocabulary and expectations about the work each phase produces.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes specific implementation details within each phase but includes the overall structure and purpose of the model in instructional design processes.

## Core Explanation

The ADDIE Model is a foundational framework in instructional design that breaks down the development process into five distinct phases: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation. Each phase serves a specific purpose; for instance, Analysis identifies the needs of learners and the context of instruction, while Design outlines objectives, sequence, and assessments. The Development phase focuses on producing the actual instructional materials, followed by Implementation where these materials are delivered to learners. Finally, Evaluation assesses both formative (during) and summative (after) outcomes to ensure effectiveness.

In practice, ADDIE operates as a structured yet flexible process that allows for iterative improvements. For example, during the Design phase, formative evaluation can identify gaps or issues that require adjustments back in the Analysis or Design phases. This iterative nature ensures continuous refinement of instructional materials and delivery methods. The model's flexibility is particularly valuable in cross-organizational teams where different stakeholders may have varying levels of expertise and experience.

Theoretical roots of ADDIE trace back to systems theory, which emphasizes a holistic approach to problem-solving. By breaking down complex tasks into manageable phases, ADDIE aligns with the principles of instructional systems design (ISD), ensuring that each component is carefully considered before moving on to the next phase. This structured approach helps in creating coherent and effective learning experiences.

Historically, ADDIE has been influential since its adoption in the 1970s, reflecting a shift towards more systematic approaches in education and training. Its longevity can be attributed to its ability to provide a common language and framework for instructional designers, making it easier to collaborate across different organizations and projects.

## Mechanism

The ADDIE Model is not a strict waterfall process but includes formative evaluation loops that allow feedback from the Evaluation phase to influence earlier phases. For instance, if issues are identified during Implementation, they can be traced back to Design or even Analysis for necessary adjustments. This iterative approach ensures continuous improvement and alignment with learner needs.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, ADDIE provides a clear roadmap that helps teams stay organized and focused on their goals. By breaking down the process into distinct phases, it ensures that all necessary components are considered before moving forward, leading to more effective learning materials.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Cross-organizational collaboration**
> ADDIE facilitates cross-organizational collaboration by providing a shared vocabulary and expectations. Teams from different organizations can use ADDIE's framework to communicate effectively about their roles and responsibilities in the development process.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> ADDIE focuses on intrinsic load, which is related to the inherent difficulty of learning tasks. In contrast, extraneous load refers to unnecessary cognitive demands introduced by instructional materials or methods. ADDIE helps minimize extraneous load by ensuring that instructional design aligns with learner needs and objectives.

## Key Figures

- **John Sweller** — While not the sole originator, John Sweller's work on cognitive load theory has significantly influenced the development of ADDIE, particularly in emphasizing the importance of instructional design that minimizes extraneous cognitive load.

## Synthesis

The ADDIE Model's enduring relevance lies in its ability to provide a structured yet flexible framework for instructional design. By offering a common language and process, it enhances collaboration across diverse teams and ensures that learning materials are aligned with learner needs. Its integration into broader systems theory and cognitive load principles makes it a valuable tool not only in traditional educational settings but also in modern training and development programs.

ADDIE's adaptability to both waterfall and iterative processes positions it as a versatile model, capable of supporting various instructional design methodologies. This flexibility is particularly important in today's rapidly changing educational landscape, where the ability to quickly respond to feedback and adjust instruction is crucial.

## Evidence

The ADDIE Model's longevity reflects its function as a language of organization for ID work rather than as a prescriptive method. Its iterative nature, with formative evaluation loops back to earlier phases, ensures continuous improvement and alignment with learner needs. This flexibility has made it the lingua franca of instructional design despite the rise of more agile alternatives.
