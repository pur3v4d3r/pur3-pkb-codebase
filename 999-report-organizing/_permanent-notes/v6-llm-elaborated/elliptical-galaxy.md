# Elliptical Galaxy

> [!definition] **Elliptical Galaxy**
> An Elliptical Galaxy is a galaxy characterized by its smooth, ellipsoidal distribution of older red stars and minimal ongoing star formation, dynamically supported by velocity dispersion rather than ordered rotation; it falls under the broader category of Galaxy Morphological Types. Unlike spiral or irregular galaxies, ellipticals lack prominent structures such as arms or discs, distinguishing them from these other types.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> Elliptical galaxies are distinguished from spiral or irregular galaxies by their lack of prominent structure such as arms or discs. They should not be confused with dwarf spheroidal galaxies, which are smaller and less massive.

## Core Explanation

Elliptical galaxies are defined by their ellipsoidal stellar distribution and a population dominated by older red stars, indicating minimal recent star formation activity. This characteristic is often misleadingly simplified to imply that ellipticals are uniformly 'old' systems, but modern integral-field spectroscopy reveals complex heterogeneity within these galaxies, including varied ages of stellar populations, kinematic substructure, and embedded disc components.

The dynamics of an elliptical galaxy are fundamentally different from those of spiral or irregular galaxies. Rather than being supported by the ordered rotation typical in spiral galaxies, ellipticals rely on velocity dispersion to maintain their structure. This dynamic characteristic is a key factor in classifying these galaxies within the Hubble sequence as E0–E7 based on projected ellipticity.

The formation and evolution of elliptical galaxies are now understood through the lens of hierarchical assembly theory, particularly emphasizing 'dry' mergers where gas-poor disc galaxies combine to form larger, more massive systems. This process contrasts sharply with in situ star formation-driven growth seen in spiral galaxies, highlighting a fundamental difference in galactic evolutionary pathways.

## Mechanism

Elliptical galaxies are primarily formed through hierarchical assembly and dry mergers. In this process, smaller disc galaxies merge without significant gas content, leading to the creation of larger elliptical structures. Over time, these systems can grow even more massive by accreting smaller satellite galaxies, a mechanism that is particularly evident in the formation of cD galaxies at the centers of galaxy clusters.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Cosmological Models**
> Understanding elliptical galaxy formation through hierarchical assembly and dry mergers provides critical insights into cosmological models. These processes help refine our understanding of large-scale structure in the universe, including how galaxies cluster and evolve over cosmic time. Ignoring these mechanisms could lead to misinterpretations of observational data regarding galactic clustering patterns and the distribution of dark matter.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Galactic Evolution Studies**
> The study of elliptical galaxy formation is crucial for advancing our knowledge of galactic evolution. By examining how galaxies transition from disc-dominated systems to more massive, gas-poor ellipticals through mergers and accretion events, researchers can better understand the full lifecycle of galaxies and their role in shaping the universe's structure.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Elliptical vs Spiral Galaxy Structure**
> The primary distinction between elliptical and spiral galaxies lies in their structural characteristics. Ellipticals are characterized by a smooth, ellipsoidal distribution of stars with little to no ongoing star formation, whereas spirals feature prominent disc structures and arms that indicate active star formation processes. This difference is not merely superficial; it reflects fundamental differences in the dynamics and evolutionary histories of these galaxy types.

## Key Figures

- **Vicent Franx** — Franx's work has been pivotal in elucidating the role of hierarchical assembly and dry mergers in the formation of elliptical galaxies. His research has provided significant evidence supporting the idea that these processes are key to understanding how massive, gas-poor systems evolve over time.

## Open Questions

> What are the detailed mechanisms of dry mergers in forming elliptical galaxies?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Detailed simulations and observational studies could provide insights into the specific processes involved in these mergers, including how gas-poor systems combine to form larger ellipticals.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How do cD galaxies form and evolve within galaxy clusters?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Further research on the accretion of smaller satellite galaxies by central cluster members could clarify the mechanisms behind the formation and evolution of these massive, gas-poor systems.

## Synthesis

Understanding elliptical galaxies is crucial for comprehending large-scale structure and dynamics within the universe. These galaxies serve as key indicators of galactic evolutionary pathways, particularly in the context of hierarchical assembly and dry mergers. By studying these processes, researchers can gain deeper insights into how galaxies cluster and evolve over cosmic time, contributing to a more comprehensive understanding of cosmological models.
