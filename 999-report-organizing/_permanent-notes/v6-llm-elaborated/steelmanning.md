# Steelmanning

> [!definition] **Steelmanning**
> Steelmanning is a practice within critical thinking that involves engaging with and strengthening the strongest, most defensible version of an opposing argument before offering a critique. It contrasts with strawmanning, which attacks a weaker or misrepresented version of an opponent's argument, by requiring intellectual empathy to reconstruct arguments accurately.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It is distinct from strawmanning, which involves attacking a weaker or misrepresented version of an opponent's argument. Steelmanning requires intellectual empathy and fair-mindedness to reconstruct arguments accurately.

## Core Explanation

Steelmanning is a critical thinking technique that enhances the quality and depth of debates by ensuring that critiques are directed at the most robust form of an opposing viewpoint. This practice not only elevates the discourse but also ensures that any refutation addresses the core issues rather than peripheral weaknesses. By engaging with the strongest version of an argument, one can more effectively challenge underlying assumptions or logical structures.

The process of steelmanning requires a deep understanding and fair-minded reconstruction of an opponent's position. It involves identifying the key premises and evidence supporting the argument and then strengthening these elements where necessary to create a more robust case. This approach demands intellectual empathy, as it necessitates putting oneself in the shoes of the other party to understand their perspective fully.

Steelmanning is rooted in epistemological principles that value rigorous scrutiny and fair engagement with ideas. It encourages participants in debates or discussions to focus on substantive issues rather than engaging in rhetorical gamesmanship. By adhering to this practice, individuals can contribute more meaningfully to the collective pursuit of truth and understanding.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional settings, steelmanning can be used to foster a deeper understanding among students by encouraging them to engage with the strongest forms of opposing arguments. This approach not only helps in clarifying complex concepts but also trains students in critical thinking and argumentation skills. For instance, when discussing controversial topics, instructors might present both sides of an issue using their most compelling arguments, prompting students to analyze and critique these robust positions.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Policy debates**
> In policy-making contexts, steelmanning can lead to more informed decision-making by ensuring that all stakeholders engage with the strongest versions of opposing viewpoints. This practice helps in identifying potential flaws or weaknesses in proposed policies before they are implemented. For example, during a debate on environmental regulations, proponents and opponents might present their most defensible arguments, allowing for a thorough examination of the policy's implications.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Steelmanning vs Strawmanning**
> The key distinction between steelmanning and strawmanning lies in how they approach opposing arguments. Steelmanning involves engaging with and strengthening the strongest version of an argument, ensuring that critiques are directed at a robust position. In contrast, strawmanning entails attacking a weaker or misrepresented version of an opponent's argument, which does not address the core issues effectively.
