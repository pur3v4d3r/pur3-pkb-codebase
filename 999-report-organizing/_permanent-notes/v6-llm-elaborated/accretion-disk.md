# Accretion Disk

> [!definition] **Accretion Disk**
> An Accretion Disk is a differentially-rotating disc of gas that forms around compact objects such as black holes and neutron stars, where in-falling material loses angular momentum through viscous stress and radiates gravitational binding energy as it spirals inward. This process powers the observed luminosity of X-ray binaries, AGN, and quasars. It falls under astrophysical accretion structures but excludes other types such as spherical accretion or jet formation.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> The concept excludes other types of accretion structures such as spherical accretion or jet formation. It should not be confused with stellar disks that form planets or stars.

## Core Explanation

At its core, an Accretion Disk is a dynamic structure where gas spirals inward towards a central compact object due to gravitational forces. As the material loses angular momentum through viscous stress, it heats up and emits radiation, converting gravitational potential energy into electromagnetic radiation with remarkable efficiency. This process not only illuminates the disk but also provides insights into the properties of the central object.

The theoretical framework for understanding accretion disks was pioneered by Shakura and Sunyaev in 1973, who introduced a parameterized viscosity model to describe the transport of angular momentum within the disk. However, modern studies reveal that this viscosity is driven by magnetic instabilities rather than simple fluid dynamics, complicating our understanding of how energy is converted and dissipated.

Observationally, accretion disks are crucial for studying black holes and neutron stars because they emit copious amounts of X-rays and other forms of radiation. These emissions can be used to infer the mass and spin of the central object, as well as the disk's structure and composition.

## Mechanism

The conversion of gravitational potential energy into radiation in an accretion disk occurs through a combination of viscous stress and magnetic instabilities. As gas spirals inward, it experiences frictional forces that dissipate angular momentum, causing the material to heat up and emit X-rays and other forms of electromagnetic radiation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Black Hole Studies**
> Understanding accretion disks is essential for studying black holes because they provide a direct means of observing these elusive objects. By analyzing the emitted radiation from an accretion disk, astronomers can infer properties such as the mass and spin of the central black hole.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Active Galactic Nuclei**
> AGN are powered by supermassive black holes at their centers, which feed on surrounding material through accretion disks. Studying these disks helps astronomers understand how AGN produce their intense radiation and how they influence the evolution of galaxies.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Neutron Star Research**
> Accretion disks around neutron stars are key to understanding their behavior, especially in X-ray binaries where material from a companion star falls onto the neutron star. Observations of these systems provide insights into the extreme conditions and processes occurring near compact objects.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Thin Disc vs Thick Disc**
> Accretion disks can be categorized as thin or thick based on their vertical structure, with thin discs being geometrically flat and optically thick, while thick discs are more vertically extended. This distinction is important because it affects the disk's radiative efficiency and stability.

## Key Figures

- **Sergei Shakura** — Shakura, along with Rashid Sunyaev, developed a seminal model for accretion disks that introduced the α-prescription to describe viscosity in these systems. This work laid foundational groundwork for understanding how angular momentum is transported and dissipated within accretion disks.
- **Rashid Sunyaev** — Sunyaev, alongside Sergei Shakura, formulated a critical model of accretion disks that has been widely used to understand the dynamics and radiation processes in these systems. Their work introduced key concepts like the α-prescription for viscosity.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What are the exact mechanisms of energy conversion in accretion disks?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Detailed simulations and observational studies that can probe the microphysics within accretion disks would help resolve this question, potentially revealing new insights into how gravitational potential energy is converted to radiation.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How do magnetic instabilities influence the efficiency and structure of accretion disks?
>
> *What would resolve it:* High-resolution numerical simulations coupled with observational data could provide answers by showing how magnetic fields affect disk dynamics, stability, and radiative processes.

## Synthesis

Understanding accretion disks is crucial for astrophysics because they are key to studying some of the most energetic phenomena in the universe. By providing a window into black holes, neutron stars, and AGN, accretion disks offer insights into extreme gravitational fields, high-energy processes, and the evolution of galaxies.
