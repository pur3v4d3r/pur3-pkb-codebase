# Quark Star

> [!definition] **Quark Star**
> A Quark Star is a theoretical compact celestial body composed of deconfined quarks, primarily strange quark matter, which would form under extreme densities if the QCD ground state transitions into a color-superconducting phase rather than remaining in ordinary hadronic states. Unlike neutron stars, which are made up of neutrons and do not contain deconfined quarks, Quark Stars remain speculative without observational confirmation. It falls under the category of Hypothetical Compact Objects.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It should not be confused with neutron stars, which are composed of neutrons and do not contain deconfined quarks. Quark stars remain speculative without observational confirmation.

## Core Explanation

Quark Stars represent a fascinating theoretical possibility in astrophysics where matter is pushed to such extreme densities that it transitions from hadronic states into deconfined quarks, forming strange quark matter. This transformation hinges on the conditions within compact objects like neutron stars, where gravitational pressures are immense and could potentially induce phase changes in nuclear matter. The concept of Quark Stars emerged from theoretical models exploring quantum chromodynamics (QCD) at high densities, suggesting that under these extreme conditions, ordinary hadronic matter might not be the ground state.

The idea of deconfined quarks within a compact object challenges our understanding of stellar interiors and the nature of matter at its most fundamental level. If confirmed, Quark Stars would represent a new phase of matter distinct from neutron stars, with implications for high-density physics and the QCD phase diagram. Theoretical models predict that such objects could exist beyond the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit, which describes the maximum mass of a compact object before gravitational collapse into a black hole.

The speculative nature of Quark Stars is underscored by their lack of observational confirmation. While some studies have proposed candidates based on anomalous observations of neutron stars, these claims are often met with skepticism and alternative explanations involving exotic equations of state for neutron matter. The key claim that Quark Stars would be self-bound if Witten's strange-matter hypothesis holds true adds a layer of complexity to their theoretical framework, suggesting they could have distinct mass-radius relations compared to conventional neutron stars.

Theoretical developments in QCD and astrophysics continue to refine our understanding of the conditions necessary for quarks to deconfine. This ongoing research not only seeks to confirm or refute the existence of Quark Stars but also deepens our comprehension of high-density physics, potentially revealing new phases of matter that could exist under extreme cosmic conditions.
