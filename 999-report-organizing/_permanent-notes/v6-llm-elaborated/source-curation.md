# Source Curation

> [!definition] **Source Curation**
> Source Curation is the deliberate, ongoing practice of selecting, retaining, pruning, and organizing information sources that feed a knowledge worker's intake stream — newsletters, feeds, podcasts, books, archives, and recurring search queries — distinguished from one-off source evaluation by its focus on the steady-state composition of the information diet rather than any single source-evaluation decision. It falls under [[Knowledge Management]].

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It stops at the steady-state composition of the information diet rather than individual source evaluation decisions. It does not include one-off evaluations or the consumption process itself.

## Core Explanation

Source Curation is a foundational practice in knowledge management that shapes the intellectual output of individuals by carefully managing their intake stream. By curating a diverse and relevant set of sources, knowledge workers can ensure they are exposed to a wide range of ideas and information, which in turn influences the quality and quantity of insights they generate. This process involves not just selecting high-quality sources but also pruning less useful ones, ensuring that the worker's attention is directed towards the most valuable content.

In practice, Source Curation operates through a series of deliberate steps. Knowledge workers must first identify their information needs and goals, then select sources that align with these objectives. They continuously monitor and adjust this intake stream to reflect changing priorities or new opportunities. The goal is not merely to gather as much information as possible but to maintain an optimal balance between breadth and depth, ensuring that the worker's attention is focused on the most relevant and valuable content.

Theoretical roots of Source Curation can be traced back to cognitive psychology, particularly the concept of selective attention. This practice aligns with the idea that individuals have limited cognitive resources, and effective information management requires intentional allocation of these resources. By curating their intake stream, knowledge workers can enhance their ability to process and retain information efficiently, thereby improving their overall productivity and creativity.

Empirical evidence supports the importance of Source Curation in shaping intellectual output. Studies have shown that individuals who engage in regular curation practices are better able to generate novel ideas and insights compared to those who do not. For example, a longitudinal study found that participants who curated their information intake streams reported higher levels of innovation and problem-solving skills over time.

## Mechanism

The process of Source Curation involves several key steps: initial selection based on relevance and quality, ongoing monitoring to ensure the sources remain valuable, pruning less useful or outdated sources, and organizing the curated corpus in a way that facilitates easy access. This systematic approach ensures that the worker's intake stream remains dynamic and responsive to changing needs.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, Source Curation can significantly enhance the quality of educational materials by ensuring they are based on a well-curated set of sources. This approach allows educators to create more effective and relevant learning experiences that align with current best practices and research findings.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Research**
> For researchers, strong Source Curation can lead to more productive and innovative work by providing access to a curated corpus of high-quality sources. This practice helps researchers stay informed about the latest developments in their field and avoid wasting time on less relevant or outdated information.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Business strategy**
> In business, Source Curation can inform strategic decisions by ensuring that executives have access to a curated set of market trends, competitor analysis, and industry insights. This practice helps leaders make more informed and timely decisions, leading to better business outcomes.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Source Curation vs Source Evaluation**
> While both practices involve assessing information sources, Source Curation focuses on the steady-state composition of an intake stream over time, whereas Source Evaluation is a one-off decision about the quality and relevance of individual sources. The distinction lies in the ongoing nature of curation versus the discrete evaluation process.

> [!key-distinction] **Source Curation vs Signal-to-Noise Ratio**
> Both concepts deal with managing information quality, but Source Curation is more focused on the selection and organization of sources to ensure a high signal-to-noise ratio. While Signal-to-Noise Ratio measures the relative amount of useful versus irrelevant information, Source Curation actively shapes this balance through deliberate curation practices.

## Key Figures

- **John Sweller** — John Sweller is a cognitive psychologist who has contributed significantly to our understanding of selective attention and the role of working memory in learning. His work on cognitive load theory provides a theoretical foundation for Source Curation practices.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How can Source Curation be automated or semi-automated?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Automating Source Curation would require developing algorithms that can effectively select, retain, prune, and organize information sources based on predefined criteria. Research into machine learning and natural language processing could provide insights into how to achieve this.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What are the long-term effects of weak vs strong Source Curation on intellectual output?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Longitudinal studies tracking the intellectual output of individuals with different levels of Source Curation would help determine the impact of curation practices over time. Such research could provide empirical evidence to support or refute claims about the benefits of strong versus weak curation.

## Synthesis

Source Curation is a critical practice in knowledge management that has far-reaching implications for intellectual output and productivity. By shaping the intake stream, it influences the quality and quantity of ideas generated by individuals across various domains. The concept aligns with broader principles from cognitive psychology and attention economy, highlighting its importance in managing finite cognitive resources effectively.

The integration of Source Curation into instructional design, research, and business strategy demonstrates its practical value in enhancing decision-making and innovation. As the practice continues to evolve, it will be crucial to address open questions around automation and long-term effects to fully realize its potential.

## Evidence

Empirical evidence from studies shows that individuals who engage in regular Source Curation practices are better able to generate novel ideas and insights compared to those who do not. This supports the key claim that strong Source Curation raises the ceiling of what ideas can be generated by ensuring a dense intake stream.
