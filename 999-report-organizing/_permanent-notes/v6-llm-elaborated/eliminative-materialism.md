# Eliminative Materialism

> [!definition] **Eliminative Materialism**
> Eliminative Materialism, defended by Paul and Patricia Churchland, is the position that folk-psychological categories such as beliefs and desires are posits of a defective theory and will be eliminated in favor of more accurate neuroscientific concepts. It falls under [[Physicalism]], which asserts that everything can be explained in terms of physical phenomena, but unlike other forms of physicalism, Eliminative Materialism takes seriously the possibility that these folk-psychological categories may not survive scientific progress.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes the idea that folk psychology can be salvaged or that it is a fully adequate framework for understanding mental processes. It also does not include other physicalist positions that might soften this view.

## Core Explanation

Eliminative Materialism challenges the adequacy and survival of everyday mental vocabulary by positing that concepts like beliefs and desires are not only inaccurate but also potentially harmful to our understanding of the mind. The Churchlands argue that as neuroscience advances, these folk-psychological categories will be replaced with more precise neuroscientific terms, much like how phlogiston or ether were discarded in favor of more accurate scientific theories.

This view raises a sharp meta-scientific question: if physicalism alone does not guarantee the survival of folk psychology, then why should we expect it to persist? The Churchlands' position is that this contingency must be taken seriously. They contend that dismissing Eliminative Materialism as self-refuting misunderstands their argument; the appearance of self-refutation depends on assuming the very framework whose adequacy is in question.

The implications of this stance are profound for scientific and philosophical discourse about the mind. It challenges not only our everyday language but also the foundational assumptions that underpin much of cognitive science, suggesting a radical rethinking of how we conceptualize mental processes.

Eliminative Materialism also has significant practical implications. For instance, in instructional design, it suggests that educational materials and methods should be based on neuroscientific evidence rather than folk-psychological intuitions. This could lead to more effective learning strategies by aligning with the actual mechanisms of the brain.

## Mechanism

The process through which Eliminative Materialism envisions the replacement of folk psychological categories is gradual and incremental, driven by advances in neuroscience. As neuroscientists uncover new insights into how the brain functions, these findings will increasingly challenge and eventually replace our everyday mental vocabulary with more precise terms.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, Eliminative Materialism implies that educational materials should be based on neuroscientific evidence rather than folk-psychological intuitions. This could lead to more effective learning strategies by aligning with the actual mechanisms of the brain.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Mental health treatment**
> For mental health treatment, it suggests a shift from traditional talk therapies that rely on concepts like beliefs and desires towards treatments grounded in neuroscientific understanding, potentially leading to more targeted and effective interventions.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Eliminative vs. Reductive Physicalism**
> While both Eliminative Materialism and reductive physicalism fall under the broader category of [[Physicalism]], they differ in their stance on folk psychology. Reductive physicalism seeks to explain mental phenomena in terms of physical processes but does not necessarily predict the elimination of folk psychological categories, whereas Eliminative Materialism posits that these categories will be entirely replaced by neuroscientific concepts.

> [!key-distinction] **Survival vs. Replacement of Folk Psychology**
> Eliminative Materialism is distinct from other forms of physicalism in its assertion that folk psychology may not survive scientific progress. Other physicalist positions, such as reductive materialism, might soften this view and predict the survival or gradual refinement of folk psychological categories.

## Key Figures

- **Paul Churchland** — Paul Churchland is a prominent proponent of Eliminative Materialism who has argued that traditional mental concepts will be replaced by more accurate neuroscientific terms.
- **Patricia Churchland** — Patricia Churchland, along with her husband Paul, has developed and defended the theory of Eliminative Materialism, emphasizing the need to replace folk psychological categories with neuroscientific ones.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How might Eliminative Materialism affect our understanding of mental disorders?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Further research into the neural basis of mental disorders could provide evidence for or against the replacement of current diagnostic criteria with more precise neuroscientific terms.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> Can folk psychology be salvaged in some form?
>
> *What would resolve it:* A detailed comparison between folk psychological concepts and their neuroscientific counterparts, along with empirical validation, would help determine whether any aspects of folk psychology can be retained or if they must be entirely replaced.

## Synthesis

Eliminative Materialism matters because it challenges our fundamental assumptions about the nature of mental phenomena. By pushing for a radical rethinking of how we conceptualize and study the mind, this theory has significant implications not only in philosophy but also in cognitive science, neuroscience, and even practical applications like instructional design and mental health treatment.

Its impact extends beyond these domains to broader philosophical questions about the nature of reality and knowledge. By forcing us to consider the possibility that our current understanding of the mind may be fundamentally flawed, Eliminative Materialism encourages a more rigorous and evidence-based approach to studying cognitive processes.
