# Information Diet

> [!definition] **Information Diet**
> An Information Diet is the curated set of regular information sources that an individual consumes over time, designed to enhance cognitive function through deliberate selection and pruning of inputs, falling under [[cognitive-architecture]]. It excludes incidental consumption driven by recommender systems or passive exposure, focusing on intentional design rather than accidental accumulation.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes incidental consumption driven by recommender systems or passive exposure. It focuses on intentional design rather than accidental accumulation of information.

## Core Explanation

An Information Diet is a carefully selected regimen of information sources that individuals consume to optimize their cognitive performance. By pruning low-quality and redundant inputs, the diet enhances associative recall and cognitive integration, ensuring that nutritive sources have sufficient residency time to integrate effectively into one's knowledge framework.

In practice, an individual might start by identifying recurring noise in their daily information intake—such as repetitive news articles or social media posts—and systematically removing them. This process not only reduces cognitive load but also allows more space for high-quality content that can enrich and expand one’s mental models.

Theoretical roots of the Information Diet trace back to cognitive psychology, particularly the work of John Sweller on intrinsic vs. extraneous cognitive load. By focusing on intrinsic load—essential for learning and understanding—the diet helps individuals avoid the pitfalls of extraneous load, which can hinder effective information processing and retention.

Historically, the concept has evolved from early discussions on media consumption to a more nuanced approach that considers the epistemic value of different sources. For instance, maintaining a balanced exposure across viewpoints is often recommended, but this must be done with domain-specific epistemic judgment to avoid false-balancing pathologies.

## Mechanism

The mechanism behind an Information Diet involves pruning low-quality recurring inputs to make room for more nutritive sources. This process ensures that high-value information has the necessary residency time in one's cognitive system, leading to better integration and recall. By reducing extraneous noise, individuals can focus their cognitive resources on meaningful content.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, an Information Diet can be applied by curating a set of high-quality educational materials and removing redundant or low-value resources. This approach ensures that learners are exposed to the most relevant and enriching content, enhancing their understanding and retention.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Professional development**
> For professionals, maintaining an Information Diet involves regularly reviewing and updating one's sources of professional information. By pruning outdated or irrelevant materials, individuals can stay current with the latest developments in their field, improving their expertise and productivity.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> An Information Diet focuses on intrinsic cognitive load—essential for learning and understanding—while avoiding extraneous load that can hinder effective information processing. Intrinsic load is managed by selecting high-quality, relevant sources, whereas extraneous load is reduced by pruning low-value inputs.

## Key Figures

- **John Sweller** — John Sweller originated the concept of intrinsic vs. extraneous cognitive load in his work on cognitive architecture, providing a theoretical foundation for understanding how an Information Diet can enhance cognitive function.

## Synthesis

Understanding and applying an Information Diet is crucial for cognitive health and productivity because it helps individuals manage their information intake more effectively. By curating high-quality sources and pruning low-value inputs, the diet enhances associative recall and cognitive integration, leading to better learning outcomes and decision-making abilities.

The concept of an Information Diet intersects with other related concepts such as signal-to-noise ratio and source-curation, but it offers a more deliberate and intentional approach. By focusing on intrinsic cognitive load and domain-specific epistemic judgment, the diet provides a practical framework for optimizing information consumption in various domains.
