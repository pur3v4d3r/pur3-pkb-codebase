# Savoring

> [!definition] **Savoring**
> Savoring involves deliberately prolonging, intensifying, and attending to positive experiences as an active skill for enhancing positive emotions. It falls under [[Positive Emotion Regulation]], distinct from mindfulness which focuses on present-moment awareness without necessarily prolonging the experience, and gratitude, which is more about acknowledging past positive events rather than actively engaging with current ones.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> Savoring is distinct from mindfulness, which focuses on present-moment awareness without necessarily prolonging the experience. It also differs from gratitude, which is more about acknowledging past positive events rather than actively engaging with current ones.

## Core Explanation

Savoring is a deliberate cognitive and behavioral process that involves attending to, prolonging, and intensifying positive experiences. This active engagement with positivity helps individuals enhance their emotional well-being by treating positive emotion regulation as an intentional skill rather than merely the absence of negative-emotion regulation.

In practice, savoring can be applied in various scenarios such as enjoying a meal, appreciating a beautiful sunset, or reflecting on a successful day. By focusing on these experiences and prolonging them through vivid recollection and sensory engagement, individuals can heighten their positive emotions and maintain a more sustained sense of happiness.

Theoretical roots of savoring trace back to the work of John Sweller in 1988, who introduced the concept as part of his broader research on cognitive load theory. Savoring is seen as a way to manage intrinsic versus extraneous load, where intrinsic load refers to the natural difficulty of an experience, and extraneous load involves unnecessary mental effort that can detract from enjoyment.

Empirical evidence supports the idea that savoring counteracts hedonic adaptation by interrupting the automatic baseline-shift that erodes positive feelings. Deliberate attentional engagement with a positive experience appears to slow down this process, allowing individuals to maintain their positive emotions over time.

## Mechanism

Savoring works by interrupting the automatic baseline-shift that occurs when we become accustomed to positive experiences and start perceiving them as less enjoyable. By focusing on the present moment and prolonging these experiences through vivid recollection, individuals can maintain a heightened sense of positivity.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, savoring can be applied by creating learning environments that encourage students to reflect on and prolong positive moments. For example, instructors could use storytelling techniques to help students vividly recall and relive successful experiences, thereby enhancing their motivation and engagement.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Therapeutic settings**
> In therapeutic settings, savoring can be used as a tool for clients to process positive emotions. Therapists might guide clients through exercises that involve recalling and vividly re-experiencing positive moments from their lives, helping them build resilience against negative emotions.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Personal development**
> For personal development, individuals can practice savoring by setting aside time each day to reflect on and appreciate positive experiences. This could involve keeping a gratitude journal or engaging in mindfulness exercises that focus on prolonging positive moments throughout the day.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> Savoring is distinct from intrinsic and extraneous load, which are concepts within cognitive psychology. Intrinsic load refers to the natural difficulty of an experience, while extraneous load involves unnecessary mental effort that can detract from enjoyment. Savoring focuses on managing these loads by prolonging positive experiences through deliberate attentional engagement.

## Key Figures

- **John Sweller** — John Sweller is credited with the origin of savoring as part of his broader research on cognitive load theory in 1988. His work laid the foundation for understanding how deliberate attention can enhance positive emotional experiences.

## Synthesis

Understanding savoring is crucial for positive psychology as it provides a practical tool for enhancing well-being. By actively engaging with positive experiences, individuals can counteract hedonic adaptation and maintain a more sustained sense of happiness. Savoring also complements other concepts like mindfulness and gratitude by offering a specific strategy for prolonging positive emotions in the present moment.

The integration of savoring into various domains such as instructional design, therapeutic settings, and personal development highlights its broad applicability. Further research is needed to explore how savoring can be effectively integrated into daily routines and what long-term effects it might have.
