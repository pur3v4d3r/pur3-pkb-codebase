# Matter Antimatter Asymmetry

> [!definition] **Matter Antimatter Asymmetry**
> Matter Antimatter Asymmetry is the observed dominance of matter over antimatter in the present universe, quantified by the baryon-to-photon ratio η ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁰. This phenomenon excludes discussions on individual particle creation and annihilation processes, focusing instead on the imbalance at a cosmological scale. It falls under the broader domain of Cosmology.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes discussions on the creation and annihilation processes of individual particles and focuses solely on the imbalance between matter and antimatter at a cosmological scale.

## Core Explanation

Matter Antimatter Asymmetry is a fundamental aspect of our universe's composition, where matter significantly outweighs antimatter despite initial conditions suggesting symmetry. This asymmetry is crucial for understanding how galaxies and stars formed from the Big Bang. The concept challenges theoretical physicists to explain why this imbalance exists when the laws of physics should treat matter and antimatter equally.

Theoretical models suggest that during the early universe, a slight excess of matter over antimatter could have led to the observed dominance today. This requires mechanisms capable of generating baryon asymmetry from an initially symmetric state. The Standard Model provides some clues but falls short in explaining the full extent of this imbalance, necessitating beyond-the-Standard-Model physics.

Empirical evidence for Matter Antimatter Asymmetry comes from measurements of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) and big-bang nucleosynthesis abundances. These observations independently confirm the baryon-to-photon ratio η ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁰, providing a robust foundation for theoretical models to build upon.

Understanding Matter Antimatter Asymmetry is pivotal in cosmology as it addresses fundamental questions about the universe's origins and evolution. It also motivates searches for new physics beyond the Standard Model that could explain this asymmetry.

## Mechanism

To generate baryon asymmetry dynamically from a symmetric initial state, three Sakharov conditions must be met: baryon-number violation, C and CP violation, and departure from thermal equilibrium. The Standard Model satisfies these only marginally, with insufficient CP violation and an electroweak phase transition that is not strongly first-order.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Modeling the Early Universe**
> Understanding Matter Antimatter Asymmetry is crucial for developing accurate models of the early universe. These models must account for the observed baryon-to-photon ratio and predict how matter dominated over antimatter in the aftermath of the Big Bang.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Beyond-the-Standard-Model Physics**
> The inadequacy of the Standard Model to fully explain Matter Antimatter Asymmetry motivates searches for new physics. This includes exploring theories that introduce additional particles or interactions capable of generating sufficient CP violation and baryon-number violation.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Matter Antimatter Asymmetry vs Missing Antimatter**
> It is a common misconception to interpret Matter Antimatter Asymmetry as evidence for 'missing antimatter' elsewhere in the universe. However, deep searches for antimatter-dominated regions have placed stringent limits on their existence, indicating that there are no significant reservoirs of antimatter hiding in the visible universe.
