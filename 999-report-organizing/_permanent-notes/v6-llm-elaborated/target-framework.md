# TARGET Framework

> [!definition] **TARGET Framework**
> The TARGET Framework is a six-dimensional design tool developed by Carol Ames to configure classroom environments towards mastery or performance orientations, influencing students' goal orientations and learning outcomes. It falls under [[Educational Psychology]], focusing on the configuration of task, authority, recognition, grouping, evaluation, and time dimensions within classrooms without measuring orientation but providing guidelines for instructional design.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It focuses on the configuration of task, authority, recognition, grouping, evaluation, and time dimensions within classrooms. It does not measure orientation but provides guidelines for instructional design.

## Core Explanation

The TARGET Framework decomposes classroom climate into six key dimensions: Task, Authority, Recognition, Grouping, Evaluation, and Time. Each dimension can be configured to push the overall climate towards a mastery or performance orientation. For instance, tasks that are open-ended and challenging encourage mastery orientation, while those that focus on rote memorization promote performance orientation.

In practice, teachers must consider how these dimensions interact. For example, if Recognition is heavily focused on public ranking (a form of extrinsic motivation), it can override the effects of a mastery-oriented Task design. Similarly, Evaluation practices such as frequent low-stakes assessments can support both orientations but require careful calibration to avoid shifting towards performance orientation.

Theoretical roots of TARGET lie in goal orientation theory, which posits that students' goal orientations (mastery or performance) significantly impact their learning outcomes and motivation. Ames's empirical work on classroom-level climate effects established the importance of environmental contributions to orientation adoption, making TARGET a crucial tool for instructional designers.

Empirically, Ames’s research demonstrated that comprehensive reconfigurations of these dimensions reliably shift students' goal orientations towards mastery or performance. For example, in one study, classrooms configured with high levels of autonomy and intrinsic motivation (low extraneous load) showed significant improvements in student engagement and achievement.

## Mechanism

The mechanism by which TARGET influences classroom climate is through the interaction of its six dimensions. Changes in any dimension can affect students' goal orientations, but these effects are compositional. For instance, a mastery-oriented Task design can be undermined if Evaluation practices emphasize performance over learning.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> Teachers can use TARGET to design classrooms that promote mastery orientation by carefully configuring each dimension. For example, incorporating open-ended tasks and providing formative assessments can support a mastery climate, while avoiding public ranking systems and high-stakes testing can prevent the shift towards performance orientation.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Professional development**
> Professional development programs for teachers should include modules on TARGET to help them understand how to configure their classrooms. This knowledge enables teachers to create environments that foster intrinsic motivation and deep learning, leading to better student outcomes.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Mastery vs Performance Orientations**
> Mastery orientation is characterized by a focus on learning for the sake of understanding and personal growth, while performance orientation emphasizes demonstrating competence through grades or public recognition. TARGET helps in fostering a mastery climate by configuring dimensions to support deep learning and intrinsic motivation.

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> Intrinsic load refers to the inherent difficulty of tasks that students must overcome, while extraneous load includes unnecessary cognitive demands imposed by instructional design. TARGET helps in managing extraneous load by ensuring that task designs are not overly complex or distracting, thus supporting intrinsic motivation.

## Key Figures

- **Carol Ames** — Carol Ames is the originator of the TARGET Framework and has conducted extensive empirical work on classroom-level climate effects. Her research established the importance of environmental contributions to orientation adoption, making TARGET a standard reference for instructional designers.

## Synthesis

The significance of TARGET lies in its ability to translate goal orientation theory into actionable instructional practices. By providing a structured framework for configuring classroom environments, it offers educators a powerful tool to influence students' learning outcomes and motivation. The broader implications extend beyond education, as the principles transfer to workplace learning contexts where continuous professional development is crucial.

TARGET's impact on educational psychology is profound, as it bridges theoretical insights with practical applications. Its role in fostering mastery orientations aligns with broader goals of deep learning and student engagement, making it a cornerstone concept in the field.
