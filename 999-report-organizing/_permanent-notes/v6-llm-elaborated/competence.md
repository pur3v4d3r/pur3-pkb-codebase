# Competence

> [!definition] **Competence**
> Competence refers to the experience of being effective in one's environment, capable of producing desired outcomes, and growing in capability over time. It supports internalization by fostering a sense of personal efficacy and autonomy, falling under Self-Determination Theory.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> Competence is distinguished from objective competence (which may exist without subjective experience) and achievement (a possible outcome but not the substance).

## Core Explanation

Competence is fundamentally about experiencing effectiveness in one’s environment, which involves the ability to produce intended effects and grow in capability. This experience is distinct from objective competence, which may exist without subjective satisfaction, or achievement, a possible outcome but not the substance of need satisfaction.

In practice, competence manifests as the feeling that one can handle tasks effectively and improve over time. For instance, a student who feels they are mastering new skills in class experiences high levels of competence, which enhances their motivation to continue learning. This experience is crucial for internalization, moving from introjection (accepting external rules) to identification (embracing those rules as personally meaningful).

Theoretical roots of competence lie within Self-Determination Theory, where it is one of the three basic psychological needs alongside autonomy and relatedness. Competence specifically supports the development of identified regulation, which is a more stable form of motivation that persists even when faced with challenges.

Empirically, competence has been shown to be particularly important in educational settings. For example, students who feel competent are more likely to engage actively in class discussions and persist through difficult tasks, whereas those lacking this sense may disengage or give up easily.

## Mechanism

The process of how competence supports internalization involves a progression from introjection to identification. Initially, behaviors are accepted due to external pressure (introjection). Over time, as competence is experienced and grows, these behaviors become personally meaningful (identification), leading to more stable and autonomous motivation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, fostering competence can significantly enhance student engagement. By providing tasks that are appropriately challenging but achievable, educators can help students develop a sense of efficacy. This not only improves learning outcomes but also promotes long-term motivation and resilience.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Therapeutic interventions**
> In therapeutic settings, competence-building activities can be crucial for clients to feel empowered and capable in managing their challenges. Therapists can design tasks that gradually increase in difficulty, allowing clients to experience success and build confidence over time.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Competence vs Achievement**
> While competence is about the subjective experience of being effective, achievement refers to external outcomes or goals. For example, a student who feels competent in math may not necessarily achieve high grades if the grading system is flawed. Conversely, achieving high grades does not guarantee that a student feels competent.

## Key Figures

- **Edward L. Deci** — A key contributor to Self-Determination Theory, Edward L. Deci has extensively researched the role of competence in motivation and development, emphasizing its importance for internalization processes.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How does competence interact with other needs in different cultural contexts?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Cross-cultural studies examining how competence interacts with autonomy and relatedness across diverse populations could provide insights into these dynamics.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What are the long-term effects of chronic competence frustration?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Longitudinal research tracking individuals over extended periods, particularly those experiencing persistent feelings of incompetence, would help understand the lasting impacts on motivation and well-being.

## Synthesis

Competence is a critical component within Self-Determination Theory, supporting internalization by fostering personal efficacy. Its importance extends beyond education into therapeutic practices, where building competence can empower individuals to manage their challenges more effectively. Understanding and nurturing competence has broader implications for motivation and development across various domains, including work and social interactions.

By recognizing the unique role of competence in promoting stable and autonomous motivation, practitioners can design interventions that enhance internalization processes, leading to better outcomes and more resilient individuals.
