# Controlling Teaching Styles

> [!definition] **Controlling Teaching Styles**
> Controlling Teaching Styles are interpersonal patterns that thwart students' autonomy by establishing an external locus of causality for their learning behaviors, often through pressuring language or contingent rewards. It falls under Self-Determination Theory as it operates through a distinct causal pathway with its own need-frustration consequences, rather than being merely the absence of supportive structures.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes teaching styles that support autonomy and internal motivation. It is distinct from the absence of supportive structures but operates through a different causal pathway with unique consequences.

## Core Explanation

Controlling Teaching Styles are characterized by their focus on external locus of causality and the establishment of an authoritative control over students' learning behaviors. This is often achieved through pressuring language such as 'you must,' 'you should,' or contingent rewards that link performance to external validation, thereby undermining students' internal motivation.

In practice, these styles operate by creating a dependency on external factors for success and satisfaction in the learning process. For instance, a teacher might use evaluative comparisons to rank students, which can lead to feelings of inadequacy among those who are not at the top. This external focus shifts responsibility away from the student's own efforts and achievements, fostering a sense of helplessness and reducing their intrinsic motivation.

Theoretical roots of controlling teaching styles lie in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), particularly its emphasis on autonomy as one of three basic psychological needs. SDT posits that when these needs are thwarted, such as through the imposition of external controls, students' well-being and engagement suffer. The concept is distinct from the absence of supportive structures because it actively undermines internal motivation rather than simply failing to provide support.

Empirical evidence supports this view, showing that controlling teaching styles reliably degrade autonomous motivation, engagement quality, conceptual learning, and overall well-being. For example, a study by Deci et al. (1980) demonstrated that students who experienced pressuring language or contingent rewards showed lower levels of intrinsic motivation compared to those in autonomy-supportive environments.

## Mechanism

Controlling Teaching Styles operate through several mechanisms, including the use of evaluative comparisons and conditional rewards. Evaluative comparisons create a competitive environment where students are ranked against each other, leading to feelings of inadequacy among lower-ranked individuals. Conditional rewards, such as praise contingent on performance, can also undermine intrinsic motivation by linking success to external validation rather than personal achievement.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, controlling teaching styles might manifest through the use of rigid lesson plans and frequent testing. This approach can lead to superficial learning where students focus on memorizing facts for exams rather than understanding concepts deeply. Educators should instead adopt flexible, student-centered designs that encourage exploration and self-directed learning.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Classroom management**
> Controlling teaching styles in classroom management often involve strict rules and constant surveillance of student behavior. While this might maintain order temporarily, it can stifle creativity and critical thinking. Teachers should focus on creating a supportive environment where students feel safe to express themselves and take risks.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Student feedback**
> Providing feedback through pressuring language or contingent rewards can demotivate students by making them feel judged rather than supported. Instead, teachers should offer constructive, non-evaluative feedback that focuses on growth and improvement without linking it to external validation.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> Intrinsic load refers to the inherent difficulty of a task, while extraneous load is imposed by instructional design. Controlling teaching styles increase extraneous load by adding unnecessary complexity and pressure, whereas autonomy-supportive practices reduce it by making learning more manageable and meaningful.

## Key Figures

- **Edward Deci** — Co-originator of Self-Determination Theory (SDT) in 1980, along with Richard Ryan, who together developed the concept of controlling teaching styles as a distinct form of motivational practice.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How can educators balance control and autonomy?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Further research on effective strategies for integrating supportive structure without imposing external controls could provide insights into this question.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What are the long-term effects of controlling teaching styles on student motivation?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Longitudinal studies tracking students' motivational trajectories over time would help clarify these effects and inform best practices in education.

## Synthesis

Understanding controlling teaching styles is crucial for educators and researchers because it highlights the active role of instructional practices in shaping student motivation. By recognizing how external controls can undermine intrinsic motivation, teachers can adopt more supportive strategies that foster autonomy and well-being. This concept also intersects with other motivational theories such as need-frustration and perceived locus of causality, enriching our understanding of what constitutes effective teaching.

The implications extend beyond the classroom to broader educational policies and practices. By addressing controlling teaching styles, we can promote a more equitable and engaging learning environment that supports all students in achieving their full potential.
