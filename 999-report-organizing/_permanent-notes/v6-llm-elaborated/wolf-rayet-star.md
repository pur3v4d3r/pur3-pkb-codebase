# Wolf Rayet Star

> [!definition] **Wolf Rayet Star**
> A Wolf Rayet Star is a massive evolved star (initial mass ≳ 25 M☉) that has shed its outer hydrogen envelope through powerful stellar winds, exposing hot helium-burning or carbon-oxygen layers and producing characteristic broad emission lines. This concept excludes stars that have not yet reached the Wolf Rayet phase and those in later stages of evolution beyond this specific wind-driven mass loss stage. It falls under evolved massive stars.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes stars that have not yet reached the Wolf Rayet phase and those that are in later stages of evolution beyond this specific wind-driven mass loss stage. It should not be confused with main sequence stars, which retain their hydrogen envelopes.

## Core Explanation

Wolf Rayet stars represent a critical evolutionary phase for massive stars, marking their transition from hydrogen-burning main sequence to helium or carbon burning cores. These stars are characterized by intense stellar winds that strip away the outer layers of hydrogen, revealing deeper layers where heavier elements like helium and nitrogen (WN type) or carbon and oxygen (WC type) dominate. This process is driven by radiation pressure on metal lines in their atmospheres, which become increasingly important as the star evolves.

Theoretical models predict that Wolf Rayet stars are immediate progenitors of Type Ib/c supernovae, a class of stripped-envelope core-collapse events. Observations confirm this link, showing quantitative agreement between WR-star number densities and mass-loss rates with SN Ib/c rates, thus closing the late-life evolutionary cycle of massive stars at first-order accuracy.

Empirical studies have shown that Wolf Rayet stars are found in regions of active star formation, often associated with young stellar clusters. Their presence is a key indicator of recent or ongoing high-mass star formation and can be used to trace the history of star formation within galaxies.

## Mechanism

Wolf Rayet stars lose their hydrogen envelope through powerful stellar winds driven by radiation pressure on metal lines in their atmospheres. These winds are characterized by mass-loss rates of ~10⁻⁵ M☉/yr, which can strip away the outer layers of hydrogen and expose deeper helium-burning or carbon-oxygen layers.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Understanding Supernova Progenitors**
> Wolf Rayet stars are crucial in understanding supernova progenitors. Their intense stellar winds strip away the outer hydrogen envelope, leading to Type Ib/c supernovae when they eventually collapse. This direct link between WR stars and specific types of supernovae helps astronomers predict and observe these events.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Long-Duration Gamma-Ray Bursts**
> The study of Wolf Rayet stars also sheds light on the origins of long-duration gamma-ray bursts (GRBs). These high-energy phenomena are thought to be associated with the collapse of massive stars, and WR stars represent a key phase in this process. However, metallicity levels can significantly affect the evolution and properties of WR stars, complicating predictions about GRB progenitors.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Helium-Burning vs Carbon-Oxygen Layers Exposed**
> Wolf Rayet stars are distinguished by the layers they expose due to their powerful stellar winds. WN type WR stars reveal helium-burning layers, while WC type WR stars expose carbon-oxygen burning cores. This distinction is crucial for understanding the evolutionary path and final fate of these massive stars.

## Key Figures

- **Charles Wolf** — Wolf identified the first known Wolf Rayet star, which was later named after him and his colleague Georges Rayet. His work laid the foundation for understanding this class of evolved massive stars.
- **Georges Rayet** — Rayet collaborated with Charles Wolf to study unusual emission-line spectra in certain stars, leading to the identification of a new type of star now known as Wolf-Rayet stars. Their work has been fundamental in astrophysics for over a century.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How do metallicity levels affect the evolution and properties of Wolf Rayet stars?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Observational studies comparing WR star populations across galaxies with different metallicities would provide insights into how metallicity influences their mass-loss rates, lifetimes, and final fates.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What are the exact mechanisms that trigger the shedding of hydrogen envelopes in Wolf Rayet stars?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Detailed modeling of WR star atmospheres and winds could reveal the specific conditions under which radiation pressure becomes strong enough to strip away the outer layers, leading to a clearer understanding of their evolution.

## Synthesis

Wolf Rayet stars are pivotal in astrophysics for understanding the late stages of massive stellar evolution and their role as progenitors of supernovae and gamma-ray bursts. Their study bridges theoretical models with observational data, providing critical insights into cosmic phenomena such as star formation histories and nucleosynthesis processes.
