# Hypernova

> [!definition] **Hypernova**
> A Hypernova is an exceptionally energetic core-collapse supernova, typically releasing energy levels of at least 10⁴⁵ joules, which is significantly higher than ordinary core-collapse events. It falls under the broader category of Core-Collapse Supernovae but distinguishes itself through its association with the formation of a rapidly rotating black hole and often being linked to long-duration gamma-ray bursts via the collapsar mechanism.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> While all hypernovae are core-collapse supernovae, not all core-collapse supernovae reach the energy levels or produce the phenomena characteristic of hypernovae. Hypernovae should not be confused with ordinary supernovae that do not exhibit such extreme energies and gamma-ray bursts.

## Core Explanation

Hypernovae represent an extreme form of stellar explosion, characterized by their immense energy output and unique end-state involving the creation of a rapidly spinning black hole. This phenomenon is not just a theoretical construct but has been observed in various astronomical events, providing empirical evidence for its existence. The collapsar model proposed by MacFadyen & Woosley offers a compelling explanation for how these high-energy explosions occur: as a massive star's core collapses under gravity, it forms a black hole that can spin at relativistic speeds if the progenitor was sufficiently rotating. This rapid rotation powers jets of material that punch through the stellar envelope, producing both gamma-ray bursts and an associated broad-line Type Ic supernova.

The collapsar mechanism is not just a theoretical framework; it has been supported by observational data from hypernova events linked to long-duration gamma-ray bursts. These observations have helped refine our understanding of how black holes form in the universe and provided insights into the extreme conditions that exist within collapsing stars. The energy released during these events can be so intense that they outshine entire galaxies for brief periods, making them some of the most luminous phenomena known.

Understanding hypernovae is crucial not only for explaining their immediate observational signatures but also for unraveling broader questions about stellar evolution and black hole formation. While the collapsar model provides a robust theoretical foundation, there remains ongoing debate regarding specific progenitor masses and rotation rates that lead to these events. This uncertainty underscores the need for continued research into hypernova phenomena.

The study of hypernovae has significant implications for astrophysics beyond just understanding stellar explosions. By providing insights into the conditions necessary for black hole formation, hypernovae contribute to our broader knowledge of how galaxies evolve over cosmic time. Additionally, the association with gamma-ray bursts makes them a key area of interest in high-energy astrophysics.

## Mechanism

The collapsar mechanism describes how a rapidly rotating massive star's core collapse leads to the formation of a black hole and the subsequent production of long-duration gamma-ray bursts. As the core collapses, it forms a proto-neutron star that then implodes into a black hole if its mass exceeds the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff limit. The rapid rotation of this newly formed black hole can launch jets of material at relativistic speeds, which interact with the surrounding stellar envelope to produce both gamma-ray bursts and an associated supernova.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Observational Astronomy**
> In observational astronomy, identifying hypernovae is crucial for understanding their role in stellar evolution and black hole formation. Hypernovae are distinguished by their unusually high kinetic energy and broad-line features, which can be detected through spectroscopic analysis of the light emitted during these events. Ignoring these characteristics could lead to misclassification of supernova types and a misunderstanding of the underlying physics driving these extreme explosions.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Gamma-Ray Burst Research**
> For researchers studying gamma-ray bursts, hypernovae provide a key link between stellar collapse and high-energy phenomena. By focusing on events associated with long-duration gamma-ray bursts, scientists can gather data that helps refine models of black hole formation and the conditions necessary for producing such energetic jets. This research is essential for advancing our understanding of how these powerful cosmic explosions occur.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Hypernova vs Ordinary Supernova**
> The primary distinction between hypernovae and ordinary supernovae lies in their energy output and end-state. Hypernovae release significantly more energy, often on the order of 10⁴⁵ joules or higher, compared to typical core-collapse events. Additionally, they are associated with the formation of rapidly rotating black holes and long-duration gamma-ray bursts, whereas ordinary supernovae do not exhibit such extreme characteristics.

## Key Figures

- **MacFadyen & Woosley** — Proposed the collapsar mechanism that links hypernovae to long-duration gamma-ray bursts, providing a theoretical framework for understanding these high-energy stellar explosions and their role in black hole formation.

## Synthesis

Understanding hypernovae is crucial for advancing astrophysics because they offer a window into some of the most energetic and complex processes in the universe. By studying these events, researchers can gain deeper insights into stellar evolution, black hole formation, and the mechanisms behind gamma-ray bursts. This knowledge not only enriches our understanding of individual stars but also contributes to broader questions about galaxy evolution and the distribution of matter and energy across cosmic scales.
