# Baryogenesis

> [!definition] **Baryogenesis**
> Baryogenesis is a fundamental process in cosmology that explains how matter came to dominate antimatter in the universe's early stages, despite initial symmetry. This concept falls under the broader domain of physics and specifically addresses the generation of cosmic matter-antimatter asymmetry through mechanisms beyond the Standard Model.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes direct laboratory testing at its energy scales and should not be confused with other cosmological processes or phenomena that do not specifically address baryon asymmetry generation.

## Core Explanation

Baryogenesis is a critical unsolved problem in fundamental physics that seeks to explain why our universe contains more matter than antimatter. This imbalance, known as baryon asymmetry, is crucial for understanding the composition of the cosmos and its evolution since the Big Bang. The process requires mechanisms that satisfy the Sakharov conditions: baryon number violation, CP (charge-parity) symmetry breaking, and departure from thermal equilibrium. These conditions are necessary but not sufficient to explain the observed matter-antimatter asymmetry.

The Standard Model of particle physics, while successful in many respects, is inadequate for explaining baryogenesis due to its inherent symmetries that prevent the generation of a net baryon number. Therefore, any viable mechanism must involve beyond-the-Standard-Model physics. The leading candidate among these mechanisms is leptogenesis, which posits that the asymmetry was first generated in the lepton sector through the decays of heavy Majorana neutrinos. This process ties baryogenesis to neutrino mass physics and provides a framework for indirect experimental probes.

The challenge in studying baryogenesis lies in its energy scales, typically ranging from 10⁹ to 10¹⁵ GeV, far beyond the reach of current laboratory experiments. Consequently, researchers must rely on indirect methods such as precision measurements within the Standard Model and searches for new physics signatures like neutrinoless double-beta decay or electric dipole moments.

Despite significant theoretical progress, baryogenesis remains an open question in cosmology. The exact mechanisms and energy scales involved are still under debate, highlighting the need for further research to unravel this fundamental aspect of our universe.

## Mechanism

Electroweak baryogenesis occurs during or shortly after the electroweak phase transition at around 100 GeV. This mechanism relies on the breaking of CP symmetry in the Higgs sector, leading to a net baryon number production through out-of-equilibrium conditions.

GUT (Grand Unified Theory) baryogenesis proposes that baryon asymmetry is generated during or after GUT-scale phase transitions at around 10¹⁵ GeV. This mechanism involves spontaneous symmetry breaking and subsequent baryon number violation in the early universe.

Leptogenesis, a widely studied scenario, suggests that the matter-antimatter imbalance was first established in the lepton sector through the decays of heavy Majorana neutrinos. The asymmetry is then converted into a baryon asymmetry via sphalerons, hypothetical topological defects in the electroweak theory.

The Affleck-Dine mechanism proposes that the matter-antimatter imbalance arises from quantum fluctuations during or after inflation. These fluctuations can lead to a coherent condensate of scalar fields with a net baryon number.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Precision Measurements**
> Indirect experimental probes of Baryogenesis rely heavily on precision measurements within the Standard Model, such as those involving electroweak parameters. These measurements can reveal deviations from expected values that hint at new physics beyond the Standard Model, potentially providing evidence for mechanisms like leptogenesis.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Neutrinoless Double-Beta Decay**
> The search for neutrinoless double-beta decay is a key experimental probe of Baryogenesis. If observed, it would imply that neutrinos are their own antiparticles (Majorana particles), which could be indicative of the leptonic sector's role in generating baryon asymmetry.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Electric Dipole Moments**
> The measurement of electric dipole moments for elementary particles, such as electrons or neutrons, can provide indirect evidence for CP violation beyond the Standard Model. Such measurements could point to new physics that is necessary for generating baryon asymmetry.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Electroweak Baryogenesis vs GUT Baryogenesis**
> These mechanisms differ in their energy scales and theoretical frameworks. Electroweak baryogenesis operates at the electroweak scale (around 100 GeV), while GUT baryogenesis occurs at much higher energies (around 10¹⁵ GeV). Each mechanism requires distinct conditions for CP violation and baryon number violation, reflecting different phases of the early universe.

> [!key-distinction] **Leptogenesis vs Affleck-Dine Mechanism**
> Both leptogenesis and the Affleck-Dine mechanism propose ways to generate baryon asymmetry through processes involving scalar fields. However, they differ in their origins: leptogenesis relies on heavy Majorana neutrinos decaying out of equilibrium, whereas the Affleck-Dine mechanism involves quantum fluctuations during or after inflation.

## Synthesis

Baryogenesis is a critical unsolved problem that bridges fundamental physics with cosmology. Understanding how matter came to dominate antimatter in the early universe not only sheds light on the composition of our cosmos but also provides insights into the underlying principles governing particle interactions at high energies. The quest for mechanisms beyond the Standard Model continues, driven by indirect experimental probes and theoretical advancements.
