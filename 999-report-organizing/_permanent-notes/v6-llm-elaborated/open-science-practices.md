# Open Science Practices

> [!definition] **Open Science Practices**
> Open Science Practices are a cluster of methodological reforms — including preregistration, registered reports, open data, and others — aimed at enhancing the credibility and reproducibility of scientific research by constraining researcher degrees of freedom. It falls under [[Research Methodology]].

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes individual practices not part of a coordinated set of reforms designed to address issues in the scientific literature, such as p-hacking or selective reporting alone.

## Core Explanation

Open Science Practices are a set of methodological reforms designed to address issues in the scientific literature, such as selective reporting and p-hacking. These practices include preregistration, where researchers commit to their research design before data collection begins, reducing the opportunity for bias. Registered reports, another key component, involve peer review based on the proposed methodology rather than preliminary results, ensuring that the study's integrity is maintained from inception.

In practice, these reforms operate by setting clear guidelines and expectations for how research should be conducted and reported. For instance, preregistration requires researchers to specify their hypotheses, data analysis plans, and criteria for statistical significance before collecting any data. This process constrains researcher degrees of freedom, making it more difficult to manipulate results post hoc.

Theoretical roots of Open Science Practices can be traced back to the replication crisis in science, which highlighted significant issues with reproducibility and replicability across various fields. These practices aim to align the published record more closely with empirical reality by reducing selective reporting and other forms of bias. The concept also draws on meta-science, a field that seeks to improve research methods and reproducibility through systematic analysis.

Empirically, studies have shown that registered-reports show a dramatically lower rate of statistically significant 'positive' findings compared to conventional studies. This suggests that the conventional literature's success rate is driven less by veridical signal than by selective reporting and analytic flexibility, which Open Science Practices are specifically designed to remove.

Open Science Practices also emphasize transparency in data analysis and reporting, which is crucial for ensuring that research findings can be independently verified by other scientists. This includes making raw data, analysis scripts, and detailed methodologies publicly available, allowing others to replicate the study's results and assess its validity.

Moreover, these practices foster a culture of collaboration and openness within the scientific community. By sharing their work openly from the outset, researchers encourage peer scrutiny and feedback, which can lead to more robust and reliable research outcomes.

## Mechanism

Preregistration works by requiring researchers to commit to their research design before data collection begins. This process constrains researcher degrees of freedom, making it more difficult to manipulate results post hoc. Registered reports involve peer review based on the proposed methodology rather than preliminary results, ensuring that the study's integrity is maintained from inception.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, Open Science Practices can lead to more robust and replicable educational interventions. By preregistering their designs and using registered reports for peer review, educators ensure that their methods are transparent and free from bias, leading to better-informed pedagogical strategies.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Clinical trials**
> In clinical trials, these practices can enhance the reliability of drug efficacy studies. Preregistration ensures that trial designs are robust and unbiased, while registered reports provide a rigorous peer review process before data collection begins, reducing the risk of selective reporting and increasing confidence in the results.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Academic publishing**
> In academic publishing, Open Science Practices can lead to more credible research outputs. By requiring open access to data, materials, and code, researchers ensure that their findings are transparent and verifiable, fostering a culture of trust and reproducibility within the scientific community.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Preregistration vs Selective Reporting**
> Preregistration requires researchers to commit to their research design before data collection begins, constraining researcher degrees of freedom. In contrast, selective reporting involves choosing which results to report based on statistical significance, leading to biased and unreliable findings.

> [!key-distinction] **Open Data vs Closed Data**
> Open data refers to the practice of making raw data freely available for independent reanalysis, while closed data is restricted or not made accessible. Open data enhances reproducibility and allows others to verify results, whereas closed data can lead to unverifiable findings.

<!-- enhancement-pass:1 (2026-05-02) -->

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Motivation in Adopting Open Science Practices**
> Researchers may adopt open science practices for intrinsic reasons such as a genuine commitment to scientific integrity or extrinsic motivations like career advancement. Intrinsic motivation is linked to personal values and can lead to sustained engagement with these practices, whereas extrinsic motivation might wane if external incentives diminish.

> [!key-distinction] **Reflective vs Reactive Thinking in Implementing Open Science Practices**
> Implementing open science practices requires reflective thinking where researchers plan their studies meticulously before data collection. This contrasts with reactive thinking, which involves making decisions based on immediate feedback or results, potentially leading to biases and selective reporting.

## Common Misconceptions

> [!warning] **Misconception** — Open Science Practices are only about sharing data.
>
> While open data is a key component of these practices, they encompass a broader set of reforms including preregistration, registered reports, and transparent reporting. These measures collectively aim to enhance the credibility and reproducibility of scientific research by constraining researcher degrees of freedom.

## Synthesis

Open Science Practices are crucial for advancing scientific knowledge by addressing issues such as selective reporting and enhancing reproducibility. By constraining researcher degrees of freedom, these practices improve the credibility and reliability of research findings. They align with broader efforts in meta-science to improve research methods and foster a culture of transparency and trust within the scientific community.

These practices also play a key role in addressing the replication crisis by ensuring that studies are designed and conducted in ways that can be independently verified. By promoting open access to data, materials, and code, they facilitate collaboration and reproducibility across different research domains.
