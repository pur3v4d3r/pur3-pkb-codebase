# Metaphor and Cognition

> [!definition] **Metaphor and Cognition**
> Metaphor and Cognition is the theory that metaphors are not just linguistic devices but fundamental cognitive operations through which we structure our reasoning about abstract concepts using more concrete ones. It falls under [[Cognitive Architecture]], as it posits that these mappings from concrete to abstract domains are central to how we think, reason, and make decisions.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes metaphorical language as a mere stylistic choice, focusing instead on the underlying cognitive processes of mapping between domains.

## Core Explanation

At the heart of Metaphor and Cognition lies the idea that our understanding of complex concepts like time, argumentation, or morality is deeply intertwined with more tangible experiences. For instance, when we say 'time is running out,' we are not merely using a figure of speech; rather, this metaphor reflects an underlying cognitive mapping from the concrete domain of space (where things can run out) to the abstract concept of time. This mapping allows us to reason about time in terms of spatial concepts, which can influence our judgments and decisions.

This theory operates by systematically structuring abstract domains through mappings from more concrete ones. For example, when we argue that 'we attacked his argument,' we are not just using a metaphor; the underlying cognitive process involves mapping the domain of war (where attacks occur) onto the domain of argumentation to reason about the strength or weakness of an argument. This mapping is not merely linguistic but deeply embedded in our cognitive architecture, influencing how we think and reason.

The theoretical roots of Metaphor and Cognition can be traced back to George Lakoff and Mark Johnson's work on Conceptual Metaphor Theory. They argue that these mappings are pervasive and fundamental, shaping our understanding of abstract concepts through the lens of more concrete experiences. This theory has found empirical support in priming studies, where subtle manipulations of metaphors have been shown to measurably shift judgments about crime policy, economic decisions, and moral reasoning.

Empirical evidence supports this theory by demonstrating that even when no explicit metaphorical language is present, the underlying cognitive mappings can still influence our reasoning. For instance, a study might show that participants who are primed with spatial metaphors (like 'time is running out') tend to make different judgments about time-related tasks compared to those not exposed to such metaphors.

## Mechanism

The process of analogical mapping in Metaphor and Cognition involves several stages. First, a concrete source domain (e.g., space) is identified as the basis for understanding an abstract target domain (e.g., time). Second, specific mappings are established between elements of these domains (e.g., 'running out' from space to 'ending' in time). Third, the inferential structure of the source domain is imported into reasoning about the target domain. Finally, this mapping influences our judgments and decisions by providing a familiar framework for understanding abstract concepts.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, Metaphor and Cognition suggests that using concrete metaphors can enhance learning. For example, explaining complex mathematical concepts through spatial metaphors (like 'the number line') can help students better understand abstract ideas by mapping them onto familiar spatial experiences.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Economic decisions**
> In economic decision-making, Metaphor and Cognition implies that framing financial choices in terms of concrete domains like war or health (e.g., 'investing in the economy is a battle') can influence how individuals perceive risks and rewards. This can lead to different investment strategies based on these metaphorical mappings.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Moral reasoning**
> In moral reasoning, Metaphor and Cognition suggests that metaphors like 'cleanliness' or 'purity' can shape judgments about ethical issues. For instance, describing a political candidate as 'dirty' can influence perceptions of their integrity, even if the metaphor is not explicitly used in discourse.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Metaphorical language vs. underlying cognitive mappings**
> While metaphorical language and the underlying cognitive mappings are closely related, they are distinct. Metaphorical language refers to the surface-level use of metaphors in communication, whereas the cognitive mappings refer to the deeper, systematic ways these metaphors structure our reasoning. Understanding this distinction is crucial for grasping how metaphors influence thought and decision-making.

## Key Figures

- **George Lakoff** — Co-originator of Conceptual Metaphor Theory, George Lakoff has been instrumental in developing the foundational ideas that underpin Metaphor and Cognition. His work emphasizes the pervasive role of metaphors in shaping our cognitive architecture.
- **Mark Johnson** — Co-originator with Lakoff, Mark Johnson's contributions include exploring how metaphorical mappings are not just linguistic but deeply embedded in our cognitive processes, influencing reasoning and decision-making across various domains.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What are the limits of metaphorical priming effects in shaping judgments?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Further research is needed to determine under what conditions and to what extent subtle metaphors can reliably shift judgments. Controlled experiments that manipulate the strength and type of metaphors could help clarify these limits.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How can we better understand and measure the cognitive processes involved in metaphorical reasoning?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Advancements in neuroimaging techniques and cognitive modeling could provide deeper insights into how metaphors are processed in the brain, allowing for more precise measurement of their effects on reasoning.

## Synthesis

Metaphor and Cognition is a crucial concept that bridges linguistic theory with cognitive science. By understanding how abstract concepts are structured through metaphorical mappings from concrete domains, we can gain insights into the nature of human thought and decision-making. This theory has implications across various fields, including education, economics, and ethics, by highlighting the pervasive influence of metaphors on our reasoning processes.

Moreover, Metaphor and Cognition connects to broader theories like Conceptual Blending and Embodied Cognition, enriching our understanding of how we process information and make decisions. By recognizing the role of metaphorical mappings in shaping our cognitive architecture, we can develop more effective strategies for communication, education, and policy-making.

## Evidence

Empirical evidence supports Metaphor and Cognition by demonstrating that subtle manipulations of metaphors can measurably shift judgments. For instance, studies have shown that priming participants with spatial metaphors (like 'time is running out') influences their time-related decisions differently compared to those not exposed to such metaphors.
