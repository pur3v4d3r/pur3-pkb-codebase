# Prior Knowledge Activation

> [!definition] **Prior Knowledge Activation**
> Prior Knowledge Activation involves instructional strategies that bring relevant existing knowledge into working memory before new content is introduced, enhancing comprehension and retention. It falls under [[Cognitive Architecture]], focusing on the activation of relevant background knowledge to support encoding and understanding.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes the activation of irrelevant or incorrect prior knowledge, which can hinder learning. It also does not encompass all forms of pre-reading activities but focuses on those specifically designed to activate relevant background knowledge.

## Core Explanation

Prior Knowledge Activation enhances learning by preparing students with relevant information from their prior experiences or knowledge base. This strategy ensures that new content is anchored in existing cognitive structures, making it easier for learners to integrate and retain new information. For instance, advance organizers like KWL charts (What I Know, What I Want to Know, What I Learned) help students identify what they already know about a topic before delving into new material.

The effectiveness of Prior Knowledge Activation is particularly pronounced when the activated knowledge is relevant and correct. According to the source report, this strategy reliably improves comprehension and retention, especially for challenging texts that are outside the learner's comfort zone. However, it can backfire if incorrect prior knowledge is activated without subsequent revision, as an unprompted brainstorming session might entrench misconceptions.

Theoretical roots of Prior Knowledge Activation trace back to cognitive load theory, which posits that working memory has limited capacity and that activating relevant prior knowledge reduces the intrinsic load by providing a framework for new information. This aligns with John Sweller's work in 1988, where he emphasized the importance of reducing extraneous cognitive load through instructional design.

Empirical evidence supports the benefits of Prior Knowledge Activation. For example, studies have shown that using advance organizers like KWL charts can significantly enhance reading comprehension and retention, particularly for complex texts. This is because these tools help students make connections between new information and their existing knowledge, facilitating deeper understanding.

## Mechanism

Prior Knowledge Activation works by engaging learners in activities that elicit relevant prior knowledge before introducing new content. For instance, advance organizers such as KWL charts prompt students to think about what they already know (What I Know), what they want to learn (What I Want to Know), and what they have learned after the lesson (What I Learned). This process helps activate existing knowledge structures in working memory, making it easier for new information to be integrated.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, Prior Knowledge Activation can significantly improve learning outcomes by ensuring that students are well-prepared before engaging with new material. For example, using KWL charts at the beginning of a lesson helps students identify their prior knowledge and set clear learning goals, which enhances engagement and retention.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Classroom management**
> In classroom settings, Prior Knowledge Activation can be used to manage student behavior by engaging them in meaningful activities that activate relevant background knowledge. This not only improves learning but also helps maintain a positive classroom environment where students are more likely to stay focused and motivated.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Online education**
> In online education, Prior Knowledge Activation can be particularly effective due to the lack of face-to-face interaction. Using interactive tools like KWL charts or pre-questions in digital platforms helps students prepare for new content, making remote learning more engaging and effective.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> Prior Knowledge Activation is distinct from strategies that focus on reducing extraneous cognitive load. While both aim to optimize working memory capacity, Prior Knowledge Activation specifically targets the activation of relevant prior knowledge to enhance intrinsic processing. In contrast, strategies like worked examples primarily reduce the cognitive burden by providing step-by-step solutions.

## Key Figures

- **John Sweller** — John Sweller is credited with originating the concept of Prior Knowledge Activation in his work on cognitive load theory. His research highlighted the importance of reducing extraneous cognitive load through instructional design, which laid the foundation for strategies like advance organizers.

## Synthesis

Prior Knowledge Activation is a critical component in instructional design because it leverages existing cognitive structures to enhance learning. By activating relevant prior knowledge before introducing new content, this strategy supports deeper understanding and better retention. It aligns with broader principles of cognitive architecture and has empirical support from various studies. Integrating Prior Knowledge Activation into educational practices can significantly improve learning outcomes across different domains, making it a valuable tool for educators.
