# Propositional Knowledge

> [!definition] **Propositional Knowledge**
> Propositional Knowledge is knowledge of facts expressible in declarative sentences that can be evaluated for truth and justified through articulated reasoning. It falls under the broader domain of Epistemology but excludes forms such as skill (knowledge-how) or direct familiarity (knowledge-by-acquaintance), which cannot be fully reconstructed propositionally without significant loss of meaning.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It excludes forms of knowledge such as skill (knowledge-how) and direct familiarity (knowledge-by-acquaintance), which are not easily reconstructed propositionally without significant loss of meaning or detail.

## Core Explanation

Propositional Knowledge is a central concept in epistemological inquiry, focusing on the type of knowledge that can be articulated and justified through reasoning. This form of knowledge is distinct from other types such as skill or perceptual familiarity because it involves declarative sentences that can be evaluated for truth. The analysis of propositional knowledge has been a cornerstone of philosophical investigation into what constitutes knowledge, with its roots in classical theories like the justified true belief model.

In practice, propositional knowledge operates through cognitive processes where individuals acquire, store, and retrieve information that is expressible as declarative sentences. This contrasts sharply with non-propositional forms such as skill or perceptual familiarity, which are often more intuitive and less easily articulated. The theoretical roots of this concept lie in the tradition of analyzing knowledge as justified true belief, a framework that has been extensively debated within epistemology.

The distinction between propositional and other forms of knowledge is crucial because it highlights the limitations of focusing solely on declarative information when considering human understanding. While propositional knowledge is essential for many cognitive tasks, treating it as exhaustive can lead to significant oversights in educational practices and theories of mind.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, focusing on propositional knowledge means tailoring educational materials to facilitate the acquisition, storage, and retrieval of declarative information. This approach can enhance learning outcomes by ensuring that students are able to articulate and justify their understanding through reasoning. However, it is important to balance this with other forms of knowledge such as skill development and perceptual familiarity to provide a more comprehensive education.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Curriculum planning**
> When planning curricula, educators must consider the implications of focusing on propositional knowledge. While it is crucial for developing critical thinking skills, an exclusive focus can neglect other important aspects of learning such as practical application and experiential understanding. A balanced approach that integrates various forms of knowledge ensures a more holistic educational experience.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Propositional vs Non-propositional Knowledge**
> The distinction between propositional and non-propositional knowledge is fundamental in epistemology. Propositional knowledge involves facts expressible as declarative sentences, evaluable for truth, and amenable to articulated justification. In contrast, non-propositional forms such as skill or perceptual familiarity are more intuitive and less easily reconstructed propositionally without significant loss of meaning.

## Key Figures

- **John Sweller** — Sweller's work on cognitive load theory has implications for understanding how propositional knowledge is acquired, stored, and retrieved. His research highlights the importance of balancing intrinsic cognitive load (related to the inherent complexity of learning materials) with extraneous load (unnecessary cognitive effort imposed by instructional design).
