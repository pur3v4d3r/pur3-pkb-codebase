# Cognitive Load Theory

> [!definition] **Cognitive Load Theory**
> Cognitive Load Theory (CLT) is a framework that explains how the human cognitive system processes information during learning tasks, focusing on intrinsic, extraneous, and germane loads. It falls under [[Cognitive Architecture]], building upon the concept of working memory to explain cognitive processing.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> The theory stops at its core constructs and does not include broader educational strategies or specific teaching methods. It should not be confused with other cognitive theories like Dual-Process Theory.

## Core Explanation

At its core, CLT distinguishes between three types of load: intrinsic, which arises from the inherent difficulty of a task; extraneous, which is imposed by instructional design and can hinder learning; and germane, which involves the mental effort required to construct schemas or knowledge structures. These loads interact in complex ways during problem-solving, with excessive extraneous load potentially overwhelming working memory.

In practice, these loads affect how learners process information. For instance, a task that is too difficult (high intrinsic load) can lead to cognitive overload if not scaffolded properly. Conversely, poorly designed instruction (high extraneous load) can distract from the learning objectives, while germane load helps in building long-term knowledge.

The theory's roots lie in Sweller’s 1988 work on problem-solving and goal-free problems, which laid the groundwork for understanding how different types of cognitive loads impact learning. The introduction of intrinsic, extraneous, and germane loads in the 1990s further refined these concepts to better explain observed phenomena.

Empirically, CLT has been supported by studies showing that worked examples can reduce cognitive load compared to problem-solving alone, leading to better retention and transfer of knowledge. The theory also aligns with evolutionary educational psychology, suggesting that primary biological knowledge is more easily processed than secondary knowledge.

## Mechanism

CLT operates through the interaction between working memory and long-term memory. Working memory has a limited capacity, which can be overwhelmed by high cognitive load, leading to poorer learning outcomes. By reducing extraneous load and optimizing germane load, instructional design can enhance the efficiency of information processing.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, CLT suggests that using worked examples instead of problem-solving tasks can reduce cognitive load. This approach allows learners to focus on understanding the underlying principles rather than struggling with the task itself.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Multimedia learning principles**
> According to CLT, multimedia learning should be designed to minimize extraneous load by avoiding unnecessary text and images that do not contribute to the learning objectives. This ensures that germane load is maximized for effective knowledge construction.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> Intrinsic load refers to the inherent difficulty of a task, while extraneous load is imposed by instructional design. Intrinsic load cannot be reduced but can be managed through scaffolding and prior knowledge. Excessive extraneous load can hinder learning, whereas well-designed instruction can minimize it.

## Key Figures

- **John Sweller** — Sweller is the originator of CLT, introducing the theory in his seminal work from 1988. His research laid the foundation for understanding how different types of cognitive loads impact learning.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What is the best way to measure cognitive load?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Developing reliable and valid measures of cognitive load would help in better understanding its effects on learning and in refining instructional strategies.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How does germane load contribute to long-term learning?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Further research is needed to determine the extent to which germane load aids in the construction of schemas and how it can be optimized for better long-term retention.

## Synthesis

Cognitive Load Theory matters because it provides a robust framework for understanding cognitive processing during learning. By differentiating between intrinsic, extraneous, and germane loads, educators can design more effective instructional strategies that enhance learning outcomes. This theory also aligns with broader educational goals of optimizing working memory capacity and promoting long-term knowledge retention.

Moreover, CLT's integration with multimedia learning principles and its alignment with evolutionary educational psychology underscores its relevance across various domains of educational research and practice.
