# Red Giant

> [!definition] **Red Giant**
> A Red Giant is a low- to intermediate-mass star (≲ 8 M☉) that has exhausted its core hydrogen and entered the phase characterized by a contracted helium core surrounded by a hydrogen-burning shell, an expanded outer envelope, and a position on the red-giant branch in the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram. It falls under post-main-sequence stellar phases, distinct from the asymptotic giant branch (AGB) phase which follows core-helium burning.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> The concept of Red Giant should not be confused with the asymptotic giant branch (AGB) phase which follows core-helium burning. The two phases differ in interior structure, surface chemistry, and observational signatures.

## Core Explanation

Red Giants are stars that have transitioned from their main sequence phase after exhausting hydrogen in their cores. This exhaustion triggers a series of changes within the star's structure and chemistry, marking the beginning of its red giant branch (RGB) evolution. During this phase, the core contracts due to gravitational forces while the outer layers expand significantly, leading to a cooler surface temperature and redder coloration. The expansion is driven by hydrogen burning in a shell around the helium core, which provides the energy necessary for the star's luminosity.

The transition into the Red Giant phase involves significant changes in stellar chemistry due to dredge-up processes that bring nuclear-processed material from deeper layers to the surface. This process alters the surface abundances of elements such as carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen (CNO cycle products), along with lithium, which is destroyed at high temperatures but can be brought back to the surface through mixing events. These changes in surface chemistry are crucial for understanding stellar evolution and provide valuable information about a star's history.

Asteroseismology, the study of oscillations within stars, has become an invaluable tool for studying Red Giants. By analyzing these oscillations, scientists can determine precise ages for individual stars, which is critical for Galactic-archaeology surveys that aim to reconstruct the formation and evolution of our galaxy over billions of years.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Galactic Archaeology**
> Asteroseismology of red giants provides precise individual-star age determinations, which are crucial for Galactic-archaeology surveys. By studying the oscillation patterns in Red Giants, researchers can infer the star's internal structure and age with high accuracy. This information helps to map out the history of our galaxy by tracing back the formation and evolution of different stellar populations over time.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Red Giant vs Asymptotic Giant Branch (AGB)**
> While both Red Giants and AGB stars are post-main-sequence, they differ significantly in their interior structure, surface chemistry, and observational signatures. Red Giants have a helium core surrounded by a hydrogen-burning shell, whereas AGB stars undergo more complex nuclear processes involving the s-process and dust formation. These differences make it essential to distinguish between the two phases for accurate stellar evolution studies.

Understanding Red Giants is crucial for astrophysics as it provides insights into stellar evolution and the history of our galaxy. By studying these stars, researchers can trace back the formation and evolution of different stellar populations over billions of years, contributing to a comprehensive understanding of galactic archaeology.
