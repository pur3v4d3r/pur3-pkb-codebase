# Coherence Theory Of Truth

> [!definition] **Coherence Theory Of Truth**
> The Coherence Theory Of Truth posits that a proposition's truth is determined by its coherence within a system of interconnected propositions, emphasizing logical compatibility and mutual support among beliefs without reference to an external reality. It falls under Theories of Truth as it offers a distinct approach to understanding the nature of truth.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This theory excludes any notion of correspondence between propositions and an external reality, focusing solely on internal consistency among beliefs or statements.

## Core Explanation

At its core, Coherence Theory Of Truth relocates the concept of truth from correspondence with an external world to internal consistency within a system of beliefs or propositions. This theory asserts that for a proposition to be true, it must cohere with other propositions in such a way that they form a maximally coherent whole. The coherence among these propositions is not merely coincidental but rather a structured and interdependent relationship where each proposition supports the others.

In practice, this means that truth is assessed based on how well a statement fits into an existing framework of beliefs or knowledge. This approach allows for a more accessible understanding of truth within epistemological frameworks since it does not require direct verification against external reality. However, critics argue that this internal coherence can lead to multiple coherent but mutually exclusive systems of belief, each claiming to be true.

The theoretical roots of Coherence Theory Of Truth trace back to ancient philosophers like Plato and Aristotle, who emphasized the interconnectedness of knowledge. Modern proponents include thinkers such as Hegel and Dewey, who further developed these ideas into more systematic frameworks for understanding truth. The theory's nuances lie in its ability to provide a framework for evaluating beliefs based on their internal consistency rather than external validation.

Despite its theoretical appeal, Coherence Theory Of Truth faces significant challenges, particularly the plurality objection. This objection highlights that multiple coherent systems can exist without any clear way of determining which one is true or more accurate. This challenge underscores the theory's reliance on supplementary criteria to resolve conflicts between competing coherent systems.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, Coherence Theory Of Truth can guide educators in structuring curricula that emphasize the interconnectedness of knowledge. By presenting information as a coherent system where each piece supports and is supported by others, learners are more likely to integrate new concepts into their existing frameworks of understanding. This approach contrasts with fragmented teaching methods that may not highlight these connections.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Legal reasoning**
> In legal reasoning, Coherence Theory Of Truth can be applied to assess the consistency and coherence of legal arguments within a broader framework of laws and precedents. A coherent argument is one that aligns with established principles and supports other related legal doctrines, enhancing its validity within the system.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Coherent vs Correspondent Truth**
> The distinction between Coherent and Correspondent truth lies in their differing approaches to validating propositions. While Coherence Theory Of Truth relies on internal consistency among beliefs, Correspondence Theory asserts that truth is determined by the alignment of statements with an external reality. This difference impacts how one evaluates the validity of claims within each framework.

## Key Figures

- **John Dewey** — Dewey contributed significantly to Coherence Theory Of Truth through his emphasis on experiential learning and the interconnectedness of knowledge. He argued that truth is a process of inquiry and verification within a coherent system of beliefs.

>
> *What would resolve it:* Identifying supplementary criteria that can distinguish between competing coherent systems without reverting to correspondence would address this issue.
