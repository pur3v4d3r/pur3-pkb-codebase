# Relatedness

> [!definition] **Relatedness**
> Relatedness is the experience of being meaningfully connected to others, caring and being cared for, and belonging to a relational matrix. It supports the developmental work of moving from external regulation to introjection and provides the relational context within which all subsequent internalization occurs. It falls under Self-Determination Theory (SDT), where it is distinguished from mere social contact or instrumental relationships.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes mere social contact or instrumental relationships; it focuses on meaningful connections that support psychological development.

## Core Explanation

At its core, relatedness involves a deep sense of connection with others that goes beyond superficial interactions. This need is particularly crucial in the context of SDT, as it forms the relational foundation upon which other psychological needs like autonomy and competence are built. Relatedness ensures that individuals feel valued and supported by their social environment, fostering a secure base from which they can explore and grow.

In practice, relatedness manifests in various ways, such as through meaningful relationships with teachers, peers, or family members. For instance, consider a patient with type 2 diabetes whose physician has prescribed a daily blood glucose monitoring routine. The behaviors are extrinsic (none of them is performed for its own sake; all serve the outcome of disease management) and the prescription is, at the moment of receipt, externally regulated. Whether and how this regulation internalizes depends on the need-satisfaction conditions around these behaviors: does the patient feel cared about as a person in their treatment relationship? This sense of being cared for is central to relatedness.

Theoretical roots of relatedness can be traced back to SDT's emphasis on the importance of social contexts in psychological development. The three basic needs—autonomy, competence, and relatedness—are proposed not arbitrarily but based on specific developmental work each does in the internalization mechanism. Relatedness is particularly important for the initial taking-in of behaviors, ensuring that individuals feel supported and valued before they can fully internalize them.

Empirical evidence supports the role of relatedness in psychological development. For example, consider a feedback episode where a writing instructor returns a graduate student's draft with substantial revision suggestions. The moment-to-moment processing of this feedback involves interpreting it informationally, which partially satisfies the competence need and preserves autonomy. However, the long-term internalization of these behaviors depends on whether the student feels cared for by their instructor, highlighting the importance of relatedness in fostering meaningful connections.

## Mechanism

The mechanism through which relatedness supports internalization involves a step-by-step process where individuals first take in behaviors from an external source. This initial taking-in is facilitated by feeling supported and valued, which is the essence of relatedness. Once taken in, these behaviors can then be integrated into one's self-concept through processes like introjection and identification, ultimately leading to more stable internal regulation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, relatedness plays a critical role in creating an environment where students feel supported and valued. For example, incorporating group projects that encourage collaboration can enhance relatedness among peers, leading to better internalization of learning objectives. Teachers who take the time to build meaningful relationships with their students are more likely to see long-term engagement and motivation.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Clinical settings**
> In clinical settings, relatedness is essential for patient care and treatment adherence. A therapist who builds a strong therapeutic alliance based on trust and empathy can help patients internalize behaviors necessary for their recovery. For instance, a patient with chronic pain might be more likely to adhere to prescribed physical therapy if they feel supported by their healthcare provider.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Relatedness vs Autonomy**
> While both relatedness and autonomy are fundamental needs in SDT, they serve different developmental functions. Relatedness is about feeling connected to others and being cared for, whereas autonomy focuses on the experience of volition and self-endorsed behavior. A person can feel autonomous without necessarily feeling a strong sense of relatedness, but internalization often requires both.

> [!key-distinction] **Relatedness vs Competence**
> Competence is about feeling effective in one's environment, while relatedness is about feeling connected and cared for. Although both are necessary for internalization, competence ensures that individuals feel capable of achieving their goals, whereas relatedness provides the emotional support needed to sustain motivation over time.

## Key Figures

- **Edward L. Deci** — Deci is a key contributor to SDT and has extensively researched the role of relatedness in psychological development, emphasizing its importance for internalization processes.
- **Frank Martela** — Martela has conducted significant research on relatedness within SDT, exploring how it influences various aspects of well-being and motivation across different contexts.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How does the concept of relatedness apply in algorithmically mediated environments?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Empirical research that investigates the role of digital interactions in fostering relatedness would help clarify its applicability and limitations in these contexts.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> Can relatedness be fully satisfied through digital means?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Studies comparing the effectiveness of online versus face-to-face interactions in satisfying relatedness needs could provide insights into this question.

## Synthesis

The concept of relatedness is crucial for understanding how individuals develop a sense of self and motivation. By fostering meaningful connections, it supports the internalization process from external to introjection regulation, ensuring that behaviors are integrated into one's identity in a sustainable way. This has significant implications not only in educational settings but also in clinical and therapeutic contexts, where building strong relationships can enhance treatment outcomes.

Beyond its role within SDT, relatedness intersects with other psychological needs like autonomy and competence, highlighting the interconnected nature of human motivation. Its importance extends to broader domains such as organizational behavior and social psychology, underscoring its relevance across various fields.
