# Identified Regulation

> [!definition] **Identified Regulation**
> Identified regulation is a regulatory style in Self-Determination Theory where an individual consciously endorses the value of a behavior, recognizing it as personally important even when not inherently enjoyable. It falls under [[self-determination-theory]], distinguished from introjected and external regulations by its conscious endorsement and intrinsic importance to the individual.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes behaviors that are endorsed without conscious value-endorsement (introjected regulation) or fully integrated into one's self-value structure (integrated regulation).

## Core Explanation

Identified regulation is a critical component of autonomous motivation within Self-Determination Theory, emphasizing that individuals recognize the value of behaviors they engage in. This recognition can be seen in various contexts, such as a patient with type 2 diabetes who monitors their blood glucose levels to manage their condition effectively. The individual may not find this behavior inherently enjoyable but recognizes its importance for their health and well-being.

The process of internalizing behaviors into identified regulation involves the initial acceptance of external contingencies (like medical advice) followed by a deeper understanding of why these actions are beneficial. This shift from externally to internally motivated behavior is crucial for sustained engagement in activities that may not be inherently enjoyable but are essential for personal growth and well-being.

Theoretical roots of identified regulation lie in the need-satisfaction framework of Self-Determination Theory, where autonomy, competence, and relatedness play pivotal roles. Autonomy supports the move from introjection to identification by fostering a sense of volition and self-endorsement. Competence ensures that once behaviors are identified as valuable, they can be stabilized against challenges, while relatedness provides the social context necessary for initial internalization.

Empirical evidence suggests that promoting identified regulation in educational settings enhances student engagement and academic performance. For instance, when teachers provide autonomy-supportive teaching styles, students are more likely to engage in behaviors like homework completion with a sense of personal importance rather than external pressure.

## Mechanism

The process of moving from introjected regulation to identified regulation involves several stages. Initially, individuals may adopt behaviors due to internalized contingencies (like avoiding shame or gaining pride). Over time, as these behaviors become more integrated into their value structure and they experience the benefits firsthand, they begin to endorse them consciously. This shift is supported by autonomy-supportive environments that foster a sense of volition and self-endorsement.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, promoting identified regulation can enhance student engagement in learning activities. For example, teachers who provide choices and encourage students to set their own goals are more likely to foster a sense of personal importance for the tasks at hand, leading to higher levels of intrinsic motivation.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Healthcare**
> In healthcare settings, encouraging patients to recognize the value of prescribed behaviors can improve adherence. For instance, a patient with diabetes who understands the long-term benefits of monitoring blood glucose and making dietary changes is more likely to engage in these behaviors voluntarily.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Digital environments**
> In digital learning platforms, integrating features that allow users to set personal goals and track progress can promote identified regulation. This approach helps learners recognize the value of their actions and fosters a sense of autonomy and self-endorsement.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Identified vs Introjected Regulation**
> Identified regulation involves conscious endorsement, whereas introjected regulation lacks this conscious recognition. Individuals in identified regulation see behaviors as personally important, while those in introjected regulation may feel pressured by internal contingencies like shame or pride.

> [!key-distinction] **Identified vs External Regulation**
> External regulation is driven by external contingencies and does not involve personal endorsement. In contrast, identified regulation involves recognizing the value of a behavior from within, making it more sustainable over time.

## Synthesis

Understanding identified regulation is crucial for promoting autonomous motivation, as it bridges the gap between externally imposed behaviors and intrinsic personal values. By fostering a sense of volition and self-endorsement, educators, healthcare providers, and digital platform designers can enhance engagement and long-term commitment to beneficial behaviors.

The concept of identified regulation also has broader implications across various domains, including education, health, and technology. Its integration into these fields can lead to more effective interventions that support sustainable behavior change and personal growth.
