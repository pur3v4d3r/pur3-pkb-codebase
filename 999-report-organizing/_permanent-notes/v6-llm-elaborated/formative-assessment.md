# Formative Assessment

> [!definition] **Formative Assessment**
> Formative Assessment involves using assessment evidence — in-task, between-task, or end-of-unit — to adjust ongoing teaching and learning rather than producing a terminal grade, operationalized through low-stakes retrieval, diagnostic questioning, peer review, and structured self-assessment aimed at surfacing misconceptions and recalibrating effort while the learning is still in progress. It falls under [[Self-Regulated Learning]], as it supports students in monitoring their own understanding and adjusting their strategies accordingly.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept excludes activities that do not adjust instruction or feedback based on the assessment evidence, such as frequent summative testing that does not inform teaching adjustments.

## Core Explanation

At its core, Formative Assessment serves to scaffold student learning by providing ongoing feedback that helps both teachers and learners adjust instructional methods and study techniques. This process is crucial for metacognitive monitoring, enabling students to recognize their own misconceptions and areas of difficulty, which in turn allows them to refine their approaches and deepen their understanding.

The mechanism through which Formative Assessment operates involves a variety of low-stakes activities such as retrieval practice, where students are prompted to recall information from memory, and structured self-assessment, which encourages learners to evaluate their own work. These practices not only help in identifying gaps in knowledge but also foster a growth mindset by promoting the idea that effort and reflection can lead to improvement.

Theoretical roots of Formative Assessment trace back to educational psychology, particularly the principles of working memory and retrieval practice. By engaging students in these activities, teachers can effectively surface misconceptions and recalibrate their efforts, ensuring that learning is both efficient and effective. This aligns with the broader goal of self-regulated learning, where learners take an active role in managing their own cognitive processes.

Empirical evidence supports the effectiveness of Formative Assessment, as highlighted by Black and Wiliam (1998) and subsequent research. The single most evidence-supported instructional intervention in K-16 education, it couples the consolidation mechanism of retrieval practice with the calibration mechanism of correcting fluency illusions within a single instructional move.

## Mechanism

Formative Assessment works through specific mechanisms such as low-stakes retrieval and structured self-assessment. Low-stakes retrieval involves brief quizzes or prompts that encourage students to recall information, helping to reinforce learning and identify areas of difficulty. Structured self-assessment tasks require students to evaluate their own work, providing them with immediate feedback on their understanding and encouraging reflection.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, Formative Assessment can be integrated into lesson plans through the use of quick quizzes or exit tickets. This allows teachers to gauge student understanding in real-time and adjust subsequent lessons accordingly, ensuring that instruction is tailored to meet students' needs.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Student engagement**
> By incorporating Formative Assessment techniques such as peer review and structured self-assessment, educators can increase student engagement by making learning more interactive and reflective. This not only enhances the learning experience but also promotes a sense of ownership over one's education.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Teacher practice**
> Teachers who implement Formative Assessment regularly find that it improves their instructional strategies. By receiving immediate feedback on student understanding, teachers can make informed decisions about which concepts to revisit or extend, leading to more effective and efficient teaching practices.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> Formative Assessment focuses on intrinsic load by helping students manage their cognitive processes effectively. In contrast, extraneous load is associated with unnecessary complexity in the learning environment that does not contribute to understanding. Formative Assessment reduces extraneous load by providing clear and actionable feedback, allowing learners to focus on what matters most.

## Synthesis

Formative Assessment is a critical component in educational psychology because it directly supports self-regulated learning by providing ongoing feedback that helps learners monitor and adjust their own cognitive processes. By integrating these practices into teaching, educators can create more effective and engaging learning environments, ultimately contributing to broader learning goals such as deep understanding and lifelong learning.

The concept of Formative Assessment also intersects with other related concepts like worked examples and intrinsic vs extraneous load, further enriching our understanding of how students learn. Its application in conjunction with these principles can enhance the effectiveness of educational interventions, making it a valuable tool for both teachers and learners.
