# Dark Energy

> [!definition] **Dark Energy**
> Dark Energy is an inferred component that makes up approximately 68% of the cosmic energy density and drives the accelerating expansion of the universe at late times through its negative pressure. It falls under cosmology, serving as a placeholder for an empirical phenomenon rather than being tied to any specific microphysical model like the Cosmological Constant or Quintessence.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> Dark Energy should not be confused with specific microphysical models like the cosmological constant or quintessence; it is a placeholder name for an empirical phenomenon that requires further explanation.

## Core Explanation

Dark Energy emerged from observations in 1998 when Type Ia supernovae were used to measure cosmic expansion. The data revealed that the universe's expansion was not slowing down as expected due to gravity but accelerating, indicating an unknown force pushing galaxies apart at increasing speeds.

This discovery forced cosmologists to incorporate Dark Energy into their models of the universe. Its existence is a requirement based on observational evidence, regardless of which specific theoretical explanation is favored among competing models such as the Cosmological Constant or Quintessence.

Understanding Dark Energy's role in the cosmic energy budget has profound implications for our comprehension of the universe’s structure and evolution. It challenges traditional views of gravity and raises questions about the ultimate fate of the cosmos, whether it will continue to expand indefinitely or reach a point where expansion ceases.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Cosmological Models**
> Incorporating Dark Energy into cosmological models is crucial for accurately predicting the universe's future. Without accounting for its accelerating effect, models would incorrectly predict a decelerating expansion, leading to significant errors in estimating the age and size of the universe.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Future Expansion**
> Understanding Dark Energy helps cosmologists forecast how the universe will evolve over time. If Dark Energy continues to dominate, it could lead to a 'Big Rip' scenario where galaxies, stars, and even atoms are torn apart as space expands at an ever-increasing rate.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Dark Energy vs Cosmological Constant**
> While Dark Energy is the empirical phenomenon observed in cosmic expansion, the Cosmological Constant is one theoretical explanation for it. The distinction lies in that Dark Energy remains a placeholder term until its true nature is understood, whereas the Cosmological Constant proposes a static energy density of space.

> [!key-distinction] **Dark Energy vs Quintessence**
> Quintessence represents another theoretical model explaining Dark Energy. Unlike the Cosmological Constant, which assumes a constant value, Quintessence posits that Dark Energy can vary over time and space, offering a dynamic alternative to explain cosmic acceleration.

## Key Figures

- **Saul Perlmutter** — Perlmutter led the Supernova Cosmology Project which used Type Ia supernovae as standard candles to measure the expansion rate of the universe, leading to the discovery of Dark Energy.
- **Brian Schmidt** — Schmidt was a key member of the High-Z SN Search Team that independently confirmed Perlmutter's findings on cosmic acceleration through supernova observations.

## Open Questions

> [!open-question] **Question**
> What is the true nature of Dark Energy?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Determining whether it is a constant energy density (Cosmological Constant) or a dynamic field (Quintessence) would resolve this question.

> [!open-question] **Question**
> How does Dark Energy affect the future expansion and fate of the universe?
>
> *What would resolve it:* Observations that track cosmic acceleration over time could provide evidence for whether Dark Energy's influence will continue to dominate or diminish in the far future.

## Synthesis

Understanding Dark Energy is crucial for advancing cosmology as it challenges our fundamental understanding of gravity and the universe’s structure. Its role in driving cosmic acceleration not only impacts predictions about the universe's fate but also informs theoretical models that aim to unify general relativity with quantum mechanics.
