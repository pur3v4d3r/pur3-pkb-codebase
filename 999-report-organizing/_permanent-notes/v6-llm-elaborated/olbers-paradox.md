# Olbers Paradox

> [!definition] **Olbers Paradox**
> Olbers Paradox is a historical puzzle formalized by Heinrich Olbers in 1823 that questions why the night sky is dark if the universe is infinite, eternal, and uniformly populated with stars. This paradox does not delve into detailed mechanisms of cosmological expansion or specific astronomical observations beyond its context, focusing instead on the fundamental question of stellar distribution and light propagation. It falls under Historical Cosmological Problems.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept stops at explaining the paradox itself and its resolution through finite age of the universe and redshifting. It does not delve into detailed mechanisms of cosmological expansion or specific astronomical observations beyond this context.

## Core Explanation

Olbers Paradox challenges our understanding of the universe's structure by questioning why an infinite, eternal cosmos filled with stars would not result in a uniformly bright night sky. The paradox arises from the assumption that every line of sight should terminate on a stellar surface due to the uniform distribution of stars across space. This foundational mechanism highlights the interplay between light propagation and the spatial distribution of celestial bodies.

In practice, Olbers Paradox operates by highlighting inconsistencies in our understanding of the universe's age and expansion. The paradox was initially posed as a challenge to the prevailing belief in an eternal, static cosmos. It forced astronomers and cosmologists to reconsider their assumptions about the nature of space and time, leading to significant theoretical developments.

Theoretical roots of Olbers Paradox are deeply intertwined with early cosmological models that assumed an infinite universe without beginning or end. This paradox challenged these models by suggesting that if stars were uniformly distributed throughout an eternal cosmos, every point in the sky would be illuminated by a star, resulting in a perpetually bright night sky.

Historically, Olbers Paradox has been pivotal in shaping our understanding of cosmology. It was not until the 20th century, with the advent of modern cosmological theories such as the Big Bang model and the concept of an expanding universe, that scientists were able to provide a satisfactory explanation for why the night sky is dark.

## Mechanism

The resolution to Olbers Paradox lies in understanding both the finite age of the universe and the effects of cosmological redshifting. The finite age means that light from distant stars has not had enough time to reach us, while redshifting further reduces the brightness of more distant objects by shifting their emitted light into longer wavelengths.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **finite age vs eternal universe**
> A common misconception about Olbers Paradox is that it can be resolved solely by considering an expanding universe. However, the primary resolution lies in recognizing the finite age of the universe, which means light from distant stars has not had enough time to reach us. Redshifting provides a secondary contribution but does not fully resolve the paradox on its own.

## Key Figures

- **Heinrich Wilhelm Olbers** — Formalized the paradox in 1823, challenging prevailing notions of an eternal and static universe by questioning why the night sky is dark despite an infinite number of stars.
