# Embedding Evidence-Based Strategies in a Self-Directed Learning Curriculum

> [!definition] Embedding Evidence-Based Strategies in a Self-Directed Learning Curriculum
> Embedding evidence-based strategies in a self-directed learning curriculum involves integrating proven educational techniques such as retrieval practice, interleaving, and self-explanation into the design, progressively shifting responsibility to learners to apply these strategies independently while providing support through metacognitive monitoring.

## Examples

> [!example] Embedding Evidence-Based Strategies in a Self-Directed Learning Curriculum
> A curriculum for developing research skills might embed [[Desirable Difficulties (Robert Bjork, 1994)|retrieval practice]] by beginning each session with low-stakes retrieval exercises on material from previous sessions, [[Desirable Difficulties (Robert Bjork, 1994)|interleaving]] by mixing methodological concepts with substantive research questions rather than teaching methodology in isolation, and [[self-explanation-effect|self-explanation]] by requiring learners to articulate why they are choosing particular methods for particular questions. In the early stages, the curriculum structures these practices explicitly. In the middle stages, learners are asked to design their own retrieval schedules and self-explanation protocols. In the final stages, learners are expected to deploy these strategies independently, with the curriculum providing only periodic [[metacognitive-monitoring|metacognitive monitoring]] prompts to encourage reflection on which strategies are being used and how effectively.
