# Specific Performance Goals vs. Learning Goals on Complex Tasks

> [!definition] Specific Performance Goals vs. Learning Goals on Complex Tasks
> Specific Performance Goals vs. Learning Goals on Complex Tasks refers to a debate in motivation science regarding whether setting specific performance goals or fostering learning goals leads to better outcomes, with evidence suggesting that learning goals are more effective for tasks requiring strategy acquisition.

## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Specific Performance Goals vs. Learning Goals on Complex Tasks
> **Position A:** Specific, difficult performance goals produce higher performance across task types (classical Locke & Latham 1990).
> **Position B:** On tasks where strategy must be acquired, learning goals outperform performance goals; the original prescription must be refined accordingly (Locke & Latham 2002 refinement; [[achievement-goal-theory]]).
> **Current state:** Largely resolved in favor of Position B, with the boundary now formally part of the mature theory.
> **This report's stance:** Position B is correct and the resolution represents a genuine sharpening of the theory's predictive precision.
