# competence-need

> [!definition] competence-need
> - **Key-Term**: [[competence-need]]
> - **Definition**: Competence-need refers to the individual's perception of their own ability to perform a task effectively, which influences their motivation and engagement in learning activities.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept of competence-need is rooted in self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985), where it is seen as one of the basic psychological needs that drive human behavior. When learners feel competent, they are more likely to be motivated and engaged in their learning process.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, competence-need can be fostered through various instructional strategies such as providing clear feedback, setting achievable goals, and offering opportunities for mastery experiences. Teachers and educators play a crucial role in creating an environment that supports students' sense of competence.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between perceived competence (how learners view their own abilities) and actual competence (their true ability level). Misalignments between these perceptions can lead to motivational issues.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors can enhance student engagement by designing tasks that are appropriately challenging, providing timely feedback, and recognizing students' achievements.

> [!example] Application
> Understanding competence-need helps educators create a supportive learning environment where students feel capable of succeeding, which in turn boosts their motivation and persistence.
