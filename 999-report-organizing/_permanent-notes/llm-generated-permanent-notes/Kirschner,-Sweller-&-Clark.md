# Kirschner,-Sweller-&-Clark

> [!definition] Kirschner,-Sweller-&-Clark
> - **Key-Term**: [[Kirschner,-Sweller-&-Clark]]
> - **Definition**: Kirschner, Sweller & Clark refer to the cognitive load theory framework that emphasizes the role of biologically primary knowledge in learning and instruction, suggesting that instructional design should minimize extraneous cognitive load to facilitate deep processing and long-term retention.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The core mechanism involves distinguishing between intrinsic (inherent to the task), germane (related to learning the task), and extraneous (not related to the task) cognitive loads. Instructional strategies are designed to reduce extraneous load, allowing learners to focus on processing information more effectively.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means breaking down complex tasks into manageable chunks, providing worked examples rather than just problem-solving exercises, and sequencing instructions in a way that builds upon prior knowledge.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the importance of pre-training for germane cognitive load reduction and the role of prior knowledge in determining what is considered biologically primary.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, this theory suggests that instructional materials should be designed to minimize distractions and focus on essential information, thereby enhancing learning efficiency.

> [!example] Application
> In training programs, it implies that initial phases should provide clear guidance and examples before moving to more independent problem-solving tasks.
