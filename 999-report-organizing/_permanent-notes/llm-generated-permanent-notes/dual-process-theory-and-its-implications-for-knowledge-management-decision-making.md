# Dual-Process Theory and its Implications for Knowledge Management Decision-Making

> [!definition] Dual-Process Theory and its Implications for Knowledge Management Decision-Making
> An application of cognitive psychology's dual-process theory to decision-making in personal knowledge management.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Dual-Process Theory and its Implications for Knowledge Management Decision-Making
> **Description:** An investigation of how the dual-process framework (System 1/System 2; Kahneman, 2011; Stanovich & West, 2000) applies to the decisions PKM practitioners make — decisions about what to capture, how to encode, when to link, whether to review. System 1 processes (fast, intuitive, automatic) govern most moment-to-moment PKM decisions; System 2 processes (slow, deliberate, effortful) are required for deep encoding and strategic planning. This investigation would examine how PKM system design can calibrate the balance between automaticity and deliberation.
>
> **Connection to this report:** Provides a complementary cognitive framework to CLT and metacognition, offering a different lens on the automation vs. engagement tension throughout this report.
