# The Autonomy-Independence Conflation

> [!definition] The Autonomy-Independence Conflation
> The Autonomy-Independence Conflation is the mistaken belief that autonomy in self-directed learning (SDL) requires learners to function independently without seeking guidance or collaboration, when in fact autonomy involves volitional self-endorsement of one's actions.

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Autonomy-Independence Conflation
> One of the most persistent and damaging misconceptions in the SDL literature is the conflation of autonomy with independence. Autonomy, in the SDT sense, refers to the experience of volitional self-endorsement — the feeling that one's actions emanate from oneself rather than being imposed from without. This is entirely compatible with seeking guidance, collaborating with peers, and relying on mentors. Independence, by contrast, refers to functioning without reliance on others. A learner can be highly autonomous (choosing to learn, valuing the learning, experiencing the process as self-directed) while simultaneously being highly interdependent (seeking expert guidance, participating in study groups, leveraging community knowledge). The conflation of these concepts has led to educational practices that abandon learners under the banner of "respecting their autonomy" — a misapplication that SDT's own architects have repeatedly criticized.
