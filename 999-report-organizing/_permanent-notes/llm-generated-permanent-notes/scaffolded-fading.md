# scaffolded-fading

> [!definition] scaffolded-fading
> - **Key-Term**: [[scaffolded-fading]]
> - **Definition**: Scaffolded fading is an instructional strategy where initial support (or scaffolding) provided to learners during the learning process is gradually reduced as they become more competent, eventually leading to independent performance.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Scaffolded fading involves providing temporary and targeted assistance that supports students in understanding or performing a task. As learners gain confidence and competence, this support is systematically withdrawn, encouraging them to take on more responsibility for their learning.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This process typically begins with high levels of teacher intervention, such as modeling, prompting, and direct instruction, which are gradually reduced over time. The goal is to help students develop the skills necessary to perform tasks independently while minimizing dependency on external support.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the timing and nature of fading, which can vary based on individual learner needs and task complexity.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors can use scaffolded fading to enhance student engagement by gradually increasing their autonomy in learning activities.

> [!example] Application
> This strategy supports formative assessment practices, allowing teachers to monitor progress and adjust support levels accordingly.
