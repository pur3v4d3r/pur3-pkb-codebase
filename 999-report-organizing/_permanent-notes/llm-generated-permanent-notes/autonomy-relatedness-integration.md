# autonomy-relatedness-integration

> [!definition] autonomy-relatedness-integration
> - **Key-Term**: [[autonomy-relatedness-integration]]
> - **Definition**: Autonomy-relatedness-integration refers to the process by which individuals balance their need for autonomy (self-determination) and relatedness (social connection) in order to achieve optimal motivation and well-being.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This concept is rooted in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), particularly as articulated by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan. It posits that individuals are motivated when they feel a sense of autonomy, competence, and relatedness within their environment or activities.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means that people strive to engage in activities where they can make choices freely (autonomy) while also feeling connected to others (relatedness). This balance is crucial for fostering intrinsic motivation and psychological well-being.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The theory suggests that when these needs are satisfied, individuals experience a state of integration, where their actions align with their values and goals.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, teachers can promote autonomy-relatedness-integration by providing students with choices in assignments while also fostering a supportive classroom environment.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace settings, managers can enhance employee motivation by offering opportunities for self-direction alongside strong team cohesion.
