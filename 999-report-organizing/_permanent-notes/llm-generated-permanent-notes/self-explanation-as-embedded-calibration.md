# Self-Explanation as Embedded Calibration

> [!definition] Self-Explanation as Embedded Calibration
> Self-explanation as embedded calibration involves a learner actively explaining concepts to themselves while reading or engaging with material, which helps identify comprehension gaps and calibrates their understanding.

## Examples

> [!example] Self-Explanation as Embedded Calibration
> A student reading a passage about why mass extinctions occur reaches a sentence claiming that asteroid impacts cause global cooling through atmospheric dust suspension. A self-explainer pauses and asks: *Why* does dust cause cooling? *How* much dust would be required? *What* connects the cooling to the extinction? The first question may be answerable from the text or from prior knowledge; the second may not be answerable at all without further research; the third may require an inference the text leaves implicit. Each question that cannot be cleanly answered identifies a comprehension gap the student's monitoring system would have left silent. The act of trying to explain produces calibration data the act of reading alone could not.
