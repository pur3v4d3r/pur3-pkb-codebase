# knowledge-telling-vs.-knowledge-transforming

> [!definition] knowledge-telling-vs.-knowledge-transforming
> - **Key-Term**: [[knowledge-telling-vs.-knowledge-transforming]]
> - **Definition**: The distinction between knowledge-telling and knowledge-transforming refers to the difference between conveying information versus facilitating the transformation of that information into meaningful understanding and application in new contexts.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Knowledge-telling involves the straightforward transmission of facts, procedures, or principles from one individual to another. This can be done through lectures, textbooks, or other forms of explicit instruction.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In contrast, knowledge-transforming focuses on fostering deep understanding and the ability to apply knowledge flexibly across different situations. This often involves activities such as problem-solving, reflection, and collaboration that encourage learners to engage with information in a more active and meaningful way.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of metacognition, self-regulation strategies, and the integration of new knowledge with existing schemas.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, emphasizing knowledge-transforming can lead to better long-term retention and transfer of skills. For example, in clinical settings, medical students who engage in case-based learning are more likely to apply their knowledge effectively in patient care.

> [!example] Application
> In athletic training, focusing on knowledge-transforming through practice and feedback helps athletes develop the ability to adapt their techniques under varying conditions.
