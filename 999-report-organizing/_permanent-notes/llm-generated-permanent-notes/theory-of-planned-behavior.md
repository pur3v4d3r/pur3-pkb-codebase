# theory-of-planned-behavior

> [!definition] theory-of-planned-behavior
> - **Key-Term**: [[theory-of-planned-behavior]]
> - **Definition**: The Theory of Planned Behavior is a model that explains how people's attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control influence their intentions to perform specific behaviors and subsequently their actual behavior.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This theory posits that individuals' intentions to engage in a particular behavior are determined by three key factors: their attitude toward the behavior (how favorable or unfavorable they perceive it), subjective norms (the perceived social pressure from important others to perform or not perform the behavior), and perceived behavioral control (their belief about how much control they have over performing the behavior).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> These factors interact in a way that stronger intentions lead to higher likelihood of actual behavior. The theory has been widely applied in health, education, and environmental contexts to predict and change behaviors.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between intention and actual behavior, as well as the role of self-efficacy (a subset of perceived behavioral control) in influencing both.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In public health, it has been used to design interventions aimed at increasing vaccination rates or promoting healthy eating habits.

> [!example] Application
> In education, it helps in understanding and modifying students' intentions towards academic behaviors like studying or attending classes.
