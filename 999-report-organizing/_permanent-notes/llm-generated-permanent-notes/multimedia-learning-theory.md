# multimedia-learning-theory

> [!definition] multimedia-learning-theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[multimedia-learning-theory]]
> - **Definition**: Multimedia learning theory is the idea that combining verbal and visual information can enhance learning and retention, particularly when the two types of information are presented in a way that aligns with how humans process and store information.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The core mechanism of multimedia learning theory suggests that learners benefit from the integration of text and images because it allows for dual coding, where verbal and visual information is processed separately but can be linked together to form more robust mental representations.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means that educational materials should include both words and pictures in a way that supports the learner's understanding and memory. For example, diagrams paired with explanatory text or videos combined with written summaries are effective strategies.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the importance of aligning verbal and visual information to avoid cognitive overload, as well as the role of prior knowledge in facilitating learning.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In e-learning design, multimedia learning theory suggests that incorporating both text and visuals can improve comprehension and retention.

> [!example] Application
> Educators should carefully consider how to integrate these elements to ensure they complement each other rather than compete for cognitive resources.
