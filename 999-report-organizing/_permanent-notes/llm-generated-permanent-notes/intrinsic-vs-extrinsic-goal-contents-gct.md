# Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Goal Contents (GCT)

> [!definition] Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Goal Contents (GCT)
> Intrinsic goals orient the individual toward outcomes that directly satisfy basic psychological needs — personal growth (competence), meaningful relationships (relatedness), community contribution (relatedness and autonomy), and health (competence). Extrinsic goals orient the individual toward outcomes that serve as substitutes for or proxies of need satisfaction — wealth, fame, and image — which provide external indicators of worth rather than genuine need fulfillment.
>
> **Boundary:** GCT does not claim that earning money or caring about appearance is inherently harmful. It claims that the *relative centrality* of extrinsic goals — when they dominate one's aspiration structure at the expense of intrinsic goals — predicts negative well-being outcomes. Moderate extrinsic aspirations alongside robust intrinsic aspirations do not carry the same predictive weight.
>
> **See also:** [[goal-contents-theory]], [[aspiration-index]], [[Eudaimonic-Well-Being]]
