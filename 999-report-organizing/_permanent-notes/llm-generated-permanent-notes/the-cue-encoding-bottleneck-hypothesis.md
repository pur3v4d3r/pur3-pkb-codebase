# The Cue-Encoding Bottleneck Hypothesis

> [!definition] The Cue-Encoding Bottleneck Hypothesis
> The Cue-Encoding Bottleneck Hypothesis posits that the quality of cue encoding is the primary factor limiting the effectiveness of implementation intentions, suggesting that improving cue selection and specification could significantly enhance their success.

## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] The Cue-Encoding Bottleneck Hypothesis
> The collected failure-mode evidence suggests an integrative hypothesis worth surfacing explicitly: the most common single point of failure for implementation intentions is the *quality of cue encoding*, and improving cue encoding may be the single most leveraged intervention for increasing the construct's effectiveness in practice. Most of the failure modes catalogued above can be reframed as cue-encoding problems — vague cues, overloaded cues, drifting cues, cues whose discriminability has been eroded by environmental change. If this hypothesis is correct, then practitioner training in implementation-intention use should focus disproportionately on cue selection and cue specification rather than on motivational priming or behavioral elaboration. This is a testable prediction: an intervention that trains cue-encoding skill should outperform an intervention of equal length that trains motivational commitment, holding constant the underlying behavioral target. To my knowledge this comparison has not been performed directly in the literature, and it would be a productive direction for future research.
> *— [[implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
