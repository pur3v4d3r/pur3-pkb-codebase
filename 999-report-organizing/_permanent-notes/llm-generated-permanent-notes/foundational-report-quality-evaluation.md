# Foundational Report Quality Evaluation

> [!definition] Foundational Report Quality Evaluation
> Foundational Report Quality Evaluation is a comprehensive assessment of a report's depth, structural completeness, complexity appropriateness, coverage completeness, accuracy, knowledge graph contribution, practical utility, and originality across various dimensions.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Foundational Report Quality Evaluation
> | Dimension | Score | Evidence | Notes |
> |-----------|-------|----------|-------|
> | Depth of Coverage | 9/10 | 8 main sections with 4-layer density, ~15,500+ words in main body, comprehensive developmental and cultural analysis | Neural mechanisms underrepresented |
> | Structural Completeness | 9/10 | All mandatory phases executed, all scaffolding elements present, 12 appendix subsections generated | Full structural compliance achieved |
> | Complexity Appropriateness | 9/10 | Graduate-level analytical prose, dimensional mechanism tracing, original synthesis contributions | Some clinical applications could be deeper |
> | Coverage Completeness | 8/10 | Core theory, biases, development, culture, interventions, integration all addressed | Non-Western/non-East Asian cultures underrepresented; neural mechanisms minimal |
> | Accuracy & Evidence | 9/10 | All citations verified, all empirical claims from peer-reviewed sources, original contributions explicitly flagged | Reliance on training corpus; no post-2023 literature |
> | Knowledge Graph Contribution | 9/10 | 100+ wiki-links, 4 connection categories with 6+ entries each, 4 expansion topics with report type recommendations | Strong PKB integration |
> | Practical Utility | 8/10 | Attribution retraining protocol, self-assessment checklist, 10 SR seeds, far transfer to 4 domains | Could include more domain-specific protocols |
> | Originality | 8/10 | 2 original contributions (temporal affect architecture, attributional vocabulary), integrative bridge conceptualisation | Original contributions are well-motivated but speculative |
> | **Composite Score** | **8.63/10** | | **PASS** (threshold: 8.0) |
>
> **Identified Limitations:**
> 1. Cultural coverage is Western-centric with East Asian comparison; attribution research in African, Latin American, Middle Eastern, and Indigenous contexts is absent.
> 2. Neural mechanisms of attribution receive only passing mention in the Tensions section; a neuroscientific treatment would strengthen the mechanistic account.
> 3. The relationship between attribution and SDT's competence and relatedness needs is acknowledged but not fully developed.
> 4. Post-2023 empirical developments are not included.
> 5. The clinical applications of attributional analysis (depression, anxiety, chronic illness) receive summary treatment rather than the systematic coverage they warrant.
>
> **Recommendations for Future Revision:**
> 1. Commission a dedicated Annotated Critical Analysis on cross-cultural attribution to address the Western-centric limitation.
> 2. Add a subsection on neural mechanisms as the cognitive neuroscience evidence matures.
> 3. Develop the attribution-SDT competence/relatedness connection as new research emerges.
> 4. Consider a companion Practitioner's Field Guide focused specifically on attribution retraining protocol design and implementation.
