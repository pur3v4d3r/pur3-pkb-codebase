# Precision Note on Reversibility

> [!definition] Precision Note on Reversibility
> Precision Note on Reversibility refers to the dynamic perspective that integration in OIT is not a permanent stage but a regulatory architecture which can revert under adverse conditions, reflecting successful model prediction rather than empirical inadequacy.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Precision Note on Reversibility
> The reversibility of integration is sometimes presented as a problem for SDT, on the grounds that a "developmental" model ought to produce stable end-state achievements. The dynamic perspective reframes this: integration is not a stage in the sense of Piagetian developmental stages, where reaching a stage entails permanent reorganization of the underlying capacity. Integration in OIT is a regulatory architecture whose maintenance depends on continued conditions of the kind that produced it, and whose reversal under hostile conditions is not a failure of the model but a successful prediction of it. The model would be empirically inadequate if integrated regulations did not regress under chronic need-frustrating conditions, because that would mean the dynamic substrate had no consequences once the regulation reached its rightmost continuum position.
