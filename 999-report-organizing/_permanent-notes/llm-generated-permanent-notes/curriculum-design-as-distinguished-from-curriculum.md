# Curriculum Design (as distinguished from Curriculum)

> [!definition] Curriculum Design (as distinguished from Curriculum)
> Curriculum design refers to the deliberate, systematic process of planning, structuring, and organising the elements of a learning experience — including content selection, sequencing, pedagogy, assessment, and resource allocation — to achieve specified learning outcomes. It is distinguished from "curriculum" (which can refer simply to the content or syllabus) by its emphasis on the intentional engineering of the learning environment.
>
> **Boundary:** Curriculum design is not synonymous with teaching. A curriculum can be well-designed and poorly taught, or poorly designed and salvaged by an exceptional teacher. The design concerns the architecture; the teaching concerns the enactment.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** In this report, curriculum design is reframed as developmental architecture — the engineering of environments that build and then withdraw from the learner's growing capacity for self-direction.
>
> **See also:** [[instructional-design]], [[pedagogy]], [[self-directed-learning]]
