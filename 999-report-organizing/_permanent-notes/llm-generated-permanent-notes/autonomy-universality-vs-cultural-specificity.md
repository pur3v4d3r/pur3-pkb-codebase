# Autonomy Universality vs. Cultural Specificity

> [!definition] Autonomy Universality vs. Cultural Specificity
> Autonomy Universality vs. Cultural Specificity refers to the debate over whether the need for autonomy is a universally experienced psychological drive or if its expression varies significantly across different cultural contexts.

## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Autonomy Universality vs. Cultural Specificity
> **The tension:** SDT claims autonomy is a universal need; critics argue it reflects Western individualistic values.
> **Position A (SDT):** Autonomy means endorsement (volition), not independence. Cross-cultural evidence (Chen et al., 2015) supports functional universality when measured correctly. Advocates: Deci, Ryan, Chen, Vansteenkiste.
> **Position B (Critics):** The concept remains culturally loaded despite redefinition; self-report measures may not capture genuine cross-cultural differences in the meaning of autonomy. Advocates: Iyengar, Markus, Kitayama.
> **Current evidence:** Increasingly favors Position A when autonomy is operationalized as endorsement, but measurement concerns persist.
> **Why it matters:** If autonomy is not universal, SDT's foundational claim collapses.
> **This report's stance:** The evidence supports qualified universality — functional universality of the need with cultural variability in satisfaction pathways.
