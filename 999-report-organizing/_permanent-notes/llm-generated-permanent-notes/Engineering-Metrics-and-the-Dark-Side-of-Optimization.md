# Engineering-Metrics-and-the-Dark-Side-of-Optimization

> [!definition] Engineering-Metrics-and-the-Dark-Side-of-Optimization
> - **Key-Term**: [[Engineering-Metrics-and-the-Dark-Side-of-Optimization]]
> - **Definition**: The use of metrics to optimize engineering processes and outcomes, but the potential negative consequences that arise from overly narrow focus on quantitative measures at the expense of qualitative aspects such as safety, ethics, or human well-being.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Engineering-Metrics-and-the-Dark-Side-of-Optimization refers to the practice of using metrics to improve efficiency and performance in engineering projects. While this can lead to significant advancements, it also poses risks when these metrics are prioritized over other important factors like safety or ethical considerations.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, engineers often use various metrics such as cost, time, and quality to optimize their designs and processes. However, an excessive focus on these quantitative measures can sometimes result in unintended negative consequences, including compromised safety standards, unethical practices, or reduced human well-being.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> This concept is rooted in the broader discussion of how optimization through metrics can lead to a narrow focus that may overlook qualitative aspects such as user experience, environmental impact, and long-term sustainability.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In healthcare engineering, excessive focus on cost-cutting measures might result in reduced safety standards or inadequate patient care.

> [!example] Application
> In autonomous vehicle development, prioritizing speed and efficiency over ethical considerations could lead to accidents involving pedestrians or other vulnerable road users.
