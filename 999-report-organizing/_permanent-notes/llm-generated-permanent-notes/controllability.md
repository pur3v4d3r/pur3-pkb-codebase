# controllability

> [!definition] controllability
> - **Key-Term**: [[controllability]]
> - **Definition**: Controllability refers to the ability of an individual to influence or control events and outcomes, particularly in terms of attributing causes for those events.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Controllability is central in attribution theory, where individuals assess whether they can control their environment. This concept helps explain how people attribute success or failure to themselves or others based on perceived controllability.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, high controllability leads to internal attributions (attributing outcomes to personal factors), while low controllability results in external attributions (blaming circumstances).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Theorists like Fritz Heider and Bernard Weiner have extensively explored the role of controllability in shaping attributions and emotional responses.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, understanding a patient's sense of controllability can inform treatment approaches for conditions like depression.

> [!example] Application
> Educational interventions often aim to enhance students' perceived controllability over their academic outcomes to improve motivation and performance.
