## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> John Dewey's philosophy emphasized that learning is a process of active engagement with the environment. He argued that education should be centered around practical experiences and problem-solving rather than rote memorization.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In his view, schools should provide opportunities for students to apply what they learn in real-world situations, fostering critical thinking and adaptability. This approach aims to prepare individuals for a dynamic and changing world.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Dewey's ideas have influenced various educational theories, including constructivism and progressive education.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In practice, Dewey's philosophy has led to the development of project-based learning and inquiry-based teaching methods in classrooms.

> [!example] Application
> Educators now often design curricula that encourage students to explore real-world problems and develop solutions through hands-on activities.
