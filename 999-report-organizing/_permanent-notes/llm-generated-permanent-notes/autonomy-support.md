# autonomy-support

> [!definition] autonomy-support
> - **Key-Term**: [[autonomy-support]]
> - **Definition**: Autonomy-support refers to the provision of psychological support that respects and facilitates individuals' sense of autonomy, enabling them to make their own choices and take initiative in their actions.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Autonomy-support is a core component of Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that people are motivated by three innate psychological needs: competence, relatedness, and autonomy. Autonomy-supportive environments provide individuals with the freedom to explore and pursue goals according to their own values and interests.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, autonomy-support involves providing choice, encouraging self-regulation, and validating personal perspectives while avoiding controlling or pressuring behaviors that undermine an individual's sense of volition.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include distinguishing between autonomy-support and independence, as well as understanding the universality versus cultural specificity of these concepts within different contexts.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, teachers who practice autonomy-support can foster higher levels of intrinsic motivation among students, leading to better academic performance and engagement.

> [!example] Application
> In organizational management, leaders who adopt an autonomy-supportive style can enhance employee well-being and job satisfaction, reducing turnover rates.
