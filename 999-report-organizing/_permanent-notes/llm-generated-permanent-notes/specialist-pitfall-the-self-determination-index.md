# Specialist Pitfall: The "Self-Determination Index"

> [!definition] Specialist Pitfall: The "Self-Determination Index"
> The 'Self-Determination Index' is a composite score derived by weighting and summing four regulatory styles in self-determination theory, but it is theoretically problematic as it assumes these styles lie on a single dimension of self-determination, which can misrepresent individual regulatory configurations.

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] Specialist Pitfall: The "Self-Determination Index"
> Researchers and practitioners sometimes compute a single "Relative Autonomy Index" or "Self-Determination Index" by weighting the four regulatory styles (typically -2, -1, +1, +2 from external through integrated, often with intrinsic motivation added at +3) and summing. This composite is convenient but theoretically problematic: it presupposes that the four styles lie on a single dimension of "self-determination amount," which is precisely what OIT denies. A person with high external regulation and high integrated regulation for the same behavior (a configuration that can occur, for example, in a clinical context where externally imposed treatment has been integrated into the patient's recovery identity) may receive an average composite score that misrepresents both regulations; a person with low external and low integrated may receive a similar score for entirely different reasons. Specialists should interpret RAI scores with caution and prefer profile-level analyses where possible.
