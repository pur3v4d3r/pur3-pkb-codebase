# motivational-interviewing

> [!definition] motivational-interviewing
> - **Key-Term**: [[motivational-interviewing]]
> - **Definition**: Motivational interviewing is a client-centered counseling style for eliciting behavior change by helping clients to explore and resolve ambivalence.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Motivational interviewing is rooted in the belief that individuals have the capacity to make positive changes but may struggle with internal conflicts or lack of motivation. It aims to facilitate this process through empathetic listening, open-ended questions, affirmations, and reflective listening techniques.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> During a motivational interview, the counselor works collaboratively with the client to identify their own reasons for change, explore any ambivalence they might have about making changes, and build their confidence in taking steps towards their goals. This approach is particularly effective in settings such as healthcare, education, and sports where behavior modification is necessary.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the importance of maintaining a non-judgmental stance, recognizing and validating the client's perspective, and fostering self-efficacy and autonomy.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, motivational interviewing can lead to improved patient engagement and adherence to treatment plans.

> [!example] Application
> In educational contexts, it can enhance student motivation and academic performance by addressing underlying issues that may be hindering their progress.
