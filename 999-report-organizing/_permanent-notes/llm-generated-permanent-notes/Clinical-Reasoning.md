# Clinical-Reasoning

> [!definition] Clinical-Reasoning
> - **Key-Term**: [[Clinical-Reasoning]]
> - **Definition**: Clinical reasoning is the process by which healthcare professionals use their knowledge, experience, and clinical judgment to make decisions about patient care based on individual patient circumstances.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Clinical reasoning involves integrating various sources of information such as patient history, physical examination findings, diagnostic test results, and current medical literature. It is a dynamic process that requires the ability to generate hypotheses, evaluate evidence, and make informed decisions in complex and often uncertain situations.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This process typically includes several stages: problem identification, data collection, hypothesis generation, hypothesis testing, decision making, and evaluation of outcomes. Clinicians must also consider ethical, legal, and patient preference factors when formulating their reasoning.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of heuristics (mental shortcuts) in clinical reasoning, which can sometimes lead to biases or errors if not properly managed.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In practice, effective clinical reasoning can improve diagnostic accuracy and treatment outcomes, leading to better patient care. It also helps clinicians adapt their approach based on individual patient needs and circumstances.

> [!example] Application
> Clinical reasoning is crucial for decision-making in emergency situations where time is limited and information may be incomplete.
