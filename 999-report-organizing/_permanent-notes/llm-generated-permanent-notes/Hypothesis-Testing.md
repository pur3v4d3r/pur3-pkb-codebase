# Hypothesis-Testing

> [!definition] Hypothesis-Testing
> - **Key-Term**: [[Hypothesis-Testing]]
> - **Definition**: Hypothesis-testing is a statistical method used to evaluate the validity of a hypothesis by comparing observed data against expected outcomes under the null hypothesis, and determining whether the results are statistically significant.
> - **Domain**: philosophy
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Hypothesis-testing provides a framework for making decisions based on evidence. It involves formulating a null hypothesis (typically stating no effect or no difference) and an alternative hypothesis (stating there is an effect or difference).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The process includes collecting data, calculating test statistics, and comparing them to critical values from probability distributions to decide whether the observed data supports rejecting the null hypothesis.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the choice of significance level (alpha), the type of error (Type I and Type II), and the power of the test. Sub-variants like one-tailed and two-tailed tests, as well as different statistical methods such as t-tests or chi-squared tests, are used depending on the data and research question.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In scientific research, hypothesis-testing helps researchers determine whether their findings are likely due to chance or if they reflect a true effect.

> [!example] Application
> In quality control, it is used to ensure that products meet certain standards by testing samples against expected specifications.
