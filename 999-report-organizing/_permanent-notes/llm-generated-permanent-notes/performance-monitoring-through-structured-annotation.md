# Performance Monitoring Through Structured Annotation

> [!definition] Performance Monitoring Through Structured Annotation
> Performance Monitoring Through Structured Annotation involves systematically marking key claims, definitions, uncertainties, and connections within educational materials using standardized tags to enhance metacognitive awareness and normalize the recording of uncertainties.

## Examples

> [!example] Performance Monitoring Through Structured Annotation
> Consider a learner reading a research paper on [[Desirable Difficulties (Robert Bjork, 1994)|desirable difficulties]] in education. Using a PKB annotation workflow, the learner applies structured callouts to the source material:
>
> - `[!key-claim]` — "Introducing difficulties during learning (spacing, interleaving, retrieval practice) slows initial acquisition but enhances long-term retention"
> - `[!definition]` — "Desirable difficulties are learning conditions that create challenge during encoding but improve durable retention and transfer"
> - `[!uncertainty]` — "The boundary conditions for when difficulty becomes *undesirable* are not well specified — what determines the tipping point?"
> - `[!connection]` — "This connects to cognitive load theory: desirable difficulties must increase *germane* load without exceeding total capacity"
>
> The `[!uncertainty]` annotation is particularly significant. By creating a structured space for recording uncertainties, the template *normalises* uncertainty as a legitimate and valuable monitoring output, rather than something to be ignored or concealed. The learner who records uncertainties is engaging in more honest monitoring than the learner who annotates only what is understood.
