# Self-Determination-Theory-in-Education

> [!definition] Self-Determination-Theory-in-Education
> - **Key-Term**: [[Self-Determination-Theory-in-Education]]
> - **Definition**: Self-Determination-Theory-in-Education is a framework that explains how intrinsic motivation and well-being are fostered through the satisfaction of three basic psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness in educational settings.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The theory posits that individuals have an inherent drive to direct their own lives, develop skills, and establish positive social connections. In education, this means creating environments where students can experience these needs through meaningful choices, challenges, and supportive relationships.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Teachers and educators can apply the theory by providing opportunities for student choice in assignments, offering constructive feedback that enhances competence, and encouraging collaboration to foster a sense of relatedness.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, where intrinsic motivation is more likely to lead to sustained engagement and better learning outcomes. The theory also emphasizes the importance of context-specific strategies to meet these needs effectively.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors can design lessons that allow students to make choices about their learning paths, which enhances autonomy.

> [!example] Application
> Formative assessments should focus on providing feedback that helps students understand and improve their performance, thereby supporting competence.

> [!example] Application
> Creating a classroom culture where students feel connected to each other and the teacher is crucial for relatedness.
