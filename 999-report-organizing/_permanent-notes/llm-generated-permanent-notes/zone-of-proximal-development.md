# zone-of-proximal-development

> [!definition] zone-of-proximal-development
> - **Key-Term**: [[zone-of-proximal-development]]
> - **Definition**: The zone of proximal development refers to the range of tasks that a learner can perform with guidance and support from more knowledgeable others, but cannot yet do alone.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept was introduced by Lev Vygotsky in his work on child psychology. It describes the difference between what a learner can achieve independently and what they can accomplish with assistance.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, it is used to tailor educational experiences so that learners are challenged but not overwhelmed, promoting effective learning through guided discovery and collaboration.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of scaffolding, where support is gradually removed as the learner becomes more competent. This concept also emphasizes the importance of social interaction in cognitive development.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Educators can use this framework to design lessons that are appropriately challenging, ensuring students are neither bored nor frustrated.

> [!example] Application
> In coaching and mentoring, understanding the zone of proximal development helps practitioners provide targeted support that enhances learning without doing too much for the learner.
