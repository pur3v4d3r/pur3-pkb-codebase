# internalization-continuum

> [!definition] internalization-continuum
> - **Key-Term**: [[internalization-continuum]]
> - **Definition**: The internalization-continuum refers to the process by which individuals adopt and integrate external motivations into their own personal values, leading to more autonomous forms of motivation over time.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept is rooted in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), particularly as articulated by Deci and Ryan. It describes a spectrum from controlled to autonomous internalization, where individuals initially engage with external rewards or pressures but eventually adopt these motivations as part of their self-concept.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This process involves three key psychological needs: competence, autonomy, and relatedness. As people experience satisfaction in meeting these needs through the external motivation, they become more intrinsically motivated to continue engaging in the behavior.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> There are different sub-variants such as introjected regulation (where individuals engage in behaviors to avoid negative consequences) and identified regulation (where individuals see the value in the behavior).

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding the internalization-continuum can help educators design interventions that promote intrinsic motivation by addressing students' psychological needs.

> [!example] Application
> In organizational contexts, managers can use this concept to foster a culture where employees are more intrinsically motivated and engaged in their work.
