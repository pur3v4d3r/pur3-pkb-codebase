# Flavell's-Metacognitive-Framework

> [!definition] Flavell's-Metacognitive-Framework
> - **Key-Term**: [[Flavell's-Metacognitive-Framework]]
> - **Definition**: Flavell's Metacognitive Framework is a theoretical model that describes the cognitive processes involved in monitoring and regulating one’s own thinking, including planning, monitoring, and evaluating one’s understanding and performance.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The framework posits that metacognition involves three main components: knowledge about cognition (understanding how different types of tasks are processed), regulation of cognition (strategies to control cognitive processes), and meta-awareness (self-knowledge and self-assessment).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> It works by enabling individuals to plan their approach to a task, monitor their progress during the task, and evaluate the effectiveness of their strategies after completing it. This process helps in adjusting future approaches based on past experiences.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between procedural knowledge (knowing how to do something) and declarative knowledge (understanding why one does something). The framework also emphasizes the role of self-assessment in metacognitive processes.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding Flavell's Metacognitive Framework can help teachers design instructional strategies that promote students' ability to monitor and regulate their learning.

> [!example] Application
> It is applied in cognitive-behavioral therapy to help individuals recognize and change maladaptive thought patterns.
