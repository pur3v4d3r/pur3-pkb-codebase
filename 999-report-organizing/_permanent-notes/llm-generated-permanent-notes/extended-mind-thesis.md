# Extended Mind Thesis

> [!definition] Extended Mind Thesis
> A philosophical position, advanced by Clark and Chalmers (1998), holding that cognitive processes can extend beyond the biological brain to include external artefacts when those artefacts are reliably available, regularly consulted, and functionally integrated into cognitive workflows. The thesis challenges the assumption that cognition is skull-bound.
>
> **Boundary:** The extended mind thesis is a philosophical framework, not an empirical finding. It remains contested in philosophy of mind. Its application in this report is as a *justificatory framework* for treating PKB scaffolds as constitutive (not merely supportive) components of metacognitive architecture.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Provides the philosophical foundation for the report's central thesis that PKBs are not merely tools that *assist* metacognition but can become *part of* the metacognitive system.
>
> **See also:** [[externalized-metacognition]], [[extended-metacognition]], [[cognitive-offloading]]
