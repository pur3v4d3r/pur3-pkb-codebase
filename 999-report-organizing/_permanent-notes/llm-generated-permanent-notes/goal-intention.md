# goal-intention

> [!definition] goal-intention
> - **Key-Term**: [[goal-intention]]
> - **Definition**: A goal-intention is a specific plan to initiate and maintain behavior towards achieving a desired outcome, often involving the formation of implementation intentions which specify when, where, and how one will take action toward the goal.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Goal-intentions are central in self-regulation theory, particularly in the Action Phase Model proposed by Heckhausen and Gollwitzer (1987). They serve as a bridge between general goals and specific actions needed to achieve them. Implementation intentions, which are part of goal-intentions, enhance the likelihood of goal attainment by specifying when, where, and how one will take action.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> For example, if someone's goal is to exercise more regularly, an implementation intention might be 'I will go to the gym immediately after work on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays.' This specificity increases the chances that the person will actually go to the gym as planned.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between proximal (near-term) and distal (long-term) goals. Proximal goals are more likely to be influenced by implementation intentions, while distal goals require additional strategies for maintenance.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, goal-intentions can help students plan their study schedules effectively, leading to better academic performance.

> [!example] Application
> In health behavior change, forming specific implementation intentions can significantly improve adherence to exercise and dietary plans.
