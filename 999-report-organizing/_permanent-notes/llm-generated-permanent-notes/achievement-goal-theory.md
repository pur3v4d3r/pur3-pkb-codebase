# achievement-goal-theory

> [!definition] achievement-goal-theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[achievement-goal-theory]]
> - **Definition**: Achievement-goal theory is a psychological framework that explains how individuals set and pursue goals related to achievement, focusing on their motivation and the impact of different goal orientations on performance and well-being.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The theory posits that people can have two primary types of goal orientations: mastery-approach (aiming for improvement) and performance-approach (focusing on outperforming others).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Mastery-approach goals lead to better learning outcomes, higher intrinsic motivation, and more adaptive coping strategies compared to performance-approach goals, which can foster anxiety and a fear of failure.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between mastery-avoidance (aiming to avoid failure) and performance-avoidance (focusing on not looking bad relative to others).

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, fostering mastery-approach goals can enhance student engagement and academic achievement.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace environments, promoting a culture that values learning over competition can improve employee well-being and productivity.
