# SR Seed 3 — Distinction

> [!definition] SR Seed 3 — Distinction
> SR Seed 3 — Distinction refers to a level of educational scaffolding that is carefully tailored to support advanced learners by fading instructional aids in a way that enhances metacognitive monitoring and control, thereby optimizing learning outcomes.

## Flashcards

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 3 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the expertise reversal effect and why does it matter for curriculum design?
> **A:** The expertise reversal effect (Kalyuga et al.) occurs when instructional scaffolding that helps novices actually impedes learning for more advanced learners because it creates redundant cognitive processing. It matters because it demonstrates that scaffolding must be faded, not merely reduced — maintained scaffolding actively harms advanced learners.
> **Source:** Section 4
> **Difficulty:** Advanced
> **Tags:** #cognitive-load #scaffolding #expertise-reversal

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 3 — Distinction
> **Q:** What is the difference between metacognitive monitoring and metacognitive control?
> **A:** Monitoring detects the current state of understanding or performance (e.g., "I don't understand this paragraph"). Control selects and deploys strategies to address detected gaps (e.g., "I'll re-read more slowly and try self-explanation"). Effective self-regulation requires tight coupling of both.
> **Source:** Section 2.2
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
