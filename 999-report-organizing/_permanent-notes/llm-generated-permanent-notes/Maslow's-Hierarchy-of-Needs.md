# Maslow's-Hierarchy-of-Needs

> [!definition] Maslow's-Hierarchy-of-Needs
> - **Key-Term**: [[Maslow's-Hierarchy-of-Needs]]
> - **Definition**: Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs is a motivational theory in psychology comprising a five-tier model of human needs, often depicted as a pyramid where each level must be satisfied before the next higher one can be addressed.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The hierarchy starts with the most basic physiological needs such as food and shelter at the base, followed by safety needs like security and stability. Social needs for love and belonging come next, then esteem needs including respect and recognition from others. At the top is self-actualization, the need to fulfill one's potential.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Maslow proposed that individuals must satisfy lower-level needs before moving on to higher ones. For example, a person cannot focus on social relationships if their basic physiological needs are not met. The theory suggests that once these needs are fulfilled, people seek to achieve self-actualization and personal growth.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> While Maslow's hierarchy is widely known, it has been criticized for being too universalistic and not accounting for cultural differences in the prioritization of needs.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In management and organizational behavior, understanding this hierarchy can help leaders design environments that support employee well-being by addressing basic needs before focusing on more advanced ones.

> [!example] Application
> Educators use Maslow's hierarchy to create supportive learning environments where students' basic needs are met so they can focus on academic achievement.
