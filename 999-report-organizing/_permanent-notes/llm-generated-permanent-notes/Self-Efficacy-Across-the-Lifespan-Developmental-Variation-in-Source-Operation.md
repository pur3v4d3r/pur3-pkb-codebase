# Self-Efficacy-Across-the-Lifespan-Developmental-Variation-in-Source-Operation

> [!definition] Self-Efficacy-Across-the-Lifespan-Developmental-Variation-in-Source-Operation
> - **Key-Term**: [[Self-Efficacy-Across-the-Lifespan-Developmental-Variation-in-Source-Operation]]
> - **Definition**: Self-efficacy across the lifespan refers to an individual's belief in their capability to organize and execute courses of action required to manage prospective situations, varying by age and developmental stage.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept of self-efficacy across the lifespan is rooted in Albert Bandura's social cognitive theory. It posits that individuals' beliefs about their own efficacy change as they grow older and encounter different challenges.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Self-efficacy is developed through experiences, modeling, verbal persuasion, and emotional arousal. As people age, these sources of self-efficacy may vary, influencing how they approach tasks and challenges.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the varying weights individuals place on different sources of efficacy (e.g., mastery experiences vs. vicarious experiences) throughout their lives.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding developmental variations in self-efficacy can inform strategies for fostering resilience and motivation among students at different ages.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical practice, recognizing the sources of self-efficacy in patients can help tailor therapeutic interventions to address specific needs and build confidence.
