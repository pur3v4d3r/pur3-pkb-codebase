# summative-assessment

> [!definition] summative-assessment
> - **Key-Term**: [[summative-assessment]]
> - **Definition**: Summative assessment is an evaluation conducted at the end of an instructional unit to determine what students have learned and whether they can apply their knowledge in new situations, often used for grading purposes.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Summative assessments are typically high-stakes tests or projects that measure student learning outcomes against specific criteria. They serve as a final evaluation of the educational program's effectiveness and provide a snapshot of what students have mastered by the end of a course, unit, or academic year.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, summative assessments can take various forms such as standardized tests, final exams, essays, presentations, or portfolios. These evaluations are often used to make decisions about student advancement, certification, or awarding grades and diplomas.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the fact that while formative assessments focus on ongoing feedback and improvement, summative assessments emphasize the completion of learning objectives and provide a comprehensive evaluation of student achievement.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors can use summative assessment results to inform decisions about course content and instructional strategies for future classes.

> [!example] Application
> Administrators may use these evaluations to assess the effectiveness of educational programs or curricula, leading to potential changes in teaching methods or resource allocation.
