# Cognitive-Behavioural-Therapy

> [!definition] Cognitive-Behavioural-Therapy
> - **Key-Term**: [[Cognitive-Behavioural-Therapy]]
> - **Definition**: Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is a form of psychotherapy that focuses on the interplay between thoughts, feelings, and behaviors to address psychological issues such as depression and anxiety by changing unhelpful cognitive distortions and behaviors.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Paragraph 1: Foundational context and core mechanism. CBT was developed in the mid-20th century and is based on the idea that our thoughts, feelings, and actions are interconnected. It aims to help individuals identify and challenge negative thought patterns (cognitive distortions) and develop more adaptive behaviors.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Paragraph 2: How it works or is applied in practice. CBT typically involves structured sessions where therapists guide clients through identifying problematic thoughts, emotions, and behaviors, followed by strategies to modify these elements. Techniques include cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, and exposure therapy.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Paragraph 3: Key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots. There are several variants of CBT, such as Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy (REBT) and Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), each with its own specific focus and techniques.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Implication 1: concrete application or real-world consequence. CBT has been widely used in clinical settings to treat various mental health conditions, showing effectiveness through numerous empirical studies.

> [!example] Application
> Implication 2: a second distinct application. It is also applied in educational and organizational contexts to improve problem-solving skills and emotional regulation.
