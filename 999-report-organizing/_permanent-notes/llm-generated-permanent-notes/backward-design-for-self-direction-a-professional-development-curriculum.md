# Backward Design for Self-Direction: A Professional Development Curriculum

> [!definition] Backward Design for Self-Direction: A Professional Development Curriculum
> Backward Design for Self-Direction: A professional development curriculum that employs backward design principles to foster self-directed learning among participants, focusing on developing their ability to independently identify, address, and assess gaps in their knowledge and practice.

## Examples

> [!example] Backward Design for Self-Direction: A Professional Development Curriculum
> A professional development curriculum for early-career teachers, designed using backward design principles with self-direction as an explicit goal, might specify its ultimate transfer goal as: "Graduates will independently identify gaps in their professional knowledge, locate and evaluate resources for addressing those gaps, design and implement professional learning experiences, and assess the impact of their learning on their practice — without institutional direction or support." Working backward from this goal, the curriculum designer would identify evidence of this capacity (e.g., a self-designed professional learning portfolio), then design learning experiences that progressively develop each component (e.g., structured exercises in need identification, guided resource evaluation, facilitated learning design, and mentored self-assessment) before systematically withdrawing the facilitation, guidance, and mentoring across the programme.
