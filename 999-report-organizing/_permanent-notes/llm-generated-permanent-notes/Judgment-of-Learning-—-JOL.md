# Judgment-of-Learning-—-JOL

> [!definition] Judgment-of-Learning-—-JOL
> - **Key-Term**: [[Judgment-of-Learning-—-JOL]]
> - **Definition**: A Judgment-of-Learning (JOL) is an individual's subjective estimate of how well they have learned or will remember information after studying it, often used in educational and cognitive psychology to assess metacognitive processes.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Judgments-of-Learning are a fundamental aspect of metacognition, the ability to monitor and regulate one’s own learning. They involve individuals estimating their level of understanding or memory retention following an attempt at learning new information.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, JOLs can be used in various educational settings to gauge students' confidence in their knowledge, which can inform further study strategies. For example, if a student judges that they have learned material well, they might move on to new content; conversely, if the judgment is low, additional review may be warranted.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the accuracy of these judgments and how they relate to actual performance. Research has shown that individuals often overestimate their learning (the illusion-of-knowing effect) or underestimate it (the feeling-of-knowledge effect).

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, JOLs can help students develop better study habits by encouraging them to reflect on their learning and adjust their strategies accordingly.

> [!example] Application
> In artificial intelligence, self-monitoring systems use similar principles to assess the reliability of information or predictions.
