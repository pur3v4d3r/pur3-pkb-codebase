# Metacognition-—-Foundational-Concepts

> [!definition] Metacognition-—-Foundational-Concepts
> - **Key-Term**: [[Metacognition-—-Foundational-Concepts]]
> - **Definition**: Metacognition refers to the ability to think about and reflect on one's own cognitive processes, including planning, monitoring, and evaluating these processes in order to enhance learning and problem-solving effectiveness.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Metacognition involves self-awareness of one’s thought processes and strategies used during learning. It includes understanding what one knows or does not know, setting goals for learning, and adjusting study methods accordingly.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, metacognitive skills are applied in various domains such as academic settings where students plan their study sessions, monitor their progress, and evaluate the effectiveness of different learning techniques. In athletic coaching, coaches help athletes develop self-awareness about their performance to improve skill development.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between procedural (knowing how) and declarative (knowing that) knowledge in metacognition. Theoretical roots trace back to cognitive psychology but have been further developed through interdisciplinary research.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering metacognitive skills can lead to more effective learning strategies and improved academic performance.

> [!example] Application
> In professional settings, enhancing metacognitive abilities can improve decision-making processes and problem-solving capabilities.
