# The Three Basic Psychological Needs

> [!definition] The Three Basic Psychological Needs
> - **[[autonomy-need|Autonomy]]:** The need to experience one's behavior as self-endorsed, as an authentic expression of one's own values and interests, as flowing from oneself rather than being imposed from outside. Autonomy in SDT is **not** independence or self-sufficiency; it is volition, the felt sense that "this is what I want to be doing."
> - **[[competence-need|Competence]]:** The need to experience oneself as effective in interaction with the environment, capable of producing desired outcomes, growing in capability over time. Competence is satisfied not by ease but by the right level of challenge — by experiences in which the person can see their capability expanding.
> - **[[relatedness-need|Relatedness]]:** The need to experience oneself as connected to others who matter, as cared for and as caring, as belonging to a relational matrix in which one's existence is acknowledged and valued. Relatedness is satisfied by warm, responsive, non-contingent connection, and is undermined by [[Conditional Regard|conditional regard]] regardless of the warmth of the underlying relationship.
