# SR Seed 1 — Definition

> [!definition] SR Seed 1 — Definition
> SR Seed 1 refers to a foundational element in cognitive psychology that addresses the limited capacity of working memory and its relationship with long-term memory, forming the basis for instructional design principles.

## Flashcards

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 1 — Definition
> **Q:** What is cognitive load theory, and what architectural constraint does it address?
> **A:** Cognitive load theory is an instructional design framework that derives design principles from the limited capacity of working memory (~4±1 elements for novel information) and its relationship to the unlimited capacity of long-term memory. It predicts that instructional effectiveness depends on managing the processing demands imposed on working memory during learning.
> **Source:** Section 1, Lexicon (A.1)
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Tags:** #clt #definition #working-memory
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 1 — Definition
> **Q:** What is the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression and what are its five phases?
> **A:** An integrative framework describing how curriculum transforms dependent learners into self-directed ones through five phases: (1) Guided Dependence, (2) Structured Exploration, (3) Negotiated Autonomy, (4) Supported Independence, and (5) Autonomous Sovereignty. The progression transfers regulatory authority from the curriculum to the learner through scaffolding-fading.
> **Source:** Section 4
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate
> **Tags:** #curriculum-design #SDL #scaffolding
> *— [[curriculum-design-for-lifelong-learning-and-self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 1 — Definition
> **Q:** What are the three phases of Zimmerman's cyclical model of self-regulated learning?
> **A:** Forethought (planning, goal-setting, strategy selection before learning), Performance (monitoring and strategy deployment during learning), and Self-Reflection (evaluation, calibration, and adaptive planning after learning).
> **Source:** Section 2.3
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Tags:** #srl #zimmerman #self-regulated-learning
> *— [[pkb-metacognitive-scaffolding-for-study-and-planning-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 1 — Definition
> **Q:** What is the levels-of-processing framework, and what does it predict about memory durability?
> **A:** The levels-of-processing framework (Craik & Lockhart, 1972) proposes that memory durability is determined by the depth of processing at encoding — deep semantic processing produces more durable traces than shallow perceptual processing.
> **Source:** Section 2
> **Difficulty:** Basic
> **Tags:** #encoding, #levels-of-processing, #memory
> *— [[pkb-pkm-planning-and-cognitive-science-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 1 — Definition
> **Q:** What are the three basic psychological needs identified by Self-Determination Theory?
> **A:** Autonomy (self-endorsement of behavior), Competence (perceived effectiveness), and Relatedness (genuine connection and care).
> **Source:** Section 2
> **Difficulty:** Basic
