# The Lindeman-Dewey Bridge

> [!definition] The Lindeman-Dewey Bridge
> The Lindeman-Dewey Bridge refers to the shared epistemological foundation between John Dewey's experience-based education and Edgar Dale's situation-centered curriculum design, highlighting their common commitment to knowledge as an active construction by individuals rather than a passive transfer.

## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] The Lindeman-Dewey Bridge
> What is often underappreciated in histories of adult education is the degree to which Lindeman's situation-centred curriculum design and Dewey's experience-based education share a common epistemological commitment — namely, the conviction that knowledge is not a substance to be transferred but a relationship between a person and their world that must be actively constructed. When one recognises this shared foundation, the entire trajectory from [[progressive-education|progressive education]] to [[andragogy|andragogy]] to [[heutagogy|heutagogy]] appears not as a series of independent innovations but as the progressive working-out of a single philosophical insight through increasingly specific institutional contexts. Each generation of theorists, encountering the limitations of their predecessors' formulations, extends the logic further — but the root insight is Dewey's, and it concerns the nature of experience, not merely the age of the learner.
> *— [[curriculum-design-for-lifelong-learning-and-self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
