# Debiasing-Interventions

> [!definition] Debiasing-Interventions
> - **Key-Term**: [[Debiasing-Interventions]]
> - **Definition**: Debiasing interventions are techniques designed to reduce the influence of cognitive biases on decision-making processes, often by prompting individuals to consider alternative perspectives or follow structured procedures.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Debiasing interventions operate under the assumption that people's judgments and decisions can be systematically biased due to mental shortcuts (heuristics) and other psychological factors. These techniques aim to mitigate such biases through various methods, including cognitive forcing functions, which guide individuals to think more carefully about their assumptions.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> For example, one common debiasing intervention is the use of checklists or decision trees that force decision-makers to consider all relevant information before making a choice. Another method involves prompting individuals to imagine alternative scenarios or perspectives to challenge their initial judgments.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the fact that while these interventions can be effective, they may not always eliminate biases entirely and can sometimes introduce new ones if not used appropriately.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In clinical reasoning, debiasing interventions can improve diagnostic accuracy by reducing overconfidence in initial impressions.

> [!example] Application
> In machine learning, debiasing techniques can help reduce algorithmic bias by ensuring that training data and model outputs are free from systematic errors.
