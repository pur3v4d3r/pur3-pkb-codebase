# Bridging to Prior Knowledge

> [!definition] Bridging to Prior Knowledge
> Bridging to Prior Knowledge involves leveraging existing cognitive structures and skills to facilitate learning and knowledge management, emphasizing the role of working memory, declarative and procedural knowledge, and metacognitive monitoring.

## Additional Material

> [!schema-activation] Bridging to Prior Knowledge
> The reader already familiar with [[Cognitive Architecture|cognitive architecture]] — the functional constraints imposed by [[working-memory|working memory]] capacity, the distinction between [[declarative-memory|declarative]] and [[procedural-memory|procedural knowledge]], and the role of [[Schema Construction|schema construction]] in expert performance — possesses the conceptual scaffolding necessary to engage with this report's central argument. Consider how existing knowledge of [[Desirable Difficulties (Robert Bjork, 1994)|retrieval practice]] and [[spaced-repetition|spaced repetition]] already implies a set of design constraints for any system that aspires to support durable learning. Consider further how the [[metacognition|metacognitive]] monitoring that skilled learners perform — assessing what they know, what they do not know, and what strategies might close the gap — represents precisely the kind of self-regulatory activity that a well-designed PKM system should externalize, scaffold, and eventually automate. The guiding question for this report is therefore not whether cognitive science is relevant to PKM planning, but rather: *What specific cognitive mechanisms must a personal knowledge base be designed to support, and what are the consequences of ignoring them?*
