# Organisational Learning and Professional Development

> [!definition] Organisational Learning and Professional Development
> Organisational Learning and Professional Development involves a structured approach to training new employees that gradually reduces external support, fostering self-regulation and metacognitive skills, while considering organizational constraints.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Organisational Learning and Professional Development
> The scaffolding-fading mechanism maps directly onto the challenge of developing self-directed professionals within organisations. New employees typically receive extensive onboarding — structured training, mentoring, standard operating procedures, performance checklists — that functions as scaffolding within the [[zone-of-proximal-development|zone of proximal development]]. The question that most organisations fail to address systematically is when and how this scaffolding should be faded. Organisations that maintain high levels of procedural prescription indefinitely produce compliant but dependent professionals who cannot adapt to novel situations, while organisations that remove support prematurely produce anxious, undertrained workers prone to costly errors. The Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression suggests a deliberate, phased withdrawal of organisational scaffolding — from prescribed procedures through guided judgement to autonomous professional practice — with explicit attention to developing the metacognitive and self-regulatory capacities that enable professionals to manage their own continued development.
>
> **Boundary condition:** Organisational learning operates within power dynamics, incentive structures, and liability frameworks that constrain the degree of autonomy that can be granted, regardless of the learner's developmental readiness.
