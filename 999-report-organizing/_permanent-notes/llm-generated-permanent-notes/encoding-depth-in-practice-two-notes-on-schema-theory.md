# Encoding Depth in Practice: Two Notes on Schema Theory

> [!definition] Encoding Depth in Practice: Two Notes on Schema Theory
> Encoding depth in practice refers to the extent to which note-taking captures not just surface-level information but also the underlying schemas and implications that shape understanding, thereby enhancing future retrieval.

## Examples

> [!example] Encoding Depth in Practice: Two Notes on Schema Theory
> Consider two notes on [[schema-theory-and-knowledge-organization|schema theory]], both created from the same source chapter. The first is a summary: "Schema theory, developed by Bartlett and later Rumelhart, proposes that knowledge is organized into schemas — mental frameworks that guide perception and memory." The second is a note-made artifact: "Schema theory suggests that new information is not stored in isolation but is *assimilated into existing schemas* — a process that necessarily distorts the original in the direction of prior knowledge. This explains why two readers of the same text may extract genuinely different meanings: their schemas filter differently. The implication for PKM is that note-making should make the schema being activated *explicit*, so that future retrieval can account for the interpretive lens through which the information was encoded." The second note encodes at dramatically greater depth: it generates an implication, connects to PKM practice, and makes the schema transparent. Retrieval of the second note will be supported by a richer web of cues.
