# Creative Writing and Artistic Practice

> [!definition] Creative Writing and Artistic Practice
> Creative Writing and Artistic Practice refers to the process of generating original works through metacognitive scaffolding, including planning, monitoring, and reflection, which helps writers produce more polished work by systematically applying structural principles.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Creative Writing and Artistic Practice
> The creative process, despite its reputation for spontaneity, benefits from metacognitive scaffolding. Writers who plan story structure before drafting (forethought), track narrative coherence during drafting (monitoring), and revise based on systematic self-assessment after drafting (reflection) produce more polished work than writers who rely solely on inspiration. Writing templates, outlining systems, and revision checklists function as metacognitive scaffolds — and the developmental arc is vividly observable. Beginning writers need detailed outlines and comprehensive revision checklists; experienced writers have internalised structural principles and need scaffolding only for ambitious or unfamiliar projects.
>
> **Structural principle:** Scaffolded fading follows a universal developmental trajectory from external prompting through internalisation to independent self-regulation, regardless of domain content.
