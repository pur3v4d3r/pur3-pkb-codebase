# shame-vs.-guilt-in-attribution-theory

> [!definition] shame-vs.-guilt-in-attribution-theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[shame-vs.-guilt-in-attribution-theory]]
> - **Definition**: Shame-vs.-guilt-in-Attribution-Theory explores the psychological mechanisms by which individuals attribute blame to themselves or others, focusing on how feelings of shame and guilt influence these attributions in different contexts.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This theory posits that the attribution process is influenced by whether an individual feels shame (a negative evaluation of one's self-worth) or guilt (a negative evaluation of one's actions).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, it helps explain why people might attribute their failures to internal factors when feeling guilty but externalize blame when experiencing shame.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of social context and individual differences in how these emotions are processed.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Understanding this theory can aid in developing interventions for mental health issues, such as depression or anxiety, by addressing maladaptive attribution patterns.

> [!example] Application
> It also informs therapeutic approaches aimed at retraining negative thought patterns to promote healthier coping mechanisms.
