# replication-crisis-in-psychology

> [!definition] replication-crisis-in-psychology
> - **Key-Term**: [[replication-crisis-in-psychology]]
> - **Definition**: The replication crisis in psychology refers to the widespread inability of researchers to replicate the results of previous studies, casting doubt on the reliability and validity of many psychological findings.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The replication crisis emerged due to issues such as publication bias, p-hacking, and insufficient sample sizes. Researchers often struggle to reproduce key findings from earlier experiments, leading to a loss of confidence in the scientific literature.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This phenomenon is particularly problematic because it undermines the foundational principles of science, which rely on reproducibility to establish robust knowledge. Efforts have been made to address these issues through better research practices and more rigorous statistical methods.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include debates over the appropriate standards for replication studies and the role of preregistration in reducing bias.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In practice, this crisis has led to calls for reform in psychological research, including increased transparency, larger sample sizes, and more stringent statistical analyses.

> [!example] Application
> It also highlights the need for researchers to adopt best practices such as pre-registration of studies and open data sharing.

## Open Threads

> [!open-question] Question
