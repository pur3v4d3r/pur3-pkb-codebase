# The-Actor-Observer-Asymmetry-in-Social-Cognition

> [!definition] The-Actor-Observer-Asymmetry-in-Social-Cognition
> The tendency to attribute one's own actions to external factors while attributing others' actions to their personalities.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The-Actor-Observer-Asymmetry-in-Social-Cognition
> **Description:** A focused investigation of the actor-observer asymmetry — the systematic divergence between how people explain their own behaviour and how they explain others'. Would cover the original Jones and Nisbett (1972) formulation, subsequent empirical challenges and refinements, the informational vs. perceptual vs. motivational accounts, cultural moderation, and implications for conflict resolution, empathy training, and interpersonal understanding.
>
> **Connection to this report:** Section 5 treats the actor-observer asymmetry as one of several systematic distortions. A dedicated report would provide the depth this rich topic deserves, particularly the applied implications for interpersonal reasoning and conflict resolution.
