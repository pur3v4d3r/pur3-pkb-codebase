# The Most Common Misapplication

> [!definition] The Most Common Misapplication
> Setting a difficult performance goal on a task for which the performer lacks adequate strategy, leading to decreased performance due to unproductive effort mobilization.

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Most Common Misapplication
> Setting a difficult performance goal on a task for which the performer does not yet possess adequate strategy is the single most common and most damaging misapplication of Goal Setting Theory in practice; the theory's own mature formulation predicts that under such conditions performance will not merely fail to improve but will actively decline, because effort is mobilized in the absence of any direction in which it can be productively spent.
