# Self-Determination-Theory-—-Foundational-Overview

> [!definition] Self-Determination-Theory-—-Foundational-Overview
> - **Key-Term**: [[Self-Determination-Theory-—-Foundational-Overview]]
> - **Definition**: Self-Determination-Theory (SDT) is a psychological theory that posits that human beings have innate needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness, which when satisfied, lead to optimal functioning and well-being.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Paragraph 1: Foundational context and core mechanism. SDT was developed by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan in the late 20th century and is based on the idea that individuals are motivated by their basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness. When these needs are met, people experience intrinsic motivation and thrive.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Paragraph 2: How it works or is applied in practice. SDT suggests that when students feel autonomous (having choice and control), competent (feeling effective at tasks), and related (connected to others), they are more likely to engage in activities with intrinsic motivation, leading to better academic performance and psychological well-being.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Paragraph 3: Key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots. SDT distinguishes between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, where intrinsic motivation is driven by personal interest and enjoyment, while extrinsic motivation is driven by external rewards or pressures. The theory also emphasizes the importance of context in determining whether basic needs are satisfied.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 4
> Optional paragraph 4: Historical development or origin. SDT emerged from a combination of cognitive and humanistic psychology, integrating insights from both fields to explain motivation and well-being.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Implication 1: concrete application or real-world consequence. Teachers can foster intrinsic motivation by providing students with choices in their learning activities, offering opportunities for mastery, and creating a supportive classroom environment.

> [!example] Application
> Implication 2: a second distinct application. Organizations can enhance employee engagement and productivity by promoting autonomy, recognizing individual contributions, and fostering a sense of community.
