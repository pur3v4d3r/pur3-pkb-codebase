# The Scaffolding Paradox Within Inquiry

> [!definition] The Scaffolding Paradox Within Inquiry
> The Scaffolding Paradox Within Inquiry refers to the challenge faced by curriculum designers in providing sufficient structure for inquiry-based learning without constraining students' autonomy, ensuring productive engagement while avoiding frustration or compliance.

## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] The Scaffolding Paradox Within Inquiry
> The tension between structure and autonomy that characterises the broader challenge of curriculum design for self-direction is replicated in miniature within every instance of inquiry-based learning. The curriculum designer must provide enough structure to prevent the inquiry from becoming aimless (clear guiding questions, access to appropriate resources, milestones for progress, opportunities for feedback) while resisting the temptation to provide so much structure that the inquiry degenerates into a guided exercise with a predetermined endpoint. The practical consequence is that designing effective inquiry-based curriculum is considerably more demanding than designing traditional lecture-based curriculum, because it requires the designer to anticipate multiple possible inquiry trajectories while maintaining the structural conditions for productive engagement along each of them. This is why many implementations of inquiry-based learning fail not because the principle is wrong but because the scaffolding has been poorly calibrated — either too loose (producing frustration and disengagement) or too tight (producing compliance without genuine inquiry).
