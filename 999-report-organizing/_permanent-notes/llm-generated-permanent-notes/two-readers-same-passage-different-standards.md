# Two Readers, Same Passage, Different Standards

> [!definition] Two Readers, Same Passage, Different Standards
> Two readers encountering the same passage may interpret it differently based on varying standards of coherence, such as propositional versus structural cohesion.

## Examples

> [!example] Two Readers, Same Passage, Different Standards
> Consider two readers encountering a paragraph that argues, through a chain of three implicit inferences, for a counterintuitive claim. Reader A applies a propositional cohesion standard: each sentence parses, each connects loosely to the next, no coherence break is signaled. Reader A finishes the paragraph with the sense of having read it. Reader B applies a structural cohesion standard: as each sentence arrives, the meta level checks not only local connection but contribution to the developing argument, and when the third inference is left implicit, Reader B's monitoring system flags the gap as a structural coherence break. Reader B pauses, generates the bridging inference, integrates it into the situation model, and proceeds with a representation that captures the argument's actual structure. The text was the same; the reading apparatus was the same; the standard of coherence diverged, and with it the comprehension that resulted.
