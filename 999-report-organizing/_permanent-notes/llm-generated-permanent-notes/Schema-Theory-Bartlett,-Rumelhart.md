# Schema-Theory-Bartlett,-Rumelhart

> [!definition] Schema-Theory-Bartlett,-Rumelhart
> - **Key-Term**: [[Schema-Theory-Bartlett,-Rumelhart]]
> - **Definition**: Schema theory, developed by Frederic Bartlett and later expanded upon by David Rumelhart, is a cognitive model that explains how people organize and interpret information based on pre-existing knowledge structures called schemas.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Schemas are mental frameworks or templates that help us understand and process new information. They allow us to make sense of the world by categorizing experiences and filling in gaps with our existing knowledge.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> When we encounter new situations, we use these schemas to interpret the information, often leading to selective attention and memory biases. For example, a schema about 'doctor' might influence how we perceive and remember interactions with medical professionals.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Schemas can be simple or complex, ranging from basic concepts like 'dog' to more intricate frameworks such as 'political system'. They are dynamic and can evolve over time based on new experiences.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding schema theory helps in designing instructional materials that align with students' existing knowledge structures, making learning more effective.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical decision-making, recognizing the role of schemas can help healthcare providers avoid cognitive biases and make more informed decisions.
