# Zimmerman's-Cyclical-Model-of-Self-Regulation

> [!definition] Zimmerman's-Cyclical-Model-of-Self-Regulation
> - **Key-Term**: [[Zimmerman's-Cyclical-Model-of-Self-Regulation]]
> - **Definition**: A theoretical framework that describes the self-regulatory processes involved in achieving educational goals, emphasizing the cyclical nature of goal-setting, planning, and execution phases.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The model posits that self-regulation is a cyclic process involving three interrelated phases: forethought (planning), performance (execution), and reflection. In the forethought phase, individuals set goals and develop strategies to achieve them. During the performance phase, they implement these plans while monitoring their progress. The reflection phase involves evaluating outcomes and adjusting future plans accordingly.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This model is widely applied in educational settings to understand how students manage their learning processes, but it also has implications for other domains such as athletic practice, musical training, clinical behavior change, and engineering discipline.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between autonomous and controlled commitment, where autonomous commitment involves intrinsic motivation, while controlled commitment relies more on external factors.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Educators can use this model to design interventions that support students in setting realistic goals and developing effective strategies for achieving them.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, healthcare providers can apply the model to help patients set achievable health goals and monitor their progress over time.
