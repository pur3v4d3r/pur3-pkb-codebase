## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> In their 2006 paper, Vansteenkiste, Lens, & Deci delve into the concept of autonomous motivation, which is a core component of SDT. Autonomous motivation refers to engaging in activities because they are personally fulfilling or interesting, rather than due to external pressures or rewards.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> They argue that fostering environments and practices that support autonomy—such as providing choice, promoting self-determination, and valuing intrinsic motivations—can lead to higher levels of well-being and better performance outcomes. This is achieved by satisfying the basic psychological needs for competence, relatedness, and autonomy.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The study highlights how different types of motivation (e.g., amotivation, external regulation, introjected regulation) can be transformed into more autonomous forms through supportive conditions.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
