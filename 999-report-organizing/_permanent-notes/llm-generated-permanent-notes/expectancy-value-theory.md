# expectancy-value-theory

> [!definition] expectancy-value-theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[expectancy-value-theory]]
> - **Definition**: Expectancy-value theory is a psychological framework that explains how individuals make decisions based on their expectations of success and the value they place on the outcomes of those decisions.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The theory posits that people are more likely to engage in activities or pursue goals when they believe they have a high probability of success (expectancy) and when the outcome is valuable to them (value).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> For example, a student might choose to study for an exam if they expect to do well and value good grades.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The theory also includes sub-variants that consider additional factors such as effort and utility.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding expectancy-value can help educators design interventions that increase students' motivation by enhancing their belief in their ability to succeed and the perceived value of academic achievement.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace settings, it can guide managers in creating environments where employees feel competent and motivated.
