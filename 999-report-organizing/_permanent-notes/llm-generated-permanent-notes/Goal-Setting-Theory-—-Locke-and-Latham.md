# Goal-Setting-Theory-—-Locke-and-Latham

> [!definition] Goal-Setting-Theory-—-Locke-and-Latham
> - **Key-Term**: [[Goal-Setting-Theory-—-Locke-and-Latham]]
> - **Definition**: Goal-Setting-Theory-—-Locke-and-Latham is a psychological theory that suggests specific and challenging goals lead to better performance than easy or vague ones, provided the goals are accepted by the individual.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The theory posits that setting clear, specific, and challenging goals enhances motivation and performance. Goals must be both difficult enough to challenge the individual but not so hard as to be discouraging.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Individuals who set such goals tend to perform better because they focus their attention more effectively on relevant tasks and persist longer in the face of challenges.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The theory also emphasizes the importance of commitment, feedback, and task complexity in achieving optimal performance.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, setting specific learning objectives can improve student performance by guiding their efforts and providing clear targets for improvement.

> [!example] Application
> In sports, coaches use goal-setting to enhance athletes' focus and motivation, leading to better training outcomes and competitive performance.
