# implicit-theory-of-intelligence

> [!definition] implicit-theory-of-intelligence
> - **Key-Term**: [[implicit-theory-of-intelligence]]
> - **Definition**: An implicit theory of intelligence refers to an individual's underlying belief about the nature and malleability of intelligence, which can be either fixed (believing that intelligence is stable and unchangeable) or growth-oriented (believing that intelligence can develop through effort and learning).
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept was introduced by Carol Dweck and her colleagues to explain how people's beliefs about the nature of intelligence influence their motivation, behavior, and emotional responses in academic settings.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Individuals with a fixed theory of intelligence tend to avoid challenges, fear failure, and show less persistence when faced with difficulties. In contrast, those with a growth mindset are more likely to embrace challenges, see effort as necessary for improvement, and recover from setbacks more effectively.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The theory has been applied in various educational settings to understand how students' beliefs about intelligence affect their learning behaviors and outcomes.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Educators can use this understanding to promote a growth mindset among students by praising effort over innate ability, providing opportunities for mastery experiences, and encouraging reflection on the process of learning.

> [!example] Application
> Parents and coaches can also apply these insights to foster resilience and motivation in children and athletes.
