# Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2

> [!definition] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
> Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2 refers to the development of a three-dimensional taxonomy for causal attribution, which classifies attributions into locus, stability, and controllability dimensions, setting the stage for understanding their psychological consequences.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
> **Key Entities:** Causal attribution (the process); the three-dimensional taxonomy (the structural architecture); locus, stability, controllability (the three dimensions); Weiner (architect of the taxonomy); Kelley and Jones & Davis (input-side theorists); the eight-cell taxonomic space.
> **Causal Map:** Triggering conditions → causal search → causal explanation → classified along three dimensions → each dimension maps to a specific psychological consequence (locus → self-regard; stability → expectancy; controllability → moral evaluation).
> **Structural Overview:** We now have both the *when/why* of attribution (Section 1) and the *what* — the dimensional structure through which attributions are organised. The next question is *what happens* when these dimensions operate on emotion and expectancy.
> **Evolution This Section:** The model gains its central structural feature — the three-dimensional taxonomy. The architecture is now in place; its consequences remain to be traced.
> **Emerging Patterns:** The system's elegance lies in generating diverse psychological outcomes from a small set of dimensional combinations. The classification of causes into dimensions is itself variable (not fixed), which creates space for intervention (previewed).
> **Open Threads:** How exactly does the stability dimension operate on expectancy? How do the dimensions generate specific emotions? These mechanisms are the subject of Sections 3 and 4.

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
> **Key Entities (added):** *De Groot*, *Chase*, *Simon*, *Miller*, *meaningful position*, *random position*, *recoding*, *chunk*.
> **Causal Map (extended):** Domain experience → accumulation of long-term memory configurations → ability to recode incoming material → fewer, larger units in working memory → superior recall and faster decision-making on domain-typical material.
> **Structural Overview:** The phenomenology of Section 1 now has a mechanism. Working memory is not enlarged; its contents are enriched.
> **Evolution This Section:** Introduced the chunk as a formal construct. Established the historical lineage and the experimental method that grounds the theory. Provided the first formal definition.
> **Emerging Patterns:** The pattern of "general capacity stable, domain-specific encoding variable" is now explicit. The role of long-term memory in shaping working memory operations is foregrounded.
> **Open Threads:** What is a chunk *mechanically* — what does it look like inside the cognitive architecture? How do chunks differ from templates? How does long-term memory deliver chunks to working memory fast enough to be useful?

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
> **Key Entities (added):** [[Technical Detail: The relationship between element interactivity and working-memory load]] (the deep mechanism), *information elements* (the units of interactivity), *relational structure* (the configuration of interactions), [[long-term-memory]] and [[Schema Construction]] (the relativizers).
> **Causal Map (updated):** material has *element interactivity relative to the learner* → forces simultaneous representation in working memory → generates intrinsic load → consumes working-memory capacity → leaves residual capacity for schema construction. The container picture from Level 1 has been replaced by a relational-computation picture.
> **Structural Overview (updated):** intrinsic load is no longer a primitive; it is the consequence of a more basic construct. The three-load taxonomy still holds at the surface, but its intrinsic component is now reanalyzed.
> **Evolution This Section:** the central reorganization of modern CLT was made visible — intrinsic load is element interactivity under another name.
> **Emerging Patterns:** the relativization of element interactivity to the learner's schemas is going to do most of the work in subsequent levels.
> **Open Threads:** what *exactly* is an element? When is the relation between two things constitutive of one of them? How does the count change as expertise develops?

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
> **Key Entities:** Dewey (philosophical foundation: education as organised experience), Lindeman (situation-centred adult education), Knowles (andragogy and learning contracts), Tough (empirical documentation of self-directed learning projects), Candy (distinction between self-direction as trait and as instructional mode), Hase/Kenyon (heutagogy and self-determined learning). The learner has shifted from being an object of curriculum to an increasingly active co-designer.
> **Causal Map:** Dewey's reconceptualisation of experience → Lindeman's application to adult contexts → Knowles' operationalisation through andragogy and learning contracts → Tough's empirical evidence that adults already self-direct → Candy's analytical refinement (trait vs. process) → Hase's radical extension to self-determination. Each step both builds upon and reveals limitations in the previous formulation.
> **Structural Overview:** A progressive intellectual lineage where each generation extends the degree of learner sovereignty that curriculum must accommodate, creating an escalating design challenge.
> **Evolution This Section:** The historical dimension has been added. The central paradox identified in Section 1 now has an intellectual genealogy. The field has moved from "structure as provocation" (Dewey) to "structure as launching architecture" (heutagogy).
> **Emerging Patterns:** The lineage is progressive but not linear — each advance creates new problems (e.g., Knowles' assumptions about adult learners were criticised as overgeneralised). The distinction between self-direction as trait and process (Candy) may be the most analytically powerful contribution.
> **Open Threads:** What psychological infrastructure does self-direction actually require? How does the scaffolding-to-sovereignty progression work mechanistically? What does the evidence say about which curriculum mechanisms are most effective?

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
> **Key Entities:** Type 1 cluster (autonomous, parallel, fast, opaque, perception-flavoured) and Type 2 cluster (serial, deliberate, slow, transparent, judgment-flavoured, depletable). The monitoring relationship between them.
> **Causal Map:** Working-memory engagement drives the Type 2 cluster; absence of working-memory demand drives the Type 1 cluster. The monitoring relationship is asymmetric and biased toward concurrence — Type 2 endorses Type 1 by default.
> **Structural Overview:** Two characterised modes plus a (frequently failing) monitoring relationship. The framework's predictive power lies in identifying when monitoring fails and when it succeeds.
> **Evolution This Section:** Pinned down the operational features of each mode and identified the monitoring relationship as the locus where the framework becomes predictive of systematic error.
> **Emerging Patterns:** The same architectural fact (working-memory dependence) generates the cluster of differences AND the failure mode (monitoring laxness).
> **Open Threads:** The mechanism by which monitoring fails — the substitution mechanism — needs to be examined in detail. The historical context that produced this framework remains to be traced.

> **Structural Overview:** Two layers visible: the substrate (conception of ability) and the orientation (mastery vs performance) it makes possible.

> **Emerging Patterns:** A theme of *conceptual frames as the load-bearing structure* is consolidating — frames make orientations possible, orientations shape behavior, behavior produces outcomes that feed back into frames. The pattern is recursive.

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
> **Key Entities (added):** The four mediating mechanisms — directive (attention), energizing (effort intensity), persistence (effort duration), strategic (search). The performer's task knowledge and strategy repertoire have entered as critical context.
> **Causal Map (extended):** Goal commitment → activates the four mechanisms → which compound → producing performance gains, conditional on ability, attainability, accessible strategy, and adequate task knowledge.
> **Structural Overview:** We now see that the theory's "core" is not a single relationship but a four-channel transmission: each channel converts goal commitment into a different kind of behavioral output, and the theory's predictive power arises from the combined operation of all four.
> **Evolution This Section:** The simple specificity-difficulty relation has been resolved into a structured causal architecture; the introduction of performance vs. learning goals refines the original claim and previews the later integration with [[achievement-goal-theory]].
> **Emerging Patterns:** Conditionality is the recurring theme — every mechanism has presuppositions, and the theory's predictive accuracy depends on those presuppositions being met.

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
> **Key Entities:** *(carried forward)* deliberating actor, goal intention, intention-behavior gap, five failure modes; *(added)* Heckhausen's four action phases (deliberation, preplanning, action, evaluation), deliberative vs. implemental mind-sets, Bratman's plans, the if-then format, the goal-intention/implementation-intention distinction.
> **Causal Map:** *(updated)* Goal commitment marks the transition from deliberation to preplanning; the implemental mind-set in the preplanning phase makes anticipatory commitments to action possible; if-then plans formed during preplanning create cognitive structures that govern subsequent enactment.
> **Structural Overview:** *(refined)* The layered architecture introduced in Section 1 is now specified as a temporal sequence of four phases with characteristic mind-sets and characteristic regulatory tasks; implementation intentions are precisely engineered for the preplanning-to-action transition.
> **Evolution This Section:** The report has supplied the theoretical scaffolding (Heckhausen + Bratman) within which implementation intentions are best understood, and has named the categorical distinction (goal intention vs. implementation intention) that will be central to all subsequent analysis.
> **Emerging Patterns:** A consistent theme of *delegation* is becoming visible — implementation intentions delegate the action-initiation work to a cue-triggered subsystem, just as Bratman's plans delegate momentary deliberation to an anticipatory commitment.
> **Open Threads:** What is the cognitive mechanism by which the if-then format produces strategic automaticity? What is the empirical evidence that it actually works? Where are its limits?

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
> **Key Entities:** Now extended to include the *object level* and *meta level* as formal architectural components; the *monitoring flow* (information from object → meta); the *control flow* (regulation from meta → object); the *coherence standard* against which monitoring evaluates representations; the *resource ecology* (working memory, attention, prior knowledge, metacognitive knowledge) on which the loop draws.
> **Causal Map:** Object-level processing → monitoring samples representation → comparison against implicit standard → metacognitive feeling generated (or not) → control response selected (or not) → object-level conditions modified → next cycle. Failure can occur at any link in the chain; failures at different links require different interventions.
> **Structural Overview:** A feedback loop between two levels of processing, sampling-based rather than continuous, embedded in a dynamic resource pool.
> **Evolution This Section:** Introduced the formal architecture (Nelson–Narens); separated monitoring from control as distinct subsystems with distinct failure modes; introduced calibration as the translational problem between monitoring sensitivity and useful regulation.
> **Emerging Patterns:** The same indirection theme from Section 1 reappears at the architectural level — the meta level never directly observes comprehension, only samples its outputs.
> **Open Threads:** What *is* the implicit standard against which the meta level evaluates representations? Section 3 will examine this — the *standard of coherence* — as the next layer of the architecture.

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2

> **Causal Map:** Flavell's taxonomy → provided conceptual vocabulary but not architecture; Hart/Brown's empirical work → demonstrated recursive monitoring phenomena requiring multi-level explanation; Nelson's gamma correlation → enabled quantification of monitoring accuracy → made the imperfection of the meta-level model empirically tractable → all three pressures converged → Nelson & Narens 1990 formal model.
> **Structural Overview:** We now have the problem (Section 1) and the intellectual preconditions (Section 2). The next section will present the formal architecture itself.

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
> **Key Entities:** The four phases (forethought, monitoring, control, reaction); the four areas (cognition, motivation, behavior, context); the sixteen cells of the matrix; the cross-area dynamics that generate the framework's deepest insights.
> **Causal Map:** Forethought conditions performance; monitoring generates regulatory signals during performance; control acts on those signals; reaction updates beliefs that condition subsequent forethought. Within each phase, the four areas regulate in parallel and routinely substitute for, support, or undermine each other.
> **Structural Overview:** The matrix is a diagnostic instrument, not a procedure. Skilled regulation is largely automatic; the matrix makes regulation *inspectable* when intervention is required.
> **Evolution This Section:** Introduced the full architectural matrix and the logic by which the two axes generate diagnostic specificity; clarified the warning against procedural misuse; surfaced the centrality of cross-area dynamics.
> **Emerging Patterns:** The framework's value lies in its ability to localize regulatory failure to specific cells; the most generative theoretical claims concern interactions across cells rather than the cells themselves.
> **Open Threads:** How does each phase actually unfold mechanistically? What are the specific operations that constitute forethought, monitoring, control, and reaction? Section 3 begins with the forethought phase.

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 2
