# Research Methodology and Epistemic Transparency

> [!definition] Research Methodology and Epistemic Transparency
> Research Methodology and Epistemic Transparency refers to the systematic approach of integrating cognitive-scientific evidence with PKM design principles through a seven-problem framework, while clearly distinguishing between established findings and original integrative contributions.

## Methodology & Sources

> [!methodology-and-sources] Research Methodology and Epistemic Transparency
> **Traditions and Disciplines Synthesized:**
> This report draws on six intellectual traditions: (1) *cognitive psychology* (levels of processing, schema theory, spreading activation, chunking, retrieval dynamics), (2) *educational psychology* (self-regulated learning, metacognition, desirable difficulties), (3) *motivational psychology* (Self-Determination Theory, self-efficacy, habit formation), (4) *instructional design* (cognitive load theory), (5) *information science* (knowledge organization, information architecture), and (6) *PKM practice literature* (Zettelkasten method, atomic note principles, PKB design discourse). The synthesis is original in its attempt to map cognitive-scientific evidence onto PKM design principles through a unified seven-problem framework.
>
> **Claim Type Taxonomy:**
>
> | Claim Type | Epistemic Status | Examples in This Report |
> |------------|-----------------|-------------------------|
> | Cognitive mechanism descriptions | Established (peer-reviewed) | Levels of processing, spreading activation, testing effect |
> | Empirical findings | Established (replicated) | Spacing effect, desirable difficulties, habit formation duration |
> | Framework descriptions | Established (scholarly consensus) | Schema theory, CLT, SDT, SRL |
> | PKM design principles | Well-motivated (inferred from established science) | Encoding depth over breadth, associative over hierarchical |
> | Original integrative frameworks | Speculative (original to this report) | Seven-problem planning framework, PKB-as-externalized-schema-network, metacognitive prosthesis |
> | Transfer claims | Interpretive (structurally motivated) | Organizational KM parallels, software architecture parallels |
>
> **Distinction Between Established Findings and Original Contributions:**
> The individual cognitive-scientific findings reported here (levels of processing, testing effect, spacing effect, CLT, SDT, SRL, habit formation) are well-established in their respective literatures. The *application* of these findings to PKM design — while motivated by the work of Ahrens (2017) and others — involves inferential steps that have not been experimentally validated in PKM-specific contexts. The original integrative frameworks (seven-problem framework, externalized schema network, metacognitive prosthesis) are offered as theoretically grounded syntheses, not as empirically verified models.
>
> **Limitations:**
> - The cognitive-scientific evidence was generated primarily in laboratory and classroom settings; ecological validity for naturalistic PKM practice is assumed but not verified.
> - The report focuses on individual-level cognition and does not systematically address social, cultural, or institutional dimensions of knowledge management.
> - The seven-problem framework is presented as comprehensive, but additional cognitive dimensions (e.g., creativity, insight, affect regulation) may warrant inclusion.
> - Tool-specific claims (about Obsidian, Anki, etc.) are based on feature analysis, not comparative empirical studies.
