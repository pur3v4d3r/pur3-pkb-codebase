# Anchoring-and-Adjustment

> [!definition] Anchoring-and-Adjustment
> - **Key-Term**: [[Anchoring-and-Adjustment]]
> - **Definition**: An anchoring-and-adjustment process is a cognitive bias where individuals rely too heavily on an initial piece of information (the 'anchor') when making subsequent judgments, adjusting it insufficiently to reach a final estimate or decision.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept of anchoring-and-adjustment was introduced by Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman in their seminal work. It describes how people often start with an initial value (the anchor) and then make adjustments based on additional information, but these adjustments are typically insufficient.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> For example, when asked to estimate the population of a city, if given an initial number as a starting point (the anchor), individuals may adjust their estimates up or down from this number without sufficient consideration of other relevant factors. The adjustment is often too small relative to the distance between the anchor and the true value.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> This bias can be seen in various contexts, including financial forecasting, medical diagnosis, and everyday decision-making.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In AI alignment, anchoring-and-adjustment can lead to biased predictions or decisions if initial values are not properly validated against new data.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical reasoning, doctors might rely too heavily on a patient's first symptom (the anchor) and fail to consider other diagnostic possibilities.
