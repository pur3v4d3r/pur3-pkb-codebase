## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Richard Ryan, along with Edward Deci, co-developed Self-Determination Theory (SDT) which posits that human behavior is driven by three basic psychological needs: competence, relatedness, and autonomy. Autonomy refers to the experience of volition or self-determination in one's actions.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Ryan’s work emphasizes how satisfying these needs can lead to higher levels of intrinsic motivation, well-being, and overall life satisfaction. He has explored various contexts where these needs are met or thwarted, such as educational settings, workplace environments, and therapeutic interventions.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between autonomous and controlled forms of motivation, with autonomous forms being more beneficial for long-term outcomes.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering autonomy can lead to higher student engagement and better academic performance.

> [!example] Application

> [!example] Application
> In therapy, addressing the need for autonomy can help clients develop a stronger sense of self-determination.
