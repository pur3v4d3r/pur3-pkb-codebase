# Need Satisfaction as Dynamic Equilibrium — Toward an Ecological SDT

> [!definition] Need Satisfaction as Dynamic Equilibrium — Toward an Ecological SDT
> Need Satisfaction as Dynamic Equilibrium refers to the idea that need satisfaction fluctuates over time and is maintained through ongoing interactions between individuals and their environments, rather than being a static state.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Need Satisfaction as Dynamic Equilibrium — Toward an Ecological SDT
> The criticisms surveyed here point toward a reconceptualization that SDT has begun but not completed: the shift from a snapshot model of need satisfaction to a dynamic equilibrium model. Most SDT research measures need satisfaction at a single time point and correlates it with outcomes at another. But need satisfaction is not a stable property of the person-environment system — it fluctuates across hours, days, and contexts, and the organism responds not only to the current level of satisfaction but to the trajectory. An individual whose autonomy satisfaction is moderate but improving may be in a qualitatively different motivational state than an individual whose autonomy satisfaction is moderate but declining, even though a single-timepoint measure would code them identically. The daily diary and experience sampling work of Reis and colleagues has begun to capture these dynamics, and the results suggest that fluctuations in need satisfaction across days predict fluctuations in well-being with remarkable precision. The implication is that SDT's explanatory power may be substantially enhanced by shifting from a static-nutriment metaphor to an ecological-equilibrium metaphor — one in which needs are not merely satisfied or frustrated but maintained in dynamic balance through ongoing organism-environment transactions. This reframing would also address the cultural critique by focusing on whether need equilibrium is maintained through culturally diverse means rather than asking whether static need levels differ across cultures.
