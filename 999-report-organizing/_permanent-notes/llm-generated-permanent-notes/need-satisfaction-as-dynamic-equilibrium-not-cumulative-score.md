# need-satisfaction-as-dynamic-equilibrium-not-cumulative-score

> [!definition] need-satisfaction-as-dynamic-equilibrium-not-cumulative-score
> - **Key-Term**: [[need-satisfaction-as-dynamic-equilibrium-not-cumulative-score]]
> - **Definition**: The concept of need satisfaction as dynamic equilibrium not cumulative score refers to the idea that psychological needs are met through an ongoing process of balance and adjustment, rather than accumulating points or scores over time.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This concept is rooted in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that individuals have basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness. These needs are considered satisfied when the individual experiences a sense of control, effectiveness, and social connection in their environment.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, need satisfaction as dynamic equilibrium means that these needs fluctuate based on situational factors and personal goals. For example, an individual may feel more autonomous in one situation but less so in another, leading to changes in their overall psychological well-being over time.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the idea that satisfaction is not a static state but rather a dynamic process where individuals continuously seek to balance these needs against external demands and internal desires.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding need satisfaction as dynamic equilibrium can inform teaching strategies aimed at fostering students' autonomy, competence, and relatedness in an adaptive manner.

> [!example] Application
> In organizational settings, this concept suggests that managers should focus on creating environments where employees can experience a sense of balance across their needs rather than trying to maximize one aspect over another.
