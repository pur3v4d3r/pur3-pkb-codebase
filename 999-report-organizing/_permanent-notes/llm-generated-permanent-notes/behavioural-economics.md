# behavioural-economics

> [!definition] behavioural-economics
> - **Key-Term**: [[behavioural-economics]]
> - **Definition**: Behavioural economics is an interdisciplinary field that combines insights from psychology and economics to understand how people make decisions in economic contexts, often deviating from the predictions of traditional economic models due to cognitive biases and heuristics.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Paragraph 1: Foundational context and core mechanism. Behavioural economics emerged as a response to the limitations of classical economic theory, which assumes that individuals are rational actors who always make decisions in their best interest based on complete information and perfect foresight. However, psychological research has shown that people often exhibit systematic deviations from these assumptions due to cognitive biases such as loss aversion, confirmation bias, and the endowment effect.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Paragraph 2: How it works or is applied in practice. Behavioural economists use experimental methods and field studies to observe how individuals make choices under various conditions, and they develop models that incorporate psychological factors like bounded rationality, limited attention, and social preferences. These insights are then used to design policies, financial products, and marketing strategies that take into account human cognitive limitations.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Paragraph 3: Key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots. The field includes various sub-disciplines such as decision-making under uncertainty, time inconsistency, and social norms. It also draws on theories from psychology like prospect theory (Kahneman & Tversky) and the concept of cognitive miserliness (Stanovich).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 4
> Optional paragraph 4: Historical development or origin. The origins of behavioural economics can be traced back to the work of psychologists such as Daniel Kahneman, Amos Tversky, and Richard Thaler in the late 20th century.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 5
> Optional paragraph 5: Relationship to neighboring concepts. Behavioural economics intersects with fields like cognitive psychology, social psychology, and neuroeconomics, which also study decision-making processes.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Implication 1: concrete application or real-world consequence. For example, behavioural economics has led to the development of nudges in public policy, such as default options for organ donation that increase participation rates.

> [!example] Application
> Implication 2: a second distinct application. In finance, it explains phenomena like the disposition effect and overconfidence bias, which can be used to design more effective investment strategies.
