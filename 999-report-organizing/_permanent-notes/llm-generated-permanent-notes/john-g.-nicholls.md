## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Achievement goal theory, developed by John G. Nicholls, posits that individuals have different orientations towards success in various domains such as education and sport. These orientations include mastery goals (focusing on improving one's abilities) and performance goals (focusing on outperforming others).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this theory helps educators and coaches understand how students or athletes set their personal standards of success and the strategies they employ to achieve these goals. It also aids in designing interventions that can support learners in adopting more adaptive goal orientations.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between intrinsic (mastery) and extrinsic (performance) motivations, as well as the role of feedback and social context in shaping these orientations.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding students' achievement goals can help teachers tailor their teaching methods to better support student learning and motivation.

> [!example] Application
> For athletes, coaches can use this theory to develop training programs that foster a growth mindset and resilience among their players.
