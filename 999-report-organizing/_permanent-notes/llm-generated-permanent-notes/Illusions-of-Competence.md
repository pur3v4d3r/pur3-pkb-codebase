# Illusions-of-Competence

> [!definition] Illusions-of-Competence
> - **Key-Term**: [[Illusions-of-Competence]]
> - **Definition**: Illusions-of-Competence refers to the tendency of individuals to overestimate their own ability, knowledge, and performance, often leading to poor decision-making and underestimating the complexity of tasks or problems they face.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Paragraph 1: Foundational context and core mechanism. Illusions-of-Competence is a cognitive bias that occurs when people believe they understand something better than they actually do, which can lead to overconfidence in their abilities and decisions.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Paragraph 2: How it works or is applied in practice. This phenomenon often arises from the lack of feedback or difficulty in recognizing one's own mistakes, especially in complex tasks where errors are not immediately apparent.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Paragraph 3: Key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots. It can be exacerbated by factors such as over-reliance on heuristics and insufficient calibration of one’s metacognitive judgments.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Implication 1: concrete application or real-world consequence. In educational settings, students may underestimate the difficulty of exams or assignments, leading to poor preparation.

> [!example] Application
> Implication 2: a second distinct application. In professional contexts, illusions-of-Competence can lead to risky decisions in fields like medicine or finance.
