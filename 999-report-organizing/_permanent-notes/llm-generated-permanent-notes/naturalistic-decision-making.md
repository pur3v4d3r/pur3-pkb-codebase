# naturalistic-decision-making

> [!definition] naturalistic-decision-making
> - **Key-Term**: [[naturalistic-decision-making]]
> - **Definition**: Naturalistic decision making refers to the process by which experienced individuals make decisions in real-world, complex, and often ambiguous situations without relying on formal procedures or models.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Naturalistic decision making is a concept that emerged from research in cognitive psychology and human factors. It focuses on how experts operate in high-stakes environments where they must quickly assess and respond to novel situations using their accumulated knowledge, experience, and intuition.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, naturalistic decision makers use heuristics, mental models, and pattern recognition to make rapid decisions under uncertainty. They often engage in what is known as 'chunking'—breaking down complex information into manageable units of knowledge that they can quickly access and apply.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of tacit knowledge, the importance of experience, and the cognitive biases that can influence decision-making. Sub-variants include different levels of expertise and the impact of situational factors on performance.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In real-world applications, understanding naturalistic decision making can help organizations develop training programs that enhance expert performance by focusing on experiential learning and scenario-based practice.

> [!example] Application
> It also informs the design of decision support systems for professionals such as pilots, firefighters, and medical practitioners who must make critical decisions under time pressure.
