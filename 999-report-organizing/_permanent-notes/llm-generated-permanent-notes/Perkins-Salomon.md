# Perkins-Salomon

> [!definition] Perkins-Salomon
> - **Key-Term**: [[Perkins-Salomon]]
> - **Definition**: The Perkins-Salomon framework is a theoretical model that integrates the concepts of commitment and autonomy within self-determination theory (SDT) to explain how individuals engage in and persist with various activities, particularly in educational and athletic contexts.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This framework posits that commitment can be understood as both a quantitative variable (how much one is committed) and a quality differentiated variable (the type of commitment, such as autonomous or controlled).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> It applies these concepts to understand how individuals develop and maintain commitments in different domains, emphasizing the role of autonomy-supportive environments in fostering healthy forms of commitment.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The framework highlights that while high levels of commitment are beneficial, the nature of this commitment (autonomous vs. controlled) significantly impacts outcomes.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, it can inform goal-setting strategies to promote autonomous commitment among students, leading to better academic performance and well-being.

> [!example] Application
> In athletic practice, it can guide coaches in creating environments that support athletes' intrinsic motivation and long-term engagement with their sport.
