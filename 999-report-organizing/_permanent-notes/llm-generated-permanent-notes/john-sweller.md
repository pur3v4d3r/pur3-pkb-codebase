# john-sweller

> [!definition] john-sweller
> - **Key-Term**: [[john-sweller]]
> - **Definition**: John Sweller is an educational psychologist known for his work on cognitive load theory and instructional design principles that optimize learning by managing the complexity of information presented to learners.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> John Sweller's foundational contribution lies in cognitive load theory, which posits that human working memory has limited capacity. This means that when too much information is presented at once, it can overwhelm the learner and hinder effective learning.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Sweller developed instructional design principles such as worked examples, spacing effects, and problem sequencing to minimize extraneous cognitive load and enhance intrinsic cognitive load, thereby improving learning outcomes.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between germane cognitive load (which aids in schema formation) and extraneous cognitive load (which hinders learning). Sweller's work also emphasizes the importance of instructional strategies that align with how learners process information.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors can use worked examples to demonstrate problem-solving processes, reducing the initial cognitive load on students.

> [!example] Application
> Spacing out practice and review sessions over time (spaced repetition) helps in long-term retention by allowing for consolidation of knowledge without overwhelming working memory.

> [!example] Application
> Sequencing problems from simple to complex aids in building a coherent schema, making it easier for learners to understand and apply new information.
