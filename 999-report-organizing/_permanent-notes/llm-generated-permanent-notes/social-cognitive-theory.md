# social-cognitive-theory

> [!definition] social-cognitive-theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[social-cognitive-theory]]
> - **Definition**: Social-cognitive theory is a framework that explains how people learn from observing others and how their thoughts, behaviors, and emotions are interconnected in social contexts.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This theory posits that individuals acquire knowledge, skills, and attitudes through observation of others' actions and the consequences of those actions. It emphasizes the role of cognitive processes such as attention, memory, and self-efficacy in this learning process.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, social-cognitive theory is applied to understand how people develop problem-solving strategies, learn new behaviors, and manage their emotions based on observed models. This includes the influence of self-regulation, goal-setting, and feedback mechanisms.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the concept of reciprocal determinism, where personal factors, behavior, and environmental influences are seen as interdependent. Sub-variants include Albert Bandura's work on self-efficacy and modeling.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, social-cognitive theory informs strategies for effective teaching and learning through demonstration and feedback.

> [!example] Application
> In therapy, it guides the use of role-playing and modeling to help clients develop new coping skills or behaviors.
