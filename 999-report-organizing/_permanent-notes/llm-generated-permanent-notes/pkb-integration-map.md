# PKB Integration Map

> [!definition] PKB Integration Map
> PKB Integration Map is a conceptual framework that illustrates the interconnections between attribution theory and other domains in social cognition and motivation science, highlighting dependencies, applications, lateral enrichments, and theoretical extensions.

## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] PKB Integration Map
> **1. Upstream Dependencies (This report builds on):**
>
> - [[social-cognition]] — Attribution theory is a subdivision of social cognition; it inherits the broader framework's assumptions about humans as intuitive scientists constructing mental models of social causality.
> - [[cognitive-biases]] — The systematic distortions documented in Section 5 (fundamental attribution error, actor-observer asymmetry, self-serving bias) are specific instances of the broader cognitive bias literature.
> - [[emotion-theory]] — The attribution-emotion mediation model (Section 4) depends on appraisal theories of emotion, particularly the principle that emotions are generated by cognitive evaluations rather than by raw events.
> - [[motivation-theory]] — Attribution theory positions itself as a cognitive theory of motivation; it inherits and extends the expectancy-value tradition.
> - [[locus-of-control]] — Rotter's concept provided the conceptual seed for Weiner's locus of causality dimension, though the two are theoretically distinct.
> - [[cross-cultural-psychology]] — Section 6's analysis of cultural variation draws on the individualism-collectivism framework and holistic-analytic cognitive style research.
>
> **2. Downstream Applications (This report enables):**
>
> - [[self-regulated-learning]] — Attribution provides the causal interpretation layer that SRL models require; understanding attribution illuminates why learners persist or withdraw.
> - [[growth-mindset]] — Dweck's framework is revealed as an upstream constraint on the stability dimension; understanding attribution clarifies the *mechanism* through which mindset operates.
> - [[feedback-design]] — Effective feedback requires understanding how recipients will attributionally interpret it; this report provides the dimensional framework for designing attributionally adaptive feedback.
> - [[wise-interventions]] — Attribution retraining is a canonical wise intervention; this report provides the theoretical grounding for the intervention design principles.
> - [[instructional-design]] — The attribution-emotion-behaviour chain has direct implications for how instructional environments should present failure, provide models, and scaffold causal reasoning.
> - [[metacognition]] — Attributional self-monitoring is a metacognitive skill; the attributional vocabulary concept connects attribution to the metacognitive regulation of learning.
>
> **3. Lateral Connections (Mutual enrichment):**
>
> - [[self-efficacy-theory]] — The attribution-efficacy interface operates bidirectionally: attributions shape efficacy beliefs, and efficacy beliefs constrain attributional patterns. Each theory deepens understanding of the other.
> - [[self-determination-theory]] — SDT's perceived locus of causality dimension directly maps onto Weiner's locus of causality, revealing structural parallels between the two major motivational frameworks.
> - [[Implicit-Theories-of-Intelligence]] — Entity and incremental theories calibrate the stability dimension, creating a productive intersection between personality-level beliefs and situation-level causal cognition.
> - [[dual-process-theory]] — The sequential model of attribution (automatic dispositional inference → effortful situational correction) instantiates the broader dual-process architecture.
> - [[Cognitive-Behavioural-Therapy]] — CBT's cognitive restructuring techniques share theoretical foundations with attribution retraining; both target the causal interpretations that mediate between events and emotional responses.
>
> **4. Strengthened Nodes (Existing notes this report enriches):**
>
> - [[bernard-weiner]] — This report provides the comprehensive treatment of Weiner's theoretical contributions that the biographical note cross-references.
> - [[fundamental-attribution-error]] — Section 5's analysis adds the cultural moderation and dual-process mechanism dimensions to the core concept note.
> - [[expectancy-value-theory]] — The stability-expectancy mechanism demonstrates the specific cognitive process through which expectancy is updated, enriching the expectancy-value framework.
> - [[learned-helplessness]] — The attributional reformulation (Section 7) provides the causal mechanism linking uncontrollability perception to helpless behaviour.

> [!cross-domain-connection] PKB Integration Map
> **1. Upstream Dependencies (This Report Builds On):**
>
> - **[[self-directed-learning]]** — The foundational construct that this entire report takes as its central educational goal. Understanding SDL as a developmental capacity rather than a fixed trait is the prerequisite for the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression.
>
> - **[[Scaffolded Fading]]** and **[[zone-of-proximal-development]]** — The Vygotskian mechanisms that provide the operational basis for the report's core argument about how curriculum structure develops internal capacity. Without understanding the ZPD, the scaffolding-fading progression lacks its theoretical grounding.
>
> - **[[self-regulated-learning]]** and **[[zimmerman-srl-model]]** — The self-regulation framework that specifies what internal capacities the curriculum must develop. Zimmerman's cyclical model of forethought, performance, and self-reflection maps the regulatory architecture that replaces external scaffolding.
>
> - **[[self-determination-theory]]** — The motivational framework that explains how autonomous motivation develops through the satisfaction of basic psychological needs. SDT provides the theoretical engine for the motivational ecology concept.
>
> - **[[metacognition]]** and **[[metacognitive-regulation]]** — The higher-order cognitive processes that enable learners to monitor, evaluate, and adjust their own learning. Metacognitive development is the psychological mechanism through which scaffolding-fading achieves its effects.
>
> - **[[Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)]]** — The cognitive architecture framework that explains why scaffolding helps novices and why the [[Expertise Reversal Effect (Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler, Sweller, 2003)|expertise reversal effect]] means scaffolding must be faded. Provides the cognitive science basis for developmental sequencing.
>
> **2. Downstream Applications (This Report Enables):**
>
> - **[[personal-learning-environments]]** — The design of technology-mediated environments for self-directed learning can draw on the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression for design guidance on how much structure to provide and when to withdraw it.
>
> - **[[lifelong-learning-system-design]]** — The broader systems-level design of institutions, policies, and technologies that support lifelong learning across the lifespan. This report provides the curriculum-level design principles.
>
> - **[[evidence-based-learning-strategies]]** — The specific learning strategies (retrieval practice, spaced repetition, interleaving, self-explanation) that this report positions as curriculum content to be taught explicitly during the scaffolding phase.
>
> - **[[feedback-design-for-autonomy-and-mastery]]** — The design of feedback systems that develop self-evaluative capacity, building on Section 7's analysis of assessment for autonomy.
>
> - **[[online-learning-design]]** — The design of digital learning environments can draw on this report's framework to address the specific challenges of scaffolding self-direction in technology-mediated contexts.
>
> **3. Lateral Connections (Mutual Enrichment):**
>
> - **[[andragogy]]** — This report situates andragogy within a developmental progression (andragogy → heutagogy) and enriches the understanding of Knowles's contributions while also noting their limitations. The andragogy note is enriched by this report's historical and critical analysis.
>
> - **[[heutagogy]]** and **[[self-determined-learning]]** — This report positions heutagogy as the endpoint of the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression, enriching the understanding of what self-determination means developmentally while drawing on heutagogy's vision for the progression's destination.
>
> - **[[transfer-of-learning]]** and **[[far-transfer]]** — This report applies transfer theory to curriculum design and extends it through the Far Transfer section. The transfer notes are enriched by concrete application in the curriculum design domain.
>
> - **[[formative-assessment]]** — This report reframes formative assessment as the evaluative dimension of self-direction development, enriching the understanding of formative assessment's developmental function beyond its usual framing as an instructional technique.
>
> - **[[growth-mindset]]** — The mindset research connects to the motivational ecology through the role of beliefs about ability in sustaining engagement with difficulty and challenge.
>

> - **[[long-term-memory]]** — The effectively unlimited storage capacity and associative structure of long-term memory provides the target architecture that PKB design principles aim to support. > [!cross-domain-connection] PKB Integration Map
> **1. Upstream Dependencies (this report builds on):**
> - [[humanistic-psychology]] — SDT's organismic metatheory descends directly from Rogers and Maslow's humanistic tradition. This report's Section 1 traces the intellectual genealogy in detail, and the connection is so fundamental that understanding SDT without understanding humanistic psychology is like understanding a branch without understanding the tree from which it grows.
> - [[effectance-motivation]] — White's 1959 reconceptualization of competence motivation provided the conceptual foundation for SDT's competence need. The connection is historically constitutive: Deci's early work begins with White's insight and extends it by embedding effectance motivation within a broader motivational framework.
> - [[eudaimonia]] — SDT's conception of well-being is explicitly eudaimonic rather than hedonic — flourishing through virtue and full functioning rather than pleasure maximization. The eudaimonic commitment shapes the theory's entire evaluative framework, including its treatment of intrinsic versus extrinsic goals.
> - [[Organismic Metatheory]] — The philosophical foundation that distinguishes SDT from mechanistic motivational theories. SDT's metatheoretical commitment to an active, growth-oriented organism generates all of its specific theoretical predictions.
> - [[abraham-maslow]] — Maslow's hierarchy provides the most prominent prior attempt to identify universal psychological needs. SDT's three-need framework can be understood as a more empirically grounded, non-hierarchical revision of Maslow's insight.
> - [[carl-rogers]] — The fully functioning person concept and the importance of unconditional positive regard directly prefigure SDT's account of autonomy support and the pathogenic consequences of conditional regard.
>
> **2. Downstream Applications (this report enables):**
> - [[autonomy-supportive-teaching]] — SDT provides the theoretical foundation for autonomy-supportive pedagogical practices. This report's Section 8 traces the application; the permanent note on autonomy-supportive teaching provides the practical elaboration.
> - [[self-regulated-learning]] — SDT's account of autonomous motivation provides the motivational prerequisite for effective self-regulation. Without autonomous motivation, metacognitive strategies are deployed half-heartedly or abandoned under difficulty.
> - [[motivational-interviewing]] — MI's effectiveness can be understood through SDT's framework as a clinical technique that systematically supports autonomy, competence, and relatedness. This report's Section 8 traces the connection.
> - [[Flourishing]] — SDT's eudaimonic framework specifies the conditions under which flourishing occurs (need satisfaction) and fails to occur (need frustration). The theory provides a mechanistic account of what "flourishing" actually requires.
> - [[feedback-design-for-autonomy-and-mastery]] — CET's analysis of informational versus controlling feedback directly informs the design of effective feedback systems in educational, organizational, and clinical contexts.
>
> **3. Lateral Connections (mutual enrichment):**
> - [[flow-theory]] — Flow can be understood as the simultaneous satisfaction of autonomy and competence needs during an optimally challenging activity. The connection enriches both frameworks: SDT provides flow with a motivational mechanism, and flow provides SDT with a phenomenological target state.
> - [[achievement-goal-theory]] — AGT and SDT offer complementary lenses on academic motivation: goal content (mastery vs. performance) and regulatory quality (autonomous vs. controlled). The integration produces a more complete motivational picture than either theory alone.
> - [[metacognition]] — Metacognitive skills are deployed most effectively under autonomous motivation conditions. SDT explains why metacognitive training sometimes fails: the motivational conditions for self-regulation must be satisfied before self-regulation strategies can be sustained.
> - [[Cognitive-Load-and-Need-Satisfaction]] — Excessive cognitive load may frustrate the competence need by making tasks feel unmanageable. The connection between cognitive load theory and SDT has been underexplored and may prove productive for instructional design.
> - [[motivational-psychology]] — SDT is the most comprehensive current framework within motivational psychology. This report situates SDT within the broader disciplinary context, including its relationships to competing and complementary theories.
>
> **4. Strengthened Nodes (specific notes this report enriches):**
