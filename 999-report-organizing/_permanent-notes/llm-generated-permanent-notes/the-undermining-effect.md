# the-undermining-effect

> [!definition] the-undermining-effect
> - **Key-Term**: [[the-undermining-effect]]
> - **Definition**: The undermining effect refers to the reduction of intrinsic motivation when external pressures, such as rewards or control, are applied to an activity that was initially engaging and enjoyable for the individual.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept is rooted in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that individuals have innate psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness. When these needs are met, motivation is intrinsic and self-sustaining. However, when external pressures interfere with the satisfaction of these needs, particularly autonomy, it can lead to a decrease in intrinsic motivation—a phenomenon known as the undermining effect.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> For example, if an individual is intrinsically motivated to paint because they find it enjoyable, but then their parents start offering monetary rewards for each painting completed, this external control can undermine their intrinsic enjoyment and reduce their motivation to continue painting out of interest alone.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> This effect has been observed across various contexts, including education, work, and sports. It highlights the importance of supporting autonomy in fostering long-term engagement and motivation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, providing too much external control or rewards can diminish students' intrinsic interest in learning.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace management, overly controlling environments may reduce employee creativity and job satisfaction.

> [!example] Application
> In sports coaching, excessive focus on winning and external validation can detract from athletes' enjoyment of the sport.
