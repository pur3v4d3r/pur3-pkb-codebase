# reciprocal-teaching

> [!definition] reciprocal-teaching
> - **Key-Term**: [[reciprocal-teaching]]
> - **Definition**: Reciprocal teaching is an instructional method that involves students taking turns to lead the discussion and teach each other, often focusing on reading comprehension strategies such as summarizing, questioning, clarifying, and predicting.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Reciprocal teaching is a collaborative learning strategy where students work in small groups to discuss and understand text. It typically involves four key processes: summarizing the main points of the text, asking questions about the content, clarifying any confusing parts, and making predictions about what might come next.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> These strategies are taught explicitly by the teacher initially, but as students become more proficient, they take on the role of teachers themselves. This process not only enhances their understanding of the material but also develops their metacognitive skills and ability to engage in effective peer-to-peer learning.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The method is grounded in social constructivist theory, which posits that knowledge is constructed through interaction with others.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In classrooms using reciprocal teaching, students often show improved reading comprehension and metacognitive skills. This approach can be particularly effective for diverse learners as it encourages active engagement and peer support.

> [!example] Application
> Teachers can use reciprocal teaching to foster a collaborative learning environment where students feel more responsible for their own learning.
