# Autonomy-Supportive-Parenting

> [!definition] Autonomy-Supportive-Parenting
> - **Key-Term**: [[Autonomy-Supportive-Parenting]]
> - **Definition**: Autonomy-supportive parenting is an approach that encourages children to develop their own values and make independent choices, while providing support and guidance in the process.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Autonomy-supportive parenting is grounded in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that individuals are motivated by three basic psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Parents who practice this approach foster their children's sense of autonomy by respecting their choices and providing them with opportunities to make decisions.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, autonomy-supportive parents offer open communication, provide information rather than directives, and encourage exploration and self-discovery. They also validate their children’s feelings and thoughts, which helps build a trusting relationship where the child feels supported in making their own choices.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include distinguishing between autonomy-supportive parenting and permissive parenting, as well as understanding that it is not about independence but rather supporting the development of autonomous motivation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Children raised with autonomy-supportive parenting tend to have higher self-esteem and better social skills.

> [!example] Application
> They are more likely to engage in prosocial behaviors and exhibit less externalizing problems such as aggression or delinquency.
