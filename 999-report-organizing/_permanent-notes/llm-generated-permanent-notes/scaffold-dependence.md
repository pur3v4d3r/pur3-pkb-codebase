# scaffold-dependence

> [!definition] scaffold-dependence
> - **Key-Term**: [[scaffold-dependence]]
> - **Definition**: Scaffold-dependence refers to the reliance on external supports, such as tools, instructions, or guidance, during learning and problem-solving processes, which can hinder independent thinking once these supports are removed.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Scaffold-dependence is a concept rooted in educational psychology, particularly within the framework of constructivist theories. It describes how learners often depend on external scaffolds—such as instructional materials or guidance from teachers—to complete tasks and solve problems effectively during their learning process.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> These scaffolds are typically designed to support and facilitate understanding, but they can become crutches that prevent learners from developing independent problem-solving skills once the scaffolding is removed. This dependency can be observed in various domains including education, coaching, and clinical reasoning.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the balance between providing enough support to foster learning without over-relying on external aids, as well as the transition phase where learners move from scaffolded tasks to more independent problem-solving.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding scaffold-dependence can help educators design curricula that promote independent thinking and self-regulation skills.

> [!example] Application
> In coaching and physical skill development, it highlights the importance of gradually reducing support as learners become more proficient to ensure they can perform tasks independently.
