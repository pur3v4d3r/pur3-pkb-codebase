# mastery-avoidance-goal

> [!definition] mastery-avoidance-goal
> - **Key-Term**: [[mastery-avoidance-goal]]
> - **Definition**: Mastery-avoidance goal refers to an individual's pursuit of mastering a skill or task for the sake of learning and understanding, as opposed to avoiding failure or negative evaluation by others.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept of mastery-avoidance goal is rooted in achievement goal theory, which differentiates between goals that focus on personal improvement (mastery) versus those focused on outperforming others (performance). Mastery-oriented individuals aim to develop their skills and knowledge, while avoidance-oriented individuals are more concerned with not failing or looking incompetent.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, mastery-avoidance goal can be observed in educational settings where students might engage deeply with material for personal growth, but also fear failure, leading them to avoid challenging tasks that could reveal their weaknesses. This dual focus often results in a complex interplay between motivation and anxiety.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between intrinsic (mastering for self-improvement) and extrinsic (avoiding negative evaluation) motivations. Sub-variants might include different levels of avoidance, such as high or low avoidance, which can significantly impact learning outcomes.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding mastery-avoidance goals helps educators design assessments that promote deep learning rather than superficial performance.

> [!example] Application
> For clinical settings, recognizing these goals aids in tailoring therapeutic interventions to address both the desire for mastery and the fear of failure.
