# Domain 1: Clinical Reasoning and Medical Decision-Making

> [!definition] Domain 1: Clinical Reasoning and Medical Decision-Making
> Domain 1: Clinical Reasoning and Medical Decision-Making involves managing the interactivity of multiple patient-related elements to make diagnostic and treatment decisions, leveraging cognitive load theory principles such as scaffolded fading and worked examples to enhance decision-making processes.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Domain 1: Clinical Reasoning and Medical Decision-Making
> **Structural Principle:** Element interactivity determines processing difficulty; management of interacting elements is the core design problem.
>
> Clinical reasoning requires physicians to simultaneously consider patient history, presenting symptoms, laboratory results, medication interactions, comorbidities, and differential diagnoses — a task with extreme element interactivity. The CLT principle of managing intrinsic load through [[Scaffolded Fading]] translates directly: clinical decision support systems that present information in layered, progressive formats (chief complaint → focused differentials → targeted diagnostics) reduce the simultaneous processing demand compared to presenting all patient data at once. The worked example effect translates as case-based reasoning — expert diagnostic pathways modeled step-by-step before independent diagnostic practice. Medical education's adoption of problem-based learning, when poorly structured, can impose extraneous load through navigational complexity; CLT analysis identifies the specific design features that make problem-based learning effective versus overwhelming.
>
> **Boundary Condition:** Clinical reasoning involves time pressure, emotional stakes, and team dynamics that CLT's cognitive-only architecture does not address. The transfer is structural, not comprehensive.
>
