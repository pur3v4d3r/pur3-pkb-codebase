# Social-Cognitive-Theory-—-Bandura

> [!definition] Social-Cognitive-Theory-—-Bandura
> - **Key-Term**: [[Social-Cognitive-Theory-—-Bandura]]
> - **Definition**: Social-Cognitive-Theory, developed by Albert Bandura, is a theoretical framework that emphasizes the role of observation, imitation, and modeling in the learning process. It posits that people learn from one another through observing, retaining, transforming, and acting upon information.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The theory suggests that individuals can learn new behaviors and skills by observing others (models) perform those actions. This includes not only direct observation but also vicarious experiences where the observer sees the consequences of the model's behavior.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Learning occurs through a process involving attention, retention, reproduction, and motivation. Attention is crucial as it determines what aspects of the observed behavior are noticed; retention involves storing information about the observed behaviors; reproduction requires the ability to recall and perform the actions; and motivation influences whether one will engage in the modeled behavior.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the concept of self-efficacy, which refers to an individual's belief in their capability to execute courses of action required to produce specific performance attainments. This belief significantly impacts learning and performance.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding social-cognitive theory can inform instructional strategies that leverage modeling and observation to enhance learning outcomes.

> [!example] Application
> In sports coaching, it supports the use of video analysis and peer mentoring to improve athletes' skills and confidence.
