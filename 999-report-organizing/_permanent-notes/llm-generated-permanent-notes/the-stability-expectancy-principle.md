# The Stability-Expectancy Principle

> [!definition] The Stability-Expectancy Principle
> The stability of a cause influences how expectations change following an outcome.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Stability-Expectancy Principle
> The stability of an attributed cause is the primary determinant of expectancy change following an outcome. Attributing an outcome to a stable cause shifts expectancy in the direction of that outcome (success → expect more success; failure → expect more failure). Attributing an outcome to an unstable cause produces little or no expectancy change, leaving the field open for different outcomes in the future. This principle — not the locus dimension, and not the controllability dimension — is the engine through which attribution most powerfully shapes motivational trajectories over time.
