# Self-Monitoring Protocol for Demanding Reading

> [!definition] Self-Monitoring Protocol for Demanding Reading
> A structured approach to reading complex material that involves articulating a purpose before reading, summarizing content during breaks, addressing comprehension gaps, explaining the central argument after reading, and documenting understanding.

## Additional Material

> [!protocol] Self-Monitoring Protocol for Demanding Reading
> When reading material whose comprehension matters and where the temptation to read fluently without monitoring is strong, deploy the following structured monitoring routine.
> 1. **Before reading:** Articulate purpose. What do you need to be able to do with this material when you are done? Set the standard of coherence at the level the purpose requires (recognition? application? explanation?).
> 2. **During reading:** Pause at natural section breaks (every 3–5 paragraphs in dense text, every 1–2 pages otherwise). At each pause, attempt to summarize what was just read in one or two sentences without looking back. Note where the summary falters.
> 3. **At each falter:** Treat the falter as a calibration signal that monitoring did not generate. Decide whether the gap is local (look back to specific passage), structural (reread the section with attention to argument structure), or knowledge-based (consult an external source for missing background).
> 4. **After reading:** Attempt to explain the central argument to an imagined or actual audience without referring to the text. Note what cannot be explained. Return to those sections for re-engagement.
> 5. **At session end:** Record in your PKB or notes both what you understood and what remained unclear, treating the explicit articulation of unclarity as itself an output of the reading.
