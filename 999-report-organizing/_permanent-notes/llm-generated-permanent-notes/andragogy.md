# andragogy

> [!definition] andragogy
> - **Key-Term**: [[andragogy]]
> - **Definition**: Andragogy is an approach to adult education that focuses on the principles and methods of teaching adults, emphasizing self-directed learning and practical application of knowledge.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Andragogy was developed by Malcolm Knowles in the mid-20th century as a theory to understand how adults learn. It posits that adults are motivated to learn when they have a need for new information or skills, and that their learning is driven more by experience than by age.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, andragogy involves creating learning environments where adults can take responsibility for their own learning, set personal goals, and apply what they learn directly to real-world problems. This approach often includes collaborative learning, problem-based learning, and the use of case studies or simulations.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the idea that adult learners are more self-directed and have a wealth of life experience that can be leveraged in the learning process. Sub-variants of andragogy may focus on specific aspects such as experiential learning or technology-enhanced learning.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors using andragogy can design courses that are more engaging and relevant to adult learners, leading to higher satisfaction and better retention of knowledge.

> [!example] Application
> Organizations can implement training programs based on andragogical principles to improve employee development and performance.

> [!example] Application
> A cautionary note is that not all adults have the same level of self-motivation or experience, so instructors must be flexible in their approach.
