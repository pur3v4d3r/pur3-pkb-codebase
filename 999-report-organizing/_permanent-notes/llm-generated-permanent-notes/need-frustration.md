# need-frustration

> [!definition] need-frustration
> - **Key-Term**: [[need-frustration]]
> - **Definition**: Need-frustration refers to the experience of blocked or thwarted basic psychological needs, such as autonomy, competence, and relatedness, which are essential for well-being and motivation according to Self-Determination Theory (SDT).
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The core mechanism involves the identification of unmet fundamental needs that can lead to negative emotions and decreased intrinsic motivation. When these needs are frustrated, individuals may experience anxiety, depression, or a sense of failure.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, need-frustration is often observed in educational settings where students feel their autonomy is restricted, leading to disengagement from learning activities. Similarly, it can occur in workplace environments when employees perceive that they lack control over their work tasks or are not given opportunities for growth and recognition.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between different types of need-frustration, such as those related to autonomy versus competence, and how these frustrations can manifest differently across various contexts. Theoretical roots lie in SDT, which posits that fulfilling basic psychological needs is crucial for optimal functioning.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding need-frustration can help teachers design more supportive environments that foster autonomy and competence, thereby enhancing student engagement and motivation.

> [!example] Application
> In the workplace, recognizing need-frustration among employees can lead to improved management practices that focus on providing autonomy and opportunities for growth, which in turn can boost job satisfaction and productivity.
