# locus-of-control

> [!definition] locus-of-control
> - **Key-Term**: [[locus-of-control]]
> - **Definition**: Locus of control refers to an individual's perception of whether they have control over the outcomes in their life, attributing success and failure to internal factors (e.g., personal ability) or external factors (e.g., luck).
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept was introduced by Julian B. Rotter in the 1950s as a way to understand how people attribute causes for events in their lives, distinguishing between those who believe they have control over their fate and those who do not.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Individuals with an internal locus of control tend to believe that their actions and decisions influence outcomes, while those with an external locus of control attribute success or failure to factors beyond their control, such as luck or the actions of others.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> This concept has been applied in various fields including education, psychology, and organizational behavior to understand motivation, self-efficacy, and coping strategies.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding a student's locus of control can help tailor teaching methods that foster internal attribution and enhance learning outcomes.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, recognizing an individual’s locus of control is crucial for developing effective interventions in treating conditions like depression or anxiety.
