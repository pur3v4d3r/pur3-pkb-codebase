# metacognition-and-self-regulated-learning

> [!definition] metacognition-and-self-regulated-learning
> - **Key-Term**: [[metacognition-and-self-regulated-learning]]
> - **Definition**: Metacognition and self-regulated learning refer to the processes by which learners think about their own thinking (metacognition) and manage their learning strategies effectively (self-regulation).
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Metacognition involves understanding one's cognitive processes, such as planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's comprehension and problem-solving abilities. Self-regulated learning encompasses the ability to set goals, monitor progress, adjust strategies, and evaluate outcomes in a continuous cycle.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, learners use metacognitive skills to anticipate difficulties, plan their study sessions, and reflect on what they have learned. They apply self-regulation by setting realistic goals, breaking tasks into manageable steps, seeking feedback, and adjusting their approach based on performance.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between procedural and declarative knowledge in metacognition, as well as the role of motivation and affect in self-regulated learning.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering metacognitive skills can enhance students' ability to learn independently and effectively. Teachers can promote this by encouraging reflection on learning processes and strategies.

> [!example] Application
> Self-regulated learning can lead to improved academic performance and better long-term retention of knowledge. Students who are adept at self-regulation tend to be more resilient in the face of challenges.
