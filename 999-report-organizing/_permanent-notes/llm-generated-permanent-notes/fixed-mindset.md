# fixed-mindset

> [!definition] fixed-mindset
> - **Key-Term**: [[fixed-mindset]]
> - **Definition**: A fixed-mindset is the belief that one's abilities and intelligence are static traits, not subject to change through effort and learning. Individuals with this mindset tend to avoid challenges, as they fear failure might expose their perceived limitations.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept of a fixed-mindset was introduced by psychologist Carol Dweck in her research on motivation and achievement. People with a fixed-mindset view abilities as inherent traits that cannot be developed or improved over time, leading them to believe that effort is unnecessary and potentially harmful if it does not result in immediate success.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, individuals with a fixed-mindset often avoid challenges and new learning opportunities because they fear failure. They may also attribute their successes to luck rather than hard work, and their failures to lack of innate talent. This mindset can hinder personal growth and development by limiting the belief that one's abilities are malleable.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between fixed-mindset beliefs about intelligence versus other traits like personality or character. Additionally, research has shown that a fixed-mindset can be influenced by cultural and social factors, as well as early life experiences.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, students with a fixed-mindset may underperform because they avoid challenging tasks and do not seek feedback for improvement. This can perpetuate a cycle of low achievement and negative self-perception.

> [!example] Application
> In the workplace, employees with a fixed-mindset might resist training or development opportunities, leading to stagnation in skill levels and career advancement.
