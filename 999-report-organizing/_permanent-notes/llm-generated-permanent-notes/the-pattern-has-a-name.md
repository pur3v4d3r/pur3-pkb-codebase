# The Pattern Has a Name

> [!definition] The Pattern Has a Name
> The Pattern Has a Name refers to the goal-setting pathology, an adverse outcome in organizations where high goal difficulty, individual performance measurement, severe failure consequences, and inadequate support lead to narrow attention, distorted risk-taking, erosion of cooperation, and ethical compromise.

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Pattern Has a Name
> When all four dark-side phenomena coincide — narrow attention to measured outcomes, distorted risk taking under threshold rewards, erosion of cooperation under individual targets, and ethical compromise under unattainable difficulty — the result is the pattern that organizational scholars have come to call the goal-setting pathology, and its incidence is reliably predicted by the conjunction of high goal difficulty, individual measurement, severe failure consequences, and inadequate moderator support. The same conjunction that produces the pattern also produces the proudest success stories of the goal-setting literature; what determines which outcome appears is whether the performer's ability and strategy are adequate to the goal under honest pursuit.
> *— [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
