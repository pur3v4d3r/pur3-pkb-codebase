# The Mechanisms Compound, But Not Independently

> [!definition] The Mechanisms Compound, But Not Independently
> A framework indicating that goal-directed mechanisms such as directive, energizing, persistence, and strategic functions are interdependent and conditional upon each other; their combined effect is greater than the sum of individual parts but can also lead to significant performance degradation if any mechanism fails.

## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] The Mechanisms Compound, But Not Independently
> The four mechanisms are not parallel and additive but interlocked and conditional: the directive function presupposes that activities relevant to the goal can be recognized; the energizing function presupposes that the perceived difficulty has not yet collapsed commitment; the persistence function presupposes that sustained engagement is feasible across the relevant period; the strategic function presupposes that adequate strategies are accessible or learnable. When all four conditions are met, the mechanisms compound to produce the dramatic performance gains the empirical literature documents. When any condition fails, the corresponding mechanism either fails to operate or operates in a way that becomes counterproductive — and the more conditions fail simultaneously, the more severely performance can be degraded relative to what would have been observed in the absence of any goal at all.
