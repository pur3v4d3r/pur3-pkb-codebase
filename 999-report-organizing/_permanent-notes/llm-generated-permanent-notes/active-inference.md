# active-inference

> [!definition] active-inference
> - **Key-Term**: [[active-inference]]
> - **Definition**: Active inference is a theoretical framework that posits organisms actively infer the most likely state of their environment and their own internal states to minimize prediction errors, thereby guiding behavior and perception in an efficient manner.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Active inference operates on the principle that organisms are constantly trying to predict sensory inputs based on their current beliefs about the world. When predictions fail (i.e., when there is a mismatch between expected and actual sensory input), organisms adjust their internal models or behaviors to reduce prediction errors, thus maintaining a stable perception of reality.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this involves continuous updating of prior beliefs through Bayesian inference, where new evidence is integrated with existing knowledge to form posterior distributions. This process helps in making efficient decisions and predictions about the environment and one's own actions.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of free energy as a measure of prediction error, which drives the organism’s behavior towards states that minimize this error. Sub-variants exist, such as variational Bayes approximations to handle complex models.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In medicine, active inference can help in understanding and treating disorders related to perception and cognition by providing a framework for how these processes might go awry.

> [!example] Application
> In artificial intelligence, it offers a way to design more efficient algorithms that can learn from limited data and adapt their predictions based on new information.
