# Scaffolding-Fading Progression

> [!definition] Scaffolding-Fading Progression
> The scaffolding-fading progression describes the deliberate, contingent, and systematic process by which curriculum design provides structured support for learning and then withdraws that support as the learner develops the internal capacity — metacognitive, self-regulatory, motivational — to perform independently what was previously only possible with assistance. The mechanism operates through [[zone-of-proximal-development|Vygotsky's zone of proximal development]]: support is pitched at the frontier of the learner's current capacity, enabling engagement with material that would otherwise be inaccessible, and is progressively removed as the learner's frontier advances.
>
> **Boundary:** Scaffolding-fading is not the gradual reduction of difficulty. The difficulty of learning tasks should, if anything, increase as scaffolding is withdrawn, because the learner is developing the capacity to handle greater complexity independently. What is reduced is not the challenge but the external support — guidance, prompts, templates, worked examples, feedback frequency — that enables the learner to engage with the challenge productively.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The scaffolding-fading progression is the primary mechanism through which curriculum transforms dependent learners into self-directed ones. It is the operational answer to the autonomy-structure paradox identified in Section 1.
>
