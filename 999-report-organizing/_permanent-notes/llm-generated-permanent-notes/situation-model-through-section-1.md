# Situation Model — Through Section 1

> [!definition] Situation Model — Through Section 1
> Situation Model refers to a theoretical framework in cognitive psychology that addresses metacognitive processes, particularly monitoring and control, as well as the self-directed learning process, including its definitional components and unresolved tensions.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Through Section 1
> **Key Entities:** Metacognition (the central phenomenon); monitoring (the process by which cognition assesses its own state); control (the process by which cognition regulates its own operations); Thomas O. Nelson and Louis Narens (the architects of the formal framework); Joseph Hart (the experimental pioneer of feeling-of-knowing research).
> **Causal Map:** Hart's FOK experiments → demonstrated that metacognitive signals have predictive validity → metamemory research tradition → accumulated catalogue of monitoring phenomena → need for unifying architecture → Nelson & Narens' theoretical contribution.
> **Structural Overview:** We have established that metacognition poses a recursive structural problem (monitoring requires distinct levels), and that the empirical evidence demanded a formal architectural solution rather than further cataloguing.
> **Evolution This Section:** Introduced the core problem, the historical trigger (Hart's work), and the theoretical gap that Nelson & Narens' framework was designed to fill.
> **Emerging Patterns:** The tension between descriptive cataloguing and formal architecture as modes of scientific explanation.
> **Open Threads:** What exactly is the two-level architecture? How does information flow between levels? What are the specific types of monitoring judgments? These are addressed in Sections 2-4.
> *— [[nelson-narens-metacognitive-control-framework-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Through Section 1
> **Key Entities:** Self-Directed Learning (central construct, defined processually by Knowles); the self-directed learner (agent); learning projects (the unit of analysis in Tough's research); readiness/disposition (contested attribute); educational systems (context that either supports or constrains SDL)
> **Causal Map:** Learner initiative → diagnosis of learning needs → goal formulation → resource identification → strategy selection → outcome evaluation. This process is enabled by metacognitive capacity and motivational alignment (to be developed in Sections 2-3).
> **Structural Overview:** SDL has been established as a tripartite concept (process, disposition, goal) with a central unresolved tension about whether self-direction is trait-like or context-sensitive.
> **Evolution This Section:** Established the definitional foundation and scope boundaries.
> **Emerging Patterns:** The trait-vs-readiness tension mirrors broader debates in psychology about person-vs-situation explanations of behavior.
> **Open Threads:** How does the cognitive machinery of metacognition actually power SDL? What motivational conditions are necessary? How do the major theoretical models attempt to resolve the tripartite tension?
