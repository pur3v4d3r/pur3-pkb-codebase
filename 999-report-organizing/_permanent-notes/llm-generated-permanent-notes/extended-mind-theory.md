# extended-mind-theory

> [!definition] extended-mind-theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[extended-mind-theory]]
> - **Definition**: Extended-mind theory posits that the mind is not confined to the brain but extends into the external world, where tools and artifacts can become part of cognitive processes.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The core mechanism of extended-mind theory involves the idea that mental processes are not solely internal but also involve interactions with external entities. For example, a pen and paper might be seen as extensions of one's memory or reasoning capabilities.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means that when we use tools like calculators, computers, or even notes to help us think, these tools become part of our cognitive processes, enhancing our ability to solve problems or remember information.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between weak and strong versions of extended-mind theory. Weak versions suggest that external entities merely facilitate internal mental processes, while strong versions propose that they can actually alter those processes.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding extended-mind theory can inform how tools like digital note-taking apps or whiteboards are used to enhance learning and memory.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, it suggests that external aids such as medication reminders or physical therapy devices can be integral parts of a patient's cognitive support system.
