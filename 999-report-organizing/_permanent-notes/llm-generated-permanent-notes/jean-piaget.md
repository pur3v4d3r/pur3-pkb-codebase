# jean-piaget

> [!definition] jean-piaget
> - **Key-Term**: [[jean-piaget]]
> - **Definition**: Jean Piaget was a Swiss developmental psychologist known for his theory of cognitive development, which describes the stages through which children develop their understanding of the world and their ability to think logically.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Piaget proposed that cognitive development occurs in four distinct stages: sensorimotor (birth to 2 years), preoperational (2 to 7 years), concrete operational (7 to 11 years), and formal operational (adolescence and adulthood). Each stage is characterized by specific types of thinking and problem-solving abilities.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The theory suggests that children progress through these stages in a fixed order, with each new stage building on the previous one. For example, during the preoperational stage, children begin to use language but still struggle with logical reasoning, while in the concrete operational stage, they can think logically about concrete events but not yet abstract concepts.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the idea that cognitive development is not solely determined by age but also influenced by environmental factors and experiences. Sub-variants of Piaget's theory have been developed to account for individual differences and cultural variations.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding Piaget’s stages helps teachers design appropriate learning activities that match the cognitive abilities of their students at different ages.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, knowledge of these stages can aid in assessing children's developmental milestones and identifying potential delays or disorders.
