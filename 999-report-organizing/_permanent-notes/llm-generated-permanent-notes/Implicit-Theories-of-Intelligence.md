# Implicit-Theories-of-Intelligence

> [!definition] Implicit-Theories-of-Intelligence
> - **Key-Term**: [[Implicit-Theories-of-Intelligence]]
> - **Definition**: Implicit theories of intelligence refer to the underlying beliefs individuals hold about the nature of intelligence, whether it is fixed (entity theory) or malleable (growth theory). These beliefs influence how people perceive their own abilities and those of others, as well as their motivation and behavior in academic and other settings.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Implicit theories of intelligence are foundational to understanding individual differences in motivation and achievement. Individuals with an entity theory believe that intelligence is a fixed trait, while those with a growth theory view it as something that can be developed through effort.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> These beliefs affect how people respond to challenges: those with a fixed mindset may avoid difficult tasks for fear of failure, whereas those with a growth mindset are more likely to persist and learn from mistakes.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the impact on self-esteem, help-seeking behavior, and academic performance. Sub-variants within these theories can also influence specific behaviors and attitudes.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering a growth theory of intelligence can lead to increased student motivation and better academic outcomes.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace settings, promoting a growth mindset among employees can enhance their resilience and willingness to take on new challenges.
