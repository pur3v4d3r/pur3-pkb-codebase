# SR Seed 4 — Distinction

> [!definition] SR Seed 4 — Distinction
> SR Seed 4 — Distinction refers to the clear delineation between different levels of feedback and scaffolding in educational contexts, particularly emphasizing self-regulation feedback as crucial for developing metacognitive monitoring capacity and distinguishing it from content-based scaffolding that simplifies subject matter without targeting learning regulation.

## Flashcards

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 4 — Distinction
> **Q:** How do Hattie and Timperley distinguish between the four levels of feedback?
> **A:** Task level (correct/incorrect), Process level (strategy effectiveness), Self-regulation level (monitoring and adjusting approach), and Self level (personal evaluation). For self-direction development, self-regulation feedback is most important because it develops metacognitive monitoring capacity.
> **Source:** Section 7
> **Difficulty:** Intermediate

> [!flashcard] SR Seed 4 — Distinction
> **Q:** How does metacognitive scaffolding differ from content scaffolding?
> **A:** Content scaffolding simplifies subject matter (e.g., providing easier texts). Metacognitive scaffolding targets the *regulation* of learning — planning, monitoring, strategy selection, and reflection — without simplifying content. A metacognitive scaffold prompts "How will you study this?" not "Here's an easier version."
> **Source:** Section 1, Appendix A.1
