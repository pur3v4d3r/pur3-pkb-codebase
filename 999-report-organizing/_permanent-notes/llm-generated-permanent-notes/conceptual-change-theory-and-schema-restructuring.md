# conceptual-change-theory-and-schema-restructuring

> [!definition] conceptual-change-theory-and-schema-restructuring
> - **Key-Term**: [[conceptual-change-theory-and-schema-restructuring]]
> - **Definition**: Conceptual change theory and schema restructuring refer to the process by which individuals modify their existing cognitive frameworks, or schemas, to accommodate new information or experiences, leading to significant shifts in understanding and behavior.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Paragraph 1: Foundational context and core mechanism. Conceptual change theory posits that learning involves not just acquiring new knowledge but also reorganizing existing mental structures (schemas) to integrate new information effectively. This process is crucial for adapting to novel situations and integrating complex ideas.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Paragraph 2: How it works or is applied in practice. Schema restructuring typically occurs through processes such as assimilation, where new information is incorporated into an existing schema, and accommodation, where the schema itself changes to better fit the new information. This can be observed in various domains, from cognitive development in children to adult learning.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Paragraph 3: Key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots. The theory has several variants, including Piaget's stages of cognitive development and Vygotsky's social constructivism. These theories emphasize different aspects of how schemas are developed and modified over time.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Implication 1: Concrete application or real-world consequence. In education, understanding conceptual change theory can inform teaching strategies that promote deep learning by encouraging students to question and revise their existing beliefs.

> [!example] Application
