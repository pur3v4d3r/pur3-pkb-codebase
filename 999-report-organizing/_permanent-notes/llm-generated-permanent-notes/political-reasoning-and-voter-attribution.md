# Political Reasoning and Voter Attribution

> [!definition] Political Reasoning and Voter Attribution
> Political Reasoning and Voter Attribution refers to how citizens' attributions for national conditions influence their political behavior through emotional responses like anger or resignation, shaping support for leaders based on perceived controllability of those conditions.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Political Reasoning and Voter Attribution
> Citizens' causal attributions for national conditions — economic performance, crime rates, public health outcomes — shape political behaviour through the same dimensional mechanisms that operate in interpersonal contexts. When voters attribute economic decline to the incumbent leader's incompetence (internal to the leader, stable, controllable), the emotional response is anger, and the behavioural response is to vote for change. When voters attribute the same decline to global forces beyond any leader's control (external, uncontrollable), the emotional response is resignation or sympathy, and support for the incumbent may be maintained. Political persuasion, in this framework, is largely an exercise in attributional framing: campaigns compete not over whether the economy is bad (the outcome is shared) but over *why* it is bad — over the dimensional coordinates of the causal explanation. The controllability dimension is particularly potent in political attribution, because it determines whether the leader is perceived as morally culpable (controllable cause → blame) or as a fellow victim of circumstances (uncontrollable cause → solidarity).
>
> **Structural principle:** The controllability-moral-inference bridge operates at societal scale: public attributions for national outcomes generate collective emotions (anger, hope, outrage, compassion) that drive collective political behaviour.
