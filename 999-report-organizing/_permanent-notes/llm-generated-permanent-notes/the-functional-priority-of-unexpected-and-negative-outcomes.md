# The Functional Priority of Unexpected and Negative Outcomes

> [!definition] The Functional Priority of Unexpected and Negative Outcomes
> The Functional Priority of Unexpected and Negative Outcomes refers to the cognitive tendency to prioritize causal analysis in response to surprising or unfavorable events over expected or positive ones.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Functional Priority of Unexpected and Negative Outcomes
> Research by Wong and Weiner (1981) and Weiner (1985) has consistently demonstrated that the causal search is most reliably triggered by three conditions: unexpected outcomes, negative outcomes, and outcomes of high personal importance. The mechanism operates asymmetrically — failure prompts more attributional activity than success, and surprising results provoke more causal analysis than expected ones. This asymmetry is not a flaw in the system but a design feature: the cognitive resources devoted to causal analysis are allocated where they are most needed, which is precisely where the existing model of the world has broken down.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
