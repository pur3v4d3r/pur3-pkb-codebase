# faded-worked-example

> [!definition] faded-worked-example
> - **Key-Term**: [[faded-worked-example]]
> - **Definition**: A faded-worked-example is an instructional technique where a problem is initially solved step-by-step, and then the steps are gradually removed to encourage learners to solve similar problems on their own, promoting skill transfer and independence.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This method leverages the principle of fading support in instruction, starting with fully worked examples that provide clear guidance and progressively reducing this support as learners become more competent. The core mechanism is based on the idea that learners can better understand complex tasks by seeing them broken down into manageable steps before being asked to perform them independently.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, a faded-worked-example might begin with a detailed solution of a problem, followed by a partially worked example where some steps are omitted and require learner completion. Eventually, only the initial problem statement is provided, and learners must solve it entirely on their own using the skills they have learned from previous examples.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the rate at which support is faded, the types of problems used, and the cognitive load management to ensure that learners do not become overwhelmed.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, this technique can enhance learning by providing a scaffolded approach that supports students as they develop problem-solving skills.

> [!example] Application
> It can be particularly effective in mathematics and science education where complex problems require step-by-step reasoning.
