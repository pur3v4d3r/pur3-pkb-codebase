# The Scaffolding Progression in Practice: A Graduate Research Methods Course

> [!definition] The Scaffolding Progression in Practice: A Graduate Research Methods Course
> The Scaffolding Progression in Practice is a curriculum design approach where initial support structures like templates and frequent feedback are gradually removed, encouraging students to develop their own regulatory strategies as they advance through the course.

## Examples

> [!example] The Scaffolding Progression in Practice: A Graduate Research Methods Course
> Consider a graduate curriculum in research methodology designed to produce researchers capable of independently designing, conducting, and evaluating research projects. In the first semester, the curriculum provides extensive scaffolding: worked examples of research designs, templates for literature reviews, structured protocols for data analysis, and frequent formative feedback. In the second semester, the templates are replaced by checklists and the feedback frequency decreases, requiring the student to internalise what was previously externalised. In the third semester, the checklists are removed, feedback is provided only on request, and the student is expected to design their own research project from problem identification through methodology to analysis. By the fourth semester, the student is functioning as a peer — reviewing others' designs, mentoring newer students, and operating with minimal external scaffolding. What has changed is not the difficulty of the work (which has increased at every stage) but the source of regulation: external curriculum structures have been progressively replaced by the student's own [[metacognition|metacognitive]] and [[self-regulated-learning|self-regulatory]] capacities. The curriculum has designed itself out of necessity.
