# self-serving-attribution-bias

> [!definition] self-serving-attribution-bias
> - **Key-Term**: [[self-serving-attribution-bias]]
> - **Definition**: Self-serving attribution bias is the tendency to attribute one's own successes to internal factors such as ability and effort, while attributing failures to external factors beyond one's control.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This cognitive bias plays a significant role in how individuals perceive their achievements and setbacks. It helps maintain self-esteem by providing explanations that enhance personal competence and reduce the impact of negative outcomes on self-worth.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this bias can be observed in various settings such as academic performance, sports, and workplace evaluations. For instance, students might attribute good grades to their intelligence or hard work but blame poor test scores on difficult questions or unfair grading criteria.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The concept has deep roots in attribution theory, which was first introduced by Fritz Heider in 1958. Attribution retraining is a technique aimed at altering this bias to improve mental health and performance.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, understanding self-serving attribution bias can help therapists develop strategies to address maladaptive thinking patterns associated with depression.

> [!example] Application
> Educators can use this knowledge to design interventions that encourage students to adopt more balanced attributions for their academic outcomes.
