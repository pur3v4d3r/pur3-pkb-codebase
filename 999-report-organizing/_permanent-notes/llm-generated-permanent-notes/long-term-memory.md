# long-term-memory

> [!definition] long-term-memory
> - **Key-Term**: [[long-term-memory]]
> - **Definition**: Long-term memory is the capacity to store and retrieve information over extended periods, ranging from minutes to a lifetime, which supports our ability to learn and remember important events, skills, and knowledge.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Long-term memory (LTM) is crucial for storing detailed and complex information that can be accessed later. It is divided into different types such as episodic (personal experiences), semantic (facts and concepts), and procedural (skills and habits).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The process of encoding, storage, and retrieval in LTM involves various brain regions like the hippocampus and neocortex. Encoding requires attention and processing to transform information into a form that can be stored, while retrieval involves accessing this stored information when needed.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the capacity limits of LTM, which are influenced by factors such as age, health, and individual differences in cognitive abilities.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding how LTM works can inform teaching strategies that enhance learning and retention, such as spaced repetition and elaborative rehearsal.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, knowledge of LTM is essential for diagnosing memory disorders and developing therapeutic interventions.
