# Self-Determination-Theory-in-Educational-Contexts

> [!definition] Self-Determination-Theory-in-Educational-Contexts
> - **Key-Term**: [[Self-Determination-Theory-in-Educational-Contexts]]
> - **Definition**: Self-Determination-Theory (SDT) in educational contexts focuses on the role of autonomy, competence, and relatedness in fostering intrinsic motivation among students, leading to better academic outcomes and well-being.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Paragraph 1: Foundational context and core mechanism. Self-Determination-Theory posits that individuals have innate psychological needs for autonomy (the need to be self-governing), competence (the need to feel effective in one's actions), and relatedness (the need to connect with others). In educational settings, these needs are crucial for promoting intrinsic motivation, which is the internal drive to engage in activities for their inherent satisfaction rather than external rewards.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Paragraph 2: How it works or is applied in practice. Teachers can support students' autonomy by providing choice and encouraging self-regulation; competence by offering challenging tasks that match students' abilities; and relatedness by fostering a supportive classroom environment where students feel valued and connected to their peers and teachers.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Paragraph 3: Key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots. SDT distinguishes between autonomous and controlled motivation, with the former being more beneficial for long-term learning and well-being. The theory also emphasizes the importance of cultural context in understanding these needs and motivations.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 4
> Optional paragraph 4: Historical development or origin. SDT was developed by Edward L. Deci and Richard M. Ryan in the late 1970s, with significant contributions from their work published in 1985 and further refined over subsequent decades.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Implication 1: Concrete application or real-world consequence. Teachers who implement SDT principles can see improvements in student engagement, academic performance, and overall well-being.

> [!example] Application
> Implication 2: A second distinct application. Schools that adopt an autonomy-supportive approach may experience reduced dropout rates and higher levels of student satisfaction.
