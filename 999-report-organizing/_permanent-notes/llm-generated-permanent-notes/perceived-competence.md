# perceived-competence

> [!definition] perceived-competence
> - **Key-Term**: [[perceived-competence]]
> - **Definition**: Perceived competence refers to an individual's subjective judgment of their ability to perform tasks effectively and successfully, which is central to self-determination theory (SDT).
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Perceived competence is a fundamental aspect of self-efficacy and is closely tied to the basic psychological needs proposed by SDT. When individuals perceive themselves as competent in a particular domain, they are more likely to engage in activities related to that domain with greater enthusiasm and persistence.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In educational settings, perceived competence can influence students' motivation and academic performance. For instance, students who feel competent in their studies are more likely to take on challenging tasks and persist through difficulties, whereas those who doubt their abilities may avoid such challenges or give up easily.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Perceived competence is not just about actual skill but also involves beliefs and attitudes that shape an individual's self-assessment of their capabilities.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational contexts, fostering perceived competence can lead to increased student engagement and better academic outcomes. Teachers can support this by providing constructive feedback and setting achievable goals.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace settings, managers who recognize and affirm employees' perceived competence can enhance job satisfaction and productivity.
