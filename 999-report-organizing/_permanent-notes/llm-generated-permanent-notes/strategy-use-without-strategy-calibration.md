# Strategy Use Without Strategy Calibration

> [!definition] Strategy Use Without Strategy Calibration
> Strategy Use Without Strategy Calibration refers to the application of memorized strategies without considering their appropriateness for different situations, leading to ineffective or counterproductive reading outcomes.

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] Strategy Use Without Strategy Calibration
> The greatest practical danger in strategy instruction is the production of readers who possess strategies and apply them mechanically without conditional reasoning about which strategy fits which situation. A reader who summarizes everything will produce summaries of trivial passages whose summarization adds nothing, and will fail to summarize the dense passages where summarization would have revealed structural comprehension failures. A reader who rereads everything will reread material that was understood and fail to reread material that was not. A reader who looks up every unfamiliar term will fragment their reading into a series of dictionary consultations whose cost outweighs their benefit. Strategy possession is not strategy use; strategy use is not strategic regulation. The conditional knowledge gap remains the central instructional challenge.
> *— [[metacognition-and-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
