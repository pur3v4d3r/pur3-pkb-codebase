# the-substitution-trap

> [!definition] the-substitution-trap
> - **Key-Term**: [[the-substitution-trap]]
> - **Definition**: The substitution trap refers to the cognitive bias where people solve an easier question instead of the harder one they were originally asked, often leading to incorrect conclusions or decisions.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> In the substitution trap, individuals simplify complex problems by substituting them with simpler ones that are more easily solved. This mechanism is rooted in cognitive psychology and can lead to systematic errors if not recognized.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> For example, when faced with a difficult decision, people might substitute it with a related but easier question, such as estimating the probability of success based on past outcomes rather than considering all relevant factors.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> This bias often arises due to limited cognitive resources and the tendency to seek quick solutions. It is closely related to heuristics and biases in judgment.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In AI alignment, understanding the substitution trap helps prevent models from making incorrect decisions by substituting complex problems with simpler ones.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical reasoning, recognizing this trap can help avoid misdiagnoses when doctors simplify diagnostic questions to more easily solvable ones.
