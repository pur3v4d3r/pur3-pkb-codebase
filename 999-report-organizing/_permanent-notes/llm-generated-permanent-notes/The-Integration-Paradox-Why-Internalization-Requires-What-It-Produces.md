# The-Integration-Paradox-Why-Internalization-Requires-What-It-Produces

> [!definition] The-Integration-Paradox-Why-Internalization-Requires-What-It-Produces
> - **Key-Term**: [[The-Integration-Paradox-Why-Internalization-Requires-What-It-Produces]]
> - **Definition**: The Integration Paradox refers to the phenomenon where internalizing external motivations, such as autonomy-supportive teaching practices, paradoxically requires those same practices for effective integration into one's own values and goals.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Paragraph 1: foundational context and core mechanism. The concept arises from Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that internalization occurs when individuals adopt external motivations as part of their self-system, aligning them with personal values and goals.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Paragraph 2: how it works or is applied in practice. For instance, a student who initially engages in homework due to external rewards may eventually come to value the learning process itself, but this internalization requires continued support from teachers who provide autonomy-supportive environments that foster intrinsic motivation.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Paragraph 3: key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots. The paradox highlights the tension between the need for initial external support and the ultimate goal of self-determined behavior, suggesting that effective integration is contingent on ongoing motivational climates.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Implication 1: concrete application or real-world consequence. Educators must continue to provide autonomy-supportive practices even after students show signs of intrinsic motivation to ensure sustained internalization.

> [!example] Application
> Implication 2: a second distinct application. In organizational settings, managers should maintain supportive environments that encourage employees' self-determined behavior rather than relying solely on extrinsic rewards.
