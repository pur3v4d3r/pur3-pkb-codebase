# Bloom's-Taxonomy

> [!definition] Bloom's-Taxonomy
> - **Key-Term**: [[Bloom's-Taxonomy]]
> - **Definition**: Bloom's Taxonomy is a hierarchical model of educational objectives that categorizes knowledge and skills into six levels: remembering, understanding, applying, analyzing, evaluating, and creating.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Bloom's Taxonomy was developed in the 1950s by Benjamin Bloom and his colleagues to classify educational goals and learning outcomes. It provides a framework for educators to design assessments that measure different levels of cognitive skills.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The taxonomy is structured hierarchically, with each level building upon the previous one. For example, understanding requires remembering information, while evaluating necessitates applying and analyzing it first.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the emphasis on higher-order thinking skills such as analysis, evaluation, and creation, which are considered more complex than lower-order skills like remembering and understanding.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, Bloom's Taxonomy helps teachers create balanced lesson plans that cover all levels of cognitive skills. It also aids in designing assessments that accurately measure student learning.

> [!example] Application
> By focusing on higher-order thinking skills, educators can foster critical thinking and problem-solving abilities among students.
