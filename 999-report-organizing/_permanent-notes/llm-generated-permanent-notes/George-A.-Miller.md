# George-A.-Miller

> [!definition] George-A.-Miller
> - **Key-Term**: [[George-A.-Miller]]
> - **Definition**: George A. Miller was an American psychologist known for his work on cognitive psychology, particularly the limits of human information processing capacity and the concept of chunks in memory.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Miller is best known for his research on the limitations of short-term memory, which he quantified as the 'magical number seven, plus or minus two' (Miller, 1956). This work laid the foundation for understanding how humans process and store information.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> He introduced the concept of chunks to explain how complex pieces of information can be broken down into smaller, more manageable units that are easier to remember. Miller's research has been influential in fields such as cognitive psychology, computer science, and education.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Miller also explored the relationship between language and thought, contributing to our understanding of how we process linguistic information.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, Miller’s findings have led to instructional strategies that break down complex tasks into smaller, more manageable steps to enhance learning.

> [!example] Application
> In computer science, his work on chunking has influenced the design of user interfaces and algorithms for data compression.
