# wilson-and-linville

> [!definition] wilson-and-linville
> - **Key-Term**: [[wilson-and-linville]]
> - **Definition**: Wilson and Linville's model refers to a theoretical framework that explains how individuals attribute causes for their own behaviors and outcomes, distinguishing between stable and unstable dimensions of causality in the context of personal control and self-efficacy.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This model posits that people categorize events as either due to internal or external factors, and these attributions can be further classified into controllable (stable) versus uncontrollable (unstable) dimensions. Stable attributions are believed to lead to more enduring changes in self-perception.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, Wilson and Linville's model is used to understand how individuals perceive their own actions and outcomes, which influences their motivation and behavior. For instance, attributing success to stable internal factors like ability can boost confidence, while attributing failure to unstable external factors like luck can prevent a decline in self-esteem.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The model has been applied across various fields including psychology, education, and organizational behavior, where it helps explain phenomena such as learned helplessness and self-efficacy.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding Wilson and Linville's model can inform strategies to foster resilience by encouraging students to attribute their successes and failures correctly.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical psychology, this model is used in attribution retraining programs aimed at helping individuals with depression or anxiety change maladaptive attributions.
