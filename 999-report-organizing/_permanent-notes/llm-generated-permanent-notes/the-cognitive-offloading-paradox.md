# The Cognitive Offloading Paradox

> [!definition] The Cognitive Offloading Paradox
> The Cognitive Offloading Paradox refers to the dual nature of externalizing metacognitive processes: it temporarily reduces cognitive load but risks long-term dependency, necessitating scaffold fading to promote internalization.

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Cognitive Offloading Paradox
> Cognitive offloading simultaneously solves and creates a problem. In the short term, externalising metacognitive processes to PKB scaffolds reduces cognitive load and improves regulatory quality. In the long term, persistent offloading may prevent the internalisation of those same processes, creating dependency on external tools. The resolution of this paradox — which is the subject of Section 7 — lies in the concept of **scaffold fading**: the deliberate, progressive withdrawal of external support as internal competence develops. A PKB designed for lifelong learning must therefore include not only scaffolds but also mechanisms for detecting when scaffolds have become redundant.
