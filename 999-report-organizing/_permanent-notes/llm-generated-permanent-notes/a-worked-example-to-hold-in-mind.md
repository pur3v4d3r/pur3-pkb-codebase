# A Worked Example to Hold in Mind

> [!definition] A Worked Example to Hold in Mind
> A Worked Example to Hold in Mind refers to scenarios or examples that illustrate how different internal motivations can lead to identical external behaviors, highlighting the diverse psychological architectures underlying seemingly uniform actions.

## Examples

> [!example] A Worked Example to Hold in Mind
> Consider three adults who go for a five-kilometer run on a Saturday morning. From the outside, the behavior is identical. Inside, they may differ radically:
>
> - **Adult A** runs because if she does not, she will feel guilty and unable to enjoy the rest of her day; the run is a debt she pays to escape an internal accuser.
> - **Adult B** runs because she has decided that cardiovascular health is important to the life she wants to lead, and running is the means she has chosen to support it; she does not particularly enjoy the run while it is happening but feels good about having done it.
> - **Adult C** runs because she finds the rhythm, the breath, the physical sensation, and the meditative quality of the activity intrinsically enjoyable; the run is its own reward.
>
> All three are "self-maintaining" the behavior. None requires an external prompt. The surface is identical. The deeper architecture — and the predictions that follow about persistence, affective experience, spillover, and well-being — is radically different. This is what the next levels of magnification will reveal.
