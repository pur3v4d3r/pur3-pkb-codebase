# controlled-motivation

> [!definition] controlled-motivation
> - **Key-Term**: [[controlled-motivation]]
> - **Definition**: Controlled motivation refers to the drive to engage in an activity because of external pressures, such as rewards or avoiding punishment, rather than personal interest or intrinsic satisfaction.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Controlled motivation is a concept within self-determination theory (SDT) that describes behaviors driven by external factors like rewards and punishments. It contrasts with autonomous motivation, which involves engaging in activities for their inherent value or personal enjoyment.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, controlled motivation can be seen when students study to avoid failing a test rather than because they find the subject interesting or personally fulfilling. This type of motivation often leads to less effective learning outcomes compared to intrinsic motivation.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> There are two sub-variants: introjected and identified regulation. Introjected regulation involves internalizing external pressures, while identified regulation is more about aligning with personal values despite external influences.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering autonomous motivation can lead to better long-term learning outcomes compared to controlled motivation.

> [!example] Application
> Teachers should aim to create a supportive environment that encourages intrinsic motivation rather than relying solely on extrinsic rewards and punishments.
