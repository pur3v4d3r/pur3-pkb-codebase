# learning-contracts

> [!definition] learning-contracts
> - **Key-Term**: [[learning-contracts]]
> - **Definition**: Learning contracts are personalized agreements between learners and educators that outline specific learning goals, activities, and assessment methods to be completed by the learner within a defined timeframe.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Learning contracts are rooted in self-directed learning (SDL) theory, particularly andragogy as proposed by Malcolm Knowles. They provide a structured yet flexible framework for learners to take ownership of their educational journey, setting clear objectives and expectations that align with their individual needs and goals.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, learning contracts typically involve the learner identifying areas for improvement or new skills to acquire, agreeing on specific tasks or activities to complete, and establishing criteria for evaluating progress. These agreements can be formalized in writing and reviewed periodically to ensure alignment with the learner's objectives and to provide feedback and support.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include variations in contract length, level of detail, and the degree of flexibility allowed. Some contracts may be highly structured, while others offer more autonomy to the learner. The concept also draws on theories of assessment for learning, where formative assessments are used as tools to scaffold learners' development.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors can use learning contracts to tailor educational experiences to individual student needs, potentially leading to higher engagement and better outcomes.

> [!example] Application
> Learners benefit from the clarity and structure provided by these agreements, which can enhance motivation and self-regulation skills.

> [!example] Application
> A cautionary note is that overly rigid contracts might stifle creativity or adaptability if they do not allow for sufficient flexibility.

## Open Threads

> [!open-question] Question
