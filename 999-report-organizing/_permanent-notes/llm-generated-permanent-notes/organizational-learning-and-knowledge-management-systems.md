# Organizational Learning and Knowledge Management Systems

> [!definition] Organizational Learning and Knowledge Management Systems
> Organizational Learning and Knowledge Management Systems are enterprise-wide frameworks that facilitate the development, sharing, and application of knowledge through collaborative engagement, structured taxonomies, and active cognitive processing, mirroring individual personal knowledge bases but addressing the complexities of distributed expertise across a community.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Organizational Learning and Knowledge Management Systems
> At the organizational scale, the same cognitive principles that govern individual PKB design apply to enterprise knowledge management systems — with additional complexities arising from the distribution of knowledge across multiple minds. [[communities-of-practice|Communities of practice]] (Wenger, 1998) function as organizational equivalents of schema networks — groups within which shared knowledge structures develop through collaborative engagement. Organizational knowledge management systems that support elaborative processing (requiring contributors to contextualize and connect new knowledge rather than merely depositing it), retrieval practice (through case-based discussion and after-action reviews), and schema externalization (through structured knowledge taxonomies and relationship maps) implement the same cognitive principles at a collective level. The expertise reversal effect operates organizationally as well: knowledge management systems effective for new employees may impede expert practitioners whose internalized schemas make structured templates unnecessary. The structural principle enabling transfer: **knowledge systems at any scale benefit from architectures that demand active cognitive engagement, support progressive schema development, and adapt to the expertise level of their users.**
