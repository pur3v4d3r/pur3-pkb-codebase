# The Linear Rise and the Cliff

> [!definition] The Linear Rise and the Cliff
> The Linear Rise and the Cliff refers to a motivational model where effort increases linearly with goal difficulty up to a point perceived as unattainable by the individual, beyond which effort rapidly declines.

## Examples

> [!example] The Linear Rise and the Cliff
> In a typical Locke and Latham experiment, participants assigned a goal calibrated to require ninety percent of their maximum capacity will produce sustained near-maximum effort for the duration of the task; participants assigned a goal calibrated to require one hundred and twenty percent of their maximum will frequently abandon the task altogether, producing effort below baseline. The relationship is linear up to a cliff — and the cliff is determined not by some objective measure of difficulty but by the performer's perception of attainability, which is the bridge through which [[self-efficacy]] enters the theory.
> *— [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
