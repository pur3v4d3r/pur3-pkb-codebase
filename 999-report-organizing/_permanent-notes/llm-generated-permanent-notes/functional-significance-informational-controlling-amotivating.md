# functional-significance-informational-controlling-amotivating

> [!definition] functional-significance-informational-controlling-amotivating

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This concept is rooted in self-determination theory (SDT), which posits that individuals are motivated by three basic psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Informational and controlling factors play a crucial role in fulfilling these needs, thereby influencing motivation and behavior.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Informational factors provide the necessary knowledge and feedback to enhance understanding and facilitate learning, while controlling factors involve external influences such as rewards or punishments that can either support or undermine autonomous motivation.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between intrinsic and extrinsic motivations, where informational factors are more likely to foster intrinsic motivation by enhancing perceived competence and autonomy.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, providing students with meaningful feedback and fostering a supportive environment can enhance their intrinsic motivation and learning outcomes.

> [!example] Application
> In organizational contexts, managers can use informational and controlling strategies to promote autonomous motivation among employees, leading to higher job satisfaction and productivity.
