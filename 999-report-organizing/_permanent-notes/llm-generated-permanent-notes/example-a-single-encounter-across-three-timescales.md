# Example: A Single Encounter Across Three Timescales

> [!definition] Example: A Single Encounter Across Three Timescales
> A Single Encounter Across Three Timescales refers to a feedback episode in which immediate interpretations influence subsequent behavior over short-term (weeks to months) and long-term (years to decades) timescales, shaping the recipient's regulatory style and autonomy orientation.

## Examples

> [!example] Example: A Single Encounter Across Three Timescales
> Consider a feedback episode in which a writing instructor returns a graduate student's draft with substantial revision suggestions. The objective event is a marked-up document. Its consequences play out across three timescales:
>
> - **Moment-to-moment (the next hour):** The student opens the document and processes the feedback. If she interprets it informationally, she registers the suggestions as material she can use; her competence need is partially satisfied (someone competent has engaged with her work seriously) and her autonomy need is preserved (she remains the agent who will decide what to do with the suggestions). If she interprets it controllingly, she registers the suggestions as criticism; her autonomy is thwarted (she experiences the feedback as the imposition of someone else's judgment) and her relatedness may be too (the instructor seems to value her work less than she had hoped).
> - **Weeks to months (the semester):** Across many such episodes, the cumulative balance of informational vs controlling interpretations produces a stable shift in the student's regulatory style for the writing-revision behavior. If informational interpretations have predominated, her revision behavior moves toward identification or integration; if controlling interpretations have predominated, it stalls at introjection or external regulation, and she revises only when forced to.
> - **Years to decades (the career):** The cumulative balance across many semesters in many courses with many instructors produces a stable autonomy orientation that the student carries into future encounters with feedback throughout her career. By her late thirties, she has either become a writer who can use feedback as nutriment or one who experiences feedback as continuous threat — and the orientation will shape how every future feedback encounter is interpreted.
>
> The single feedback episode is one event. Its consequences cascade across timescales because each interpretation feeds the next.
