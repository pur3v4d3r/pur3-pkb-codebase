# confirmation-bias

> [!definition] confirmation-bias
> - **Key-Term**: [[confirmation-bias]]
> - **Definition**: Confirmation bias is the tendency to search for, interpret, favor, and recall information in a way that confirms one's preexisting beliefs or hypotheses.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Confirmation bias operates as a cognitive shortcut where individuals selectively expose themselves to information that supports their existing views while ignoring contradictory evidence. This mechanism can lead to flawed decision-making processes by reinforcing preconceived notions rather than considering all available data objectively.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, confirmation bias is evident in various domains such as politics, science, and everyday life. For instance, a person might only read news sources that align with their political beliefs or selectively remember information that supports their personal theories about the world.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between belief perseverance (continuing to hold onto a belief despite contradictory evidence) and motivated reasoning (interpreting new information in a way that confirms one's existing beliefs). These sub-variants highlight how confirmation bias can persist even when faced with conflicting data.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In AI alignment, confirmation bias can lead to biased training datasets or model outputs if developers only seek out examples that support their hypotheses.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical reasoning, doctors might over-rely on diagnostic tests that confirm their initial hunches rather than considering alternative possibilities.
