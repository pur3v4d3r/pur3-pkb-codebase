# conditional-regard

> [!definition] conditional-regard
> - **Key-Term**: [[conditional-regard]]
> - **Definition**: Conditional regard refers to the idea that individuals may receive affection, approval, and acceptance from others based on certain conditions or behaviors they exhibit, rather than as an unconditional aspect of their self-worth.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Conditional regard is a central concept in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), particularly within the context of basic psychological needs. It describes how individuals may feel that their worthiness for love and acceptance depends on meeting certain external criteria, such as performance or adherence to social norms, rather than being inherently valuable.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, conditional regard can manifest in various ways, from parental expectations to societal pressures. For instance, a child might believe they are loved only if they achieve good grades, while an adult may feel accepted by their peers solely based on their professional success.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between internal and external regulation of behavior, where individuals who experience conditional regard often exhibit lower levels of intrinsic motivation and higher levels of anxiety or depression.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding conditional regard can help educators foster a supportive environment that promotes intrinsic motivation rather than extrinsic rewards.

> [!example] Application
> In therapeutic contexts, recognizing the impact of conditional regard on clients' self-esteem and well-being is crucial for addressing issues related to low self-worth.
