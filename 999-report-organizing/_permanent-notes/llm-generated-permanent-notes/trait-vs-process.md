# Trait vs. Process

> [!definition] Trait vs. Process
> Trait vs. Process refers to the debate on whether self-directedness is a stable personality characteristic (trait) or a context-dependent developmental process (process) that varies across domains and contexts.

## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Trait vs. Process
> **The tension:** Is self-directedness a stable personality characteristic or a context-dependent process?
> **Position A (Trait):** Early SDLRS research assumed self-directedness as a relatively stable individual difference that could be measured.
> **Position B (Process):** Garrison, Candy, contemporary researchers — self-direction is a process that manifests differently across domains, contexts, and developmental stages.
> **Current evidence:** Strongly favors the process/developmental view. Individuals demonstrate domain-specific variation in self-directedness, and SDL capacity develops through scaffolded experience.
> **This report's stance:** Self-direction is fundamentally developmental and context-sensitive, consistent with Grow's staged model and the evidence for domain-specificity.
