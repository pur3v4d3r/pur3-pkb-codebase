# Pause and Reflect

> [!definition] Pause and Reflect
> Pause and Reflect involves stepping back to consider one's actions, motivations, and explanations for behavior from multiple perspectives, particularly distinguishing between dispositional (personal) and situational (contextual) factors.

## Additional Material

> [!active-reading] Pause and Reflect
> Before proceeding, consider a recent situation in which you evaluated someone else's behaviour negatively. In retrospect, how much of your explanation focused on *who they are* (dispositional) versus *what they were dealing with* (situational)? Now consider: if the roles had been reversed — if *you* had produced the same behaviour — how might your causal explanation have differed? The exercise is designed to make the actor-observer asymmetry experientially visible.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!active-reading] Pause and Reflect
> As you read through the psychological capacities described below, consider which of these you have consciously developed through deliberate practice and which you acquired incidentally. How did the curricula you experienced contribute to — or fail to contribute to — the development of each capacity?
> *— [[curriculum-design-for-lifelong-learning-and-self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!active-reading] Pause and Reflect
> Think about a learning project you have sustained over months or years without external incentives. What kept you going? As you read this section, compare the motivational conditions described here against those that sustained your own long-term learning.
> *— [[curriculum-design-for-lifelong-learning-and-self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!active-reading] Pause and Reflect
> Consider the last time you received feedback on your work. Did the feedback direct your attention to the task, to your process, to your self-regulation, or to you as a person? How might the feedback have been redesigned to develop your self-evaluative capacity rather than merely providing an external judgement?
> *— [[curriculum-design-for-lifelong-learning-and-self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!active-reading] Pause and Reflect
> Examine one's own aspiration structure through the GCT lens. Of the life goals one considers most important, which are oriented toward intrinsic outcomes (growth, connection, contribution, health) and which are oriented toward extrinsic indicators (wealth, recognition, image)? The diagnostic question is not whether extrinsic goals are present — they nearly always are — but whether they occupy the *center* of the aspiration structure or the periphery.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
