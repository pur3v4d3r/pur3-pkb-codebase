# cognitive-evaluation-theory-cet

> [!definition] cognitive-evaluation-theory-cet
> - **Key-Term**: [[cognitive-evaluation-theory-cet]]
> - **Definition**: Cognitive-Evaluation-Theory (CET) is a theoretical framework within cognitive psychology that explains how individuals evaluate their competence and the consequences of such evaluations on motivation and performance.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> CET posits that people continuously assess their own abilities and the outcomes associated with these assessments. These evaluations can be positive or negative, influencing intrinsic motivation and subsequent behavior.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> When individuals perceive themselves as competent in a task, they are more likely to engage in autonomous and intrinsically motivated behaviors, leading to better performance and satisfaction. Conversely, negative evaluations may lead to decreased motivation and poorer performance.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> CET is closely related to Self-Determination Theory (SDT), particularly the concept of autonomy, where individuals feel competent and have a sense of control over their actions.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding CET can help educators design tasks that foster positive self-evaluations and promote intrinsic motivation among students.

> [!example] Application
> Managers in organizations can use CET to create environments that encourage employees to evaluate themselves positively, thereby enhancing job satisfaction and productivity.
