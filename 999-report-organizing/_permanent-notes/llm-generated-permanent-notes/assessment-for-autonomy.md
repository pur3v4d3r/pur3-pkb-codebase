# Assessment for Autonomy

> [!definition] Assessment for Autonomy
> Assessment for autonomy refers to the deliberate design of assessment and feedback systems whose primary purpose is to develop the learner's capacity for accurate self-evaluation, self-monitoring, and self-directed improvement, rather than primarily serving the institutional function of grading, ranking, or certification. The mechanism operates by progressively transferring the evaluative function — the capacity to judge the quality of one's own work against appropriate standards — from the assessor to the learner, mirroring the broader scaffolding-fading progression in the domain of evaluation specifically.
>
> **Boundary:** Assessment for autonomy does not eliminate the need for external assessment. Certification, accreditation, and quality assurance serve legitimate social functions. The argument is that these functions should be subordinated to, or at minimum balanced with, the developmental function of building self-evaluative capacity. In a well-designed curriculum, external assessment becomes less frequent and less formatively important as the learner's self-evaluative capacity develops, not because external standards are abandoned but because the learner has internalised them.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Assessment for autonomy is the evaluative dimension of the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression — the mechanism through which the transfer of authority from curriculum to learner is completed in the domain of quality judgement.
>
