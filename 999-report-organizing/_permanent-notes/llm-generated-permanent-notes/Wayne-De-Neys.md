# Wayne-De-Neys

> [!definition] Wayne-De-Neys
> - **Key-Term**: [[Wayne-De-Neys]]
> - **Definition**: The Wayne-De-Neys is a cognitive model that explains how individuals can be influenced by irrelevant information when making decisions, despite knowing better. It suggests that people often rely on heuristics or shortcuts to make quick judgments, which can lead to systematic errors in reasoning.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The Wayne-De-Neys model posits that decision-making is often driven by a combination of explicit and implicit processes. Explicit processes involve conscious, deliberate reasoning, while implicit processes are automatic and often based on heuristics or biases.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> When making decisions, individuals may be influenced by irrelevant information because their cognitive system can sometimes fail to properly integrate relevant information with the heuristic-based shortcuts they use. This can lead to errors in judgment that contradict their explicit knowledge of better decision-making strategies.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the idea that the model helps explain why people might engage in attribute substitution, where they substitute a more difficult question with an easier one that shares some similarity.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In AI alignment and machine learning, understanding the Wayne-De-Neys can help developers design systems that are less prone to errors caused by irrelevant information.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical reasoning, it highlights the importance of cognitive forcing functions to prevent clinicians from falling into heuristic traps.
