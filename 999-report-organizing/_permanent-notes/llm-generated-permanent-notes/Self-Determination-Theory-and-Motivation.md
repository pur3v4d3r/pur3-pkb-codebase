# Self-Determination-Theory-and-Motivation

> [!definition] Self-Determination-Theory-and-Motivation
> - **Key-Term**: [[Self-Determination-Theory-and-Motivation]]
> - **Definition**: Self-Determination-Theory (SDT) is a psychological theory that posits that human motivation and mental health are best understood in terms of the extent to which people feel they have control over their lives, and the degree to which they experience certain basic psychological needs as satisfied. These needs include autonomy, competence, and relatedness.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> SDT suggests that individuals are motivated by three innate psychological needs: autonomy (the need to be self-governing), competence (the need to feel effective in one's actions), and relatedness (the need for positive social connections). When these needs are met, people experience intrinsic motivation, which leads to higher quality performance and well-being.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, SDT is applied in various settings such as education, workplace management, and therapy. For instance, educators can foster autonomy by allowing students to make choices about their learning activities, competence by providing opportunities for mastery, and relatedness by encouraging collaboration among peers.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> SDT has several sub-variants, including the more recent SDT 3.0 framework that emphasizes the role of values in motivation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, teachers can enhance student engagement and performance by designing tasks that allow for autonomy, provide opportunities for students to feel competent through mastery experiences, and foster a sense of relatedness among peers.

> [!example] Application
> In organizational settings, managers can improve employee satisfaction and productivity by creating work environments that support autonomy, competence, and social connections.
