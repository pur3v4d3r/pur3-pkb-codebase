# autonomy-orientation

> [!definition] autonomy-orientation
> - **Key-Term**: [[autonomy-orientation]]
> - **Definition**: Autonomy-orientation refers to an individual's preference for self-determined actions and behaviors, characterized by a sense of personal control over one’s life and activities.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Autonomy-orientation is central in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that individuals are motivated by the fulfillment of basic psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. When these needs are satisfied, particularly through experiences that support self-determined actions, individuals exhibit higher levels of intrinsic motivation and well-being.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, autonomy-orientation is fostered in educational settings through autonomy-supportive teaching strategies that encourage students to engage in self-regulated learning and make their own choices about how they learn. This approach enhances students' sense of competence and relatedness, leading to more meaningful and lasting learning outcomes.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between autonomy-orientation and independence, where the former emphasizes internal control and personal choice, while the latter may focus on external factors such as social status or parental approval.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering autonomy-orientation can lead to increased student engagement and motivation, resulting in better academic performance and higher levels of satisfaction.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace settings, promoting autonomy-orientation among employees can enhance job satisfaction and productivity by aligning work tasks with personal values and goals.
