# Attribution Retraining Design Protocol

> [!definition] Attribution Retraining Design Protocol
> Attribution Retraining Design Protocol is a structured intervention designed to modify maladaptive attribution patterns through diagnostic assessment, normative information presentation, self-monitoring, reframing, vocabulary expansion, and maintenance practices.

## Additional Material

> [!protocol] Attribution Retraining Design Protocol
> **Purpose:** Design a structured attribution retraining intervention for educational, clinical, or organisational contexts.
>
> **Stage 1 — Diagnostic Assessment:**
> 1. Identify the target population's dominant maladaptive attribution patterns (e.g., stable-uncontrollable failure attributions)
> 2. Assess through: attributional style questionnaires, causal attribution interviews, or performance-attribution diaries
> 3. Determine baseline emotional and motivational profiles associated with current attributional patterns
>
> **Stage 2 — Induction Phase (Sessions 1-3):**
> 1. Present normative information: "Most people initially struggle because of unfamiliarity with the material (unstable cause), not because of inability (stable cause)"
> 2. Provide vivid models: testimonials from similar others who overcame initial failure through strategy change (unstable, controllable attribution)
> 3. Use the Wilson & Linville (1985) paradigm: show evidence that difficulty is temporary and typical
>
> **Stage 3 — Consolidation Phase (Sessions 4-8):**
> 1. Assign attributional self-monitoring: after each outcome, record the spontaneous causal explanation and classify it dimensionally
> 2. Practice attributional reframing: generate alternative explanations for the same outcome and compare their dimensional coordinates
> 3. Build [[attributional-vocabulary]]: expand the range of causal explanations available for inner speech
>
> **Stage 4 — Maintenance:**
> 1. Space practice over increasing intervals
> 2. Generalise to new domains (near transfer → far transfer)
> 3. Monitor for relapse to maladaptive patterns
>
> **Moderating conditions:** Most effective when the target population perceives themselves as having low control, is in a novel environment, and has not yet established entrenched attributional habits.
