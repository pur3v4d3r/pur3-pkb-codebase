# Schema (Bartlett, 1932; Rumelhart, 1980)

> [!definition] Schema (Bartlett, 1932; Rumelhart, 1980)
> A [[schema-theory-and-knowledge-organization|schema]] is an organized knowledge structure in [[long-term-memory|long-term memory]] that represents a category, concept, event type, or procedure, and that guides the interpretation, encoding, and retrieval of new information. Schemas are not passive storage containers but active processing structures: they direct attention toward schema-relevant features, fill in missing information through default values, and assimilate new experiences by modifying existing representations or constructing new ones. [[Schema Construction|Schema construction]] — the building of new schemas — and [[Schema Automation|schema automation]] — the process by which well-practiced schemas operate with minimal conscious effort — are the two fundamental learning mechanisms in [[Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)|cognitive load theory]].
>
> **Boundary:** Schemas are not identical to concepts or categories; they are richer, incorporating relational structure, default values, and procedural knowledge. A schema for "academic conference" includes not only the concept but expectations about sequence, roles, spatial layout, and social norms.
>
> **See also:** [[Schema Construction]], [[Schema Automation]], [[schema-theory-and-knowledge-organization]], [[Schema-Theory-Bartlett,-Rumelhart]]
