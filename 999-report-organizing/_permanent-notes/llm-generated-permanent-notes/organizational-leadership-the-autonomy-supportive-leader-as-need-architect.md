# Organizational Leadership — The Autonomy-Supportive Leader as Need Architect

> [!definition] Organizational Leadership — The Autonomy-Supportive Leader as Need Architect
> An Organizational Leadership — The Autonomy-Supportive Leader as Need Architect is an approach that designs organizational structures, policies, and cultures to distribute decision-making authority, facilitate employees' connection of their work to the organization's purpose, and balance autonomy support with coordination needs.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Organizational Leadership — The Autonomy-Supportive Leader as Need Architect
> SDT's analysis of autonomy-supportive versus controlling management (Section 8) operates at the level of individual manager-employee interactions. At the level of organizational leadership — the design of systems, policies, and cultures — the structural principle extends further. An organization's architecture itself can be autonomy-supportive or controlling: flat hierarchies distribute decision-making authority (supporting autonomy), while steep hierarchies concentrate it (controlling). Transparent communication of strategic rationale allows employees to connect daily work to organizational purpose (facilitating identified regulation), while need-to-know information hoarding leaves employees performing tasks whose significance they cannot evaluate (producing external regulation). The boundary condition for this transfer is that organizational structure must balance autonomy support with coordination requirements — SDT's insight that structure and autonomy are complementary, not opposed (Section 4), becomes especially consequential at the organizational level where coordination failure has existential costs.
