# perceived-locus-of-causality

> [!definition] perceived-locus-of-causality
> - **Key-Term**: [[perceived-locus-of-causality]]
> - **Definition**: Perceived locus of causality refers to an individual's belief about the source of events and behaviors, whether they are seen as internally controlled (e.g., due to personal effort) or externally controlled (e.g., due to luck or circumstances).
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept is foundational in social psychology, particularly in attribution theory. It influences how individuals interpret their own actions and those of others, affecting attitudes, emotions, and behaviors.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, it operates through two stages: first, identifying the cause (e.g., internal or external), then evaluating its impact on outcomes. This process can be influenced by various factors such as situational context and personal history.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between stable versus unstable causes, global versus specific attributions, and controllable versus uncontrollable events.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, understanding perceived locus of causality is crucial for treating conditions like depression, where patients may attribute negative outcomes to internal factors.

> [!example] Application
> Educational interventions can be designed to retrain students' perceptions from attributing failure externally (e.g., due to luck) to internally (e.g., lack of effort or skill).
