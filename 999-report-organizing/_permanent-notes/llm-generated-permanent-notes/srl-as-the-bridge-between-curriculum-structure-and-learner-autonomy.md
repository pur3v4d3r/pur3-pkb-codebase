# SRL as the Bridge Between Curriculum Structure and Learner Autonomy

> [!definition] SRL as the Bridge Between Curriculum Structure and Learner Autonomy
> SRL as the Bridge Between Curriculum Structure and Learner Autonomy refers to self-regulated learning (SRL) serving as a mediating process where external curriculum structures are gradually internalized by learners, fostering their ability to manage their own learning independently.

## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] SRL as the Bridge Between Curriculum Structure and Learner Autonomy
> One way to understand the relationship between curriculum design and self-directed learning is to view self-regulated learning as the mediating mechanism — the bridge — between external structure and internal autonomy. Curriculum provides the initial external regulation (goals, strategies, feedback, evaluation criteria) that the learner gradually internalises as self-regulatory capacity through repeated cycles of practice and reflection. The curriculum designer's fundamental task, from this perspective, is to engineer the internalisation process itself — to create conditions under which external scaffolds are progressively replaced by internal regulatory mechanisms, so that by the time the curriculum ends, the learner possesses their own internal "curriculum" for managing learning. This reframes the autonomy-structure paradox as a developmental sequence rather than a contradiction: structure first produces, and then is replaced by, self-regulation.
