# The Replication Challenge to the Undermining Effect

> [!definition] The Replication Challenge to the Undermining Effect
> The replication challenge to the undermining effect refers to subsequent research that questions the consistency and mechanism of the finding that expected tangible rewards reduce intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks, suggesting alternative explanations such as shifts in task representation.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Replication Challenge to the Undermining Effect
> The [[the-undermining-effect|undermining effect]] — the finding that expected tangible rewards reduce intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks — has been subjected to replication scrutiny with mixed results. While Deci, Koestner, and Ryan's (1999) meta-analysis confirmed the effect across 128 studies with substantial effect sizes, later work by Murayama and colleagues (2010) using neuroimaging suggested that the effect may operate through a different mechanism than CET proposes — possibly through shifts in task representation rather than changes in perceived locus of causality. The effect is reliably obtained under specific conditions (tangible, expected, engagement-contingent rewards for interesting tasks) but is more fragile than early presentations suggested when conditions are varied. For SDT, the stakes are real but not existential: CET is one of six mini-theories, and the undermining effect is one of many phenomena the theory explains. If the mechanism requires revision, the broader theoretical architecture remains intact because CET's foundational insight — that external events are interpreted through the dual lens of control and information — does not depend on any single experimental paradigm.
