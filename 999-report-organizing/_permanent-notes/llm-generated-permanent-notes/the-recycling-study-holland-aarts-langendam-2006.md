# The Recycling Study (Holland, Aarts, & Langendam, 2006)

> [!definition] The Recycling Study (Holland, Aarts, & Langendam, 2006)
> The Recycling Study (Holland, Aarts, & Langendam, 2006) refers to a research investigation that demonstrated how implementation intentions—specific plans about when and where to perform an action—can lead to sustained increases in recycling behavior among office workers, even after the initial reminder is removed.

## Examples

> [!example] The Recycling Study (Holland, Aarts, & Langendam, 2006)
> A particularly elegant demonstration of the cue-detection mechanism comes from a study in which office workers were either given goal intentions to recycle more (control group) or implementation intentions specifying when and where they would carry recyclable items to the recycling bin (intervention group). Behavioral measurement over weeks following the intervention showed substantially higher recycling rates in the implementation-intention group, *and* the effect persisted in subsequent weeks even when the explicit reminder had been removed. The mechanism here is transparent: the cue-behavior link, once encoded, did not require ongoing conscious effort to operate; the office environment continued to present the cues that triggered the planned behavior, and the behavior continued to occur.
> *— [[implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
