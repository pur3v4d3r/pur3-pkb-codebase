# declarative-procedural-and-conditional-metacognitive-knowledge

> [!definition] declarative-procedural-and-conditional-metacognitive-knowledge
> - **Key-Term**: [[declarative-procedural-and-conditional-metacognitive-knowledge]]
> - **Definition**: Declarative-procedural-and-conditional-metacognitive-knowledge refers to the understanding of one's own cognitive processes, including knowing when and how to apply specific strategies or procedures based on the context and conditions of a task or problem-solving situation.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This concept involves three main components: declarative knowledge (knowing facts), procedural knowledge (knowing how to perform tasks), and conditional knowledge (understanding under what circumstances certain strategies should be applied). These types of metacognitive knowledge are crucial for effective learning, problem-solving, and decision-making.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Individuals with well-developed declarative-procedural-and-conditional-metacognitive-knowledge can reflect on their own thinking processes, recognize when they need to use specific strategies, and adjust their approach based on the situation. This self-awareness enhances their ability to monitor and control their cognitive activities effectively.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the integration of these different types of knowledge in real-time decision-making, which allows for flexible and adaptive problem-solving.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering declarative-procedural-and-conditional-metacognitive-knowledge can lead to improved learning outcomes as students become more adept at applying appropriate strategies based on the task demands.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, such knowledge is essential for accurate diagnosis and treatment planning. Healthcare professionals must be able to recognize when certain diagnostic procedures are necessary and understand the conditions under which they should be applied.
