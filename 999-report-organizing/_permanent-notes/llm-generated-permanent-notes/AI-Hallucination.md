# AI-Hallucination

> [!definition] AI-Hallucination
> - **Key-Term**: [[AI-Hallucination]]
> - **Definition**: AI-hallucination refers to the phenomenon where artificial intelligence systems generate outputs that are not based on input data, prior knowledge, or logical inference but appear plausible due to biases in training data, model architecture, or other factors.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> AI-hallucinations occur when AI models produce results that seem reasonable but are actually incorrect or nonsensical. This can happen because of biases in the training dataset, overfitting, or limitations in the model's architecture.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> For example, a language model might generate coherent sentences that do not align with reality due to exposure to biased data. Similarly, image recognition models may misclassify objects based on spurious correlations within their training set.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> These phenomena highlight the importance of understanding and mitigating biases in AI systems to ensure they produce reliable outputs.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> AI-hallucinations can lead to incorrect decisions or actions, especially in critical applications like healthcare or autonomous vehicles.

> [!example] Application
> They also pose challenges for model interpretability and trustworthiness, as users may not be aware of the underlying biases that influence AI outputs.
