# wilbert-mckeachie

> [!definition] wilbert-mckeachie
> - **Key-Term**: [[wilbert-mckeachie]]
> - **Definition**: Wilbert-McKeachie refers to the theoretical framework developed by Wilbert McKeachie that integrates metacognitive strategies and regulatory skills in educational settings, particularly focusing on how students manage their learning processes and seek help effectively.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The core of Wilbert-McKeachie's theory lies in understanding how students use metacognitive strategies to monitor and control their own learning. This involves recognizing one’s current level of knowledge (calibration) and adjusting study behaviors accordingly.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this framework emphasizes the importance of adaptive help-seeking as a regulatory skill, where students learn to identify when they need assistance and what type of support would be most beneficial for their learning process.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between different types of metacognitive monitoring and how these can vary across individuals and contexts. The theory also explores the calibration of metacognitive monitoring, which is crucial for effective self-regulated learning.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, teachers can use this framework to design interventions that help students become more aware of their own learning processes and improve their ability to seek appropriate assistance.

> [!example] Application
> This theory also has implications for instructional design, as it suggests that providing opportunities for students to practice metacognitive skills can enhance their overall academic performance.
