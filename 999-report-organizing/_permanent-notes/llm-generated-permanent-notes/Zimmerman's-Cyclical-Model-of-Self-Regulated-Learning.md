# Zimmerman's-Cyclical-Model-of-Self-Regulated-Learning

> [!definition] Zimmerman's-Cyclical-Model-of-Self-Regulated-Learning
> - **Key-Term**: [[Zimmerman's-Cyclical-Model-of-Self-Regulated-Learning]]
> - **Definition**: Zimmerman's Cyclical Model of Self-Regulated Learning is a framework that describes the processes involved in self-regulation, including goal setting, planning, monitoring, and evaluating one’s own learning progress over time.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The model posits that self-regulated learners actively engage in managing their own learning by setting clear goals, developing strategies to achieve these goals, and continuously assessing their progress. This cyclical process involves three phases: forethought (planning), performance (execution of plans), and self-reflection (evaluation).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this model is applied in educational settings where students are encouraged to take an active role in their learning by setting personal learning objectives, using various strategies to achieve these goals, and reflecting on the effectiveness of their approaches. This approach helps learners develop metacognitive skills and become more autonomous in their academic pursuits.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the emphasis on self-efficacy, which is crucial for initiating and maintaining self-regulated learning behaviors. The model also recognizes that different phases can be influenced by external factors such as teacher support and classroom environment.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors can use this model to design more effective learning activities that promote student autonomy and metacognition.

> [!example] Application
