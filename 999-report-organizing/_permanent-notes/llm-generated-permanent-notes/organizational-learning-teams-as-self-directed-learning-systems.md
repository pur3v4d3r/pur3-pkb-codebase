# Organizational Learning: Teams as Self-Directed Learning Systems

> [!definition] Organizational Learning: Teams as Self-Directed Learning Systems
> The process by which organizations enhance their collective ability to learn and adapt through team-based, self-directed learning.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Organizational Learning: Teams as Self-Directed Learning Systems
> Organizations that learn effectively function as collective self-directed learning systems. Senge's concept of the "learning organization" describes entities that can diagnose their own performance gaps, formulate learning goals, experiment with new approaches, evaluate outcomes, and revise strategies — the organizational analog of Knowles's individual SDL process. The mechanism is structural: just as individual self-directed learning requires metacognitive monitoring at the individual level, organizational learning requires what Argyris and Schön called "double-loop learning" — the organizational capacity to question not just whether current strategies are working (single-loop) but whether the governing values and assumptions that generated those strategies are still valid (double-loop). This is the organizational equivalent of Brookfield's distinction between technical and critical self-direction.
