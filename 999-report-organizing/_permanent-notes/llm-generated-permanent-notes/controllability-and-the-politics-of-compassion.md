# Controllability and the Politics of Compassion

> [!definition] Controllability and the Politics of Compassion
> Controllability and the Politics of Compassion refers to how the perception of whether a situation or individual's circumstances are within their control influences emotional responses and subsequent political attitudes, such as support for punitive versus redistributive policies.

## Examples

> [!example] Controllability and the Politics of Compassion
> Consider how the controllability dimension operates in public attitudes toward poverty. When poverty is attributed to controllable causes — laziness, poor choices, lack of discipline — the dominant emotional response is anger and moral condemnation, and support for punitive policies increases. When poverty is attributed to uncontrollable causes — structural inequality, lack of opportunity, illness, discrimination — the dominant emotional response is pity and sympathy, and support for redistributive policies increases. Weiner's (1995) research on helping behaviour demonstrated this mechanism experimentally: participants were more willing to lend lecture notes to a classmate who missed class due to illness (uncontrollable) than to a classmate who missed class to go to the beach (controllable). The causal attribution, mediated by the controllability dimension, generates the emotion, which generates the behavioural response. The mechanism operates identically whether the scale is interpersonal (lending notes) or societal (welfare policy).
