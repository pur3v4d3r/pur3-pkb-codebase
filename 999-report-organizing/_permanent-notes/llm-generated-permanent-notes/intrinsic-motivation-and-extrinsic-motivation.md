# intrinsic-motivation-and-extrinsic-motivation

> [!definition] intrinsic-motivation-and-extrinsic-motivation
> - **Key-Term**: [[intrinsic-motivation-and-extrinsic-motivation]]
> - **Definition**: Intrinsic motivation refers to the internal desire to engage in an activity for the sake of personal enjoyment, interest, or challenge, while extrinsic motivation involves performing actions to attain external rewards such as money, grades, or recognition.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> In educational psychology, intrinsic and extrinsic motivations are fundamental concepts that explain why individuals engage in learning activities. Intrinsic motivation arises from the inherent enjoyment of the task itself, leading to sustained interest and deeper engagement with the material. Extrinsic motivation, on the other hand, is driven by external factors such as rewards or punishments.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Research has shown that intrinsic motivation can lead to higher quality performance and better retention of information because it fosters a more engaged and personally invested approach to learning. However, excessive reliance on extrinsic motivators can sometimes undermine intrinsic interest in the task over time.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between direct (e.g., grades) and indirect (e.g., social recognition) forms of external rewards, as well as the concept of self-determination theory which posits that autonomy, competence, and relatedness are essential for fostering both types of motivation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors can enhance student engagement by designing tasks that align with students' interests to boost intrinsic motivation. For example, allowing students to choose their own research topics or providing opportunities for hands-on learning.

> [!example] Application
> Using extrinsic motivators like grades and rewards can be effective in certain contexts but should be balanced with fostering a sense of autonomy and competence among learners.
