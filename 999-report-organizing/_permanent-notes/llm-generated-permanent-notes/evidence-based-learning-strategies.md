# evidence-based-learning-strategies

> [!definition] evidence-based-learning-strategies
> - **Key-Term**: [[evidence-based-learning-strategies]]
> - **Definition**: Evidence-based learning strategies are instructional methods and practices that have been shown to be effective through rigorous research and empirical evidence, aiming to enhance student learning outcomes and engagement in educational settings.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> These strategies are grounded in the principles of cognitive psychology and educational neuroscience, focusing on how learners process information and construct knowledge. They often involve active learning techniques such as problem-solving, collaborative activities, and metacognitive practices that help students develop deeper understanding and retention.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, evidence-based learning strategies can be implemented through various methods like inquiry-based learning, spaced repetition, and the use of formative assessments to monitor student progress and adjust instruction accordingly. These approaches are designed to foster autonomy and critical thinking while ensuring that learners achieve specific educational goals.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the importance of tailoring these strategies to fit the individual needs and characteristics of students, as well as integrating them with other pedagogical theories such as Andragogy (Knowles) to address adult learning principles. Theoretical roots also extend into constructivist and social cognitive theories that emphasize the role of context and interaction in learning.
