# Example: The Three Needs in a Clinical Internalization Context

> [!definition] Example: The Three Needs in a Clinical Internalization Context
> The Three Needs in a Clinical Internalization Context refers to relatedness (feeling cared for), autonomy (having control over one's care), and competence (having necessary skills and support) as conditions that facilitate the internalization of health behaviors among patients.

## Examples

> [!example] Example: The Three Needs in a Clinical Internalization Context
> Consider a patient with type 2 diabetes whose physician has prescribed a daily blood glucose monitoring routine, dietary modifications, and an exercise regimen. The behaviors are extrinsic (none of them is performed for its own sake; all serve the outcome of disease management) and the prescription is, at the moment of receipt, externally regulated. Whether and how the regulation internalizes depends on the need-satisfaction conditions of the patient's life around these behaviors:
>
> - **Relatedness:** Does the patient have a treatment relationship in which they feel cared about as a person rather than as a case, family members who participate in the dietary changes rather than treating the patient as deficient, a community in which their condition is acknowledged without stigma? If yes, the regulations are more likely to be taken in at all rather than rejected or held at maximal external distance.
> - **Autonomy:** Is the patient given a meaningful rationale for each behavior, presented with options where options exist, invited to take a position on the treatment plan, treated as the agent of their own care? If yes, the regulations have a chance to move from introjected (performed out of guilt or anxiety) to identified (consciously endorsed for the value they serve).
> - **Competence:** Does the patient have the skills, the feedback, and the structural support to perform the behaviors successfully and to detect their own progress? If yes, the identified regulation can stabilize against the inevitable difficulties and the conditions for integration become possible.
>
> When all three conditions are present, the internalization trajectory can run; when one or more is absent, the trajectory stalls at whatever level the available conditions can sustain. This is not a soft, motivational story; it has direct implications for adherence, for HbA1c outcomes, for hospital readmission rates, and for the cost of care.
