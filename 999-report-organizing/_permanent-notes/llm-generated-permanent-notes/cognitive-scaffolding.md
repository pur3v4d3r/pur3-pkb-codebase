# cognitive-scaffolding

> [!definition] cognitive-scaffolding
> - **Key-Term**: [[cognitive-scaffolding]]
> - **Definition**: Cognitive scaffolding is a method of providing temporary support to learners, enabling them to perform tasks that would otherwise be too challenging for their current level of ability, and then gradually removing this support as the learner becomes more competent.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Cognitive scaffolding was introduced by Jerome Bruner, Lev Vygotsky, and others in the context of social learning theory. It involves a teacher or more knowledgeable other providing guidance to help learners understand complex concepts or solve problems they cannot handle alone.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The process typically begins with direct instruction from the scaffold provider, who then gradually shifts responsibility to the learner as their understanding grows. This can involve breaking down tasks into smaller steps, offering hints, and asking guiding questions that encourage independent thinking.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the importance of timing in providing support (too early or too late can hinder learning), and the need for scaffolds to be tailored to individual learners' needs.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, cognitive scaffolding helps ensure that students are challenged appropriately without becoming overwhelmed. It supports a more personalized approach to teaching.

> [!example] Application
> In AI-assisted learning environments, algorithms can dynamically adjust the level of support based on learner performance and feedback, enhancing efficacy.
