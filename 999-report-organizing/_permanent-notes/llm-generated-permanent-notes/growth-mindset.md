# growth-mindset

> [!definition] growth-mindset
> - **Key-Term**: [[growth-mindset]]
> - **Definition**: A growth mindset is the belief that one's abilities and intelligence can be developed through dedication and hard work, as opposed to the fixed mindset which assumes that talent alone determines success.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept of a growth mindset was introduced by psychologist Carol Dweck in her research on motivation. Individuals with a growth mindset view challenges as opportunities for learning and improvement, rather than threats or obstacles.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means that people with a growth mindset are more likely to embrace feedback, persist through difficulties, and see effort as the path to mastery. They believe that their abilities can be developed over time, which fosters resilience and continuous learning.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between incremental versus entity theories of ability, where individuals with an incremental theory (growth mindset) focus on process and improvement, while those with an entity theory (fixed mindset) focus on outcome.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering a growth mindset can lead to improved academic performance and increased motivation among students.

> [!example] Application
> In the workplace, promoting a growth mindset can enhance employee development, innovation, and overall job satisfaction.
