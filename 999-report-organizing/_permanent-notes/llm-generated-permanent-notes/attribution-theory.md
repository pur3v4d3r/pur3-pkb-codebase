# attribution-theory

> [!definition] attribution-theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[attribution-theory]]
> - **Definition**: Attribution theory is a psychological concept that explains how individuals interpret and explain the causes of their own behavior and the behavior of others, often attributing success or failure to internal factors like ability or effort, or external factors like luck or environment.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Attribution theory was developed by Fritz Heider in the mid-20th century and further elaborated by Bernard Weiner. It posits that people make sense of events by attributing their causes to either internal (personal) or external (situational) factors.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, attribution theory is used to understand how learners interpret feedback and success/failure in educational settings, which can influence their motivation and future behavior. For example, students who attribute success to effort are more likely to persist when faced with challenges compared to those who attribute it to luck or ability.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Attribution theory has several sub-variants, such as the distinction between stable vs. unstable attributions (whether a cause is expected to remain constant over time) and controllable vs. uncontrollable attributions (whether an individual can influence the outcome).

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding attribution theory helps educators design interventions that promote a growth mindset by encouraging students to attribute their successes and failures to effort rather than innate ability.

> [!example] Application
> In AI-assisted learning environments, attributions play a crucial role in how learners perceive the efficacy of these tools. Positive attributions can enhance engagement and motivation, while negative ones may lead to disengagement.
