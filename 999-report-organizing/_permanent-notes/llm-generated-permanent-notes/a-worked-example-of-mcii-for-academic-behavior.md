# A Worked Example of MCII for Academic Behavior

> [!definition] A Worked Example of MCII for Academic Behavior
> A Worked Example of MCII for Academic Behavior is a detailed illustration of how mental contrasting (visualizing future goals and current obstacles) followed by implementation intention (specifying when and where to take action) can be used to foster academic engagement with challenging material.

## Examples

> [!example] A Worked Example of MCII for Academic Behavior
> A graduate student wanting to read more difficult primary literature might apply MCII as follows. *Mental contrasting:* Vividly imagine the desired future state — a richer command of the literature, sharper conversations with peers, more original ideas in writing. Then deliberately surface the present obstacles — competing demands, the seductiveness of secondary literature, the discomfort of confusion in the face of difficult prose. The contrast either produces strong commitment (if the future feels feasible and important) or appropriate disengagement (if it does not). Assuming commitment is produced: *implementation intention:* "When I sit down at my desk after morning coffee on weekdays, I will read difficult primary literature for forty-five minutes before opening any other application or document." The mental contrasting has done the commitment work; the implementation intention does the cue-engineering work; the combination produces behavior change that either alone would be unlikely to achieve.
