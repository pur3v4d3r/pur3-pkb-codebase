# The Two-Stage Architecture of Attribution Retraining

> [!definition] The Two-Stage Architecture of Attribution Retraining
> The Two-Stage Architecture of Attribution Retraining consists of an induction stage where maladaptive attributions are identified and adaptive ones introduced, followed by a consolidation stage that reinforces the new pattern through practice and real-world application, leading to more durable effects.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] The Two-Stage Architecture of Attribution Retraining
> [[the-two-stage-attribution-intervention-architecture]] describes the characteristic structure of effective attribution retraining programmes: (1) an *induction* stage, in which the maladaptive attributional pattern is identified and the alternative, more adaptive attribution is introduced through modelling, information, or guided reflection; and (2) a *consolidation* stage, in which the new attributional pattern is reinforced through practice, feedback, and real-world application. Interventions that include both stages produce substantially larger and more durable effects than those that rely on induction alone, because the consolidation stage allows the new attributional habit to override the old one through repeated activation (Perry, Stupnisky, Hall, Chipperfield, & Weiner, 2010).
