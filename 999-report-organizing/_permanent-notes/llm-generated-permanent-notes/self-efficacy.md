# self-efficacy

> [!definition] self-efficacy
> - **Key-Term**: [[self-efficacy]]
> - **Definition**: Self-efficacy refers to an individual's belief in their capability to organize and execute the courses of action required to manage prospective situations effectively.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Self-efficacy is a fundamental concept within social cognitive theory, primarily developed by Albert Bandura. It encompasses an individual’s confidence in their ability to succeed in specific situations or accomplish a task. This belief influences how people approach challenges and persist through difficulties.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Individuals with high self-efficacy are more likely to set challenging goals for themselves, engage actively in problem-solving, and recover from setbacks more effectively. Conversely, those with low self-efficacy may avoid challenges, give up easily, or experience heightened stress when faced with obstacles.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Self-efficacy can be influenced by various factors including past experiences of success or failure, vicarious learning (observing others), verbal persuasion, and physiological states.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, fostering self-efficacy among students can lead to improved academic performance and persistence in the face of challenges. Teachers can enhance self-efficacy by providing constructive feedback, setting achievable goals, and encouraging a growth mindset.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace training programs, developing employees' self-efficacy can result in better job satisfaction, higher productivity, and reduced turnover rates.
