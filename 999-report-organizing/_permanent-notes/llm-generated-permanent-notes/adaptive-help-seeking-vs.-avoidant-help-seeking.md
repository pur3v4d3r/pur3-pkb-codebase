# adaptive-help-seeking-vs.-avoidant-help-seeking

> [!definition] adaptive-help-seeking-vs.-avoidant-help-seeking
> - **Key-Term**: [[adaptive-help-seeking-vs.-avoidant-help-seeking]]
> - **Definition**: Adaptive help-seeking and avoidant help-seeking refer to the strategies learners use when seeking assistance from others, where adaptive help-seeking involves effectively using external support to enhance learning, while avoidant help-seeking entails avoiding such support despite its potential benefits.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Adaptive help-seeking is a strategy where individuals actively seek out and utilize appropriate forms of assistance from teachers, peers, or resources to improve their understanding and performance. This approach aligns with the principles of self-regulated learning and can lead to better academic outcomes by fostering problem-solving skills and resilience.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In contrast, avoidant help-seeking involves minimizing reliance on external support, even when it could be beneficial. Learners who adopt this strategy might feel embarrassed or believe that seeking help is a sign of weakness, leading them to struggle independently despite the potential for improved learning through collaboration and guidance.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of self-efficacy, attributional styles, and cultural factors in shaping these behaviors. For instance, learners with higher self-efficacy are more likely to engage in adaptive help-seeking, while those who attribute their difficulties to stable internal factors (like low ability) may be more prone to avoidant help-seeking.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, promoting adaptive help-seeking can enhance student engagement and academic performance by encouraging a growth mindset and reducing feelings of helplessness.

> [!example] Application
> Teachers can foster an environment that supports adaptive help-seeking through strategies such as modeling the value of seeking assistance, providing constructive feedback, and creating opportunities for collaborative learning.
