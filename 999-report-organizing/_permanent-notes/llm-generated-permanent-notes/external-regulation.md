# external-regulation

> [!definition] external-regulation
> - **Key-Term**: [[external-regulation]]
> - **Definition**: External regulation refers to behaviors and motivations that are driven by external factors such as rewards, punishments, or social norms, rather than personal interest or intrinsic satisfaction.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> External regulation is a concept in Self-Determination Theory (SDT) that describes motivation stemming from external sources. It contrasts with intrinsic motivation, which arises from internal desires and interests. External regulation can manifest as controlled motivation, where individuals engage in activities to avoid negative consequences or gain positive ones.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, external regulation often involves setting goals based on external criteria such as grades, salaries, or social status. This type of motivation is typically less satisfying and can lead to lower performance and well-being compared to intrinsic motivation.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between different levels of external regulation, from amotivation (lack of motivation) to introjected regulation (where individuals engage in behaviors to avoid negative self-evaluations).

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, relying heavily on grades and rewards can diminish students' intrinsic interest in learning.

> [!example] Application
> In the workplace, excessive external regulation through performance metrics may reduce employee satisfaction and creativity.
