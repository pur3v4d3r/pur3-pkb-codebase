# Miller's-Magical-Number-Seven

> [!definition] Miller's-Magical-Number-Seven
> - **Key-Term**: [[Miller's-Magical-Number-Seven]]
> - **Definition**: Miller's Magical Number Seven refers to the capacity of short-term memory, which is approximately seven items plus or minus two items that can be held in mind simultaneously for about 20 to 30 seconds without rehearsal.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept was introduced by George A. Miller in his 1956 paper 'The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two: Some Limits on Our Capacity for Processing Information', where he argued that the human short-term memory can only hold a limited number of items.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Miller derived this figure from experiments involving memory span tasks and suggested that this capacity is due to the way information is chunked into manageable units. For example, remembering a phone number as '415-555-1234' rather than 10 individual digits makes it easier to recall.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The concept has been influential in understanding cognitive processes such as attention and working memory.

## Practical Implications
