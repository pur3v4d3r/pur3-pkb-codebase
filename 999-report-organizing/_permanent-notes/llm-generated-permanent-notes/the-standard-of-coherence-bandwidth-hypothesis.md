# The Standard-of-Coherence Bandwidth Hypothesis

> [!definition] The Standard-of-Coherence Bandwidth Hypothesis
> The Standard-of-Coherence Bandwidth Hypothesis posits that reading skill is related to the range or 'bandwidth' of cognitive levels (from lexical to informational) a reader can simultaneously monitor for coherence.

## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] The Standard-of-Coherence Bandwidth Hypothesis
> A useful way to think about the relationship between monitoring sensitivity and reader skill is to treat the standard of coherence as having a *bandwidth* — the range of levels (lexical through informational) it can simultaneously evaluate. Less skilled readers have low bandwidth: they monitor at one level, usually the lowest. More skilled readers have high bandwidth: they monitor at multiple levels simultaneously, with the higher-level signals only suppressed when lower-level processing is so disrupted that they would be uninformative anyway. What instruction in metacognitive reading appears to do, when it works, is widen the bandwidth — train readers to maintain higher-level coherence checks even while lower-level processing demands their attention. This is why short interventions often produce small effects: the bandwidth widening is not a one-shot acquisition but a slow re-architecting of the monitoring system's default configuration. (Original analytical synthesis; consistent with Baker, Pressley, and Garner but not stated in this form in the source literature.)
