# Cognitive vs. Motivational Sources of Attributional Bias

> [!definition] Cognitive vs. Motivational Sources of Attributional Bias
> The distinction between cognitive and motivational factors influencing how individuals attribute causes to events or behaviors.

## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Cognitive vs. Motivational Sources of Attributional Bias
> **The tension:** Are systematic distortions in attribution primarily products of cognitive architecture (information processing limitations, perceptual salience effects) or of motivational self-protection (self-esteem maintenance, ego defence)?
>
> **Position A (Cognitive primacy):** Gilbert, Jones, Nisbett — biases arise from the structure of information processing; the fundamental attribution error reflects automatic dispositional categorisation with incomplete effortful correction. Evidence: cognitive load increases dispositional bias; the effect is reduced when correction resources are available.
>
> **Position B (Motivational primacy):** Zuckerman, Greenberg, Kunda — biases serve self-protective functions; the self-serving bias reflects motivated reasoning rather than information deficits. Evidence: the bias disappears or reverses in depression; it is stronger in public than private conditions; it is moderated by threat to self-esteem.
>
> **Current state:** The field has largely converged on a dual-process account — both cognitive and motivational mechanisms operate simultaneously, with their relative contributions varying by context, outcome valence, and individual differences.
>
> **This report's stance:** The dual architecture is adopted as the most parsimonious account (Section 5), with the practical implication that effective debiasing must address both channels.
