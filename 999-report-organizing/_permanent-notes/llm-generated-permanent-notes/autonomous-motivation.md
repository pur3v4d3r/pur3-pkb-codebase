# autonomous-motivation

> [!definition] autonomous-motivation
> - **Key-Term**: [[autonomous-motivation]]
> - **Definition**: Autonomous motivation refers to the internal drive and willingness of an individual to engage in activities for their inherent interest, enjoyment, or personal growth, rather than external rewards or pressures.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Autonomous motivation is a key concept within self-determination theory (SDT), which posits that individuals are motivated by three basic psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. When these needs are satisfied, people experience higher levels of intrinsic motivation, leading to more effective learning and personal development.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, fostering autonomous motivation involves creating an environment where learners feel free to explore their interests, receive supportive feedback, and form meaningful connections with others. Teachers and educators can promote this by offering choice in assignments, providing constructive criticism, and encouraging collaboration.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> There are different sub-variants of autonomous motivation, such as intrinsic motivation (doing something for the inherent satisfaction) and identified regulation (choosing to engage because one sees the value or importance). These nuances help explain why some individuals may be more intrinsically motivated than others.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering autonomous motivation can lead to higher student engagement and better academic performance. Students who are autonomously motivated tend to have a deeper understanding of the material and are more likely to apply what they learn in real-world contexts.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace settings, promoting autonomous motivation among employees can enhance job satisfaction and productivity. When workers feel that their contributions are valued and meaningful, they are more likely to take initiative and innovate.
