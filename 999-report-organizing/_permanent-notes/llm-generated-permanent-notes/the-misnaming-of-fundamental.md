# The Misnaming of "Fundamental"

> [!definition] The Misnaming of "Fundamental"
> The term 'fundamental' has been criticized for oversimplifying attributional biases, particularly in Western contexts, as it fails to account for cultural variations where such biases are less prominent.

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Misnaming of "Fundamental"
> The label "fundamental" has generated more controversy than clarity. Cross-cultural research, particularly work conducted in East Asian contexts by Choi, Nisbett, and Norenzayan, has demonstrated that the correspondence bias is substantially weaker — and in some contexts absent — among participants from collectivist cultural backgrounds, who attend more to contextual and relational factors when explaining behaviour. This cultural variation (treated more fully in Section 6) suggests that the error is less "fundamental" to human cognition per se and more characteristic of a particular cognitive ecology shaped by [[individualism-collectivism-and-attribution|individualist cultural frameworks]]. The label persists, however, because it accurately describes the default attributional pattern in the Western, educated, industrialised populations that have provided the majority of the evidence base — a sampling limitation that deserves its own critique.
