# cognitive-load

> [!definition] cognitive-load
> - **Key-Term**: [[cognitive-load]]
> - **Definition**: Cognitive load refers to the total amount of mental effort being used in the working memory. It is the sum of intrinsic, extraneous, and germane loads during learning or problem-solving activities.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Cognitive load theory (CLT) posits that there are three types of cognitive load: intrinsic, which arises from the nature of the task; extraneous, which results from how information is presented; and germane, which involves the mental effort required to form new schemas or connections in long-term memory.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, managing cognitive load is crucial for effective learning. For instance, instructional designers aim to minimize extraneous load by avoiding unnecessary complexity or distractions, while maximizing germane load by encouraging active engagement with the material.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between intrinsic and extraneous loads, as well as the role of prior knowledge in modulating cognitive load.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, reducing extraneous cognitive load can enhance learning outcomes. For example, simplifying instructional materials or providing clear instructions.

> [!example] Application
> In athletic coaching, understanding cognitive load helps in designing training programs that are neither too easy nor too difficult, ensuring optimal skill acquisition.

## Open Threads

> [!open-question] Question
> The balance between intrinsic and extraneous cognitive load remains a topic of debate, as excessive simplification can sometimes hinder deep learning.
