# harold-kelley

> [!definition] harold-kelley
> - **Key-Term**: [[harold-kelley]]
> - **Definition**: Harold Kelley is a theoretical framework in attribution theory that emphasizes the role of distinctiveness, consensus, and consistency (DCC) in explaining behavior attributions.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The DCC model posits that people attribute causes to others' behaviors based on three dimensions: distinctiveness (whether the behavior is unique to this person), consensus (whether other people behave similarly), and consistency (whether the behavior occurs consistently over time).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Kelley's framework helps explain how individuals determine whether a cause is internal or external, stable or unstable, and specific or general.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> This model has been influential in understanding attributional processes in social psychology.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, Kelley’s DCC model can be used to assess patients' attributions of their own behaviors and those of others, aiding in the diagnosis and treatment of conditions like depression.

> [!example] Application
> Educational interventions might use this framework to help students understand why they attribute success or failure to certain factors.
