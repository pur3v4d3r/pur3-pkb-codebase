# The Privilege Problem in SDL Theory

> [!definition] The Privilege Problem in SDL Theory
> The Privilege Problem in SDL Theory refers to the unacknowledged socioeconomic and structural conditions that enable self-directed learning, which often disadvantage learners from less privileged backgrounds.

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Privilege Problem in SDL Theory
> Self-directed learning, as typically described in the literature, presupposes a set of conditions that are not equally available to all learners: access to diverse learning resources, disposable time that can be devoted to self-chosen learning projects, prior educational experiences that have developed metacognitive capacity, and social environments that validate and support individual intellectual initiative. Learners who lack these conditions — because of poverty, because of educational systems that have emphasized compliance over initiative, because of social contexts that penalize independent thinking, or because of cognitive or learning differences that make self-regulation more demanding — are not failing to be self-directed; they are operating under constraints that the theory often fails to acknowledge. Any adequate account of self-directed learning must grapple with the socioeconomic and structural conditions that enable it, rather than treating those conditions as invisible background.
