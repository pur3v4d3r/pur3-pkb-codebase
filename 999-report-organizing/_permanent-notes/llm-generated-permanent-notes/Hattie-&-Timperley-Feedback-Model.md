# Hattie-&-Timperley-Feedback-Model

> [!definition] Hattie-&-Timperley-Feedback-Model
> - **Key-Term**: [[Hattie-&-Timperley-Feedback-Model]]
> - **Definition**: The Hattie-Timperley Feedback Model is a framework for understanding and improving the effectiveness of feedback in educational settings, emphasizing that feedback should be specific, actionable, and aimed at enhancing learning outcomes.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This model was developed by John Hattie and Helen Timperley to provide educators with a structured approach to delivering effective feedback. It posits that feedback is most impactful when it is tailored to the learner's needs and provides clear guidance on how to improve performance or understanding.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The model consists of several key components, including setting goals, providing feedback, and encouraging self-assessment. These elements work together to create a cycle of continuous improvement in learning processes.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the importance of making feedback specific and actionable, as well as fostering an environment where learners can take ownership of their learning through self-assessment.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In practice, this model encourages teachers to provide detailed and constructive feedback that helps students understand what they need to do differently or better.

> [!example] Application
> It also promotes the use of formative assessment techniques to monitor student progress and adjust teaching strategies accordingly.
