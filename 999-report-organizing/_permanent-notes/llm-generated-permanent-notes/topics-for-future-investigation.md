> [!open-question] Topics for Future Investigation
> > [!topic-idea] **[[Retrieval-Augmented-Generation-RAG|Retrieval-Augmented Generation and PKB Architecture]]**
> > **Description:** An investigation of how retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems can integrate with personal knowledge bases to create AI-enhanced knowledge retrieval, synthesis, and connection discovery. Explores the intersection of vector embeddings, semantic search, and cognitive strategy principles — particularly whether AI-augmented retrieval constitutes a form of externalized spreading activation.
> >
> > **Connection to this report:** This report establishes that PKB linking structure externalizes schema organization and that retrieval processes can be cognitively offloaded to external systems (Sections 4, 7). RAG systems represent the next evolution of this externalization — automated retrieval across the knowledge graph.
> >
> > **Priority:** High
> > **Suggested report type:** Comparative Architecture (evaluating RAG approaches against cognitive strategy criteria)
> > **Prerequisites:** [[Extended Mind Thesis (Clark & Chalmers, 1998)]], [[semantic-memory]], [[personal-knowledge-base]], [[Vector-Embeddings]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[Spaced-Repetition-Algorithm-Comparison|Spaced Repetition Algorithms — Evidence, Implementation, and Cognitive Grounding]]**
> > **Description:** A systematic comparison of spaced repetition algorithms (SM-2, FSRS, Leitner, Anki's algorithm) evaluated against the cognitive science evidence for optimal spacing intervals. Examines how algorithm design reflects (or fails to reflect) the spacing effect research reviewed in this report, with attention to the Cepeda et al. (2006) findings on optimal lag-retention ratios.
> >
> > **Connection to this report:** Section 3 establishes the cognitive rationale for spaced repetition but does not evaluate specific algorithmic implementations. This expansion would bridge the gap between cognitive principle and computational implementation.
> >
> > **Priority:** High
> > **Suggested report type:** Foundational Report (comprehensive algorithmic and cognitive treatment)
> > **Prerequisites:** [[Desirable Difficulties (Robert Bjork, 1994)]], [[Desirable Difficulties (Robert Bjork, 1994)]], [[Ebbinghaus-Forgetting-Curve]], [[anki]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[Cognitive-Apprenticeship-in-PKM|Cognitive Apprenticeship and the Development of PKB Expertise]]**
> > **Description:** An examination of how [[cognitive-apprenticeship|cognitive apprenticeship]] theory (Collins, Brown, & Newman, 1989) — with its emphasis on modeling, coaching, scaffolding, fading, and articulation — applies to the development of PKB expertise. Investigates the progression from novice PKB practitioner to expert knowledge architect through the lens of situated cognition and legitimate peripheral participation.
> >
> > **Connection to this report:** Sections 5 and 6 identify the expertise reversal effect and metacognitive development as critical factors in PKB architecture evolution. Cognitive apprenticeship theory provides the pedagogical framework for how this expertise development can be systematically supported.
> >
> > **Priority:** Medium
> > **Suggested report type:** Practitioner's Field Guide (problem-first, practical scaffolding for developing PKB expertise)
> > **Prerequisites:** [[metacognition]], [[self-regulated-learning]], [[Schema Automation]], [[Expertise Reversal Effect (Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler, Sweller, 2003)]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[Dual-Process-Theory-and-Knowledge-Management|Dual-Process Theory and Knowledge Management — System 1/System 2 in PKB Design]]**
> > **Description:** An analysis of how [[dual-process-theory|dual-process theory]] (Kahneman's System 1/System 2, Evans's Type 1/Type 2) applies to PKB design decisions. Explores how automated, intuitive processing (System 1) and deliberate, effortful processing (System 2) interact during note-making, knowledge retrieval, and connection discovery — and how PKB architecture can be designed to leverage both processing modes appropriately.
> >
> > **Connection to this report:** The schema automation discussion in Section 4 and the cognitive load analysis in Section 5 implicitly draw on dual-process theory — automated schemas operate via System 1, while novel schema construction requires System 2 engagement. An explicit dual-process analysis would deepen these connections.
> >
> > **Priority:** Medium
> > **Suggested report type:** Dialectical Report (thesis: System 2 dominance in PKB; antithesis: System 1 as undervalued; synthesis: integrated design)
> > **Prerequisites:** [[dual-process-theory]], [[Schema Automation]], [[Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)]], [[metacognition]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[Embodied-Cognition-and-Digital-Knowledge-Tools|Embodied Cognition and Digital Knowledge Tools — The Haptic Dimension of Knowledge Work]]**
> > **Description:** An investigation of how [[embodied-cognition|embodied cognition]] theory — the thesis that cognitive processes are shaped by bodily experience and sensorimotor interaction — applies to the design of digital knowledge management tools. Examines whether the physicality of handwriting, spatial arrangement, and tangible manipulation offers cognitive benefits that digital tools must compensate for or replicate.
> >
> > **Connection to this report:** The extended mind thesis discussed in Section 7 emphasizes the coupling between cognitive processes and external structures but does not address the embodied dimension of this coupling. Embodied cognition theory adds a critical layer to the analysis of what is gained and lost in the transition from physical to digital knowledge management.
> >
> > **Priority:** Exploratory
> > **Suggested report type:** Socratic Exploration (question-driven investigation of an underexplored intersection)
> > **Prerequisites:** [[Extended Mind Thesis (Clark & Chalmers, 1998)]], [[embodied-cognition]], [[Cognitive Offloading]], [[Handwriting-vs.-Typing]]
> *— [[pkb-pkm-cognitive-strategies-for-learning-and-pkb-architecture-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> > [!topic-idea] **[[Python-Type-System-and-Static-Analysis]] — Advanced Type Annotations for Production Python**
> > **Description:** A comprehensive examination of Python's type hint system (PEP 484, PEP 604, PEP 612), mypy and Pyright static type checkers, generic types, Protocol classes, TypeVar, and the integration between type annotations and IDE intelligence. Covers the philosophical debate between Python's dynamic typing heritage and the growing adoption of static type checking in production codebases.
> > **Connection to this report:** Section 2 introduces type hints as Pylance configuration; Section 5 identifies type hints as a factor in Copilot suggestion quality. This expansion would provide the full theoretical and practical treatment that those introductions point toward.
> > **Priority:** High
> > **Suggested report type:** Foundational Report
> > **Prerequisites:** [[python-fundamentals]], [[software-engineering-principles]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[Python-Testing-Strategies-and-TDD]] — From pytest to Test-Driven Development**
> > **Description:** A deep examination of Python testing philosophy and practice: pytest fixtures, parametrized tests, mocking, coverage analysis, property-based testing with Hypothesis, test-driven development (TDD) workflow, integration testing, and the economics of testing (when tests save more time than they cost). Includes VS Code testing integration, debugging failing tests, and AI-assisted test generation.
> > **Connection to this report:** Section 7 introduces pytest as the quality assurance layer. This expansion would develop the testing topic from an introduction into a comprehensive methodology — the natural next step for practitioners who have established the project management infrastructure described in Section 6.
> > **Priority:** High
> > **Suggested report type:** Practitioner's Field Guide
> > **Prerequisites:** [[python-fundamentals]], [[complete-project-structure]], [[software-engineering-workflows]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[AI-Assisted-Development-Workflows-Comparative-Analysis]] — Copilot, Claude Code, Cursor, and the Emerging Landscape**
> > **Description:** A comparative analysis of AI code assistants: GitHub Copilot (inline + Chat), Claude Code (terminal-based agentic coding), Cursor (AI-native editor), Amazon CodeWhisperer, and emerging alternatives. Evaluates each on: integration depth, agentic capabilities, context management, cost, privacy, and suitability for different development styles. Examines the trajectory from code completion toward autonomous agent coding.
> > **Connection to this report:** Section 5 examines Copilot in depth but positions it alongside Claude Code and other tools. This expansion would provide the systematic comparison that a single-tool treatment cannot, helping practitioners make informed choices about which AI tools to integrate into their workflows.
> > **Priority:** Critical
> > **Suggested report type:** Comparative Architecture
> > **Prerequisites:** [[Claude-Code]], [[AI-Agents]], [[agentic-prompt-engineering-workflows]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[Python-Data-Analysis-Pipeline-Design]] — From Raw Data to Actionable Insight**
> > **Description:** A practitioner-oriented guide to building data analysis pipelines in Python: data acquisition (files, APIs, databases, web scraping), data cleaning and transformation with pandas, exploratory data analysis, statistical testing, visualization best practices with matplotlib/seaborn/plotly, and reproducible analysis with Jupyter notebooks in VS Code. Includes workflow integration with Git for version-controlled analysis.
> > **Connection to this report:** Section 7 introduces pandas and data visualization as advanced workflow categories. This expansion would transform that introduction into a complete methodology, providing the depth needed for practitioners who want data analysis to become a core competency.
> > **Priority:** High
> > **Suggested report type:** Practitioner's Field Guide
> > **Prerequisites:** [[python-fundamentals]], [[Data-Visualization]], [[API-Fundamentals]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[MCP-Server-Development-with-Python]] — Building AI Tool Extensions with FastMCP**
> > **Description:** A comprehensive guide to building MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers in Python using the FastMCP framework: server architecture, tool definition, resource exposure, prompt templates, authentication, deployment, and integration with Claude Code and other MCP-compatible AI assistants. Includes practical examples of servers that expose database queries, file system operations, API integrations, and custom analysis tools.
> > **Connection to this report:** Section 7 names MCP server development as the frontier where Python, VS Code, and AI integration converge. This expansion would provide the practical guide needed to reach that frontier, building directly on the project management and Python development skills established throughout this report.
> > **Priority:** High
> > **Suggested report type:** Foundational Report
> > **Prerequisites:** [[FastMCP]], [[fastmcp-development-guide]], [[mcp-servers]], [[Custom-MCP-Server-Development]], [[python-fundamentals]]

> > [!topic-idea] **[[Autonomy-Supportive-Parenting|Autonomy-Supportive Parenting — SDT's Framework for Developmental Socialization]]**
> > **Description:** A comprehensive treatment of how SDT's principles apply to parenting, covering the specific behavioral repertoire of autonomy-supportive parenting, the developmental trajectory of internalization from early childhood through adolescence, the interaction between parenting style and cultural context, and the longitudinal evidence linking autonomy-supportive parenting to adaptive developmental outcomes.
> > **Connection to this report:** Section 4's analysis of internalization conditions and the Far Transfer section's application to parenting provide the foundation. This expansion would add developmental specificity — how autonomy support manifests differently with a 4-year-old versus a 16-year-old.
> > **Priority:** High
> > **Suggested report type:** Practitioner's Field Guide
> > **Prerequisites:** [[self-determination-theory]], [[internalization]], [[autonomy-support]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[SDT-and-Digital-Technology|SDT and Digital Technology — Need Satisfaction and Frustration in Digital Environments]]**
> > **Description:** An analysis of how digital environments (social media, games, productivity tools, learning platforms) satisfy and frustrate basic psychological needs. Covers the design principles that make apps engaging (need satisfaction mechanics), the mechanisms by which some digital experiences undermine well-being (need frustration through social comparison, algorithmic control), and design frameworks for need-supportive technology.
> > **Connection to this report:** The Far Transfer application to game design identifies the structural mapping between SDT's needs and digital design features. This expansion would provide systematic treatment of the full digital landscape.
> > **Priority:** High
> > **Suggested report type:** Comparative Architecture
> > **Prerequisites:** [[self-determination-theory]], [[Basic Psychological Needs]], [[Intrinsic Motivation]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[Need-Frustration-and-Psychopathology|Need Frustration and Psychopathology — SDT's Clinical Framework]]**
> > **Description:** An in-depth treatment of SDT's account of psychopathology, covering the specific pathogenic mechanisms through which each need's frustration generates characteristic symptom patterns, the defensive compensatory strategies that emerge from chronic frustration, and the implications for clinical intervention (particularly motivational interviewing and autonomy-supportive therapeutic relationships).
> > **Connection to this report:** Section 7's analysis of need frustration dynamics and the "motivational hydraulics" model provide the starting point. This expansion would add clinical specificity and treatment implications.
> > **Priority:** Critical
> > **Suggested report type:** Dialectical Report
> > **Prerequisites:** [[self-determination-theory]], [[Need Frustration]], [[need-thwarting]], [[SDT-Clinical-Applications]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[Cross-Cultural-Motivation-Science|Cross-Cultural Motivation Science — Universality and Diversity in Human Motivation]]**
> > **Description:** A comparative examination of how motivational constructs operate across cultures, with SDT's universality thesis as the central case study. Covers the methodological challenges of cross-cultural motivation research (measurement equivalence, reference-group effects, response style artifacts), reviews the evidence for and against universal needs, and examines alternative culturally grounded frameworks.
> > **Connection to this report:** Section 9's analysis of the universality debate and the autonomy-independence distinction provides the SDT-specific foundation. This expansion would contextualize SDT's claims within the broader cross-cultural motivation literature.
> > **Priority:** High
> > **Suggested report type:** Dialectical Report
> > **Prerequisites:** [[self-determination-theory]], [[Cultural-Universality-of-Basic-Psychological-Needs-—-A-Critical-Examination]], [[autonomy]]
>
> > [!topic-idea] **[[SDT-AGT-Integration|SDT and Achievement Goal Theory — Toward an Integrated Motivational Framework]]**
> > **Description:** A detailed analysis of the convergences, divergences, and integration possibilities between SDT and Achievement Goal Theory — the two dominant motivational frameworks in educational psychology. Covers the 2×2 integration model (autonomous/controlled × mastery/performance), empirical evidence for the integrated framework, and implications for educational practice.
> > **Connection to this report:** Section 9's discussion of the SDT-AGT relationship and the existing permanent note on their integration provide the foundation. This expansion would provide the comprehensive treatment the integration deserves.
> > **Priority:** Medium
> > **Suggested report type:** Comparative Architecture
> > **Prerequisites:** [[self-determination-theory]], [[achievement-goal-theory]], [[SDT-Meets-Achievement-Goal-Theory-Convergences,-Divergences,-and-Integration-Pos]]
