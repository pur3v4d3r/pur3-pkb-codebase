# Learned-Helplessness-and-Explanatory-Style

> [!definition] Learned-Helplessness-and-Explanatory-Style
> Learned-helplessness-and-explanatory-style refers to a psychological framework that explains how individuals develop a sense of helplessness based on their attributional style, particularly in terms of internal, stable, and global explanations for negative events.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Learned-Helplessness-and-Explanatory-Style
> **Description:** A comprehensive treatment of the learned helplessness paradigm from its animal origins (Seligman & Maier, 1967) through the attributional reformulation (Abramson, Seligman, & Teasdale, 1978) to contemporary applications in clinical psychology, education, and organisational behaviour. Would cover the three-dimensional explanatory style model (internal-stable-global), the hopelessness theory revision (Abramson, Metalsky, & Alloy, 1989), measurement instruments, and clinical interventions.
>
> **Connection to this report:** Section 7 introduces explanatory style as the trait-level extension of situational attribution, and the stability-expectancy mechanism (Section 3) provides the causal pathway through which stable attributions produce helplessness. A dedicated report would develop the clinical and developmental dimensions that receive only summary treatment here.
