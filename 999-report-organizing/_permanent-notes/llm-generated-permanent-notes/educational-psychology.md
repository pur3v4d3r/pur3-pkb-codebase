# educational-psychology

> [!definition] educational-psychology
> - **Key-Term**: [[educational-psychology]]
> - **Definition**: Educational psychology is the study of how people learn and retain information, focusing on the psychological principles that influence teaching methods and learning strategies in educational settings.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Educational psychology seeks to understand the cognitive processes involved in learning, such as memory, attention, and problem-solving. It applies these insights to improve educational practices by developing effective instructional techniques and assessing student performance.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Practitioners of educational psychology often use theories like constructivism, which posits that learners build their own understanding through experiences and reflection, or cognitive load theory, which suggests that the way information is presented can affect how well it is learned. These theories guide the design of curricula and teaching methods to enhance learning efficiency.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of motivation in learning, the impact of individual differences such as age, prior knowledge, and cultural background on educational outcomes, and the integration of technology in modern education.

## Practical Implications
