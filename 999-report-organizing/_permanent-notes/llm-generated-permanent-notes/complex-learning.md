# Complex Learning

> [!definition] Complex Learning
> Complex learning refers to the acquisition, integration, and coordinated application of knowledge, skills, and attitudes across multiple qualitatively different constituent skill domains, where the performance of the whole task requires simultaneous attention to elements that interact in ways not predictable from the properties of the individual elements alone. The hallmark of complex learning is that the whole genuinely exceeds the sum of its parts — the coordination demands themselves constitute a significant and irreducible component of what must be learned.
>
> **Boundary:** Complex learning is distinct from simple or rote learning, where skills can be meaningfully decomposed and taught independently. Not all professional training qualifies as complex learning; the defining criterion is the presence of high [[Technical Detail: The relationship between element interactivity and working-memory load|element interactivity]] across constituent skills.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The concept of complex learning provides the problem definition that the 4C/ID model was designed to solve. Without understanding why complex learning resists traditional atomistic instruction, the rationale for the model's whole-task architecture remains opaque.
>
> **See also:** [[Technical Detail: The relationship between element interactivity and working-memory load]], [[Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)]], [[transfer-of-learning]], [[inert-knowledge]]
