# cognitive-load-theory

> [!definition] cognitive-load-theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[cognitive-load-theory]]
> - **Definition**: Cognitive load theory is a framework that explains how the brain processes information and suggests strategies to optimize learning by managing the amount of mental effort required to understand new information.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Cognitive load theory posits that there are three types of cognitive loads: intrinsic, extraneous, and germane. Intrinsic load is inherent in the complexity of the task itself; extraneous load arises from poor instructional design; and germane load involves the mental effort required to form new schemas or connections.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this theory guides educators and designers to minimize extraneous cognitive load by simplifying interfaces, using clear instructions, and avoiding unnecessary distractions. It also encourages the use of worked examples and scaffolding techniques to manage intrinsic and germane loads effectively.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between focal (directly related to task) and germane processing, which can influence how learners engage with new information.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, cognitive load theory suggests that breaking down complex tasks into smaller, manageable parts can enhance learning by reducing extraneous cognitive load.

> [!example] Application
> In instructional design, it implies the importance of providing clear and concise instructions to minimize confusion and allow learners to focus on the task at hand.
