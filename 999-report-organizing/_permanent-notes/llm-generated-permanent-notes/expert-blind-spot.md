# expert-blind-spot

> [!definition] expert-blind-spot
> - **Key-Term**: [[expert-blind-spot]]
> - **Definition**: An expert-blind-spot refers to the phenomenon where experts, due to their extensive knowledge and experience, may overlook certain aspects of a problem that are obvious to novices but become invisible to them because they are not part of the expert's routine cognitive framework.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept of an expert-blind-spot arises from the idea that as individuals gain expertise in a particular domain, their knowledge and skills become so deeply ingrained that certain aspects of problems or situations can be overlooked. This is because experts often rely on heuristics and automatic processes to solve problems quickly, which can lead to tunnel vision and failure to consider alternative perspectives.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, expert-blind-spots occur when an expert's extensive experience in a field leads them to focus on certain aspects of a problem while ignoring others that are crucial but not part of their habitual thought patterns. For example, a seasoned surgeon might overlook a simple mistake because they have become so accustomed to performing complex procedures that they fail to notice basic errors.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the fact that expert-blind-spots can vary depending on the specific domain and individual expertise level. They are also influenced by the context in which an expert operates and the types of problems they typically encounter.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In medical practice, expert-blind-spots can lead to diagnostic errors where experienced doctors might overlook common symptoms because they focus on rare or complex conditions.

> [!example] Application
> In software development, experts may fail to consider user interface issues that are obvious to non-experts but critical for usability.
