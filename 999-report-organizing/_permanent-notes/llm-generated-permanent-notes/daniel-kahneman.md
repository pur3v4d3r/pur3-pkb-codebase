# daniel-kahneman

> [!definition] daniel-kahneman
> - **Key-Term**: [[daniel-kahneman]]
> - **Definition**: Daniel Kahneman is an Israeli-American psychologist and Nobel laureate known for his work on decision-making processes, heuristics, and biases in human cognition.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Kahneman co-developed the dual-process theory of thinking with Amos Tversky, which posits that there are two systems in the mind: System 1 is fast, intuitive, and emotional; System 2 is slower, more deliberative, and logical. This framework helps explain how people make decisions under uncertainty.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Kahneman's work has shown that people often rely on mental shortcuts (heuristics) to process information quickly but these can lead to systematic errors in judgment and decision-making, known as cognitive biases. For example, the availability heuristic leads individuals to overestimate the likelihood of events based on how easily examples come to mind.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between descriptive and normative approaches: while Kahneman's work is primarily descriptive (explaining how people actually think), it has significant implications for normative theories that aim to describe idealized rationality.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In AI alignment, understanding cognitive biases can help in designing algorithms that are less prone to errors and more aligned with human values.

> [!example] Application
> The dual-process theory is used in marketing to create persuasive messages by appealing to both System 1 (emotions) and System 2 (logic).
