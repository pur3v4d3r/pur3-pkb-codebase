# social-cognitive-theory-bandura

> [!definition] social-cognitive-theory-bandura
> - **Key-Term**: [[social-cognitive-theory-bandura]]
> - **Definition**: Social-cognitive theory, developed by Albert Bandura, is a theoretical framework that emphasizes the role of observation, imitation, and modeling in learning new behaviors through the influence of others or media.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This theory posits that people learn from one another, not only through direct reinforcement but also by observing the behavior of others (models) and the consequences of those actions. It integrates cognitive processes with social influences to explain human behavior.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The core mechanism involves four key processes: attention, retention, production, and motivation. Attention is crucial for noticing models; retention involves remembering observed behaviors; production refers to reproducing the modeled behaviors; and motivation includes factors like outcome expectations and self-efficacy that drive action.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the concept of self-efficacy, which refers to an individual's belief in their ability to execute actions necessary to produce specific performance attainments. This theory also emphasizes reciprocal determinism, where personal factors, behavior, and environmental influences are interdependent.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, social-cognitive theory can be applied through peer tutoring and collaborative learning strategies that leverage the influence of peers on each other's learning.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical practice, it is used to enhance patient motivation and self-efficacy in managing chronic conditions or changing unhealthy behaviors.
