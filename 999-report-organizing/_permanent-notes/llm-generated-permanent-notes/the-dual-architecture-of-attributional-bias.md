# The Dual Architecture of Attributional Bias

> [!definition] The Dual Architecture of Attributional Bias
> The Dual Architecture of Attributional Bias refers to the distinction between cognitive biases such as the fundamental attribution error and actor-observer asymmetry, which arise from information processing mechanisms, and motivational biases like self-serving bias, which stem from the need to protect self-esteem. This architecture highlights that addressing both cognitive and motivational factors is necessary for effective debiasing.

## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] The Dual Architecture of Attributional Bias
> One notices, in surveying these three biases together — the fundamental attribution error, the actor-observer asymmetry, and the self-serving bias — that they emerge from two architecturally distinct sources. The fundamental attribution error and the actor-observer asymmetry are primarily *cognitive* in origin: they arise from the structure of information processing, perceptual salience, and differential information access. The self-serving bias is primarily *motivational* in origin: it arises from the need to protect self-esteem and maintain a positive self-concept. This dual architecture — cognitive biases operating alongside motivational biases — means that attributional distortions cannot be corrected through information alone. Providing people with better information about situational causes may reduce the FAE (a cognitive correction), but it will not reduce the self-serving bias, which operates through [[motivated-reasoning]] rather than through information deficits. Effective debiasing requires addressing both the cognitive and the motivational channels — a point with direct implications for the attribution retraining interventions discussed in Section 7.
