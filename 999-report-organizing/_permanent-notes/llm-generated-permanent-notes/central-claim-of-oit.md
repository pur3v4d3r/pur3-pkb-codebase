# Central Claim of OIT

> [!definition] Central Claim of OIT
> Central Claim of OIT refers to the idea that extrinsic regulatory styles in self-determination theory are qualitatively distinct rather than forming a gradient, with external regulation, introjection, identification, and integration representing ordered developmental stages.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Central Claim of OIT
> The four extrinsic regulatory styles are not points along a single dimension of "amount of internalization" but qualitatively distinct architectures of regulation, ordered developmentally because each is the precondition for the next but distinguishable functionally by the relationship between the person and the regulation. External regulation is contingency at a distance; introjection is contingency taken inside but not endorsed; identification is endorsement of the value the regulation serves; integration is coherent organization of the endorsed value with the rest of the self. The continuum is a typology, not a gradient.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-internalization-continuum-deep-dive-2026-04-21]]*
