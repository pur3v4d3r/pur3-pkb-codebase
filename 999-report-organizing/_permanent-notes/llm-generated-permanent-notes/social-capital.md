# social-capital

> [!definition] social-capital
> - **Key-Term**: [[social-capital]]
> - **Definition**: Social capital refers to the benefits that individuals and groups gain from their social networks, including trust, norms of reciprocity, and social connections that facilitate cooperation for mutual benefit.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Social capital is a concept rooted in sociology and social psychology, emphasizing the value of social networks and relationships. It encompasses the norms, values, and social structures that enable individuals to work together effectively towards common goals.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, social capital can be leveraged through various means such as networking events, community organizations, or online platforms, which help build trust and facilitate collaboration among members. This can lead to enhanced collective action and improved outcomes in areas like education, health, and economic development.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between 'bonding' and 'bridging' social capital, where bonding refers to strong ties within a group, while bridging involves connections across different groups.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, fostering social capital can enhance student engagement and academic performance by creating supportive learning environments.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical practice, building social capital among patients and their support networks can improve therapeutic outcomes and patient adherence to treatment plans.
