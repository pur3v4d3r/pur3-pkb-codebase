# amotivation

> [!definition] amotivation
> - **Key-Term**: [[amotivation]]
> - **Definition**: Amotivation refers to the lack of motivation, characterized by a person's indifference towards engaging in an activity and their inability to initiate or sustain effort toward it.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Amotivation is often discussed within the context of Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that individuals are motivated when they feel competent, autonomous, and related. When these needs are not met, amotivation can occur as a result of external pressures or lack of interest in the task at hand.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, amotivation is observed in educational settings where students show little to no engagement with their coursework, despite having the ability to perform well. This can lead to poor academic performance and disengagement from learning activities.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include distinguishing between amotivation and other forms of low motivation such as external regulation or introjected regulation. Amotivation is often seen in conjunction with feelings of competence and relatedness being thwarted, whereas these needs may still be met in cases of external or introjected regulation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding amotivation can help educators design interventions that address the underlying issues causing disengagement.

> [!example] Application
> Amotivation can lead to a cycle of poor academic performance and further disengagement, making it crucial for teachers and counselors to identify and address this issue early.
