# Reflective Questions — Section 1

> [!definition] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> Reflective Questions — Section 1 are a series of probing inquiries designed to elicit self-reflection on cognitive processes, learning experiences, and instructional design principles within specific theoretical frameworks.

## Reflections

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> Consider a recent experience where you found yourself asking "why" about an outcome. Was the outcome unexpected, negative, or personally important — and can you identify which of these conditions triggered the causal search? How might the explanation you constructed have differed if you had been prompted to search for causes under calmer, less emotionally charged conditions?
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> 1. If working memory capacity is truly architecturally fixed, what does this imply about instructional approaches that emphasize "cognitive stretching" or "brain training" for general capacity expansion?
> 2. How does the distinction between schema construction and schema automation map onto one's own experience of skill acquisition — can one identify moments where a previously effortful process became automatic?
> 3. In what ways might the engineering metaphor (load exceeding channel capacity) illuminate instructional failures that are typically attributed to motivational or dispositional factors?
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> 1. In your own learning history, when did a structured educational experience successfully prepare you for independent learning, and what features of that curriculum made the transition possible?
> 2. How might the distinction between SDL as process and SDL as personal attribute change the way a curriculum designer approaches the development of learner autonomy?
> 3. What is lost — and what is gained — when lifelong learning shifts from being understood as a systemic condition to being operationalised as an individual competency?
> *— [[curriculum-design-for-lifelong-learning-and-self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> 1. In your own domain of expertise, where have you observed the gap between component-skill mastery and integrated whole-task performance? What coordination demands were being neglected?
> 2. How does the concept of inert knowledge challenge the assumption that assessment performance predicts authentic competence?
> 3. What structural features would an instructional model need to possess in order to systematically prevent compartmentalization?
> *— [[four-component-instructional-design-model-4cid-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> 1. Why does the recursive structure of metacognition pose a problem that purely taxonomic approaches cannot resolve? What is it about recursion that demands architectural specification?
> 2. Consider Hart's feeling-of-knowing finding: what does it mean for a cognitive system to accurately predict its own future performance on a task it is currently failing? What must be preserved in memory for such predictions to carry validity?
> 3. In what ways might the shift from "catalogue" to "architecture" in metacognition research parallel similar shifts in other areas of cognitive science?
> *— [[nelson-narens-metacognitive-control-framework-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> 1. In one's own study practice, where does the gap between perceived and actual understanding tend to be largest — and what signals, if any, currently exist to detect that gap?
> 2. How might the cascading effect of monitoring errors (poor calibration → misallocated time → poor performance → misattribution) manifest in a PKB-based learning workflow?
> 3. If cognitive load constraints make self-regulation hardest precisely when it is most needed, what design principles should govern the complexity of metacognitive scaffolds themselves?
> *— [[pkb-metacognitive-scaffolding-for-study-and-planning-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> 1. In what ways does a current PKM practice already align — whether intentionally or accidentally — with established cognitive principles, and where might gaps exist?
> 2. If PKM planning is indeed applied cognitive engineering, what would it mean to *evaluate* a PKM system the way an engineer evaluates a bridge — not by aesthetics or popularity, but by whether its architecture respects the forces acting upon it?
> *— [[pkb-pkm-planning-and-cognitive-science-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> 1. When you encounter an error while running a Python script in VS Code, how would you determine whether the problem lies in your code, your interpreter selection, or your environment configuration?
> 2. What is the practical consequence of VS Code being an extensible editor rather than a purpose-built Python IDE? How does this affect what you need to configure versus what comes pre-configured?
> 3. In what ways does the Language Server Protocol's continuous analysis of your code change the relationship between writing and debugging?
> *— [[python-development-in-vscode-with-copilot-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> 1. How does the choice between an organismic and a mechanistic metatheory constrain the kinds of interventions a practitioner would design?
> 2. If the organismic integration tendency is indeed natural, what does it mean when internalization fails — is the failure always environmental, or can the organism itself be implicated?
> 3. In what ways does SDT's metatheory differ from the organismic assumptions of Piaget's developmental theory, and do these differences matter empirically?
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 1
> 1. In your own learning practice, do you experience self-directedness as a consistent trait across domains, or do you find that your capacity for self-direction varies dramatically depending on the subject matter and your existing knowledge base?
> 2. If self-direction is genuinely context-dependent, what does this imply for the design of personal knowledge management systems — should they scaffold the learner toward increasing autonomy, or should they provide consistent structure regardless of domain expertise?
