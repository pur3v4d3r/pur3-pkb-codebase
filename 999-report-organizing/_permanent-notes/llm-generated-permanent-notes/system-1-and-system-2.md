# system-1-and-system-2

> [!definition] system-1-and-system-2
> - **Key-Term**: [[system-1-and-system-2]]
> - **Definition**: System-1 and System-2 are two models of cognitive processes proposed by psychologist Daniel Kahneman, where System-1 refers to rapid, intuitive thinking, and System-2 represents slower, more deliberate reasoning.
> - **Domain**: systems-thinking
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> System-1 operates automatically and quickly with little or no effortful conscious processing. It is responsible for our basic perceptual judgments, quick decisions, and emotional reactions. System-2, on the other hand, is characterized by effortful thinking, paying attention to mental tasks, and using reasoning.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, System-1 often makes fast judgments based on heuristics or biases, while System-2 is engaged when more complex problems require conscious thought and deliberation. The interplay between these two systems influences decision-making processes in various cognitive tasks.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the fact that System-1 can be influenced by System-2 through a process known as attribute substitution, where people use simpler judgments to answer more difficult questions.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In AI alignment and machine learning, understanding how these systems work can help in designing algorithms that mimic human decision-making processes or predict biases in data.

> [!example] Application
> In application attribution retraining, the two-stage architecture leverages insights from System-1 and System-2 to improve model performance by addressing both intuitive and deliberate aspects of reasoning.
