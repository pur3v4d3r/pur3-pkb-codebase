# Universality vs. Cultural Specificity of the Fundamental Attribution Error

> [!definition] Universality vs. Cultural Specificity of the Fundamental Attribution Error
> The debate over whether the tendency to attribute others' behaviors to their character rather than situational factors is universal or varies across cultures.

## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Universality vs. Cultural Specificity of the Fundamental Attribution Error
> **The tension:** Is the dispositional bias a fundamental feature of human social cognition or a culturally specific product of individualist meaning-making systems?
>
> **Position A (Universal tendency):** Ross, Gilbert — the bias reflects basic cognitive architecture (behaviour is perceptually salient; situational adjustment is effortful) and should appear in all populations, though perhaps at varying intensities.
>
> **Position B (Cultural product):** Nisbett, Choi, Norenzayan — the bias is primarily a product of analytic cognitive styles fostered by individualist cultural frameworks; holistic cognitive styles, common in collectivist cultures, produce more situational attribution.
>
> **Current state:** Strong evidence supports cultural variation; the bias is attenuated in East Asian samples. However, some dispositional tendency appears cross-culturally, suggesting a universal base rate modulated by cultural calibration.
