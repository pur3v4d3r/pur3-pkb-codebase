# Epistemic Transparency

> [!definition] Epistemic Transparency
> Epistemic Transparency refers to the clarity and openness in presenting the reasoning, evidence, and assumptions behind epistemic claims, facilitating understanding and verification.

## Methodology & Sources

> [!methodology-and-sources] Epistemic Transparency
> This report synthesizes work from three principal traditions: (1) the German motivation-psychology tradition associated with Heckhausen and Gollwitzer, which provided the action phase model and the original implementation-intention construct; (2) the Anglo-American social-psychology tradition that developed the meta-analytic evidence base, primarily through Sheeran's collaborations with Gollwitzer and others; and (3) the philosophical tradition of planning theory, principally Bratman's, that provides the conceptual scaffolding for understanding intentions as planning states. Adjacent influences include Tulving's encoding-specificity work in cognitive psychology, Zimmerman's cyclical model of self-regulated learning in educational psychology, and Oettingen's mental-contrasting program in motivation science.
