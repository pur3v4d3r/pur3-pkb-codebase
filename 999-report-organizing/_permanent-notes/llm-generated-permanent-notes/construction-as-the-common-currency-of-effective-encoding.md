# Construction as the Common Currency of Effective Encoding

> [!definition] Construction as the Common Currency of Effective Encoding
> Construction as the Common Currency of Effective Encoding refers to the principle that learning strategies are more effective when they require learners to actively construct new knowledge through generation, explanation, or connection-making rather than passive reception.

## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] Construction as the Common Currency of Effective Encoding
> Across elaborative interrogation, generative production, and self-explanation, a single principle recurs with striking consistency: encoding strategies succeed to the extent that they require the learner to construct something — an explanation, a connection, a reformulation, a novel example — rather than merely to receive and store. This "construction principle" suggests that PKB architectures should be evaluated not by how much information they can store or how efficiently they can organize it, but by how many construction events they demand per unit of knowledge processed. A system that requires the practitioner to generate original language, create explicit links, produce summary statements, and articulate connections to existing knowledge imposes a higher construction density than one that permits passive collection — and the evidence consistently favors the higher-construction system for learning outcomes.
