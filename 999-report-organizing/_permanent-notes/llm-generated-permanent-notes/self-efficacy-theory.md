# self-efficacy-theory

> [!definition] self-efficacy-theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[self-efficacy-theory]]
> - **Definition**: Self-efficacy-theory is a psychological concept that refers to an individual's belief in their capability to execute behaviors necessary to produce specific performance attainments. It influences how people approach challenges, persist in the face of difficulties, and recover from setbacks.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The theory was developed by Albert Bandura and posits that self-efficacy is a critical determinant of motivation and action. Individuals with high self-efficacy are more likely to engage in challenging tasks, persist when faced with obstacles, and recover quickly from failure compared to those with low self-efficacy.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Self-efficacy can be influenced by various factors such as past experiences, vicarious experiences (observing others), verbal persuasion, and physiological and emotional states. These factors interact to shape an individual's belief in their own efficacy.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> There are four sources of self-efficacy: mastery experiences, social modeling, verbal persuasion, and emotional arousal.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding a student’s self-efficacy can help educators design interventions that boost confidence and improve academic performance. For example, providing students with opportunities to succeed in challenging tasks can enhance their belief in their abilities.

> [!example] Application
> In the workplace, managers can use self-efficacy theory to develop training programs that build employees' confidence and resilience, leading to better job satisfaction and productivity.
