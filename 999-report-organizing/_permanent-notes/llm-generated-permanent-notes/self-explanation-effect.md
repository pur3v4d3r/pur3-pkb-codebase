# self-explanation-effect

> [!definition] self-explanation-effect
> - **Key-Term**: [[self-explanation-effect]]
> - **Definition**: The self-explanation effect refers to the improvement in learning and retention when learners actively explain their thoughts and reasoning processes while engaging with educational materials.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The foundational context of the self-explanation effect lies in cognitive psychology, where it is seen as a strategy that enhances deep processing and understanding. When students engage in self-explanation, they are required to articulate their thought processes, which helps them connect new information with existing knowledge.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this can be applied by having learners explain why certain steps or concepts are important, how different pieces of information relate to each other, or how a problem is solved. This process aids in the construction of mental models and enhances memory retention.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the fact that self-explanation can vary in effectiveness depending on the complexity of the material and the learner's prior knowledge. Additionally, there are sub-variants such as external versus internal self-explanation, where learners either verbalize their thoughts or think them silently.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, incorporating self-explanation tasks can lead to better learning outcomes by encouraging deeper engagement with the material.

> [!example] Application
> Teachers and instructional designers can use this effect to create more effective learning materials that prompt students to explain their reasoning.
