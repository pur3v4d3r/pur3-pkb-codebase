# bernard-weiner

> [!definition] bernard-weiner
> - **Key-Term**: [[bernard-weiner]]
> - **Definition**: Bernard Weiner is a theoretical framework in attribution theory that explains how individuals attribute the causes of success and failure, emphasizing stability, locus of control, and controllability factors.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The core mechanism of Bernard Weiner's attribution theory involves categorizing reasons for success or failure into three dimensions: stability (whether a cause is internal or external), locus of control (whether the cause is within one’s own power or not), and controllability (how much influence an individual has over the outcome).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this framework helps individuals understand their experiences by attributing them to different causes. For example, if someone attributes a failure to lack of effort (internal, unstable, uncontrollable), they might feel more motivated to try harder next time.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between stable and unstable factors, which can influence how long an individual believes a cause will persist; locus of control, which affects whether individuals believe they have power over their outcomes; and controllability, which relates to the degree to which one feels able to change the outcome.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, Weiner's theory can be used to design interventions that help students attribute their academic successes and failures in a way that promotes resilience and motivation.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, understanding how patients attribute their conditions or treatment outcomes can inform therapeutic approaches aimed at improving coping strategies.
