# albert-bandura

> [!definition] albert-bandura
> - **Key-Term**: [[albert-bandura]]
> - **Definition**: Albert Bandura is an American psychologist known for his social learning theory, which emphasizes the importance of observation and modeling in the acquisition of new behaviors and attitudes.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Bandura's social learning theory posits that people learn from one another through observation, imitation, and modeling. This includes observing others' behaviors and their consequences, as well as the internal processes involved in learning such as attention, retention, reproduction, and motivation.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means that individuals can learn new skills or behaviors by watching others perform them, even without direct reinforcement of their own actions. For example, a child might learn to tie shoelaces by observing an adult do it, rather than through trial and error themselves.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of self-efficacy, which refers to one's belief in their ability to succeed in specific situations or accomplish a task. Bandura emphasized that self-efficacy influences motivation and performance.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding social learning theory can inform teaching methods such as peer modeling and demonstration techniques.

> [!example] Application
> In therapeutic settings, it supports the use of role-playing and observational learning to help clients develop new coping strategies or behaviors.
