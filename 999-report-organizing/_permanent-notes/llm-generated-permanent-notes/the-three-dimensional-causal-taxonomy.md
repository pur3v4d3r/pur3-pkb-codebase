# the-three-dimensional-causal-taxonomy

> [!definition] the-three-dimensional-causal-taxonomy
> - **Key-Term**: [[the-three-dimensional-causal-taxonomy]]
> - **Definition**: The three-dimensional causal taxonomy is a framework that categorizes causality into three dimensions: locus (internal vs. external), stability (stable vs. unstable), and controllability (controllable vs. uncontrollable). This taxonomy helps in understanding how individuals attribute causes to events, influencing their emotions and behaviors.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The framework was developed by Bernard Weiner in the late 20th century as a way to understand attribution theory, which is central to social psychology. It provides a structured approach to categorizing different types of causal attributions.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this taxonomy helps explain how people attribute success or failure to themselves or others based on these three dimensions. For example, attributing success to internal and stable factors (like ability) versus external and unstable factors (like luck).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between controllable vs. uncontrollable events, which significantly influences emotional responses and coping strategies.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, understanding this taxonomy can help in treating conditions like depression by addressing maladaptive attributional styles.

> [!example] Application
> Educational interventions can use this framework to teach students how to attribute success and failure more constructively, fostering a growth mindset.
