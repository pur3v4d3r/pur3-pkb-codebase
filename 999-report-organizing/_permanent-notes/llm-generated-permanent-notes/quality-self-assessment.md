# Quality Self-Assessment

> [!definition] Quality Self-Assessment
> Quality Self-Assessment in education curriculum design is a comprehensive evaluation that covers depth, structure, complexity, coverage, accuracy, knowledge graph contribution, practical utility, and originality, with identified limitations and recommendations for improvement.

> **Identified Limitations:**
> 1. The report's treatment of digital technology and adaptive learning systems is deliberately limited — this is a significant gap given the current transformation of educational delivery.
> 2. Cultural dimensions of self-directed learning receive acknowledgement but not the depth they warrant — an area identified for expansion.
> 3. The evidence base is stronger for individual mechanisms than for integrated curriculum systems operating over developmental timescales.
> 4. The two original contributions, while well-motivated, are integrative syntheses that have not been empirically tested as unified frameworks.
> 5. The report draws primarily on English-language scholarship, creating potential cultural and linguistic bias.
>
> **Recommendations for Future Revision:**
> 1. Commission the Technology-Enhanced Self-Directed Learning expansion topic to address the digital dimension comprehensively.
> 2. Commission the Self-Directed Learning Across Cultures expansion topic to address the cultural boundedness limitation.
> 3. Incorporate case studies of institutional implementations that have attempted to apply scaffolding-fading at the curriculum level.
> 4. Update with recent empirical findings as the evidence base for integrated SDL curriculum design develops.
> 5. Consider a Practitioner's Field Guide companion report to translate the theoretical framework into concrete institutional implementation guidance.

> **Identified Limitations:**
> 1. The empirical base for PKB-specific metacognitive scaffolding remains thin. Most evidence is transferred from formal education contexts by analogy.
> 2. Individual differences in metacognitive capacity are acknowledged but not systematically addressed. The scaffolding recommendations may not generalise equally to all learner profiles.
> 3. The two original frameworks (Calibration-Adaptation Engine and Developmental Arc Model) are theoretical proposals requiring empirical validation.
> 4. Technology-specific implementation details (Obsidian plugins, Dataview queries, Templater scripts) are deliberately excluded but would enhance practical utility.
> 5. The report does not address motivational factors (self-efficacy, goal orientation, attributional style) in depth, though these significantly moderate scaffolding effectiveness.
>
> **Recommendations for Future Revision:**
> - Add a dedicated section on individual differences and adaptive scaffolding personalisation
> - Include platform-specific implementation examples (Obsidian templates with Templater syntax)
> - Update empirical citations with post-2020 replication evidence where available
> - Develop the Developmental Arc Model with domain-specific examples and stage transition criteria
> - Address the motivational dimension of metacognitive scaffolding (self-efficacy, intrinsic motivation, identity)
