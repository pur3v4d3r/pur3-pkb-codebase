# The Athlete's Comeback: Stability and the Reconstruction of Expectancy

> [!definition] The Athlete's Comeback: Stability and the Reconstruction of Expectancy
> The Athlete's Comeback: Stability and the Reconstruction of Expectancy refers to the psychological process where an athlete's performance expectations are influenced by whether they attribute their setbacks to stable or unstable causes, impacting their motivation and persistence in training and competition.

## Examples

> [!example] The Athlete's Comeback: Stability and the Reconstruction of Expectancy
> Consider a competitive athlete who suffers a significant injury and, upon returning, performs poorly in several consecutive competitions. If the athlete attributes the poor performance to the lingering effects of the injury (unstable cause — the body is still recovering), expectancy for future performance remains cautiously optimistic. If the athlete attributes the poor performance to a permanent loss of physical capacity due to the injury (stable cause — the body will never be what it was), expectancy shifts dramatically downward, and the athlete may begin to disengage from training. An effective coach, recognising this mechanism, will actively work to frame the poor performances as consequences of unstable factors — deconditioning, unfamiliarity with competition intensity, rust — precisely because the coach understands that the stability of the attributed cause, more than the cause itself, determines whether the athlete will persist.
