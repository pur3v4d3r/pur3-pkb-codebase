# elaborative-interrogation

> [!definition] elaborative-interrogation
> - **Key-Term**: [[elaborative-interrogation]]
> - **Definition**: Elaborative-interrogation is a learning strategy where students ask themselves questions that require them to think deeply about the material and make connections between new information and their existing knowledge.
> - **Domain**: learning-science
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Elaborative-interrogation involves posing questions that encourage learners to engage with the content on a deeper level, such as 'How does this relate to what I already know?', 'Why is this important?', or 'What are some examples of this concept in action?' This strategy helps students construct more meaningful and durable knowledge structures.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, elaborative-interrogation can be applied through various methods, including self-explanation, where learners explain the steps or reasoning behind a problem or process to themselves. It is also used in teaching contexts where instructors encourage students to ask questions that promote deeper understanding of the material.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the importance of relevance and specificity in the questions asked, as well as the role of prior knowledge in facilitating meaningful elaboration.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, using elaborative-interrogation can lead to better retention and transfer of knowledge because it encourages active engagement with the material.

> [!example] Application
> In coaching or mentoring, this strategy can help learners develop a deeper understanding of their skills and how they can be applied in different contexts.
