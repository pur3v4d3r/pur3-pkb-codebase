# The Temptation of Dimensional Purity

> [!definition] The Temptation of Dimensional Purity
> The Temptation of Dimensional Purity refers to the misconception that each cause can be precisely categorized into a single dimensional profile within a three-dimensional taxonomy, ignoring individual variations and contextual influences.

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Temptation of Dimensional Purity
> A common misunderstanding in applying the three-dimensional taxonomy is the assumption that each cause maps neatly to a single dimensional profile. In practice, the dimensional classification of causes is itself subject to individual variation and contextual influence. Ability, for instance, is typically classified as internal, stable, and uncontrollable — but a person who subscribes to a [[growth-mindset]] may perceive ability as unstable and partially controllable, dramatically altering the emotional and motivational consequences of ability-based attributions. The taxonomy describes a mechanism, not a fixed mapping. The dimensions are stable; the classification of specific causes into dimensional positions is not.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
