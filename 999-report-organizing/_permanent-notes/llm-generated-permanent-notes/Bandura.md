# Bandura

> [!definition] Bandura
> - **Key-Term**: [[Bandura]]
> - **Definition**: Albert Bandura is an American psychologist known for his social learning theory, which emphasizes the role of observation and modeling in human behavior acquisition and change.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Bandura's social learning theory posits that people can learn new behaviors by observing others (models) without direct experience. This includes not only what is seen but also how it is perceived through attention, retention, reproduction, and motivation processes.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this theory has been applied in various fields such as education, therapy, and sports to enhance learning and performance through modeling and observational learning techniques.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between direct reinforcement and vicarious (indirect) reinforcement, where individuals learn by observing others being rewarded or punished.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, Bandura's theory supports the use of peer tutoring and demonstration methods to facilitate learning.

> [!example] Application
> In therapeutic contexts, it underpins techniques like modeling in cognitive-behavioral therapy to help clients learn new coping strategies by observing successful behaviors.
