# The Temporal Architecture of Post-Outcome Affect

> [!definition] The Temporal Architecture of Post-Outcome Affect
> The Temporal Architecture of Post-Outcome Affect refers to the sequential unfolding of emotional experiences in three layers: outcome-dependent affect, locus-dependent affect, and stability-dependent affect, each modifying the subjective quality of the previous one, culminating in a composite emotional state that evaluates the self, projects the future, and positions the individual within a moral-social context.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Temporal Architecture of Post-Outcome Affect
> One notices, in tracing the emotional consequences across all three dimensions simultaneously, that the attributional system generates a temporally layered emotional experience that unfolds in a characteristic sequence. The immediate layer — outcome-dependent affect — arrives within seconds: raw happiness or raw frustration. The self-evaluative layer — locus-dependent affect — arrives next, as the causal search locates responsibility: pride swells or shame descends. The prospective layer — stability-dependent affect — arrives as the mind projects forward: hope brightens or hopelessness settles. The moral-interpersonal layer — controllability-dependent affect — arrives last and most reflectively, as the attribution acquires social meaning: guilt motivates repair, anger demands accountability, pity elicits help. Each layer modifies the subjective quality of the one before it, so that the final emotional state is not a single feeling but a *composite* — a motivationally textured experience that simultaneously evaluates the self, projects the future, and positions the person within a moral-social landscape. This temporal architecture has not, to my knowledge, been explicitly formalised in the literature, though it is strongly implied by the sequence of cognitive operations Weiner describes.
