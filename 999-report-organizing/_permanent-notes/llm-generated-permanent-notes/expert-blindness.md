# expert-blindness

> [!definition] expert-blindness
> - **Key-Term**: [[expert-blindness]]
> - **Definition**: Expert-blindness refers to the phenomenon where experts, due to their extensive knowledge and experience, may overlook critical information or fail to recognize errors in their own reasoning because they rely too heavily on their established schemas and heuristics.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> In expert-blindness, experts develop highly efficient cognitive strategies that allow them to process complex information quickly. However, these same strategies can sometimes lead to a tunnel vision where they miss subtle details or alternative explanations.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This occurs because experts often have well-established mental models and heuristics that guide their decision-making processes. These models are so effective for routine tasks that they can become automatic, leading the expert to overlook anomalies or new information that doesn't fit into these established patterns.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The core mechanism involves a balance between deep expertise and flexibility in thinking. While experts excel at applying their knowledge efficiently, this efficiency can sometimes come at the cost of overlooking novel or unexpected aspects.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In professional settings, expert-blindness can lead to significant errors if not properly managed. For example, a surgeon might miss a rare complication because they are too focused on common procedures.

> [!example] Application
> Educators and trainers need to be aware of this phenomenon to design curricula that encourage critical thinking and flexibility in problem-solving.
