# stewart-hase

> [!definition] stewart-hase
> - **Key-Term**: [[stewart-hase]]
> - **Definition**: Stewart-Hase is a theoretical framework that explores the relationship between autonomy and structure in educational settings, particularly focusing on how these elements interact to support learning and development.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The Stewart-Hase model posits that effective education involves balancing autonomy (students' freedom to make choices) with structure (the guidance provided by educators). This balance is crucial for fostering a supportive environment where students can develop both independently and collaboratively.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, the framework suggests that teachers should gradually reduce their direct control over student activities as learners become more autonomous. This process is often referred to as 'scaffolding', where initial support is provided and then progressively withdrawn as students gain confidence and competence.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the idea of 'autonomy-supportive structure'—a type of structure that enhances rather than hinders autonomy. The model also emphasizes the importance of formative assessment in this process, ensuring that feedback is constructive and aimed at promoting student growth.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors can use the Stewart-Hase framework to design lessons that progressively increase students' responsibility for their learning while providing necessary support.

> [!example] Application
> Educational policies might benefit from incorporating principles of autonomy-supportive structure to create more inclusive and effective learning environments.
