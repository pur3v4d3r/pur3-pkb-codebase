# Athletic and Musical Practice

> [!definition] Athletic and Musical Practice
> Athletic and musical practice involves structured, goal-oriented sessions aimed at skill improvement, with explicit attention to strategic mechanisms, feedback from coaches, and sustained motivation through visible progress, while maintaining autonomous commitment.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Athletic and Musical Practice
> [[deliberate-practice]] frameworks can be read as disciplined applications of the High Performance Cycle to skill acquisition: each practice session is a goal cycle, the goals are calibrated to the performer's current frontier of ability with explicit attention to the strategic mechanism, the feedback loop is engineered (often with a coach as the feedback source) to support the strategic mechanism's productive operation, and the satisfaction-commitment loop is sustained by the visible accumulation of capability across cycles. The transfer is structurally faithful and the integration with [[ericsson]]'s framework is one of the most empirically productive cross-applications of Goal Setting Theory in the literature. **Boundary:** The transfer requires careful preservation of the autonomous-commitment quality SDT specifies, because controlled commitment imposed by parents or coaches reliably produces the dark-side burnout pattern in young performers.
> *— [[goal-setting-theory-locke-latham-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
