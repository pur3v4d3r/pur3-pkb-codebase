# Cognitive-Reflection-Test-and-Rationality-Quotient

> [!definition] Cognitive-Reflection-Test-and-Rationality-Quotient
> - **Key-Term**: [[Cognitive-Reflection-Test-and-Rationality-Quotient]]
> - **Definition**: The Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT) is a set of questions designed to measure one's ability to overcome intuitive but incorrect answers in favor of more deliberate and accurate reasoning, while the Rationality Quotient (RQ) is an index that combines CRT scores with other measures of cognitive reflection to provide a broader assessment of rational thinking abilities.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT) consists of three questions that require participants to resist intuitive but incorrect answers and instead engage in deeper, more reflective reasoning. The test is designed to measure the tendency to reflect on one's initial intuitions before settling on a final answer.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> How it works or is applied in practice: Participants are presented with scenarios where they must calculate an answer that goes against their first instinct. For example, 'A bat and ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost?' The correct answer requires careful reflection rather than a quick guess.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots: The CRT is often used to study cognitive biases and heuristics, particularly attribute substitution, where people rely on simpler mental shortcuts instead of engaging in complex reasoning.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Concrete application or real-world consequence: In AI alignment research, understanding the tendency towards intuitive thinking can help design more robust algorithms that avoid common cognitive pitfalls.

> [!example] Application
> A second distinct application: Educators use CRT to identify students who might benefit from additional training in critical thinking and reflective reasoning.
