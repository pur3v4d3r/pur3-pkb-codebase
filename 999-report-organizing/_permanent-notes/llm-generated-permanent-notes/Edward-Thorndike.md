> [!definition] Edward-Thorndike

> - **Definition**: Edward Thorndike was an American psychologist known for his work on animal behavior and learning theory, particularly the law of effect which posits that behaviors followed by positive reinforcement are more likely to be repeated.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Thorndike's foundational context lies in early 20th-century psychology, where he conducted experiments with animals to understand learning processes. His key contribution was the development of the law of effect, which suggests that behaviors followed by satisfying consequences are strengthened and more likely to recur.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, Thorndike applied his findings to educational settings, advocating for methods that reinforce correct responses and discourage incorrect ones to enhance learning efficiency.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Thorndike's work laid the groundwork for modern behaviorism and influenced various fields including education and psychology.
