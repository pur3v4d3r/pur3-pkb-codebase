# representativeness-heuristic

> [!definition] representativeness-heuristic
> - **Key-Term**: [[representativeness-heuristic]]
> - **Definition**: The representativeness heuristic is a cognitive bias where people judge the probability of an event based on how similar it is to a prototype or typical example, rather than by using statistical information.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The representativeness heuristic operates when individuals assess probabilities and make judgments based on the degree to which something resembles a stereotypical instance. For example, people might overestimate the likelihood of an event because it seems more 'representative' or typical of a category than it actually is.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This heuristic often leads to errors in judgment as it can ignore base rates and other relevant statistical information. It is particularly influential when dealing with categories that have clear prototypes, such as judging whether someone is likely to be a lawyer based on their appearance and behavior rather than demographic data.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The representativeness heuristic has its roots in the work of psychologists like Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky, who explored how people make judgments under uncertainty.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In legal contexts, judges might overestimate the likelihood that a defendant is guilty based on their behavior or appearance rather than statistical evidence.

> [!example] Application
> In medical diagnosis, doctors may rely too heavily on symptoms that align with common cases of a disease, leading to misdiagnosis if less representative but more statistically likely conditions are overlooked.
