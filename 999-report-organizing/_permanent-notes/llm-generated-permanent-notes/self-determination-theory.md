# self-determination-theory

> [!definition] self-determination-theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[self-determination-theory]]
> - **Definition**: Self-Determination Theory is a psychological theory that posits humans have innate needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness, which must be satisfied to foster optimal motivation and well-being in individuals.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Self-Determination Theory (SDT) was developed by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan. It suggests that when these three basic psychological needs are met, individuals experience intrinsic motivation, leading to better performance and satisfaction. The theory emphasizes the importance of autonomy (the feeling of volition), competence (a sense of efficacy), and relatedness (feeling connected with others) in promoting healthy development and engagement.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, SDT is applied in various settings such as education, workplace management, and health interventions to enhance motivation and well-being. For instance, in educational contexts, fostering autonomy-supportive teaching practices can lead to higher student engagement and academic achievement.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> SDT has several sub-variants, including the more recent concept of psychological need satisfaction in the context of technology use (e.g., digital self-determination). It also integrates with other motivational theories like goal-setting theory but focuses more on the intrinsic aspects of motivation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, SDT can be used to design curricula and teaching methods that support students' autonomy, competence, and relatedness, thereby enhancing their intrinsic motivation and academic performance.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace settings, managers can apply SDT principles by providing employees with opportunities for self-determination, which can lead to increased job satisfaction and productivity.
