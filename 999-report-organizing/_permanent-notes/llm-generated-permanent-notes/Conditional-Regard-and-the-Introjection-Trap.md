# Conditional-Regard-and-the-Introjection-Trap

> [!definition] Conditional-Regard-and-the-Introjection-Trap
> - **Key-Term**: [[Conditional-Regard-and-the-Introjection-Trap]]
> - **Definition**: Conditional regard and the introjection trap refer to the psychological phenomenon where individuals internalize external evaluations, leading them to base their self-worth on meeting certain conditions set by others, often resulting in decreased autonomy and well-being.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The concept is rooted in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that people have innate basic psychological needs for competence, relatedness, and autonomy. When these needs are not met, individuals may seek validation from others through conditional regard, leading to the introjection trap.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means that individuals might engage in behaviors or activities solely to gain approval or avoid disapproval from significant others, such as parents, teachers, or peers. Over time, this can lead to a loss of intrinsic motivation and a reliance on external validation for self-esteem.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between conditional and unconditional regard, where the former is linked to the introjection trap, while the latter supports healthy psychological development.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, students who experience conditional regard may become less motivated to learn for its own sake and more focused on achieving good grades or avoiding failure.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace settings, employees might prioritize meeting their boss's expectations over pursuing tasks that align with their personal interests or values.
