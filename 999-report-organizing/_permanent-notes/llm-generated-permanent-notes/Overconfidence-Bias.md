# Overconfidence-Bias

> [!definition] Overconfidence-Bias
> - **Key-Term**: [[Overconfidence-Bias]]
> - **Definition**: Overconfidence-bias is the tendency to overestimate one's own abilities, knowledge, or the accuracy of predictions and judgments, often leading to overoptimistic assessments.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Overconfidence-bias arises from a combination of factors including selective attention to confirming evidence, anchoring on initial information, and underestimating uncertainty. This bias can lead individuals to overestimate their performance in tasks or the accuracy of their predictions, even when they have no special expertise.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this bias is evident in various domains such as financial markets where investors might overvalue their stock picks based on limited evidence, or in everyday decision-making where people may overrate their ability to predict outcomes accurately.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between overconfidence and expertise; while experts can be more accurate, they are also aware of their limitations. Sub-variants include overestimation bias (overestimating one's abilities) and underestimation bias (underestimating others' abilities).

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In financial markets, overconfidence-bias can lead to poor investment decisions and increased risk-taking.

> [!example] Application
> In medical diagnosis, it may result in doctors overlooking alternative diagnoses due to their confidence in a particular condition.
