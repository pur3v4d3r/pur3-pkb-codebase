## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Maarten Vansteenkiste, along with Edward Deci and Richard Ryan, has been instrumental in developing SDT. The theory posits that individuals are motivated by three innate psychological needs: competence, relatedness, and autonomy. Vansteenkiste's work focuses on the role of autonomy in fostering intrinsic motivation.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, Vansteenkiste's research emphasizes the importance of providing individuals with choices and opportunities to self-regulate their behavior, which enhances their sense of autonomy and leads to higher levels of engagement and well-being.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include distinguishing between autonomous and controlled forms of motivation. Autonomous motivation is seen as more beneficial for personal growth and performance compared to controlled motivation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, Vansteenkiste's theories suggest that fostering autonomy can lead to better academic outcomes by increasing students' intrinsic interest in learning.

> [!example] Application
> In organizational settings, his work implies that managers should create environments where employees have the freedom to make choices and self-regulate their tasks, which can improve job satisfaction and productivity.
