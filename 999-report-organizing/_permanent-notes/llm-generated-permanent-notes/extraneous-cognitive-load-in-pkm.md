# extraneous-cognitive-load-in-pkm

> [!definition] extraneous-cognitive-load-in-pkm
> - **Key-Term**: [[extraneous-cognitive-load-in-pkm]]
> - **Definition**: Extraneous cognitive load refers to the mental effort required by a person when information is presented in a way that does not align with their prior knowledge and experience, thus hindering effective learning and problem-solving.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Extraneous cognitive load arises from poorly designed instructional materials or tasks that do not match an individual's existing knowledge base. This can lead to unnecessary mental strain as the learner must focus on processing irrelevant information rather than understanding new concepts.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this concept is applied in PKM by ensuring that learning resources and tools are tailored to align with users' prior knowledge, thereby reducing cognitive load and enhancing comprehension and retention.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between intrinsic (related to the nature of the task) and germane (related to the construction of new schemas) cognitive loads. Understanding these distinctions helps in designing more effective learning environments.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In PKM, reducing extraneous cognitive load can lead to improved knowledge acquisition and retention by aligning learning materials with users' existing knowledge.

> [!example] Application
> Designing checklists and systems that minimize unnecessary complexity can enhance user engagement and effectiveness in managing personal knowledge.
