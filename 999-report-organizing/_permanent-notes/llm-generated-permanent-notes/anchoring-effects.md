# anchoring-effects

> [!definition] anchoring-effects
> - **Key-Term**: [[anchoring-effects]]
> - **Definition**: Anchoring effects refer to the tendency of individuals to rely too heavily on an initial piece of information (the 'anchor') when making subsequent judgments, even if that anchor is irrelevant or misleading.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Anchoring effects are a cognitive bias where people use an initial piece of information as a starting point for further reasoning. This can lead to systematic errors in judgment because the initial value influences subsequent estimates and decisions more than it should.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> For example, when asked to estimate the population of a city, if given an arbitrary number (the 'anchor'), respondents tend to adjust their estimates based on that number rather than making an independent assessment.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> This bias is rooted in the way our brains process information, often leading us to over-rely on the first piece of data encountered.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In decision-making processes, anchoring effects can lead to suboptimal choices. For instance, in negotiations, one party might set an initial price that influences the other party's counteroffers.

> [!example] Application
> In financial markets, analysts might anchor their forecasts on past performance or economic indicators, leading to overestimation of future growth.
