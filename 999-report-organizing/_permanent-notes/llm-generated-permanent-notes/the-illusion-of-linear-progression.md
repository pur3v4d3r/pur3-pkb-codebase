# The Illusion of Linear Progression

> [!definition] The Illusion of Linear Progression
> The Illusion of Linear Progression refers to the misconception that learners will consistently advance through a curriculum in a uniform, unidirectional manner across all domains, when in fact their progress is more accurately characterized as recursive and domain-specific.

## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Illusion of Linear Progression
> While the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression is presented as a five-phase developmental arc, one must resist the temptation to interpret it as a lockstep sequence through which all learners progress at the same rate. The actual trajectory is more accurately described as recursive and domain-specific: a learner who has achieved autonomous sovereignty in one domain of knowledge may find themselves in guided dependence when encountering a radically new domain, and the capacity to recognise this shift — to know when one needs scaffolding and when one can operate independently — is itself a metacognitive achievement that the curriculum must develop. The curriculum designer's challenge is to create structures that accommodate this recursiveness rather than assuming a unidirectional developmental pathway.
