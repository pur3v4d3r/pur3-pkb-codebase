# Debiasing Self-Audit (for High-Stakes Personal Decisions)

> [!definition] Debiasing Self-Audit (for High-Stakes Personal Decisions)
> Debiasing Self-Audit is a structured process for evaluating personal decisions that involves systematically questioning one's initial judgments to reduce cognitive biases, such as by considering alternatives, base rates, and seeking outside perspectives.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Debiasing Self-Audit (for High-Stakes Personal Decisions)
> - [ ] Have I articulated the actual question I am trying to answer?
> - [ ] Have I generated alternatives to the first answer that came to mind?
> - [ ] Have I considered the base rates relevant to this judgment?
> - [ ] Have I identified the strongest argument against my preferred answer?
> - [ ] Have I asked whether my preferred answer is being served by motivated reasoning?
> - [ ] Have I considered whether this is a high-validity or low-validity environment for my intuitions?
> - [ ] Have I sought outside perspectives — particularly from people who may disagree?
> - [ ] Have I waited long enough to engage Type 2 processing?
> - [ ] Have I documented the reasoning so that future-me can audit present-me's judgment against actual outcomes?
