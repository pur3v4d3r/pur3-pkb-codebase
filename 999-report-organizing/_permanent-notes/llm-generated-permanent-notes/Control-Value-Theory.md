# Control-Value-Theory

> [!definition] Control-Value-Theory
> - **Key-Term**: [[Control-Value-Theory]]
> - **Definition**: Control-Value-Theory is a framework that explains how individuals perceive the value and control over their actions, which influences their motivation and engagement in activities such as learning and sports.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The theory posits that people have different values for controlling their own behavior (autonomy) or being controlled by external factors (competence), and these values influence their motivation. It also includes the perception of control over outcomes, which can be internalized or identified.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this theory is used to understand why some individuals are more motivated than others in educational settings or sports. For example, a student who values autonomy might be more engaged when given choices about how to complete a task, while another student who values competence might prefer structured activities where they can see clear progress and success.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between intrinsic (internal) and extrinsic (external) motivation, as well as the role of perceived control in shaping these motivations. The theory also considers different types of value orientations such as mastery-approach and performance-avoidance.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, teachers can use this theory to design activities that align with students' values for autonomy or competence, thereby increasing their engagement and motivation.

> [!example] Application
> In sports, coaches can tailor training methods to match athletes' value orientations, potentially improving performance and satisfaction.
