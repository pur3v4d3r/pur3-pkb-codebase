# learned-helplessness

> [!definition] learned-helplessness
> - **Key-Term**: [[learned-helplessness]]
> - **Definition**: Learned helplessness is a psychological condition where an individual, after experiencing uncontrollable events, comes to believe that they have no control over their situation and thus stops trying to influence outcomes, even when action could be effective.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Learned helplessness was first described by Seligman in the early 1970s through experiments with dogs. Animals that were subjected to uncontrollable electric shocks later failed to escape from similar situations, even when they had a way out, due to their belief that nothing they did would change the outcome.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In humans, this concept is often applied in contexts such as depression and educational settings where repeated failure without control can lead individuals to believe they are incapable of success, leading them to give up on efforts to improve their situation.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include distinguishing between learned helplessness and other forms of passivity or low motivation. Sub-variants might include situational versus generalized learned helplessness.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding learned helplessness can inform strategies for fostering a growth mindset and encouraging students to persist in the face of challenges.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical psychology, recognizing learned helplessness is crucial for developing interventions aimed at changing maladaptive beliefs and promoting resilience.
