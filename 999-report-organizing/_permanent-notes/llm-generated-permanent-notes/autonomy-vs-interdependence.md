# Autonomy vs. Interdependence

> [!definition] Autonomy vs. Interdependence
> Autonomy vs. Interdependence refers to the tension between individual self-direction in learning and the social and contextual factors that influence and shape this process.

## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Autonomy vs. Interdependence
> **The tension:** SDL theory foregrounds individual autonomy as the defining characteristic of mature learning, while social learning theory and situated cognition emphasize that all learning is fundamentally social and contextually embedded.
> **Position A (Autonomy priority):** Knowles, Tough — self-direction is defined by the individual's initiative and decision-making authority.
> **Position B (Interdependence priority):** Brookfield, Candy — genuine self-direction always occurs within social, cultural, and institutional contexts that shape what the learner considers worth knowing.
> **Current evidence:** The evidence supports a both/and resolution — self-direction and social embeddedness are complementary, not contradictory. The most effective self-directed learners actively seek social resources (mentors, communities, feedback) while maintaining ownership of their learning trajectory.
> **This report's stance:** The Metacognitive Sovereignty Framework incorporates contextual navigation as a co-equal pillar alongside metacognitive sovereignty and motivational alignment, reflecting the position that self-direction operates within and through social contexts.
