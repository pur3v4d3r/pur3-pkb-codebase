# entity-theory-of-intelligence

> [!definition] entity-theory-of-intelligence
> - **Key-Term**: [[entity-theory-of-intelligence]]
> - **Definition**: The entity-theory of intelligence posits that individuals believe intelligence is a fixed trait, not influenced by effort or learning.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This theory suggests people who hold an entity view of intelligence believe abilities are innate and unchangeable. They attribute success to inherent talent rather than hard work or skill development.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In contrast, those with an incremental (or growth) mindset see intelligence as malleable - believing that effort and learning can improve one's cognitive abilities over time.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> This perspective influences how individuals approach challenges, their motivation levels, and overall academic performance.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Students with an entity view may avoid difficult tasks for fear of failure, leading to lower achievement.

> [!example] Application
> Educators who understand this theory can design interventions to promote a growth mindset among students.
