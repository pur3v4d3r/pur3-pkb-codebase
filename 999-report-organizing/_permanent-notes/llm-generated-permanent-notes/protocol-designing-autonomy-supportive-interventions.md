# Protocol: Designing Autonomy-Supportive Interventions

> [!definition] Protocol: Designing Autonomy-Supportive Interventions
> Protocol: Designing Autonomy-Supportive Interventions is a structured approach to creating interventions that align with self-determination theory by providing meaningful choice, rationales for constraints, acknowledging negative affect, scaffolding competence development, avoiding contingent regard, and preserving the internalization process.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Protocol: Designing Autonomy-Supportive Interventions
> The standard SDT-informed intervention design recipe, distilled:
> 1. Provide meaningful choice within the activity (autonomy support).
> 2. Provide rationales for non-negotiable elements (autonomy support under constraint).
> 3. Acknowledge negative affect or resistance rather than dismissing it (relatedness support).
> 4. Provide structure that scaffolds competence development without controlling the path to competence (competence support).
> 5. Avoid contingent regard — do not make warmth dependent on compliance (avoid producing introjection).
> 6. Allow the timescale needed for genuine internalization rather than forcing compressed development (preserves the dynamic architecture of Level 4).
