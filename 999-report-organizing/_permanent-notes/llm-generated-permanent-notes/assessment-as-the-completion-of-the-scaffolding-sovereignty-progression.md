# Assessment as the Completion of the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression

> [!definition] Assessment as the Completion of the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression
> Assessment as the Completion of the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression refers to the transfer of evaluative functions from external assessors to learners, enabling them to independently judge the quality of their own learning.

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Assessment as the Completion of the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression
> The Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression is not complete until the evaluative function — the capacity to judge the quality of one's own learning and to adjust accordingly — has been transferred from external assessors to the learner. A curriculum may successfully scaffold and fade its structural elements (content sequencing, strategy instruction, resource provision) while continuing to maintain external assessment as the primary evaluative mechanism, and if it does so, it has produced a learner who can navigate their own learning but who remains dependent on external validation to know whether that learning is effective. Full autonomous sovereignty requires that the learner has internalised not only the regulatory capacities for managing learning but the evaluative standards for judging it.
