# The Readiness Paradox

> [!definition] The Readiness Paradox
> The Readiness Paradox in self-directed learning refers to the challenge that learners cannot develop self-direction solely through independent practice; instead, it requires a scaffolded progression guided by educators who transfer responsibility as learners' capacities grow.

## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] The Readiness Paradox
> A fundamental paradox haunts the developmental trajectory of self-directed learning: one cannot become self-directed through self-direction alone, because the capacity for self-direction requires precisely the metacognitive skills and motivational dispositions that only develop through guided practice in increasingly autonomous learning situations. The dependent learner cannot simply be told to "be self-directed" — that instruction is as incoherent as telling someone who does not know how to swim to "just swim." The development of self-direction requires a carefully scaffolded progression through stages of increasing autonomy, guided by educators or mentors who systematically transfer regulatory responsibility to the learner as their capacity grows. This paradox explains why institutions play a crucial role in developing self-directed learning capacity even though the ultimate goal of that development is the learner's capacity to function independently of institutions.
