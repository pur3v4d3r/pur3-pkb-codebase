# expertise-reversal-effect

> [!definition] expertise-reversal-effect
> - **Key-Term**: [[expertise-reversal-effect]]
> - **Definition**: The expertise-reversal-effect refers to the phenomenon where individuals with high levels of expertise perform worse on certain tasks when given detailed instructions compared to novices who benefit from such guidance.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This effect is rooted in dual-process theories, which propose that there are two types of cognitive processes: intuitive and analytical. Experts tend to rely more on their intuition, while novices use analytical thinking more often. Detailed instructions can disrupt the automaticity of experts' intuitive responses, leading to poorer performance.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means that when teaching or training experts, it is important to consider whether detailed step-by-step guidance might hinder their natural problem-solving abilities and creativity. Conversely, for novices, providing clear instructions can enhance their learning and performance by guiding them through the task more effectively.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the type of expertise (domain-specific vs. domain-general) and the nature of the task (routine vs. non-routine). The effect is more pronounced in tasks that require intuitive problem-solving rather than analytical reasoning.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, instructors should tailor their teaching methods based on students' levels of expertise to optimize learning outcomes.

> [!example] Application
> For professional development and training programs, it is crucial to understand the level of expertise among participants to design appropriate instructional materials and strategies.
