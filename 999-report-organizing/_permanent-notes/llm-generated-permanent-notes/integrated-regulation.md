# integrated-regulation

> [!definition] integrated-regulation
> - **Key-Term**: [[integrated-regulation]]
> - **Definition**: Integrated regulation refers to the state where an individual's basic psychological needs are met, leading to a harmonious and self-motivated approach towards behavior and goals, as proposed by Self-Determination Theory (SDT).
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Integrated regulation is a key concept in SDT that describes a situation where individuals experience high levels of autonomy, competence, and relatedness. When these needs are met, people engage in activities with intrinsic motivation, feeling both competent and connected to their goals.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, integrated regulation occurs when an individual's actions are driven by internal desires rather than external pressures or rewards. This state is characterized by a sense of personal control over one’s behavior and the belief that one’s actions align with personal values and goals.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the idea that integrated regulation involves not only meeting basic needs but also experiencing them as an intrinsic part of one's identity and self-concept.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, fostering environments that support integrated regulation can lead to higher student engagement and better academic performance.

> [!example] Application
> In organizational contexts, promoting conditions for integrated regulation can enhance employee motivation and job satisfaction, leading to improved productivity and innovation.
