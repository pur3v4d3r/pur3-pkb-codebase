# wise-interventions

> [!definition] wise-interventions
> - **Key-Term**: [[wise-interventions]]
> - **Definition**: Wise interventions are psychological strategies designed to help individuals reframe their attributions of success and failure, thereby improving their emotional well-being and motivation.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Wise interventions operate on the principle that our attributions for events (whether we attribute them to internal or external factors) significantly influence our emotions and behaviors. By altering these attributions, individuals can change how they perceive themselves and their situations, leading to more adaptive responses.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> These interventions typically involve guided exercises where participants are encouraged to reflect on past experiences from multiple perspectives, fostering a balanced view that is less likely to be overly self-critical or overly complacent.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the use of different attributional retraining protocols, such as those developed by Heider (1958) and Perry et al. (2010), which are designed to help individuals adopt more adaptive attributions.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, wise interventions can be used to treat depression by helping patients reframe negative self-attributions.

> [!example] Application
> Educational settings may benefit from these interventions as they can improve students' motivation and academic performance by altering their attributional styles.
