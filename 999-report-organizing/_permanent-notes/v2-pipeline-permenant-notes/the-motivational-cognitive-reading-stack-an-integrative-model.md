# The Motivational-Cognitive Reading Stack: An Integrative Model

> [!definition] **The Motivational-Cognitive Reading Stack: An Integrative Model** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> Drawing together the cognitive, motivational, and strategic dimensions analyzed in this report, a Motivational-Cognitive Reading Stack emerges as an integrative model for understanding motivated academic reading and sequencing interventions. The stack has three layers, each building upon the one below:
> 
> **Layer 1 (Foundation):** Cognitive Pre-Conditions — reading fluency, domain vocabulary, working memory management, adequate prior knowledge structures. Without this layer, higher layers cannot function. Addressed by Tier 1 strategies.
> 
> **Layer 2 (Processing):** Comprehension-Generating Strategies — elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, active annotation, retrieval practice, spaced repetition. These strategies convert cognitive capacity (Layer 1) into genuine situation model construction — the experience of understanding that feeds competence need satisfaction. Addressed by Tier 2 strategies.
> 
> **Layer 3 (Motivation Architecture):** Regulatory Quality and Goal Orientation — autonomous regulation, mastery goals, incremental ability beliefs, intrinsic goal content framing, interest generation techniques. This layer determines how persistently and deeply Layer 2 strategies are applied across extended reading programs. Addressed by Tier 3 strategies.
> 
> The diagnostic implication of the stack model is that intervention should begin by identifying the lowest layer where the system is failing. A reader who cannot sustain engagement despite good motivation needs Layer 2 strategy support. A reader who has good comprehension strategies but loses motivation for challenging texts needs Layer 3 work. A reader who has good motivation and good strategies but cannot comprehend academic texts needs Layer 1 support. Without the diagnostic, even well-evidenced strategies will fail because they address the wrong layer.

## Spaced Repetition Seeds

> [!flashcard] **Card 1** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Q:** Untitled
> **A:** **Q:** What is the distinction between textbase and situation model in Kintsch's reading comprehension model?
**A:** The textbase is a coherent propositional representation of *what the text says* — the literal semantic content of the text's sentences. The situation model is a mental representation of *what the text describes* — the state of affairs in the world or domain the text refers to. Genuine comprehension requires situation model construction; textbase representation alone supports recognition but not transfer or integration.
**Source:** Section 2
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Tags:** #comprehension #reading-theory #situation-model

> [!flashcard] **Card 2** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Q:** Untitled
> **A:** **Q:** What is the fluency illusion and why is it motivationally significant?
**A:** The fluency illusion is the metacognitive error of mistaking the ease of text processing (reading fluency) for depth of comprehension (situation model construction). It is motivationally significant because it prevents deployment of deeper processing strategies (if the reader believes they understand, they stop working to understand), produces false assessment confidence, and when assessment performance fails, this failure is misattributed to ability rather than monitoring — reinforcing entity theories and performance-avoidance goals.
**Source:** Sections 2 & 7
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Tags:** #metacognition #fluency-illusion #comprehension-monitoring

> [!flashcard] **Card 3** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Q:** Untitled
> **A:** **Q:** What are the three basic psychological needs in SDT, and how does each apply specifically to academic reading?
**A:** (1) **Autonomy** — the need to experience reading as self-initiated and volitionally chosen. In reading: supporting autonomy means providing genuine rationales, meaningful choice within required content, and framing reading for personal understanding rather than external compliance. (2) **Competence** — the need to experience efficacy and mastery. In reading: satisfied by successful comprehension experiences, progressive difficulty calibration, and strategy training that produces genuine understanding. (3) **Relatedness** — the need for connection with others. In reading: satisfied by shared reading practices, discussion communities, and meaningful connection of reading content to valued relationships and communities.
**Source:** Section 4
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Tags:** #SDT #basic-psychological-needs #reading-motivation

> [!flashcard] **Card 4** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Q:** Untitled
> **A:** **Q:** Distinguish mastery-approach, mastery-avoidance, performance-approach, and performance-avoidance goals in the context of academic reading.
**A:** **Mastery-approach:** Reading to develop genuine understanding; predicts deep processing, persistence, positive affect. **Mastery-avoidance:** Reading to avoid failure-to-understand, driven by comprehension anxiety; predicts self-monitoring and persistence from fear. **Performance-approach:** Reading to demonstrate superior comprehension relative to others; predicts adequate assessed performance but surface strategic reading. **Performance-avoidance:** Reading to avoid appearing incompetent; predicts avoidance, surface processing, learned helplessness — most consistently negative outcome profile.
**Source:** Section 5
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Tags:** #achievement-goal-theory #goal-orientation #reading-motivation

> [!flashcard] **Card 5** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Q:** Untitled
> **A:** **Q:** What is the logic of the three-tier strategy hierarchy and what does it mean to "start at the wrong tier"?
**A:** The hierarchy sequences strategies according to prerequisite logic: Tier 1 (cognitive foundation) creates conditions Tier 2 presupposes; Tier 2 (comprehension strategies) creates conditions Tier 3 presupposes. Starting at the wrong tier means applying strategies that require conditions not yet in place. Example: teaching elaborative interrogation (Tier 2) to a reader with profound vocabulary gaps (Tier 1 deficit) will fail because the reader cannot generate meaningful elaborations when basic comprehension is blocked by unknown vocabulary. The diagnostic must precede the prescription.
**Source:** Section 6
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Tags:** #strategy-hierarchy #reading-motivation #intervention-design

> [!flashcard] **Card 6** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Q:** Untitled
> **A:** **Q:** What is self-explanation as a reading strategy and why is it more effective than summarization?
**A:** Self-explanation involves generating explanatory verbalizations or written articulations of what a passage means in the reader's own words, how it relates to prior content and knowledge, and what gaps remain. It is more effective than summarization because it requires construction of causal relationships and inferential connections that the text may not state explicitly — forcing situation model construction rather than accurate textbase restatement. Summarization can be achieved at the textbase level; genuine self-explanation requires situation model access.
**Source:** Section 6.3
**Difficulty:** Basic
**Tags:** #self-explanation #comprehension-strategy #situation-model

> [!flashcard] **Card 7** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Q:** Untitled
> **A:** **Q:** What is Zimmerman's cyclical SRL model and what are the three phases applied to academic reading?
**A:** **Forethought phase:** Before reading — goal setting (what do I want to genuinely understand?), strategic planning (which strategies are appropriate for this text?), motivational self-management (intrinsic goal activation, prior knowledge preparation). **Performance phase:** During reading — strategy deployment, comprehension monitoring, fix-up strategy selection and execution when failures are detected. **Self-reflection phase:** After reading — self-evaluation of comprehension quality, causal attribution of successes and failures, adaptive planning for the next reading session. The cycle makes self-regulated reading self-improving rather than repetitively consistent.
**Source:** Section 7.2
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Tags:** #self-regulated-learning #metacognition #reading-self-regulation

> [!flashcard] **Card 8** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Q:** Untitled
> **A:** **Q:** What is interest theory's four-phase model and what distinguishes situational from individual interest?
**A:** Four phases: (1) Triggered situational interest — externally triggered by environmental features; (2) Maintained situational interest — sustained by engaging content value; (3) Emerging individual interest — beginning of stored value and re-engagement tendency; (4) Well-developed individual interest — stable, self-sustaining predisposition to engage. **Situational interest** is triggered by specific contextual features (an engaging text, a surprising demonstration) and dissipates when those features are absent. **Individual interest** is a relatively stable personal predisposition that motivates seeking out domain-relevant content across contexts, independent of specific environmental triggers.
**Source:** Section 3.3
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Tags:** #interest-theory #situational-interest #individual-interest #reading-motivation

> [!flashcard] **Card 9** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Q:** Untitled
> **A:** **Q:** Why is retrieval practice more effective than re-reading for long-term retention, and how should it be applied during academic reading?
**A:** Retrieval practice is more effective because the act of retrieving strengthens the memory trace through effortful reconstruction (a desirable difficulty that enhances encoding), whereas re-reading produces recognition familiarity (high processing fluency) without effortful retrieval — it improves performance on immediate tests but not long-term retention. **Application during reading:** After completing a section, close or cover the text and write from memory the main points and key arguments. Check accuracy against the text. The gaps identified during retrieval attempts are precisely the content requiring focused re-reading or further elaboration.
**Source:** Sections 6.3 and 7.3
**Difficulty:** Basic
**Tags:** #retrieval-practice #spaced-repetition #desirable-difficulties #reading-strategy

> [!flashcard] **Card 10** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Q:** Untitled
> **A:** **Q:** What is the Motivational-Cognitive Reading Stack and what diagnostic implication does it carry?
**A:** The Stack integrates four systems that must each meet threshold development for motivated academic reading to be self-sustaining: **Layer 1** (Cognitive Pre-Conditions: fluency, vocabulary, working memory management, prior knowledge), **Layer 2** (Comprehension-Generating Strategies: self-explanation, elaborative interrogation, retrieval practice), **Layer 3** (Motivation Architecture: autonomous regulation, mastery goals, intrinsic goal framing, individual interest). **Diagnostic implication:** Identify the lowest layer where the system is failing. A reader who cannot sustain motivation despite good comprehension skills needs Layer 3 work. A reader who is motivated but comprehends shallowly needs Layer 2 strategy training. A reader whose strategies fail needs Layer 1 development. Starting intervention at the correct layer is essential.
**Source:** Section 6.4 original synthesis
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Tags:** #motivational-cognitive-stack #original-synthesis #reading-motivation #diagnostic

## Protocols & Methods

> [!protocol] **Protocol 1: Pre-Reading Motivational Alignment Ritual (5 minutes)** *(from [[motivated-academic-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-10]])*
> **Purpose:** Activate intrinsic goal orientation before beginning a reading session.
> **Steps:**
> 1. Pause for 60 seconds. Close all other applications.
> 2. Write or verbalize: "What am I genuinely curious about in this domain?"
> 3. Write or verbalize: "What specific questions would a deeply satisfying reading of this text answer for me?"
> 4. Set a reading goal framed in mastery terms: "By the end of this session, I want to genuinely understand [specific concept or argument]."
> 5. Locate 2–3 prior knowledge hooks: "This connects to what I already know about [X, Y, Z]."
> **Frequency:** Before every academic reading session.

> **Purpose:** Ensure situation model construction rather than textbase-level reading.
> **Steps (per section or major subsection):**
> 1. **Pre-read:** Scan headings and first sentences. Generate one genuine question.
> 2. **Read:** Text segment at natural reading pace. Annotate: ? for confusions, ! for surprises, * for key claims.
> 3. **Stop:** Close or cover the text.
> 4. **Retrieve:** Write from memory: What were the 2–3 main points? What was the key argument?
> 5. **Self-explain:** Explain in your own words *why* the argument is structured as it is and how it connects to what came before.
> 6. **Check:** Re-read to verify retrieval accuracy. Note gaps.
> 7. **Forward-connect:** One sentence connecting this section to upcoming or broader material.
