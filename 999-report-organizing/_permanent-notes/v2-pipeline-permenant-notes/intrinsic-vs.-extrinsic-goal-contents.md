# Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Goal Contents

> [!definition] **Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Goal Contents** *(from [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]])*
> Intrinsic goals orient the individual toward outcomes that directly satisfy basic psychological needs — personal growth (competence), meaningful relationships (relatedness), community contribution (relatedness and autonomy), and health (competence). Extrinsic goals orient the individual toward outcomes that serve as substitutes for or proxies of need satisfaction — wealth, fame, and image — which provide external indicators of worth rather than genuine need fulfillment.
> 
> **Boundary:** GCT does not claim that earning money or caring about appearance is inherently harmful. It claims that the *relative centrality* of extrinsic goals — when they dominate one's aspiration structure at the expense of intrinsic goals — predicts negative well-being outcomes. Moderate extrinsic aspirations alongside robust intrinsic aspirations do not carry the same predictive weight.
> 
> **See also:** [[goal-contents-theory]], [[aspiration-index]], [[Eudaimonic-Well-Being]]

> **A:** **Q:** How does SDT distinguish between autonomy and independence?

> **A:** **Q:** How does Cognitive Evaluation Theory explain the undermining effect of external rewards?
**A:** External rewards shift the perceived locus of causality from internal to external, reducing the experience of autonomy. When behavior that was previously felt as self-initiated becomes experienced as externally driven, intrinsic motivation declines.

> **Q:** SR Seed 8 — Definition
> **A:** **Q:** What is the "functional significance" of an external event in CET?
**A:** The psychological meaning of an event as determined by the relative salience of its controlling aspect (pressure toward outcomes) versus its informational aspect (competence feedback). Functional significance determines whether the event enhances or undermines intrinsic motivation.
**Source:** Section 3

> **Q:** SR Seed 9 — Process
> **A:** **Q:** What three conditions does SDT identify as necessary for effective internalization?
**A:** (1) Autonomy support — providing choice and rationale rather than coercion, (2) Competence scaffolding — ensuring the individual has the skills to perform the behavior, (3) Relational context — a warm, caring relationship with the socializing agent.

> [!flashcard] **Card 10** *(from [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]])*
> **Q:** SR Seed 10 — Connection
> **A:** **Q:** How does SDT's concept of "controlled motivation" differ from behaviorist accounts of externally motivated behavior?
**A:** Behaviorism treats externally reinforced behavior as a single category. SDT distinguishes between external regulation (behavior governed by rewards/punishment) and introjected regulation (behavior governed by internal pressures like guilt or contingent self-esteem). Both are controlled, but introjection represents partial internalization — the regulation is inside the person but not experienced as self-endorsed.

## References

- **Deci, E. L. (1971). Effects of externally mediated rewards on intrinsic motivation. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 18(1), 105–115.**: The foundational experiment demonstrating the undermining effect. Participants rewarded for puzzle-solving showed decreased free-choice engagement compared to non-rewarded participants. Relevant throughout this report as the empirical origin of CET and the starting point of the SDT research program.
- **Deci, E. L., & Ryan, R. M. (1985). *Intrinsic motivation and self-determination in human behavior*. New York: Plenum.**: The first comprehensive statement of SDT, introducing the organismic metatheory, CET, and the initial formulation of OIT. This text established the theoretical framework that all subsequent development has elaborated. Essential reading for understanding SDT's intellectual architecture.
- **Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000). Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being. *American Psychologist*, 55(1), 68–78.**: The most-cited single article in the SDT literature, providing a concise overview of the theory's architecture including all six mini-theories. Recommended as a first entry point for readers new to the theory.
- **Deci, E. L., Koestner, R., & Ryan, R. M. (1999). A meta-analytic review of experiments examining the effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation. *Psychological Bulletin*, 125(6), 627–668.**: Comprehensive meta-analysis of 128 studies on the undermining effect. Confirmed robust undermining for tangible, expected, engagement-contingent rewards; identified boundary conditions; addressed Cameron & Pierce's (1994) critique. Essential reference for evaluating CET's empirical status.
- **Vansteenkiste, M., & Ryan, R. M. (2013). On psychological growth and vulnerability: Basic psychological need satisfaction and need frustration as a unifying principle. *Journal of Psychotherapy Integration*, 23(3), 263–280.**: The paper that formalized the distinction between need satisfaction and need frustration as partially independent dimensions, reconceptualizing the "dark side" of SDT. Introduced the concept of need thwarting and its distinct consequences. Relevant to Sections 2 and 7.
- **Kasser, T., & Ryan, R. M. (1996). Further examining the American dream: Differential correlates of intrinsic and extrinsic goals. *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 22(3), 280–287.**: Key empirical demonstration of Goal Contents Theory — the relative priority of extrinsic over intrinsic goals predicts diminished well-being. Introduced the Aspiration Index as the primary measure. Relevant to Section 5.
- **Reeve, J. (2006). Teachers as facilitators: What autonomy-supportive teachers do and why their students benefit. *Elementary School Journal*, 106(3), 225–236.**: Definitive statement on autonomy-supportive teaching practices, demonstrating both the specific behaviors that constitute autonomy support and the evidence that these behaviors can be taught to teachers. Relevant to Section 8.
- **Chen, B., Vansteenkiste, M., Beyers, W., Boone, L., Deci, E. L., Van der Kaap-Deeder, J., ... & Verstuyf, J. (2015). Basic psychological need satisfaction, need frustration, and need strength across four cultures. *Motivation and Emotion*, 39(2), 216–236.**: Four-country study using the Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction and Frustration Scale to test SDT's universality thesis. Found equivalent predictive relationships across China, Belgium, US, and Peru when autonomy was measured as endorsement rather than independence. Relevant to Sections 2 and 9.
- **Gagné, M., & Deci, E. L. (2005). Self-determination theory and work motivation. *Journal of Organizational Behavior*, 26(4), 331–362.**: Application of SDT to organizational behavior, proposing that workplace motivation operates along the same internalization continuum identified by OIT. Relevant to Section 8.
- **Ryan, R. M., & Connell, J. P. (1989). Perceived locus of causality and internalization: Examining reasons for acting in two domains. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 57(5), 749–761.**: Introduced the Self-Regulation Questionnaire methodology for assessing regulatory styles along the internalization continuum. Demonstrated the predicted simplex correlation pattern in academic and prosocial behavior. Relevant to Section 4.
