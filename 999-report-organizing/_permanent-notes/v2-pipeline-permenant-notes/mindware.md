# Mindware

> [!definition] **Mindware** *(from [[dual-process-theory-kahneman-system-1-system-2-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> The rules, procedures, conceptual frameworks, and analytical tools a person has internalised and made available for Type 2 deployment when needed. Mindware determines what Type 2 processing can actually do when it is engaged — a person who has not internalised base-rate reasoning cannot apply it; a person who has not learned the expected-value calculation cannot perform it under time pressure. Mindware gaps are a distinct source of judgment failure from cognitive-miserliness failures.
> 
> **Boundary:** Not synonymous with "knowledge" in general; mindware specifically denotes procedural and conceptual tools available for active deployment, not declarative knowledge that may not be operationalisable.
> 
> **See also:** [[mindware]], [[Statistical-Literacy]], [[Numeracy]]

## Spaced Repetition Seeds

> [!flashcard] **Card 1** *(from [[dual-process-theory-kahneman-system-1-system-2-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q: What is the most fundamental architectural difference between Type 1 and Type 2 processing?
> **A:** **A:** Working-memory dependence. Type 2 engages and consumes working-memory resources; Type 1 operates autonomously without doing so. All other observable differences (speed, parallelism, depletability, phenomenological flavour) cascade from this single architectural fact.
**Source:** Sections 1–2.
**Difficulty:** Basic.
**Tags:** #dual-process-theory #core-concept

> [!flashcard] **Card 2** *(from [[dual-process-theory-kahneman-system-1-system-2-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q: Define attribute substitution and explain why it produces systematic rather than random error.
> **A:** **A:** Attribute substitution is the cognitive process in which Type 1, faced with a difficult target question, unconsciously substitutes an easier heuristic question and answers that instead — while the agent experiences having addressed the original question. Errors are systematic because the same kinds of substitutions occur reliably across people for the same kinds of target questions, producing predictable directional biases (e.g., availability → probability overestimation for vivid events).
**Source:** Section 4.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate.
**Tags:** #attribute-substitution #heuristics-and-biases

> [!flashcard] **Card 3** *(from [[dual-process-theory-kahneman-system-1-system-2-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q: What are the five conditions that reliably trigger Type 2 intervention in the default-interventionist architecture?
> **A:** **A:** (1) Perceived difficulty; (2) conflict between competing intuitions or between intuition and known rule; (3) explicit instruction to deliberate; (4) metacognitive monitoring engagement; (5) sufficient time and resources (no competing cognitive load).
**Source:** Section 3.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate.
**Tags:** #default-interventionist #type-2-engagement

> [!flashcard] **Card 4** *(from [[dual-process-theory-kahneman-system-1-system-2-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q: What is the difference between the algorithmic mind and the reflective mind in Stanovich's tripartite model?
> **A:** **A:** The algorithmic mind is cognitive capacity (broadly what IQ measures) — what Type 2 *can* do. The reflective mind is the disposition to engage Type 2 monitoring — whether Type 2 *does* engage. The two are partially independent, which explains why high-IQ individuals can show characteristic biases.
**Source:** Section 5.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate.
**Tags:** #stanovich #tripartite-model #rationality

> [!flashcard] **Card 5** *(from [[dual-process-theory-kahneman-system-1-system-2-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q: What conditions distinguish high-validity from low-validity environments for the development of trustworthy intuition?
> **A:** **A:** High-validity environments have stable causal structure (same inputs → same outputs) and reliable feedback (the agent can observe whether judgments were correct). Intuition becomes trustworthy through extensive practice in such environments. Low-validity environments lack one or both conditions, and intuition in such domains is systematically miscalibrated regardless of the agent's expertise.
**Source:** Section 4.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate.
**Tags:** #expert-intuition #kahneman-klein #high-validity

> [!flashcard] **Card 6** *(from [[dual-process-theory-kahneman-system-1-system-2-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q: State the substitution-targeting principle and give one example of an intervention that instantiates it.
> **A:** **A:** Effective interventions interrupt the substitution mechanism upstream rather than correcting bias downstream. Example: differential-diagnosis prompts in clinical workflows that require articulation of three alternative diagnoses before a working diagnosis is committed — preventing premature closure by structurally requiring Type 2 engagement at the decision point.
**Source:** Section 7.
**Difficulty:** Advanced.
**Tags:** #substitution-targeting #cognitive-forcing-functions #debiasing

> [!flashcard] **Card 7** *(from [[dual-process-theory-kahneman-system-1-system-2-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q: What does the phenomenological opacity argument claim, and what is its central implication for self-knowledge?
> **A:** **A:** The phenomenological experience of having reasoned is systematically dissociable from the actual occurrence of reasoning — Type 1 substitution and Type 2 deliberation produce outputs with the same phenomenological signature. The central implication is that introspection cannot reliably distinguish considered judgment from substituted intuition, requiring intellectual virtue to rest on external markers (showing one's work, testing predictions, peer scrutiny) rather than on phenomenological self-reports.
**Source:** Section 6.
**Difficulty:** Advanced.
**Tags:** #phenomenological-opacity #metacognition #self-knowledge

> [!flashcard] **Card 8** *(from [[dual-process-theory-kahneman-system-1-system-2-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q: How does Gigerenzer's adaptive-heuristics view differ from the heuristics-as-bias view, and how can the two be reconciled?
> **A:** **A:** Gigerenzer holds that heuristics are adaptive tools that exploit environmental structure to outperform formal optimisation under realistic constraints; Kahneman-Tversky catalogue heuristics primarily as sources of bias when applied to questions exceeding Type 1's machinery. The reconciliation is that heuristics are adaptive in their natural environments and misfire when applied outside them — the dispute is about scope rather than fundamentals.
**Source:** Section 6.
**Difficulty:** Advanced.
**Tags:** #gigerenzer #ecological-rationality #heuristics-debate

> [!flashcard] **Card 9** *(from [[dual-process-theory-kahneman-system-1-system-2-foundational-report-2026-04-20]])*
> **Q:** Q: Why does education about cognitive biases produce smaller debiasing effects than structural environmental changes?
> **A:** **A:** Education adds mindware that Type 2 could in principle deploy but does not change the conditions under which Type 2 actually engages. The substitution mechanism continues to operate upstream of any educated correction, and the agent who has learned about biases is no more likely to engage Type 2 monitoring at the moment when intuition would otherwise pass unscrutinised. Structural interventions alter the triggering conditions themselves.
**Source:** Section 7.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate.
**Tags:** #debiasing #structural-intervention #mindware
