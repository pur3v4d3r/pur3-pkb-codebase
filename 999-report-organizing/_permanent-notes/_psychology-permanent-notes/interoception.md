# Interoception

> [!definition] **Interoception**
> Interoception refers to the perception of internal bodily states such as heartbeat, respiration, and temperature, falling under [[Emotion]], and is theoretically partitioned into interoceptive accuracy (objective task performance), interoceptive sensibility (self-reported attention to bodily signals), and interoceptive awareness (the metacognitive correspondence between the two).

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It does not include external sensory perceptions or cognitive processes unrelated to body state awareness.

## Core Explanation

Interoception involves the perception of internal bodily states, which are crucial for emotional experience. It encompasses three components: accuracy, which measures objective task performance; sensibility, which refers to self-reported attention to bodily signals; and awareness, which is the metacognitive correspondence between these two aspects. These components work together to influence how we perceive and respond to our internal states.

In practice, interoception plays a significant role in emotional regulation. For instance, heightened interoceptive sensibility can lead to increased awareness of bodily signals, potentially enhancing one's ability to recognize and manage emotions. However, this heightened attention alone is not always beneficial; it can also contribute to anxiety when accompanied by low accuracy, as individuals may misinterpret their bodily sensations.

Theoretical roots of interoception trace back to theories like Damasio's somatic markers and Barrett's constructed emotion framework. Somatic markers propose that emotions are based on the body's internal states, while constructed emotion suggests that our emotional experiences are constructed from these bodily signals in conjunction with cognitive processes. These theories highlight the importance of accurate interoceptive processing for healthy emotional functioning.

Empirical research supports the link between interoception and emotional experience. Studies have shown that disorders affecting interoceptive accuracy can lead to characteristic affective disturbances, such as those observed in anxiety, depression, and eating disorders. This underscores the critical role of interoception in both normal and pathological emotional states.

Recent advancements in neuroimaging techniques have allowed researchers to map out specific neural pathways involved in interoception more precisely than ever before. For instance, the anterior insula has been identified as a key region for integrating bodily signals with cognitive processes related to emotion and decision-making. This integration is crucial because it allows individuals to use their internal sensations to guide behavior and emotional responses effectively.

## Mechanism

Interoceptive signals are processed by the brain through a complex network involving the insula, anterior cingulate cortex, and other regions. These areas integrate bodily information with cognitive processes to generate emotional experiences. The insula, in particular, plays a central role in translating interoceptive signals into subjective feelings.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> Interoceptive accuracy refers to objective task performance, while extraneous load pertains to the cognitive demands of processing interoceptive signals. In contrast, intrinsic load is inherent in the task itself and does not involve additional cognitive effort. Understanding these distinctions helps clarify how different aspects of interoception contribute uniquely to emotional experience.

> [!key-distinction] **Reflective vs Reactive Thinking**
> Reflective thinking involves deliberate consideration of bodily signals, whereas reactive thinking relies on immediate responses based on these signals. In the context of interoception, reflective thinking can enhance emotional regulation by allowing individuals to interpret and manage their internal states more effectively. Conversely, reactive thinking may lead to quicker but less controlled responses, potentially exacerbating negative emotions.

## Common Misconceptions

> [!warning] **Misconception** — People often believe that interoceptive awareness is solely about being in tune with one's body.
>
> While heightened bodily awareness is a component of interoception, it also involves the cognitive interpretation and integration of these signals. This means that simply feeling sensations does not equate to effective interoception; understanding and using those sensations for emotional regulation is equally important.
