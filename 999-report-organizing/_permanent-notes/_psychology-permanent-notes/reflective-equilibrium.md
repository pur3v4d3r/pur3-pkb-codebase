# Reflective Equilibrium

> [!definition] **Reflective Equilibrium**
> Reflective Equilibrium is a method of justification that involves adjusting particular judgements, general principles, and background theories until they form a coherent whole; it does not seek a single privileged starting point for justification but rather relies on an iterative process of revision. It falls under Philosophical Methodology.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It should not be confused with foundationalist approaches that seek a single privileged starting point for justification. Reflective Equilibrium requires an iterative process of revision rather than mere intuition-checking.

## Core Explanation

Reflective Equilibrium is fundamentally about achieving coherence among our beliefs through an iterative process of adjustment and refinement. This method acknowledges that no belief stands alone; each must be considered in light of others to ensure a coherent system of thought. The core idea is that one moves back and forth between specific judgements, overarching principles, and foundational theories, revising them as necessary until they align into a mutually supportive whole.

In practice, Reflective Equilibrium operates through a dynamic interplay where particular beliefs are tested against broader principles and vice versa. This process requires an openness to revision at all levels of belief, from specific instances to overarching theories. The goal is not merely to confirm pre-existing intuitions but to achieve a balanced equilibrium where each part supports the others in a coherent framework.

The theoretical roots of Reflective Equilibrium can be traced back to John Rawls and his work on political philosophy, though it has broader applications across epistemology. It challenges foundationalist approaches by emphasizing coherence over a single privileged starting point for justification. This method is particularly useful when dealing with complex or conflicting beliefs where no clear foundation exists.

Reflective Equilibrium's iterative nature means that the process of achieving equilibrium can be lengthy and requires patience and rigor. The method demands a willingness to revise even well-established principles in light of new evidence or better-supported alternatives, ensuring that the resulting system of beliefs is as coherent and robust as possible.

Reflective Equilibrium's iterative nature is particularly evident in its application to complex ethical dilemmas, where initial intuitions may conflict with broader moral principles. This process allows for a nuanced exploration of these conflicts, leading to more robust and defensible ethical positions.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Philosophical Reasoning**
> In philosophical reasoning, Reflective Equilibrium provides a structured approach to refining one's beliefs. By systematically revisiting specific judgements and general principles in light of each other, philosophers can ensure that their arguments are coherent and well-supported. Ignoring this method might lead to inconsistencies or unsupported claims.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Decision-Making Processes**
> Reflective Equilibrium is also valuable in decision-making processes where multiple factors must be considered. By ensuring that all relevant principles and specific judgements are aligned, decisions can be made with greater confidence and coherence. Failing to apply Reflective Equilibrium could result in decisions based on conflicting or poorly supported beliefs.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Reflective Equilibrium vs Foundationalism**
> While foundationalism seeks a single privileged starting point for justification, Reflective Equilibrium operates through an iterative process of revision and adjustment. This distinction is crucial because it reflects different epistemological commitments: foundationalism relies on unshakable foundations, whereas Reflective Equilibrium emphasizes coherence across all levels of belief.

> [!key-distinction] **Reflective Equilibrium vs Rationalized Intuitionism**
> Rationalized intuitionism can be seen as a one-way filter that adjusts principles to fit pre-existing intuitions without genuine revision. In contrast, Reflective Equilibrium requires an open-minded approach where both specific judgements and general principles are subject to revision in pursuit of coherence.

> [!key-distinction] **Reflective Equilibrium vs Intuition-Checking**

This distinction highlights the iterative and dynamic nature of Reflective Equilibrium.
