# Double Loop Learning

> [!definition] **Double Loop Learning**
> Double Loop Learning, formulated by Chris Argyris and Donald Schön (1974, 1978), involves not only revising the strategies used to achieve goals but also examining and revising the underlying assumptions and goals themselves. It falls under [[Organizational Learning]], distinguishing adaptive from merely efficient organizations: single-loop learning improves execution within an existing model but cannot detect or correct a model that has become inappropriate, while Double Loop Learning allows for the evolution of these models — making it the core competency for organizations operating in non-stationary domains.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This concept stops at the examination of governing variables (goals, assumptions, framings) for revision. It does not include single-loop learning or reflective thinking within unexamined goal frames.

## Core Explanation

Double Loop Learning is a critical concept in organizational behavior that emphasizes the continuous reevaluation and revision of both strategies and underlying assumptions. This form of learning distinguishes itself from single-loop learning, which focuses solely on improving execution within an existing framework without questioning its validity. By examining and revising governing variables such as goals, assumptions, and framings, organizations can adapt more effectively to changing environments.

In practice, Double Loop Learning operates through a process that involves reflective thinking and the identification of defensive routines — systematic behaviors that suppress this form of learning. Organizations must actively foster an environment where individuals are encouraged to question their underlying beliefs and strategies, leading to more adaptive and resilient organizational structures.

Theoretical roots of Double Loop Learning can be traced back to the works of Argyris and Schön, who emphasized the importance of reflective practice in organizational settings. Their research highlighted that for organizations to thrive in dynamic environments, they must not only improve their current practices but also continuously challenge and refine their foundational beliefs and goals.

Empirical evidence supports the notion that Double Loop Learning is essential for long-term success. For instance, studies have shown that companies that engage in this form of learning are better equipped to innovate and respond to market changes, thereby maintaining a competitive edge.

Double Loop Learning is particularly relevant in today's rapidly changing business environments, where organizations must continuously adapt to survive and thrive. This concept not only aids in the development of more resilient organizational structures but also fosters a culture of innovation by encouraging employees at all levels to question established norms and explore new possibilities.

## Mechanism

The process of Double Loop Learning involves several key steps: first, individuals must recognize the need for change by identifying discrepancies between their current practices and desired outcomes. Next, they engage in reflective thinking to question and revise underlying assumptions and goals. Finally, new strategies are developed and implemented, with continuous monitoring to ensure effectiveness.

## Practical Implications

For example, a company might revise its approach to employee training based on feedback and new insights, rather than simply refining an outdated method.

For instance, a tech company might reassess its user interface based on changing market needs and customer feedback, rather than just making superficial adjustments.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Double Loop Learning vs Single-Loop Learning**
> While both forms of learning involve improving performance, Double Loop Learning goes beyond correcting strategies within an existing model by also revising the underlying assumptions and goals. This distinction is crucial because single-loop learning can lead to short-term improvements but may not address long-term issues.

> [!key-distinction] **Reflective Thinking vs Governing Variable Revision**
> Although Double Loop Learning involves reflective thinking, it extends beyond this by explicitly revising governing variables such as goals and assumptions. Reflective thinking alone does not necessarily lead to the same level of organizational change and adaptation.

<!-- enhancement-pass:1 (2026-05-02) -->

> [!key-distinction] **Reflective Thinking vs Reactive Thinking**
> Reflective thinking is a key component of Double Loop Learning, as it involves stepping back from immediate actions to critically evaluate underlying assumptions. In contrast, reactive thinking focuses on responding quickly and directly to stimuli without deeper analysis. This distinction matters because reflective thinking enables organizations to make more informed decisions that are less likely to be based on outdated or flawed premises.

> [!warning] **Misconception** — People think Double Loop Learning is only about changing strategies, but.
>
> Double Loop Learning encompasses both the revision of strategies and a deeper examination of underlying assumptions and goals. This comprehensive approach ensures that changes are not merely superficial adjustments within an existing framework but represent fundamental shifts in organizational thinking.

## Key Figures

- **Chris Argyris** — Argyris co-originated Double Loop Learning with Donald Schön, emphasizing the importance of reflective practice in organizational settings. His work highlighted the need for continuous learning to adapt to changing environments.
- **Donald Schön** — Schön collaborated with Chris Argyris on developing Double Loop Learning, focusing on how professionals can improve their practice through reflection and action. Their joint research underscored the role of reflective thinking in fostering adaptive organizational behavior.

## Synthesis

Double Loop Learning is a critical concept for understanding adaptive organizational behavior because it emphasizes continuous improvement through the revision of underlying assumptions and goals. This form of learning enables organizations to remain flexible and responsive in dynamic environments, distinguishing them from those that rely solely on single-loop learning. By fostering Double Loop Learning, organizations can enhance their resilience, innovation, and overall effectiveness.

The concept is deeply intertwined with broader themes in organizational learning and reflective practice. Its importance extends beyond individual organizations to the broader field of management science, where it informs strategies for continuous improvement and adaptation.
