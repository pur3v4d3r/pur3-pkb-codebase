# Structure Mapping Theory

> [!definition] **Structure Mapping Theory**
> Structure Mapping Theory is an account of analogical reasoning developed by Dedre Gentner, which posits that analogy involves aligning relational structures between a base domain and a target domain, with object attributes secondary to these relations. It falls under [[cognitive-architecture]], focusing on the alignment of relational structure rather than surface features.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> This theory focuses on the alignment of relational structure rather than surface features, excluding discussions about retrieval processes or other aspects of analogical reasoning not related to structural mapping.

## Core Explanation

At its core, Structure Mapping Theory proposes that analogical reasoning is fundamentally about aligning the underlying relational structures between a base domain and a target domain. This means that when we reason by analogy, we are not just comparing objects but their relationships to each other. For example, if we use the structure of a car engine to understand how a computer processor works, it's not the physical components themselves that matter most, but rather the way these components interact and function together.

The theory emphasizes that relational structures are primary in determining the quality of an analogy. This is because higher-order relations often capture more meaningful and transferable knowledge than surface features alone. Gentner’s work has shown that expert analogical reasoning relies on this deeper structural alignment, which explains why experts can make better use of analogies compared to novices who might focus too much on superficial similarities.

The systematicity principle further reinforces the importance of relational structure by favoring mappings that preserve higher-order relations. This means that good analogies are recognized and remembered for their relational rather than surface similarity, which is why expert analogical transfer is reliably superior to novice transfer. This principle has led Structure Mapping Theory to displace earlier feature-overlap accounts of similarity in cognitive science.

Empirical evidence supports the theory’s emphasis on relational structure. For instance, studies have shown that when people are asked to solve problems using analogies, they tend to focus more on structural similarities rather than surface features. This aligns with Gentner's predictions and underscores the importance of relational mapping in effective analogical reasoning.

Structure Mapping Theory also addresses how analogical reasoning can be enhanced through practice and experience. As individuals engage more frequently with analogies, they develop a richer set of relational structures in their long-term memory, making it easier to map new concepts onto existing knowledge frameworks. This process is akin to building a robust network of interconnected nodes where each node represents a relational structure from past experiences or learning.

Moreover, the theory suggests that the effectiveness of analogical reasoning can be influenced by the complexity and specificity of the relational structures involved. More complex structures may require more cognitive resources to map accurately, potentially leading to errors if the learner's working memory is overloaded. Conversely, highly specific mappings can facilitate deeper understanding but might limit transferability to other contexts.

## Mechanism

The process by which relational structures are mapped from a base domain to a target domain involves several stages. First, the relevant relational structure is identified in both domains. Then, these structures are aligned based on their similarities and differences. Finally, the aligned structures are used to transfer knowledge or solve problems in the target domain.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, Structure Mapping Theory can be applied by creating analogies that focus on relational structure rather than surface features. For example, when teaching complex concepts like electrical circuits, instructors can use the analogy of water flow in pipes to explain how electricity works. This approach helps students understand and remember the underlying principles more effectively.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Cognitive science research**
> In cognitive science research, Structure Mapping Theory provides a framework for understanding how people reason by analogy. Researchers can use this theory to design experiments that test the alignment of relational structures between different domains. This helps in identifying which aspects of analogical reasoning are most effective and why.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Educational psychology**
> In educational psychology, Structure Mapping Theory offers insights into how students learn from analogies. By focusing on relational structure, educators can design learning materials that enhance transfer of knowledge across different domains. This approach is particularly useful in fields like mathematics and science where abstract concepts need to be explained through concrete examples.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extraneous Load**
> Structure Mapping Theory focuses on intrinsic load, which refers to the inherent difficulty of a task based on its relational structure. In contrast, extraneous load is related to how information is presented and can be managed through instructional design. While Structure Mapping Theory emphasizes structural alignment, it does not address extraneous load directly.

> [!key-distinction] **Reflective vs Reactive Thinking**
> Reflective thinking involves deliberate consideration and analysis of relational structures before applying analogies, whereas reactive thinking relies on immediate, intuitive mappings. Structure Mapping Theory emphasizes reflective thinking as it requires conscious alignment of complex relational structures between domains.

> [!key-distinction] **Surface vs Deep Processing**
> While surface processing focuses on superficial features that may not align with the underlying relational structure, deep processing involves understanding and mapping these deeper structural relationships. Structure Mapping Theory underscores the importance of deep processing for effective analogical reasoning by prioritizing relational structures over surface similarities.

## Common Misconceptions

> [!warning] **Misconception** — People often believe that any two domains with similar objects can be used in an analogy.
>
> This misconception arises from the common practice of focusing on object similarity rather than relational structure. Structure Mapping Theory emphasizes that successful analogies depend more on aligning underlying relational structures, not just surface features or object similarities.

## Key Figures

- **Dedre Gentner** — Dedre Gentner is the originator of Structure Mapping Theory and has been instrumental in developing and popularizing this account of analogical reasoning. Her work has significantly advanced our understanding of how people reason by analogy.

## Open Questions

> How does Structure Mapping Theory account for the failure to retrieve structurally relevant bases?

> *What would resolve it:* Exploring the boundaries of analogical reasoning and its relationship to other forms of reasoning would help determine if Structure Mapping Theory can be applied more broadly. Comparative studies could shed light on this question.

## Synthesis

Structure Mapping Theory is a pivotal concept in cognitive science, offering a robust framework for understanding how people reason by analogy. By focusing on relational structure rather than surface features, it provides a clear explanation of why expert analogical reasoning is more effective than novice transfer. This theory has significant implications across various domains, including instructional design, educational psychology, and cognitive science research. Its emphasis on structural alignment highlights the importance of deep understanding over superficial similarities in learning and problem-solving.

The theory’s focus on relational structure also connects it to other concepts like case-based reasoning and relational thinking. While Structure Mapping Theory excels at explaining analogical reasoning, its application is limited by the need for effective memory retrieval models. Future research should aim to integrate these aspects to provide a more comprehensive understanding of analogical reasoning in real-world contexts.
