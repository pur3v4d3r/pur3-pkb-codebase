# Short-Term Memory

> [!definition] **Short-Term Memory**
> Short-Term Memory (STM) is a limited-capacity, time-limited store that briefly retains a small number of items in an immediately accessible state, classically theorised as the second stage of the multi-store model between sensory registration and consolidation into long-term memory; without active rehearsal its contents decay within roughly 15-30 seconds. It falls under cognitive psychology. It falls under [[memory-systems]].

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It should not be confused with working memory, which includes active manipulation of information in addition to storage.

## Core Explanation

Short-Term Memory (STM) is a critical component in the cognitive architecture, serving as a temporary holding space for information that needs to be processed or acted upon immediately. This memory system has a limited capacity and duration, typically lasting only about 15-30 seconds without active rehearsal. STM's primary function is to hold onto information long enough for it to be used in immediate tasks such as reading a sentence aloud or performing simple calculations.

Theoretical roots of STM can be traced back to the multi-store model proposed by Atkinson and Shiffrin, which posits that information flows through three distinct stages: sensory registration, short-term memory, and long-term memory. Within this framework, STM is seen as a passive holding buffer characterized by its limited capacity (often described as having a 'magical number' of seven items plus or minus two) and its tendency to decay over time if not rehearsed.

Empirical evidence supporting the existence and limitations of STM comes from various studies. For instance, George Miller's seminal work in 1956 highlighted the capacity limit of STM with his famous 'magical number seven,' which has since been refined but remains a cornerstone concept. More recent research has explored how factors such as attention and task complexity can influence STM performance, further refining our understanding of its role in cognitive processing.

STM's operational characteristics are also influenced by the broader context of working memory (WM). While STM is often described as a passive storage system, WM includes active manipulation of information. This distinction is crucial because conflating the two can obscure important theoretical differences and their implications for complex cognition.

Short-Term Memory (STM) plays a pivotal role in cognitive tasks that require immediate processing and decision-making, such as reading comprehension or mental arithmetic. Its transient nature ensures that the brain can focus on current information without being overwhelmed by past data, facilitating efficient task execution. However, this also means that STM is highly susceptible to interference from new incoming stimuli, which can disrupt ongoing processes if not managed properly.

## Mechanism

Information in STM is encoded through sensory input and maintained via rehearsal or attention. The process begins with sensory registration where incoming stimuli are briefly held, followed by a period of active maintenance if the information is deemed relevant. If not rehearsed, this information decays rapidly, often within 15-30 seconds.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, understanding STM's limited capacity and short duration can inform the creation of effective learning materials. For instance, breaking down complex information into smaller chunks that fit within STM’s capacity can enhance comprehension and retention. Additionally, incorporating active rehearsal techniques such as summarization or self-explanation can extend the duration of information in STM, making it more accessible for subsequent use.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Cognitive load theory**
> In cognitive load theory, managing the intrinsic and extraneous loads on STM is crucial. Excessive extraneous load (e.g., overly complex instructions) can overwhelm STM, leading to decreased performance. By minimizing these distractions and focusing on essential information, educators can optimize learning outcomes.

> [!example] **Application 3 — Spaced retrieval in MOOCs**
> In Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs), spaced retrieval techniques leverage the principles of STM by spacing out review sessions to enhance retention. By revisiting material at increasing intervals, learners can move information from STM into long-term memory more effectively, reducing reliance on cramming and promoting durable learning.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **STM vs Working Memory**
> While Short-Term Memory (STM) is often described as a passive holding buffer with limited capacity and duration, working memory (WM) includes active manipulation of information. STM decays rapidly without rehearsal, whereas WM allows for the temporary storage and manipulation of information to support complex cognitive tasks.

<!-- enhancement-pass:1 (2026-05-02) -->

> [!key-distinction] **Maintenance vs Elaborative Rehearsal**
> Maintenance rehearsal involves simple repetition of information to keep it in STM, whereas elaborative rehearsal involves linking new information with existing knowledge for deeper processing. While maintenance rehearsal can extend the duration of information in STM, elaborative rehearsal enhances understanding and facilitates transfer into long-term memory.

## Common Misconceptions

<!-- enhancement-pass:1 (2026-05-02) -->

> [!warning] **Misconception** — People often believe that all information in STM is lost immediately after it decays.
>
> In reality, some of the information may be transferred to long-term memory through processes like elaborative rehearsal. The misconception arises from oversimplifying the complex interplay between STM and LTM.

## Key Figures

- **George Miller** — In his influential work 'The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two' (1956), George Miller proposed the capacity limit of STM, which has become a foundational concept in cognitive psychology.
- **Alan Baddeley** — Alan Baddeley's revision of the multi-store model introduced the working memory framework, distinguishing between passive storage (STM) and active manipulation (WM), thereby clarifying the functional differences between these memory systems.

<!-- enhancement-pass:1 (2026-05-02) -->
- **Elizabeth Loftus** — Loftus's work on memory distortion provides insights into how STM interacts with perception and attention, influencing what information is encoded and retained. Her research underscores the malleability of memory processes.

## Synthesis

Understanding Short-Term Memory (STM) is crucial for grasping broader cognitive processes, particularly in the context of learning and education. By recognizing its limited capacity and time-limited nature, educators can design more effective instructional strategies that enhance memory retention and comprehension. Furthermore, STM's distinction from working memory highlights the importance of active manipulation in complex cognition, underscoring the need to integrate both passive storage and active processing in cognitive models.

STM also plays a significant role in the broader field of cognitive psychology by serving as a bridge between sensory input and long-term memory consolidation. Its study contributes to our understanding of how information is processed and retained over time, with implications for various domains including education, clinical psychology, and human-computer interaction.
