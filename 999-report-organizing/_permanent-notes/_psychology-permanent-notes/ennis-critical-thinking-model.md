# Ennis Critical Thinking Model

> [!definition] **Ennis Critical Thinking Model**
> The Ennis Critical Thinking Model by Robert Ennis defines critical thinking as a process of reasonable and reflective decision-making aimed at regulating beliefs and actions through specific abilities and dispositions. Unlike models that focus solely on argument evaluation, this model emphasizes practical application in personal belief and action regulation, thereby broadening the scope of critical thinking beyond mere academic critique. It falls under the broader concept of Critical Thinking.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It is distinct from models that focus solely on argument evaluation; it emphasizes practical application in personal belief and action regulation rather than just academic critique.

## Core Explanation

Robert Ennis's model of critical thinking is grounded in a framework that integrates both cognitive abilities and dispositional attitudes to foster effective decision-making. At its core, this model emphasizes the practical application of critical thinking skills in everyday life rather than limiting it to academic or theoretical contexts. The model posits that critical thinking involves not just evaluating arguments but also making reasoned decisions about personal beliefs and actions.

The operationalization of these abilities includes processes such as clarification, basis assessment, inference, supposition, and integration. These cognitive tools are designed to help individuals navigate complex information environments by systematically analyzing data, considering multiple perspectives, and drawing well-supported conclusions. This approach ensures that critical thinking is not merely an abstract concept but a tangible set of skills that can be taught and practiced.

Ennis's model also places significant emphasis on dispositions such as truth-seeking, open-mindedness, and respect for others' dignity. These dispositional elements are crucial because they influence how individuals apply their cognitive abilities in real-world situations. For instance, someone who is dispositionally committed to seeking the truth will be more likely to engage deeply with information and less likely to accept conclusions without scrutiny.

The theoretical roots of Ennis's model can be traced back to philosophical traditions that emphasize rationality and ethical responsibility. By integrating these elements into a practical framework for critical thinking, Ennis provides educators and practitioners with a comprehensive approach to fostering cognitive development in learners.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, the Ennis Critical Thinking Model can guide the creation of learning activities that promote both cognitive skills and dispositional attitudes. For example, a teacher might design an activity where students must evaluate multiple sources on a controversial topic, requiring them to clarify their understanding, assess the credibility of each source, infer conclusions based on evidence, and integrate these insights into a coherent argument. This process not only enhances critical thinking abilities but also encourages dispositions such as open-mindedness and respect for diverse viewpoints.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Personal decision-making**
> In personal decision-making contexts, the model can help individuals make more reasoned choices by applying its cognitive skills and dispositional attitudes. For instance, when faced with a significant life decision, such as choosing a career path or making an important purchase, one might use critical thinking to clarify their goals, assess relevant information, infer potential outcomes, and integrate these insights into a well-reasoned decision. Additionally, the model's emphasis on dispositions like truth-seeking can encourage individuals to seek out accurate information rather than relying solely on personal biases.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Action-regulation vs Argument-evaluation focus**
> The Ennis Critical Thinking Model distinguishes itself from other models, such as the Facione model, by emphasizing action regulation over argument evaluation. While both models include similar cognitive skills, Ennis's framework places a greater emphasis on how these skills are applied in practical decision-making contexts rather than just evaluating arguments. This distinction is crucial because it broadens the scope of critical thinking to encompass personal belief and action regulation, making it more relevant for everyday life.

> [!key-distinction] **Reflective vs Reactive Thinking**
> Ennis's model supports reflective thinking over reactive thinking by encouraging individuals to pause and consider multiple perspectives before making decisions. This distinction is crucial because it promotes a more thoughtful approach to problem-solving, which can lead to better outcomes in both personal and professional settings.

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Motivation**
> The model also highlights the importance of intrinsic motivation over extrinsic motivation for fostering critical thinking. While external rewards may prompt initial engagement, internal drive is more likely to sustain long-term commitment to developing these skills.
