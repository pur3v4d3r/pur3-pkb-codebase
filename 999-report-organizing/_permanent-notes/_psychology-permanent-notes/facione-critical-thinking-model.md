# Facione Critical Thinking Model

> [!definition] **Facione Critical Thinking Model**
> The Facione Critical Thinking Model is a consensus-driven framework that characterizes critical thinking through six core cognitive skills—interpretation, analysis, evaluation, inference, explanation, and self-regulation—paired with dispositional components. It falls under the broader category of Critical Thinking Frameworks but should not be mistaken as the definitive account; it represents one expert panel's synthesis among various frameworks.

> [!attention] **Boundary**
> It should not be confused as the definitive account of critical thinking; it is one consensus product among several frameworks that emphasize different decompositions and approaches to critical thinking.

## Core Explanation

The Facione Critical Thinking Model was developed by Peter Facione and his team for the American Philosophical Association’s Delphi project. This model identifies six core cognitive skills that are essential to critical thinking: interpretation, analysis, evaluation, inference, explanation, and self-regulation. Each skill is crucial in its own right but becomes more powerful when combined with dispositional components such as open-mindedness, truth-seeking, and confidence.

Interpretation involves understanding the meaning of information presented through various media or contexts. Analysis breaks down complex ideas into simpler parts to understand their structure and relationships. Evaluation assesses the credibility and relevance of evidence and arguments. Inference draws logical conclusions from available data. Explanation articulates thoughts clearly and coherently, while self-regulation monitors one's own thinking processes for accuracy and effectiveness.

The dispositional components are equally important as they provide the motivational underpinning that drives individuals to engage in critical thinking consistently. For instance, open-mindedness encourages consideration of alternative viewpoints, fostering a more nuanced understanding of issues. Truth-seeking motivates individuals to pursue accurate information over convenient or popular beliefs. Confidence enables one to trust their reasoning abilities and act on conclusions drawn from evidence.

The dual structure of cognitive skills and dispositions is designed to address the observation that mere possession of critical thinking skills does not guarantee effective application in real-world scenarios without the corresponding dispositional support. This model thus provides a comprehensive framework for both teaching and assessing critical thinking.

The Facione Critical Thinking Model's emphasis on dispositional components reflects a broader trend in educational psychology towards understanding not just what students know, but how they use that knowledge. This shift acknowledges the role of motivation and attitude in learning outcomes, suggesting that critical thinking is as much about mindset as it is about cognitive skills.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] **Application 1 — Instructional design**
> In instructional design, educators can use the Facione Critical Thinking Model to create learning activities that foster both cognitive skills and dispositional components. For example, a lesson on evaluating sources might include exercises where students analyze different types of evidence for reliability and bias, thereby practicing evaluation while also developing truth-seeking dispositions.

> [!example] **Application 2 — Assessment**
> The model provides a clear framework for assessing critical thinking skills in educational settings. By focusing on specific cognitive skills and dispositional components, educators can design rubrics that measure not just the ability to perform tasks but also the underlying motivations and attitudes that support effective reasoning.

## Key Distinctions

> [!key-distinction] **Facione's model vs Ennis' model**
> While both models aim to define critical thinking, they differ in their emphasis. Facione’s model focuses on a dual structure of cognitive skills and dispositional components, whereas Ennis’ model emphasizes the logical reasoning processes involved in critical thinking without explicitly addressing dispositions.

<!-- enhancement-pass:1 (2026-05-13) -->

> [!key-distinction] **Reflective vs Reactive Thinking**
> Reflective thinking involves deliberate consideration and evaluation of information before responding, aligning closely with the Facione model's emphasis on self-regulation. In contrast, reactive thinking is immediate and often automatic, lacking the reflective component that critical thinking requires. This distinction highlights why dispositional components like open-mindedness are crucial for fostering a reflective approach to problem-solving.

> [!key-distinction] **Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Motivation**
> Facione's model implicitly supports intrinsic motivation by emphasizing dispositions such as truth-seeking and confidence, which drive individuals to engage in critical thinking independently of external rewards. This contrasts with extrinsic motivations that might encourage rote learning or surface-level engagement without fostering deep understanding.

> [!warning] **Misconception** — People think the Facione model is just another set of skills to teach, but.
>
> The Facione Critical Thinking Model goes beyond mere skill acquisition by integrating dispositional components. These dispositions are essential for consistent application of critical thinking in real-world scenarios. Without fostering these attitudes, students may possess critical thinking skills without using them effectively.

## Key Figures

- **Peter Facione** — As the leader of the Delphi project, Peter Facione played a pivotal role in developing and refining the Facione Critical Thinking Model. His work has been instrumental in shaping how critical thinking is understood and taught.

## Synthesis

The dual structure of cognitive skills and dispositions in the Facione Critical Thinking Model is crucial for effective pedagogy because it recognizes that both intellectual abilities and motivational factors are necessary for consistent application of critical thinking. This holistic approach ensures that students not only learn how to think critically but also develop the inclination to do so, making the model a powerful tool in educational settings.
