# self-regulated-learning

> [!definition] self-regulated-learning
> - **Key-Term**: [[self-regulated-learning]]
> - **Definition**: Self-regulated learning is the process by which learners take control of their own learning, setting goals, monitoring progress, and adjusting strategies accordingly.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Self-regulated learning involves several key components such as metacognition (thinking about one's thinking), motivation, and self-efficacy. Learners who are self-regulated can effectively manage their study environments and resources to enhance their academic performance.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means that students might use various techniques like setting specific goals for what they want to learn, using different strategies based on the material or task at hand, and reflecting on their learning process to identify areas of improvement. They also monitor their own progress towards these goals and adjust their methods as needed.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include individual differences in self-regulation skills, which can affect how effectively learners apply these strategies. Additionally, there are different models and frameworks that attempt to explain the mechanisms behind self-regulated learning.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, fostering self-regulated learning can lead to improved academic outcomes as students become more independent and effective learners.

> [!example] Application
> Teachers can support this by providing tools and strategies for goal-setting, feedback, and reflection. This approach also helps in developing lifelong learning skills beyond the classroom.
