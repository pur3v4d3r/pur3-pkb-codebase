# Attribution-Dependent Emotion (Weiner, 1985, 1995)

> [!definition] Attribution-Dependent Emotion (Weiner, 1985, 1995)
> An attribution-dependent emotion is an affective state whose specific quality is determined not by the success or failure of an outcome per se but by the causal attribution constructed to explain that outcome. Each dimension of the causal taxonomy maps onto a characteristic family of emotions: locus maps onto self-referent affects (pride, shame), stability maps onto future-oriented affects (hope, hopelessness), and controllability maps onto social-moral affects (anger, guilt, pity, gratitude). The mapping is systematic: changing the dimensional profile of an attribution changes the emotional response, even when the outcome itself remains constant.
>
> **Boundary:** Attribution-dependent emotions are distinct from outcome-dependent emotions (happiness/sadness), which are elicited by the outcome itself rather than by its causal interpretation. The temporal sequence matters: outcome-dependent affects emerge first and rapidly; attribution-dependent affects emerge later and require causal processing.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This concept is the keystone of the attribution-motivation architecture, because it demonstrates that emotions are not just consequences of attribution but mediators between attribution and behaviour — the mechanism through which dimensional classifications acquire motivational force.
>
