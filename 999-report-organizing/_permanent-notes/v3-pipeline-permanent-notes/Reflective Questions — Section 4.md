## Reflections

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 4
> Think of a time when you felt shame rather than guilt in response to a failure. Can you identify the attribution behind each emotion — was the cause of the failure something you perceived as uncontrollable (producing shame) or something controllable (which would have produced guilt instead)? How might the emotional response have been different — and the motivational consequence different — if the same failure had been attributed to a cause on the other side of the controllability divide?
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 4
> 1. Does the loss of germane load as an independent construct weaken or strengthen CLT's practical utility for instructional designers? What is gained, and what is lost?
> 2. If germane processing is simply effortful engagement with intrinsic complexity, how should one distinguish between productive struggle and unproductive overload — is the boundary clear in practice?
> 3. The reconceptualization emerged from *within* CLT's own research programme. What does this suggest about the maturity and self-corrective capacity of the theory?
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 4
> 1. In the five-phase Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression, which phase do you find most challenging to design for, and why?
> 2. How might the concept of productive failure be integrated into a curriculum without undermining learner self-efficacy, particularly for learners who have had extensive experiences of unproductive failure?
> 3. If the scaffolding-fading progression requires adaptive, learner-responsive design, what are the implications for large-scale educational systems that must serve hundreds or thousands of learners with standardised curricula?
> *— [[curriculum-design-for-lifelong-learning-and-self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 4
> 1. How does the concept of "variability within task classes" connect to the distinction between surface features and deep structure in transfer research?
> 2. What is the difference between a mental model and a cognitive strategy, and why does the 4C/ID model require both as forms of supportive information?
> 3. Can you think of a learning experience where you received excellent conceptual instruction but inadequate authentic practice — or vice versa? How did the imbalance affect your learning outcomes?
> *— [[four-component-instructional-design-model-4cid-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 4
> 1. Why are delayed JOLs more accurate than immediate JOLs? What does this tell us about the nature of the cues that the meta-level relies upon?
> 2. The FOK operates after retrieval failure — how is it possible for the mind to have information about a memory it cannot currently access? What does this imply about the structure of memory representation?
> 3. If metacognitive accuracy is not a stable trait but a variable outcome dependent on available cues, what are the implications for educational assessment practices that ask students to rate their own understanding?
> *— [[nelson-narens-metacognitive-control-framework-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 4
> 1. How frequently does one's current study practice include explicit forethought — and when forethought is omitted, what are the observable consequences?
> 2. Does one's PKB currently support implementation intentions — specific if-then plans — or only abstract goals? What structural changes would bridge that gap?
> 3. Across the three forethought timescales (micro, meso, macro), which level is most developed in one's current workflow — and which level, if strengthened, would produce the greatest improvement in overall learning effectiveness?
> *— [[pkb-metacognitive-scaffolding-for-study-and-planning-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 4
> 1. Does the current PKM workflow include any form of deliberate retrieval practice — active recall, self-testing, or spaced review — or does it rely exclusively on search and browsing for access?
> 2. How might the concept of desirable difficulties be applied to PKM review routines, deliberately introducing productive friction into an otherwise frictionless system?
> *— [[pkb-pkm-planning-and-cognitive-science-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 4
> 1. If a traceback shows a `TypeError` on line 47, but the actual cause is an incorrect assignment on line 12, how would you use the debugger to trace back from the symptom to the cause?
> 2. When would a conditional breakpoint be more useful than an unconditional one? What kinds of bugs are difficult to diagnose without conditional breakpoints?
> 3. How does the debugging workflow described in this section compare to the "add print statements" approach? What are the specific advantages and costs of each?
> *— [[python-development-in-vscode-with-copilot-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 4
> 1. Consider a behavior one performs regularly without intrinsic interest — what regulatory style currently governs it, and what conditions would need to change for further internalization to occur?
> 2. Is integrated regulation genuinely distinguishable from intrinsic motivation in its functional consequences, or does the distinction matter primarily at the phenomenological level?
> 3. Can internalization proceed in reverse — can a previously integrated regulation become externalized again, and if so, through what mechanisms?
> *— [[self-determination-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!reflection] Reflective Questions — Section 4
> 1. Which of the SDL models most closely describes your own experience of self-directed learning? Does your practice resemble Garrison's three-dimensional integration, or is it more accurately captured by one of Candy's four distinct phenomena?
> 2. Does the concept of heutagogy — where the learner determines not only how to learn but what constitutes learning itself — describe a genuinely distinct phenomenon, or is it simply the most advanced form of what SDL theorists have always meant by self-direction?
