# extrinsic-motivation

> [!definition] extrinsic-motivation
> - **Key-Term**: [[extrinsic-motivation]]
> - **Definition**: Extrinsic motivation refers to the performance of an activity in order to gain some external reward or avoid punishment, such as grades, money, or praise from others.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Extrinsic motivation is driven by factors outside the individual's interest in the task itself. It often involves engaging in activities with the primary goal of obtaining a tangible outcome like rewards or avoiding negative consequences.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In educational settings, extrinsic motivators can include grades, certificates, and praise from teachers or peers. These external incentives can influence students' behavior and effort levels, but their impact on long-term learning outcomes is often debated.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Extrinsic motivation can be further categorized into direct (e.g., tangible rewards) and indirect (e.g., avoiding punishment) forms.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In classrooms, using extrinsic motivators like grades or rewards can increase short-term student engagement but may not necessarily lead to deeper learning or long-term interest in the subject.

> [!example] Application
> Educators must balance extrinsic and intrinsic motivation to foster a well-rounded educational experience that encourages both immediate performance and sustained interest.

## Open Threads

> [!open-question] Question
> The debate over whether extrinsic motivators can undermine intrinsic motivation and long-term learning remains unresolved.
