## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Clinical and Medical Education: The Diagnostic Self-Director
> In clinical education, the development of diagnostic expertise follows a trajectory remarkably parallel to the development of self-directed learning capacity. The novice clinician requires structured protocols, supervision, and explicit feedback; the expert clinician has internalized diagnostic frameworks to the point where they can self-monitor their own reasoning, recognize their knowledge boundaries, seek appropriate consultation, and continuously update their clinical knowledge through self-directed engagement with the literature. The concept of "[[reflective-practice|reflective practice]]" in medicine — as developed by Donald Schön and subsequently integrated into medical education curricula — is essentially a domain-specific instantiation of the metacognitive monitoring-control loop that drives self-directed learning. The structural principle: *in any domain where practitioners must maintain expertise in the face of evolving knowledge, the capacity for self-directed learning is not optional but constitutive of competence itself.*
> *— [[self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
