## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Paragraph 1: foundational context and core mechanism. This concept is rooted in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that autonomy, competence, and relatedness are fundamental human needs. When these needs are frustrated, individuals may experience negative emotions and engage in maladaptive behaviors.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Paragraph 2: how it works or is applied in practice. Frustration of basic psychological needs can lead to a decrease in intrinsic motivation and an increase in extrinsic motivation, often resulting in superficial engagement with tasks and reduced well-being. This concept helps explain why individuals might resort to unhealthy coping mechanisms when their fundamental needs are not met.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Paragraph 3: key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots. The dark side of motivation includes phenomena such as learned helplessness, depression, and even aggression. These outcomes can vary based on the specific need that is frustrated (e.g., autonomy vs. competence) and the individual's personality traits and environmental factors.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Implication 1: concrete application or real-world consequence. In educational settings, understanding this concept can help educators create supportive environments that meet students' basic psychological needs, thereby reducing negative behaviors and improving academic performance.

> [!example] Application
> Implication 2: a second distinct application. In organizational psychology, managers can use this knowledge to foster a positive work environment that supports employees' autonomy, competence, and relatedness, leading to higher job satisfaction and productivity.
