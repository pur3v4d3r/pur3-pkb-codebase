# Heutagogy (Hase & Kenyon)

> [!definition] Heutagogy (Hase & Kenyon)
> [[heutagogy]], or [[self-determined-learning|self-determined learning]], extends andragogy by proposing that the ultimate goal of education is not merely self-directed learning (where the learner controls how they learn) but self-determined learning (where the learner also determines what they learn and by what criteria their learning is evaluated). In a heutagogical framework, the curriculum exists primarily as provocation rather than prescription.
>
> **Boundary:** Heutagogy presupposes a high level of metacognitive sophistication, domain knowledge, and self-efficacy that not all learners possess. It represents the endpoint of a developmental progression, not a starting point for curriculum design.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Heutagogy defines the endpoint of the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression — autonomous sovereignty — and provides the theoretical vision toward which lifelong learning curriculum design aspires.
>
> **See also:** [[stewart-hase]], [[self-determined-learning]], [[metacognition]], [[andragogy]]

## Additional Material (Merged)

> [!definition] Heutagogy (Hase & Kenyon, 2000)
> A theory of self-determined learning that extends andragogy by emphasizing the learner's capacity to determine not only what and how to learn but also to modify and reconstruct their own conceptual frameworks and meaning-making structures. Where andragogy describes learners who direct their own learning within established domains, heutagogy describes learners who can fundamentally revise their understanding of the domains themselves.
>
> **Boundary:** Heutagogy describes a developmental aspiration rather than a universal capacity. It presupposes a level of metacognitive sophistication and epistemic maturity that represents the most advanced end of the self-direction continuum.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Represents the theoretical ceiling of self-directed learning — the point at which self-direction extends to the revision of one's own epistemological frameworks.
>
> **See also:** [[heutagogy]], [[andragogy]], [[Transformative-Learning]], [[double-loop-learning]]
