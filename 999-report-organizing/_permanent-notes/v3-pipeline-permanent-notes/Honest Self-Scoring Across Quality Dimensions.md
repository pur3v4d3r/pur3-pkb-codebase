> ### Identified Limitations
> 1. **Anglophone bias:** The literature surveyed is predominantly English-language and predominantly North American/British; productive work in other research traditions is underrepresented and would qualify or extend several claims.
> 2. **Cognitive-component privilege:** The treatment focuses on cognitive components and gives less sustained attention to motivational, affective, and social-relational dimensions of reading that interact substantially with the cognitive architecture.
> 3. **Architectural cleanness:** The four-layer architecture is presented as cleaner and more separable than the empirical reality; component interactions, individual differences, and developmental variation produce messier patterns than the abstract description conveys.
> 4. **Frontier-section uncertainty:** Sections on disciplinary reading, digital reading, and AI-assisted reading rely on a research base still in early development; specific claims about AI-assisted reading effects in particular may need substantial revision as the literature matures.
> 5. **Original integrations as proposals:** The three explicitly marked original syntheses are well-motivated but not empirically established; they should be read as candidates for further investigation, not as documented findings.
> 6. **Pedagogical specificity:** The pedagogical implications are drawn from the most reliable findings, but educational interventions are notoriously context-dependent; what works in one classroom does not always work in another, and the report cannot supply implementation guidance specific to particular contexts.
>
> ### Recommendations for Future Revision
> 1. Add a section on motivational and affective dimensions, drawing on the engagement and self-efficacy literatures to complement the cognitive architecture.
> 2. Develop the cross-cultural and individual-differences dimensions that are currently condensed.
> 3. Update the AI-assisted reading section as the empirical literature matures over the next two to three years; the current treatment is anticipatory and may need substantial revision.
> 4. If the report is positioned within a broader series on metacognition or on reading, add the cross-report navigation section that was omitted in the standalone version.
> 5. Test the original integrations (standard-of-coherence bandwidth hypothesis, calibration-as-translational-bottleneck framing, PKB-as-externalized-coherence-standard hypothesis) against further reading and revise accordingly; convert from speculative-but-well-motivated to empirically grounded as evidence accumulates.
