# Bandura, A. (1997). *Self-efficacy: The exercise of control*. W. H. Freeman.

> [!definition] Bandura, A. (1997). *Self-efficacy: The exercise of control*. W. H. Freeman.
> The belief in one's capabilities to organize and execute the courses of action required to manage prospective situations.

## Methodology & Sources

> [!cite] Bandura, A. (1997). *Self-efficacy: The exercise of control*. W. H. Freeman.
> The definitive statement of self-efficacy theory, including detailed treatment of how mastery experiences are attributionally interpreted. Chapter 3 on sources of self-efficacy is particularly relevant to the attribution-efficacy interface discussed in Section 8.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!cite] Bandura, A. (1997). *Self-efficacy: The exercise of control*. W. H. Freeman.
> Comprehensive treatment of self-efficacy theory, including the four sources of efficacy and the mechanisms by which self-efficacy beliefs influence cognitive, motivational, and behavioral functioning. Essential for understanding the self-efficacy dimension of SDL motivation.
