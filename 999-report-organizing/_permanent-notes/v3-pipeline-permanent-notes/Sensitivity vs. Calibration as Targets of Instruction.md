## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Sensitivity vs. Calibration as Targets of Instruction
> *Position A:* The most productive instructional target is *sensitivity* — the discriminative ability to distinguish well-comprehended from poorly-comprehended material — because sensitivity supports the relative comparisons that drive most regulatory decisions. Strongest advocates: many strategy-instruction researchers who emphasize practice with comprehension self-checks.
> *Position B:* The most productive instructional target is *calibration* — the absolute alignment between confidence and performance — because miscalibration is what suppresses regulatory action even when sensitivity is adequate. Strongest advocates: calibration researchers (Hacker, Bol, others) who have documented persistent calibration failure even with well-trained monitoring.
> *Current state of evidence:* Both targets matter, but interventions that address them separately produce smaller effects than interventions that address them together; the most effective approaches incorporate external checkpoints (testing, explanation) that exercise both sensitivity and calibration simultaneously.
> *Why it matters:* The tension determines what instructional designs receive emphasis and what success metrics are used to evaluate them.
> *This report's stance:* Both must be addressed, with calibration treated as a structural problem that requires external workarounds rather than as a defect of monitoring that can be corrected directly.
> *— [[metacognition-and-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
