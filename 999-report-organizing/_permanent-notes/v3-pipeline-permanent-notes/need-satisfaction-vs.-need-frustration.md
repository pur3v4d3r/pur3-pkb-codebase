# need-satisfaction-vs.-need-frustration

> [!definition] need-satisfaction-vs.-need-frustration
> - **Key-Term**: [[need-satisfaction-vs.-need-frustration]]
> - **Definition**: The concept of need-satisfaction-vs.-need-frustration refers to the psychological state resulting from whether basic needs are met or not, which significantly influences motivation and well-being according to Self-Determination Theory (SDT).
> - **Domain**: other

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> In SDT, individuals have three fundamental needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. When these needs are satisfied, it fosters intrinsic motivation and psychological growth. Conversely, when they are frustrated, it can lead to decreased motivation and negative outcomes such as stress or burnout.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The theory posits that satisfying these basic psychological needs is crucial for optimal functioning and well-being. Frustration of these needs can result in a variety of maladaptive behaviors and emotional states, impacting both personal and professional domains.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> SDT emphasizes the universality of these needs across cultures but also acknowledges cultural variations in how they are expressed and met.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding need-satisfaction can help teachers create supportive environments that foster intrinsic motivation among students.

> [!example] Application
