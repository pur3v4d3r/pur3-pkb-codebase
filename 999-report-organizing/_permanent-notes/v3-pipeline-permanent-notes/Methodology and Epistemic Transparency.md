> [!methodology-and-sources] Methodology and Epistemic Transparency
> **Traditions and Disciplines Synthesised:**
> This report draws on four distinct but converging intellectual traditions: (1) adult education theory (Dewey, Lindeman, Knowles, Tough, Candy, Hase, Brookfield), providing the philosophical and practical frameworks for self-directed learning; (2) motivational psychology (Deci & Ryan's self-determination theory, Hidi & Renninger's interest theory, Wigfield & Eccles' expectancy-value theory, Csikszentmihalyi's flow theory), providing accounts of the motivational dynamics that sustain or undermine engagement; (3) instructional design and curriculum theory (Wiggins & McTighe's backward design, Merrill's first principles), providing programme-level architectural frameworks; and (4) cognitive science and educational psychology (Vygotsky's ZPD, Sweller's cognitive load theory, Zimmerman's self-regulated learning, Bjork's desirable difficulties, Kapur's productive failure), providing the mechanisms through which learning and development occur. The report also engages with critical perspectives on self-directed learning (Brookfield, Brockett & Hiemstra) to avoid naïve advocacy.

> **Distinction Between Established Findings and Original Contributions:**
> The vast majority of this report synthesises established research. The two explicitly original contributions — the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression and the Motivational Ecology Model for Curriculum Design — are clearly marked as integrative syntheses that reorganise established constructs into novel configurations. Neither has been empirically validated as a unified framework. They should be treated as well-motivated theoretical proposals rather than as established findings.
>
> **Methodology Limitations:**
> 1. This report draws primarily on English-language scholarship, creating a linguistic and cultural bias in the evidence base.
> 2. The empirical evidence is strongest for individual components (scaffolding, formative assessment, self-efficacy) and weakest for integrated curriculum systems — the whole-system claims involve extrapolation from component-level evidence.
> 3. Longitudinal evidence linking specific curriculum design features to long-term self-directed learning outcomes is limited, as noted in Section 8.
> 4. The report draws on the author's training data rather than a systematic literature review, introducing potential selection bias toward well-known and frequently cited studies.
