## Practical Implications

> [!warning] The Illusion of Understanding Through Summarisation
> Summarisation is perhaps the most common note-making strategy and, when performed well, a valuable one. However, summarisation carries a specific monitoring risk: it is possible to produce a competent summary of material that one does not deeply understand. Summarisation extracts and compresses key claims but does not require the learner to explain *why* those claims hold, *how* the mechanisms work, or *what* would follow from different premises. A learner who has only summarised may feel confident (high fluency) while possessing only surface-level comprehension. Self-explanation and elaborative interrogation are more demanding but more diagnostically reliable — they reveal gaps that summarisation conceals.
