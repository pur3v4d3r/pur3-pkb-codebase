# organismic-integration-tendency

> [!definition] organismic-integration-tendency

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This concept is rooted in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which posits that individuals have innate psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Organismic-integration-tendency is a key mechanism through which these needs are met by fostering an integrated sense of self.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> It operates by guiding individuals to align their actions with their values and goals, thereby enhancing personal well-being and motivation. This tendency can be observed in various contexts, such as education, work, and social relationships, where it influences how people make decisions and form identities.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The concept is closely tied to the idea of psychological integration, which refers to the degree to which a person's self-concept is coherent and consistent across different domains. High levels of integration are associated with greater well-being and lower levels of psychological distress.
