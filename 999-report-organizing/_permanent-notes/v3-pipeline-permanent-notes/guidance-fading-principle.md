# guidance-fading-principle

> [!definition] guidance-fading-principle
> - **Key-Term**: [[guidance-fading-principle]]
> - **Definition**: The guidance-fading-principle refers to the gradual reduction of instructional support as learners become more competent, allowing them to apply knowledge independently while still receiving necessary assistance when needed.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This principle is rooted in cognitive load theory and dual-process models of cognition. It suggests that initial instruction should provide substantial guidance to prevent cognitive overload, but this support should be systematically reduced as learners develop their skills and understanding.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, the guidance-fading-principle can be applied through various instructional strategies such as scaffolding, where teachers or learning systems gradually remove supports like hints, prompts, and feedback as students demonstrate competence. This approach helps learners build confidence and independence while ensuring they do not become overly reliant on external assistance.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the balance between providing enough support to prevent frustration and too much that hinders independent problem-solving. Sub-variants of this principle might involve adaptive guidance systems that adjust the level of support based on individual learner performance.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, applying the guidance-fading-principle can lead to more effective learning outcomes by fostering independence and deeper understanding.

> [!example] Application
