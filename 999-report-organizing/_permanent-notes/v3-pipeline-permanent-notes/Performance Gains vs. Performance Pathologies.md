## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Performance Gains vs. Performance Pathologies
> **Position A (Locke & Latham, 1990, 2002, 2013):** The dark-side phenomena are real but represent misapplications of the theory rather than failures of the theory itself; the theory specifies its own moderating conditions and disclaims responsibility for outcomes in conditions where those moderators are violated.
> **Position B (Ordóñez, Schweitzer, Galinsky, Bazerman, 2009 and after):** The dark-side phenomena are not aberrations but predictable consequences of the same mechanisms that produce performance gains; the theory's failure to integrate the systematic risks into its prescriptive recommendations leaves practitioners without the warnings they require.
> **Current state of evidence:** Both positions have substantial empirical support; the descriptive findings on dark-side phenomena are well-replicated, while the prescriptive question of how to integrate those findings into goal-setting practice remains contested.
> **Why it matters:** The resolution of this tension determines whether Goal Setting Theory should be presented to practitioners as a relatively safe productivity tool with technical caveats or as a powerful intervention requiring substantial training in its conditions of safe use.
> **This report's stance:** The dark-side critique substantially strengthens rather than weakens the theory by sharpening the conditions under which its mechanisms can be expected to operate beneficially; the prescriptive implication is that goal setting should be taught with the same care as other powerful interventions whose benefits are conditional on competent application.
