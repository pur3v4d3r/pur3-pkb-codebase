# Contiguity-Principle

> [!definition] Contiguity-Principle
> - **Key-Term**: [[Contiguity-Principle]]
> - **Definition**: The Contiguity-Principle states that people learn better when related auditory and visual information is presented close together in time rather than separated by large intervals.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This principle suggests that the proximity of audio and visual elements enhances learning efficiency, as it aligns with how human memory processes information sequentially and simultaneously.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means that when designing educational materials or multimedia presentations, placing corresponding text and images near each other can improve comprehension and retention.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The principle is rooted in cognitive load theory, which posits that the brain has limited capacity to process information.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In e-learning design, this means that captions should be placed directly under relevant video segments or audio descriptions should accompany visual content immediately after it.

> [!example] Application
> This can also apply in traditional classroom settings where instructors might use synchronized slides and spoken explanations.
