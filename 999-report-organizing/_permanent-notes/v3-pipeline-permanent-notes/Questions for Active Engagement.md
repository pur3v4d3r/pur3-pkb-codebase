## Reflections

> [!reflection] Questions for Active Engagement
> 1. Of the five failure modes named, which one most often blocks your own intentions from becoming action — and what does that tell you about the kind of intervention that would actually help you?
> 2. Why might the intention-behavior gap be larger for behaviors that require *initiating* a new action than for behaviors that require *sustaining* an ongoing one?
> 3. If the deliberative and implementational systems are partially decoupled, what other psychological phenomena might that decoupling help to explain?
> *— [[implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*

> [!reflection] Questions for Active Engagement
> 1. The Rubicon Model treats the deliberative and implemental mind-sets as distinct cognitive states with measurable consequences. What experimental predictions would distinguish them from one another?
> 2. If plans confer cognitive economy by exempting committed action from re-deliberation, what is the cost of this exemption when the situation that prompted the plan changes?
> 3. Why might the *if-then* format specifically — as opposed to other formats for encoding intended action — be the format that produces strategic automaticity?
> *— [[implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*

> [!reflection] Questions for Active Engagement
> 1. If strategic automaticity is plan-dependent and revocable, what implications does this have for using implementation intentions to break unwanted habits versus to start new behaviors?
> 2. The mechanism intervenes at perceptual, attentional, and action-selection levels. Could implementation intentions also intervene at the level of *emotional* responding? What would the construct look like in that domain?
> 3. The pre-compilation analogy treats the goal intention as source code and the implementation intention as compiled output. What does this analogy predict about which behaviors are most amenable to if-then planning?
> *— [[implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*

> [!reflection] Questions for Active Engagement
> 1. The d = 0.65 average effect size is substantial, but it leaves considerable variance unexplained. What does the unexplained variance tell us about the limits of the construct?
> 2. Why might inhibitory implementation intentions ("when X, do *not* do Y") be more difficult to engineer than facilitative ones ("when X, do Y")?
> 3. If forming too many plans dilutes the per-plan benefit, what does this imply about how the construct should be deployed in practice?
> *— [[implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*

> [!reflection] Questions for Active Engagement
> 1. If the cue-encoding bottleneck hypothesis is correct, what would change about how you currently form implementation intentions for your own behavior?
> 2. The flexibility-reliability trade-off positions implementation intentions between deliberation and habit. For which behaviors in your own life should you push toward more flexibility, and for which toward more reliability?
> 3. What does automaticity drift suggest about how often implementation intentions should be reviewed?
> *— [[implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*

> [!reflection] Questions for Active Engagement
> 1. The MCII finding that combining commitment-strengthening with cue-engineering outperforms either alone — what general principle of regulatory design does this suggest?
> 2. If implementation intentions can bridge into true habit formation, when should one *not* let this transition happen? When is the flexibility of the if-then plan more valuable than the reliability of a consolidated habit?
> 3. The construct's clinical extensions involve cue-triggered cognitive responses rather than cue-triggered actions. Does this expansion of the mechanism's scope require a corresponding expansion of the underlying theory?
> *— [[implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*

> [!reflection] Questions for Active Engagement
> 1. Which moments in your own intellectual practice are most vulnerable to the action-initiation failure that implementation intentions address — and what specific if-then plans would target those moments?
> 2. The recursive-application idea proposes that implementation intentions can chain together into behavioral architectures. What are the risks of such architectures? Could they become too rigid?
> 3. Where in your intellectual life would *not* using implementation intentions be the right choice — where is open-endedness more valuable than reliability?
> *— [[implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
