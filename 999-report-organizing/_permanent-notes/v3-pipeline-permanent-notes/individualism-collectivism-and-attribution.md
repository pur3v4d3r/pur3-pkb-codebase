## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This concept explores how cultural contexts shape attribution styles. In individualistic cultures, people tend to attribute outcomes more to personal characteristics or efforts (internal attributions), while in collectivistic cultures, outcomes are often attributed to external factors or group influences.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The theory is applied in various fields such as psychology and sociology to understand differences in emotional responses and behaviors across cultural groups. For instance, individuals from individualistic backgrounds may experience more pride or shame based on personal achievements, whereas those from collectivistic backgrounds might feel more guilt or embarrassment for actions that harm the group.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include variations within cultures and how these attributions can affect mental health outcomes, such as depression.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
