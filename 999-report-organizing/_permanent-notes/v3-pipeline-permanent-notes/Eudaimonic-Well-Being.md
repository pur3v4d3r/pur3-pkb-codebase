# Eudaimonic-Well-Being

> [!definition] Eudaimonic-Well-Being
> - **Key-Term**: [[Eudaimonic-Well-Being]]
> - **Definition**: Eudaimonic well-being refers to the state of flourishing and living a meaningful, purposeful life through self-realization and personal growth, as opposed to merely feeling happy or satisfied with one's circumstances.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Eudaimonic well-being is rooted in ancient Greek philosophy, particularly Aristotle’s concept of eudaimonia, which emphasizes the development of virtues and living a life aligned with one's true potential. It involves engaging in activities that contribute to personal growth and self-actualization.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In modern psychology, this concept is often associated with positive psychology and self-determination theory (SDT), where it is linked to fulfilling basic psychological needs such as autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Individuals who experience eudaimonic well-being tend to have a sense of purpose and are motivated by intrinsic values rather than external rewards.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between eudaimonia and hedonic well-being, which focuses on pleasure and positive emotions. Eudaimonic well-being is more about long-term satisfaction and personal development.

## Practical Implications
