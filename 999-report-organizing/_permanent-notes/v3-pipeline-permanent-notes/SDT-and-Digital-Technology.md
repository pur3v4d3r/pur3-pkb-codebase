# SDT-and-Digital-Technology

> [!definition] SDT-and-Digital-Technology
> - **Key-Term**: [[SDT-and-Digital-Technology]]
> - **Definition**: The interaction between Self-Determination Theory (SDT) and digital technology refers to how digital tools and platforms can influence and support the fulfillment of basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness as outlined in SDT, thereby impacting motivation and well-being.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Self-Determination Theory (SDT), developed by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan, posits that individuals are motivated to satisfy three innate psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Digital technology can facilitate or hinder the fulfillment of these needs through various means such as personalized feedback, social connections, and opportunities for self-expression.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> For instance, digital platforms like educational apps can provide immediate feedback and adaptive learning experiences that enhance a user's sense of competence. Social media can foster feelings of relatedness by connecting individuals with others who share similar interests or values. However, the design and use of these technologies also play crucial roles in whether they support or undermine these psychological needs.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the cultural specificity of how digital technology is perceived and used across different societies, as well as the potential for motivational pollution, where external controls and pressures can override intrinsic motivation.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, digital tools can be designed to promote autonomy by allowing students to set their own learning goals and pace.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace settings, digital platforms can enhance competence through continuous feedback and skill development opportunities.

> [!example] Application
> However, there is a cautionary note regarding the potential for technology to become addictive or overly controlling, which could undermine intrinsic motivation.
