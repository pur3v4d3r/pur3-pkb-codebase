# myside-bias

> [!definition] myside-bias
> - **Key-Term**: [[myside-bias]]
> - **Definition**: Myside-bias is the tendency to favor information that confirms one's preexisting beliefs or hypotheses, often leading to confirmation bias and selective exposure to information that supports one's existing views.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Myside-bias operates as a cognitive shortcut where individuals unconsciously seek out and give more weight to evidence that aligns with their preconceived notions. This can lead to a distorted perception of reality, as opposing viewpoints are often dismissed or ignored.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, myside-bias is evident in various contexts such as political discussions, scientific debates, and personal relationships. People may selectively expose themselves to information sources that reinforce their beliefs while avoiding those that challenge them, thereby reinforcing their existing views.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the fact that myside-bias can be subtle and difficult to detect, often operating at an unconscious level. It is closely related to confirmation bias but differs in its emphasis on personal biases rather than just seeking out confirming evidence.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In AI alignment, understanding myside-bias helps in designing algorithms that can better predict and mitigate the influence of biased inputs from users.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical reasoning, recognizing myside-bias can help healthcare professionals avoid diagnostic errors by considering a wider range of evidence.
