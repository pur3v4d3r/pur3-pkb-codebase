# humanistic-psychology

> [!definition] humanistic-psychology
> - **Key-Term**: [[humanistic-psychology]]
> - **Definition**: Humanistic psychology is an approach to psychology that emphasizes the potential for personal growth and self-actualization, focusing on subjective experiences and individual values over objective measurement and pathology.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Humanistic psychology emerged in the mid-20th century as a reaction against the deterministic views of behaviorism and psychoanalysis. It posits that individuals have an inherent drive towards personal growth and self-improvement, and that understanding this drive is crucial for psychological well-being.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Practitioners of humanistic psychology often use techniques such as client-centered therapy to facilitate self-exploration and personal development. This approach emphasizes the importance of empathy, unconditional positive regard, and genuineness in therapeutic relationships.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key figures like Carl Rogers and Abraham Maslow are central to this field, with their work on self-actualization and the hierarchy of needs respectively.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In therapy, humanistic psychology can lead to more authentic and meaningful client experiences by fostering a supportive environment that encourages personal growth.

> [!example] Application
> In education, it promotes student-centered learning approaches that prioritize individual needs and self-directed learning.
