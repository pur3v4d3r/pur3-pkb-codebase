## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Autonomy vs. Structure: The Scaffolding Paradox
> **Position A (Structure-First):** Learners, particularly novices, require substantial external structure to learn effectively. Unguided or minimally guided approaches are inefficient and can produce misconceptions. (Advocates: Kirschner, Sweller, Clark, 2006 — "Why Minimal Guidance During Instruction Does Not Work.")
> **Position B (Autonomy-First):** Excessive structure produces dependence and undermines the development of self-regulatory capacity. Learners develop autonomy through exercising autonomy, not through prolonged compliance with external prescriptions. (Advocates: Hmelo-Silver, Duncan, Chinn, 2007 — response to Kirschner et al.)
> **Current state of evidence:** Both positions are supported by evidence in specific contexts. The resolution lies in developmental sequencing (high structure for novices, progressively reduced structure as competence develops) rather than in choosing one pole.
> **Why it matters:** This tension is the central challenge of curriculum design for self-direction and drives the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression framework.
> **This report's stance:** The report resolves the tension as a developmental sequence — structure and autonomy are not opposed but temporally ordered through scaffolding-fading.
