# WEIRD-Sample-Bias

> [!definition] WEIRD-Sample-Bias
> - **Key-Term**: [[WEIRD-Sample-Bias]]
> - **Definition**: WEIRD-Sample-Bias refers to the tendency of psychological research to use samples that are Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD), leading to potential generalization errors when applying findings to more diverse populations.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The term WEIRD-Sample-Bias is used in cognitive psychology and related fields to highlight the limitations of research conducted on samples that predominantly consist of individuals from Western societies. These samples often include people who are educated, industrialized, rich, and live in democratic nations (WEIRD).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This bias can lead to incorrect assumptions about human behavior and cognition because non-WEIRD populations may exhibit different patterns or responses due to cultural, economic, and social differences.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances of WEIRD-Sample-Bias include the recognition that many psychological theories are based on WEIRD samples, which can result in a lack of generalizability when applied to other cultures or contexts.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In practice, researchers must be cautious about applying findings from WEIRD populations to non-WEIRD groups, as this can lead to misinterpretations and ineffective interventions.

> [!example] Application
> There is an increasing emphasis on diversifying research samples to include more representative populations, which helps in reducing the bias and improving the generalizability of psychological theories.
