# Scaffolding-in-Education

> [!definition] Scaffolding-in-Education
> - **Key-Term**: [[Scaffolding-in-Education]]
> - **Definition**: Scaffolding-in-Education is an instructional technique where teachers provide temporary support to help students learn complex tasks, gradually reducing that support as the student becomes more competent and independent in performing the task.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Scaffolding was first introduced by Jerome Bruner, Lev Vygotsky, and their colleagues in educational psychology. It is based on the idea of the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD), where a student can perform tasks with guidance but not independently.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, scaffolding involves breaking down complex tasks into smaller steps, providing hints or models, offering feedback, and gradually withdrawing support as students become more proficient. This approach is used across various subjects and educational levels to enhance learning efficiency and foster independent thinking.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the importance of timing in when support is provided and withdrawn, the role of metacognitive strategies, and the adaptability of scaffolding to individual student needs.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Instructors can use scaffolding to help students who are struggling with a concept by providing them with structured guidance that they can gradually internalize.

> [!example] Application
> Scaffolding also helps in promoting deeper learning by encouraging students to think critically and solve problems on their own, rather than relying solely on external support.
