> **Identified Limitations:**
> - The treatment of the affective dimensions of judgment is acknowledged as incomplete; the integration of dual-process theory with the affect-and-emotion literature deserves a dedicated report.
> - The neural-implementation question is treated briefly and dismissively; a more careful treatment would engage the contemporary cognitive-neuroscience literature on whether and how dual-process distinctions map onto neural systems.
> - The cross-cultural and developmental dimensions are mentioned but not developed; the framework's generalisability beyond WEIRD populations and across the lifespan deserves dedicated treatment.
> - The report uses Claude's interpretive framing in the original synthesis sections; readers should treat these as well-motivated proposals rather than established consensus.
> - Several specific applications (legal reasoning, public-policy design, intelligence analysis) are not covered.
>
> **Recommendations for Future Revision:**
> - Add a section or sidebar treatment of dual-process theory in moral cognition (Greene's work on utilitarian vs. deontological judgment).
> - Develop the cross-cultural dimension with reference to Henrich's WEIRD critique and emerging non-WEIRD research on heuristics and biases.
> - Engage the contemporary AI-reasoning literature more directly; the structural parallels between LLM reasoning architectures and dual-process theory have implications worth developing.
> - Consider a dedicated companion report on the philosophical implications of the phenomenological opacity argument.
