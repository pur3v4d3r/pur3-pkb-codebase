## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Dweck's foundational context lies in her research on how people perceive their abilities and what drives them to achieve. She introduced the idea that individuals can have a 'fixed mindset,' believing their qualities are static and unchangeable, or a 'growth mindset,' where effort and learning lead to development of skills.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, Dweck's work has been applied in educational settings to encourage students to embrace challenges and view failures as opportunities for growth. This approach aims to foster resilience and continuous improvement among learners.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, and how feedback can either promote a fixed or growth mindset.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, teachers can use Dweck's framework to design curricula that emphasize effort over innate ability, encouraging students to develop a growth mindset.

> [!example] Application
> Business leaders can apply these principles to create environments where employees feel safe taking risks and learning from mistakes.
