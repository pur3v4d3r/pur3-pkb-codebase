# Three Failures, Three Fix-Up Choices

## Examples

> [!example] Three Failures, Three Fix-Up Choices
> Consider three different breakdowns the same reader might experience in the same scientific paper. (1) An unfamiliar piece of terminology on page 3 — the appropriate fix-up is to look up the term, either in the paper's earlier pages, in a reference work, or by inference from context if the term is partially defined. (2) A claim on page 5 that contradicts a claim the reader thought was made on page 2 — the appropriate fix-up is to look back, locate the page-2 claim, and determine whether the reader misread it then, is misreading the page-5 claim now, or whether the contradiction is intentional and the reader has missed the writer's resolution of it. (3) A felt sense, on page 8, that the argument as a whole has slipped away from the reader without any single sentence being unclear — the appropriate fix-up is *not* rereading any individual passage but a higher-level intervention: pausing, summarizing what the argument has done so far, and reconstructing the connection from the last point of clear understanding forward. Each breakdown calls for a different move; deploying the wrong move (looking up terms when summarization was needed, summarizing when a single look-back would have sufficed) wastes effort without restoring comprehension.
> *— [[metacognition-and-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
