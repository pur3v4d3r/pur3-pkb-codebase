# scaffolding

> [!definition] scaffolding
> - **Key-Term**: [[scaffolding]]
> - **Definition**: Scaffolding is a teaching method that involves providing temporary support to learners to help them achieve tasks they would not be able to complete on their own, and then gradually removing this support as the learner becomes more competent.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Scaffolding was introduced by Jerome Bruner in the context of cognitive development theory. It is a process where educators provide structured guidance that supports students' learning until they can perform tasks independently. The key idea is to match the level of support with the learner's current capabilities, ensuring that the task remains challenging yet achievable.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, scaffolding can be implemented through various strategies such as modeling, questioning, and providing feedback. For example, a teacher might demonstrate a problem-solving technique, then guide the student step-by-step until they can solve similar problems on their own. As the learner gains confidence and competence, the teacher reduces their involvement.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Scaffolding is closely related to Vygotsky's concept of the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD), which defines the range between what a learner can do without help and what they can achieve with guidance.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In classrooms, scaffolding helps students tackle complex tasks by breaking them down into manageable parts. This approach enhances learning efficiency and ensures that all students have access to challenging material.

> [!example] Application
> Scaffolding is also used in online learning platforms where adaptive algorithms provide personalized support based on the learner's performance.
