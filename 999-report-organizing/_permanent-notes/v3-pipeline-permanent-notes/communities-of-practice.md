# communities-of-practice

> [!definition] communities-of-practice
> - **Key-Term**: [[communities-of-practice]]
> - **Definition**: Communities of practice are groups of individuals who share a common interest, skill, or problem and engage in ongoing learning and collaboration to improve their expertise and knowledge within that domain.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Communities of practice (CoP) were first introduced by Etienne Wenger, Jean Lave, and Richard Wenger in the late 20th century. They are characterized by a shared domain of interest, a community of members who interact regularly, and a collective body of knowledge that is continuously developed through these interactions.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, CoPs facilitate learning through observation, mentoring, and collaboration among members. Members contribute to the group's knowledge base and benefit from the expertise of others, leading to mutual growth and development in their field of interest.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between weak ties (loosely connected individuals) and strong ties (close-knit groups), as well as the role of a mentor or guide who helps new members integrate into the community.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, CoPs can enhance learning by providing opportunities for peer-to-peer interaction and knowledge sharing.

> [!example] Application
> In professional contexts, CoPs support career development and skill enhancement through continuous learning and collaboration.
