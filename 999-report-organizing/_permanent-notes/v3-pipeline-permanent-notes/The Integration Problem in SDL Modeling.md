## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] The Integration Problem in SDL Modeling
> What one observes across the evolution of SDL models is a progressive recognition that the phenomenon resists reduction to any single dimension — cognitive, motivational, contextual, or dispositional. Each new model adds complexity that its predecessors lacked, but no model has yet achieved a fully satisfying integration. Knowles provided the humanistic vision but underspecified the cognitive mechanisms. Garrison added the cognitive dimension through self-monitoring but may have underemphasized the developmental trajectory. Brockett and Hiemstra centered personal responsibility but left the cognitive architecture implicit. Candy achieved the greatest analytical precision through his four-fold distinction but at the cost of fragmenting a phenomenon that learners experience as unified. The field, in this sense, is still searching for a model that can hold together what lived experience holds together effortlessly — the simultaneous deployment of metacognitive skill, motivational alignment, environmental management, and personal agency in the service of self-chosen learning goals.
> *— [[self-directed-learning-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
