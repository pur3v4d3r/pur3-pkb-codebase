# Metacognitive Scaffolding

> [!definition] Metacognitive Scaffolding
> Metacognitive scaffolding refers to any external structure, tool, template, or protocol that renders invisible metacognitive processes visible, imposes regulatory structure where self-regulation is absent or unreliable, and creates feedback loops that enable learners to calibrate the accuracy of their self-assessments. In the context of a Personal Knowledge Base, metacognitive scaffolds include planning templates, monitoring prompts, reflective review workflows, and calibration protocols — all designed to externalise functions that mature self-regulators perform internally.
>
> **Boundary:** Metacognitive scaffolding is not synonymous with instructional scaffolding broadly conceived. Instructional scaffolding may support content acquisition (e.g., worked examples, hints); metacognitive scaffolding specifically targets the learner's *regulation of their own learning processes*.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** This concept provides the unifying lens through which all PKB design principles in this report are analysed — every template, protocol, and workflow examined herein is evaluated for its capacity to scaffold specific metacognitive functions.
>

> [!evidence] Metacognitive Scaffolding
> External structures, prompts, templates, and workflows embedded in a learning system (such as a PKB) that elicit, support, and systematise metacognitive processes — planning, monitoring, strategy selection, and reflection — that the learner might otherwise omit, perform poorly, or perform inconsistently.
>
> **Boundary:** Metacognitive scaffolding is distinct from content scaffolding (which simplifies subject matter) and procedural scaffolding (which provides step-by-step instructions for task completion). Metacognitive scaffolding targets the *regulation* of learning, not the *content* of learning.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Central concept of this report; the claim that PKB systems can function as metacognitive scaffolding architectures is the organizing thesis.
