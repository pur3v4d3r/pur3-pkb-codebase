## Examples

> [!example] The SRL Cycle in PKM Practice
> A practitioner sits down to process a research paper on [[Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)|cognitive load theory]]. In the *forethought phase*, the practitioner sets a specific goal (extract three key principles and connect each to existing permanent notes), selects a strategy (read the paper's results section first, then methods, then introduction — a form of strategic reading), and calibrates effort expectations (this is a dense theoretical paper; surface-level notes will not suffice). In the *performance phase*, the practitioner creates notes, pausing periodically to assess comprehension ("Do I understand the distinction between intrinsic and extraneous load well enough to explain it without the paper?"), adjusting strategy when needed ("This section requires re-reading; my initial processing was too shallow"). In the *self-reflection phase*, after the session, the practitioner reviews the notes created, evaluates whether the goal was met, and identifies what worked and what should change ("I spent too long on the introduction and ran out of attention for the methods; next time, start with the section most relevant to my current project"). This cycle — plan, execute, evaluate — is the regulatory engine that determines whether PKM practice improves over time or remains static.
