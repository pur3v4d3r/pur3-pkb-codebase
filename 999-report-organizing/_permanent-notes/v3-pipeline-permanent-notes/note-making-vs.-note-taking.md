# note-making-vs.-note-taking

> [!definition] note-making-vs.-note-taking
> - **Key-Term**: [[note-making-vs.-note-taking]]
> - **Definition**: Note-making and note-taking refer to the processes of creating and recording information, respectively, where note-making involves transforming raw information into a more structured form for better retention and understanding, while note-taking is the act of capturing information verbatim during lectures or readings.
> - **Domain**: learning-science
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Note-making and note-taking are two distinct cognitive processes in learning. Note-making involves summarizing, organizing, and structuring information to enhance comprehension and memory retention. This process often includes highlighting key points, creating diagrams, and linking new information with existing knowledge.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In contrast, note-taking is the act of recording information verbatim during lectures or while reading. It typically involves transcribing what is said or written without much modification, which can be useful for capturing all details but may not always facilitate deep understanding.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the fact that effective note-making requires active engagement with the material, whereas note-taking can sometimes be a more passive process.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, students who engage in note-making tend to perform better on exams and retain information longer than those who only take verbatim notes.

> [!example] Application
> Professionals in fields such as medicine or law often use both methods, with note-taking for capturing all details during meetings and note-making for summarizing and organizing the information later.
