# Method Transfer: Progressive Magnification as a Study Method

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Method Transfer: Progressive Magnification as a Study Method
> **Structural principle:** Any narrow topic can be studied through progressive magnification — surface, mechanism, substructure, dynamics, edge cases, frontier, speculation.
>
> **The protocol:**
> 1. Start with the surface description — what does the topic appear to be at first encounter?
> 2. Ask "how does this actually work?" — that is mechanism.
> 3. Ask "what makes the mechanism possible?" — that is substructure.
> 4. Ask "how does the substructure produce observable behavior?" — that is dynamics.
> 5. Ask "where does the standard story break down?" — that is edge cases.
> 6. Ask "what are researchers currently trying to figure out?" — that is frontier.
> 7. Ask "what would follow if the mechanism extended into conditions outside its formative environment?" — that is speculation.
>
> **Boundary condition:** Progressive magnification requires a topic narrow enough that going deeper at each step is possible. Broad topics dilute depth across too much surface area; the method fails when applied to topics that should have been narrowed first. The scope discipline that began this report is therefore a precondition for the method's success, not an incidental preliminary.
>
> **What the method produces:** An inhabited rather than surveyed account; a specialist-level working model rather than a textbook summary; a sense of where the framework is settled and where it is moving.
