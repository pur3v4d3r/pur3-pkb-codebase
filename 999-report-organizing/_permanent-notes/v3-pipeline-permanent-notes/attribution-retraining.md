# attribution-retraining

> [!definition] attribution-retraining
> - **Key-Term**: [[attribution-retraining]]
> - **Definition**: Attribution retraining is a psychological technique aimed at altering an individual's attributional style, particularly to improve emotional well-being and cognitive functioning.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Foundational context: Attribution theory posits that individuals explain the causes of their own and others' behaviors. Attribution retraining seeks to modify maladaptive attributions associated with negative emotions or experiences into more adaptive ones.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> How it works: The process typically involves identifying and challenging maladaptive attributions, followed by reinforcing new, more positive attributions through cognitive restructuring techniques such as journaling, role-playing, and feedback.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances: Attribution retraining can be tailored to specific contexts (e.g., clinical depression) or generalized for broader use. It often includes a two-stage architecture where initial assessment is followed by targeted intervention.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Concrete application: In clinical settings, attribution retraining has been used to help patients with depression shift from pessimistic attributions (e.g., 'I always fail') to more optimistic ones (e.g., 'Sometimes I succeed').

> [!example] Application
> Second distinct application: In educational contexts, it can be applied to improve students' academic motivation by helping them attribute success and failure to controllable factors.
