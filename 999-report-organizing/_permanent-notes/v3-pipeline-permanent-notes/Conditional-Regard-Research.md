# Conditional-Regard-Research

> [!definition] Conditional-Regard-Research
> - **Key-Term**: [[Conditional-Regard-Research]]
> - **Definition**: Conditional-regard research examines the impact of conditional forms of social evaluation on individuals' psychological well-being and motivation, particularly within the context of self-determination theory (SDT).
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Conditional-regard research focuses on how people's sense of worth or acceptance is tied to their performance in specific domains. This can lead to a fear of failure and decreased intrinsic motivation.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, conditional regard often manifests as parents or teachers rewarding children only when they achieve certain standards, which can undermine autonomy and foster external regulation of behavior.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between conditional positive regard (e.g., being loved if one performs well) and conditional negative regard (e.g., fear of rejection for not performing well). These different forms have varying impacts on psychological outcomes.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering unconditional support can enhance students' intrinsic motivation and resilience to failure.

> [!example] Application
> In the workplace, promoting a culture that values effort over perfection can improve employee engagement and job satisfaction.
