> **Identified Limitations:**
> 1. The measurement section (5) is the weakest analytically — it surveys approaches but does not deeply engage with the psychometric literature on construct validity.
> 2. Individual differences (spatial ability, age, prior knowledge type) are mentioned but not systematically treated.
> 3. Cultural and contextual factors in CLT application (Western vs. non-Western educational contexts, high-resource vs. low-resource settings) receive no attention.
> 4. The report focuses almost exclusively on the Sweller tradition; alternative cognitive load accounts (e.g., Brünken's dual-task approach as a distinct theoretical contribution) receive insufficient independent treatment.
> 5. The evolutionary perspective on cognitive load (Sweller & Sweller, 2006; biologically primary vs. secondary knowledge) is mentioned only in passing despite its significance for the theory's scope claims.
>
> **Recommendations for Future Revision:**
> - Expand Section 5 with deeper treatment of psychometric validation studies
> - Add a subsection on individual differences and their interaction with CLT predictions
> - Include the evolutionary/biological primary-secondary knowledge framework as a dedicated subsection
> - Develop the CLT-motivation interface more formally, potentially as a separate Annotated Critical Analysis
