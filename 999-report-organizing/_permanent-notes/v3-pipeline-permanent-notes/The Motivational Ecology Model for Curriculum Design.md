## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] The Motivational Ecology Model for Curriculum Design
> Integrating the analysis across Sections 3 through 7, this report proposes the Motivational Ecology Model for Curriculum Design as a complementary framework to the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression. Where the Scaffolding-Sovereignty Progression addresses the structural dimension (how much and what kind of support to provide), the Motivational Ecology Model addresses the sustainability dimension (how engagement is maintained across the long arc of lifelong learning). The model posits that sustainable lifelong learning requires the cultivation of a motivational ecosystem characterised by five properties: (1) *diversity* — multiple interconnected sources of motivation (intrinsic interest, identity, social belonging, practical relevance, competence satisfaction); (2) *redundancy* — each motivational source can partially compensate when others weaken; (3) *development* — motivational sources are progressively internalised from external triggers to internal dispositions; (4) *social embeddedness* — the ecosystem includes relational and communal sources that cannot be reduced to individual psychological states; and (5) *metacognitive awareness* — the learner develops the capacity to monitor and actively manage their own motivational ecology.
>
> **Epistemic status:** This is an original integrative synthesis that draws on established frameworks (SDT, interest development theory, expectancy-value theory, communities of practice) but organises them into a novel ecological metaphor with design implications. Empirical validation of the model's predictions — particularly regarding the compensatory dynamics of motivational redundancy — would be valuable.
