## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Expert Debate: Universality of Basic Needs
> **Position A (Deci, Ryan, Chen, and the SDT mainstream):** The three basic psychological needs are universal. Cross-cultural variation occurs in the contents that satisfy the needs and in the relative weight different cultures place on different need-satisfying contents, but the underlying needs themselves are constant features of human psychology. Cross-cultural research, properly designed, supports this view.
> **Position B (cross-cultural challengers, including parts of the [[Iyengar]] tradition and some self-construal researchers):** Autonomy in particular is a culturally specific value, more central to Western individualist cultures than to others, and the universality claim reflects a Western-cultural projection onto a more variable human psychology.
> **What the debate hinges on:** The operationalization of autonomy. If autonomy is operationalized as independent choice-making, the universality claim is empirically vulnerable; if it is operationalized as volitional endorsement (the SDT technical sense), the universality claim is empirically supported by the bulk of subsequent research.
> **Current state:** The SDT mainstream position is currently dominant in the literature, supported by the Chen et al. 2015 multi-cultural study and subsequent work, but the debate remains active at the boundary, particularly around whether the operationalization itself smuggles in Western assumptions about what counts as volitional endorsement.
> **Why the debate matters:** The outcome shapes whether SDT-informed interventions can be deployed cross-culturally with the same expected effects, or whether substantial cultural adaptation is required.
