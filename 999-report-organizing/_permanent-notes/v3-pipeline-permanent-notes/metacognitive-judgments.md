# metacognitive-judgments

> [!definition] metacognitive-judgments
> - **Key-Term**: [[metacognitive-judgments]]
> - **Definition**: Metacognitive judgments refer to an individual's ability to assess their own cognitive processes, such as understanding, memory, and problem-solving abilities, often in the context of learning and decision-making tasks.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Metacognitive judgments are central to self-regulated learning and involve monitoring one’s own cognitive activities. Individuals use these judgments to evaluate their current state of knowledge or skill, set goals, and adjust strategies accordingly.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> For example, when a student judges whether they have understood a concept well enough to move on to the next topic, this is a metacognitive judgment. This process can be applied in various domains, from academic settings to professional decision-making scenarios.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between explicit and implicit judgments, where explicit judgments are conscious and verbalized, while implicit judgments occur without direct awareness.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, metacognitive judgments can help students become more effective learners by encouraging them to reflect on their learning processes.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical decision-making, accurate metacognitive judgments can improve diagnostic accuracy and patient care.
