# Quality-Assurance

> [!definition] Quality-Assurance
> - **Key-Term**: [[Quality-Assurance]]
> - **Definition**: Quality-Assurance is the systematic process of ensuring that products, services, and systems meet specified quality standards to ensure reliability and customer satisfaction.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Quality-Assurance involves setting up processes, procedures, and controls to prevent defects or errors before they occur. It focuses on continuous improvement and maintaining high standards throughout the production or service delivery process.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, Quality-Assurance is applied through various methods such as inspections, audits, testing, and documentation reviews. These activities help identify potential issues early in the development cycle, allowing for corrective actions to be taken before they impact end-users.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include distinguishing between preventive measures (like design reviews) and detective measures (like post-production testing). Theoretical roots often trace back to quality management theories developed by thinkers like Walter Shewhart and W. Edwards Deming.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In software development, Quality-Assurance helps ensure that code is free from bugs and meets user requirements, leading to more reliable applications.

> [!example] Application
> In manufacturing, it ensures consistent product quality, reducing waste and improving customer trust.
