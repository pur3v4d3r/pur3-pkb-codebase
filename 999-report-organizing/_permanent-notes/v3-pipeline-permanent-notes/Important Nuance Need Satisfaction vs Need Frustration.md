## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Important Nuance: Need Satisfaction vs Need Frustration
> A subtle but consequential distinction has emerged in the modern SDT literature: the absence of need satisfaction is not the same as the presence of [[Need Frustration|need frustration]], and the two have distinct downstream consequences. The lines of research associated with [[Maarten Vansteenkiste|Vansteenkiste]] and colleagues, particularly the [[Need-Frustration-and-the-Dark-Side-of-Motivation-—-A-Focused-Analysis|need-frustration / dark side]] literature of the past fifteen years, have established that environments which actively thwart the basic needs (controlling parenting, conditional regard, relentless social comparison) produce different outcomes from environments which merely fail to support them (neutral, neglectful, or simply absent need-supportive features).
>
> - **Need frustration** is associated with defensive functioning, oppositional behavior, ill-being, and pathology.
> - **Need deprivation** (low satisfaction without active frustration) is associated with arrested motivational development and lower well-being, but without the defensive and pathological signatures of frustration.
>
> The distinction matters for intervention: removing need-thwarting conditions does not by itself produce need-supportive ones, and many real-world environments fall in the middle range — neither actively thwarting nor actively supporting — in which the developmental trajectory of internalization stalls without producing the dramatic ill-being effects associated with active frustration.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-internalization-continuum-deep-dive-2026-04-21]]*
