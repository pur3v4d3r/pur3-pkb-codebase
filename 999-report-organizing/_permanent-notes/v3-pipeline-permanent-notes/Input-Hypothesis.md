# Input-Hypothesis

> [!definition] Input-Hypothesis
> - **Key-Term**: [[Input-Hypothesis]]
> - **Definition**: The Input-Hypothesis posits that learning and expertise development are facilitated by the input of new information, which is then integrated into existing knowledge structures through deliberate practice and reflection.
> - **Domain**: philosophy
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The hypothesis suggests that learners need to be exposed to a variety of inputs, such as examples, problems, or feedback, in order to build their understanding and refine their skills. This input serves as the foundation for constructing more complex cognitive architectures.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this means engaging with challenging tasks, receiving constructive feedback, and reflecting on performance to identify areas for improvement. The hypothesis emphasizes the importance of active engagement with new information rather than passive exposure.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of deliberate practice in refining skills and the integration of input into existing knowledge structures through cognitive restructuring.
