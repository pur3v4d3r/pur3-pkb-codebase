# expert-novice-research

> [!definition] expert-novice-research
> - **Key-Term**: [[expert-novice-research]]
> - **Definition**: Expert-novice-research refers to the study of how individuals develop expertise and differ from novices in their knowledge, skills, and problem-solving abilities across various domains such as chess, music, or mathematics.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> This concept is foundational in cognitive psychology and education, focusing on the differences between experts who have extensive experience and novices who are just beginning to learn a skill. Experts can process information more efficiently and make better decisions based on their deep knowledge structures, while novices rely more on surface-level features.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, expert-novice-research often involves comparing how experts and novices solve problems or perform tasks, using techniques such as think-aloud protocols, eye-tracking, and cognitive interviews to understand the underlying mental processes. This research helps in designing effective learning strategies and educational interventions.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the role of deliberate practice, the structure of knowledge (chunks), and the impact of domain-specific expertise on problem-solving.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, understanding expert-novice differences can lead to more effective teaching methods that cater to different stages of learning.

> [!example] Application
> In training programs, this research informs the design of curricula and practice sessions to help novices develop into experts more efficiently.
