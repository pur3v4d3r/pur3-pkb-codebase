# constructivism

> [!definition] constructivism
> - **Key-Term**: [[constructivism]]
> - **Definition**: Constructivism is an epistemological theory that emphasizes the active role of individuals in constructing their own knowledge through experience and reflection, rather than passively receiving information from external sources.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Constructivism posits that learners build new ideas or concepts based on their current/past understanding. This process involves actively engaging with the environment, interacting with others, and reflecting on experiences to construct personal meaning and knowledge.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, this theory is applied in educational settings where teachers facilitate learning by encouraging students to explore problems, discuss ideas, and connect new information with prior knowledge. The goal is to help learners develop a deep understanding of concepts rather than just memorizing facts.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the recognition that different individuals may construct different meanings from the same experience due to their unique backgrounds and perspectives.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, constructivism leads to student-centered learning approaches where learners are encouraged to actively participate in the learning process through inquiry-based activities.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical settings, it can inform patient education by tailoring information to individual patients' prior knowledge and experiences, enhancing their understanding of medical conditions and treatment options.
