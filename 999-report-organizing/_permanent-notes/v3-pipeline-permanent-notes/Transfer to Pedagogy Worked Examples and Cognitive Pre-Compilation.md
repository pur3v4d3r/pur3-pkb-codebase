## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Transfer to Pedagogy: Worked Examples and Cognitive Pre-Compilation
> The educational research on worked examples has documented that providing students with fully worked solutions before asking them to solve similar problems on their own produces better learning than immediately asking them to solve unworked problems, and the mechanism behind this effect bears a structural resemblance to the cognitive pre-compilation framing of implementation intentions. The worked example serves as a kind of cognitively pre-compiled solution structure that the student can subsequently apply when they encounter a structurally similar problem; the student's perceptual system learns to detect the structural cue that signals "this is a problem of type T," and the prepared solution structure is activated in response. The boundary conditions are again instructive: worked examples fail when students cannot detect the structural cue, when the prepared solution does not actually transfer to the new problem, or when the underlying motivation to engage with the problem is absent.
