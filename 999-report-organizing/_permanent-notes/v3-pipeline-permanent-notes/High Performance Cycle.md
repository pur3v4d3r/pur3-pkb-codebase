# High Performance Cycle

> [!definition] High Performance Cycle
> The integrative dynamic model proposed by Locke and Latham (1990, refined 2002) in which specific, difficult, accepted goals — supported by adequate moderators — activate the four mediating mechanisms, produce performance, generate intrinsic and extrinsic rewards proportional to that performance, and thereby reinforce commitment to subsequent goals in a self-sustaining loop.
>
> **Boundary:** The cycle's self-sustaining dynamics depend on rewards being commensurate with achievement, on attribution of success to internal causes, and on the maintenance of perceived attainability across cycles; each of these conditions can fail, and each failure breaks the cycle in a characteristic way.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The High Performance Cycle is the integrative achievement of the mature theory and the framework against which both the dark-side phenomena and the modern integrations must be understood — the dark side describes how the cycle pathologizes when its conditions fail, while the modern integrations describe how the cycle interacts with adjacent self-regulatory architectures.
>
> **See also:** [[High Performance Cycle]], [[self-efficacy]], [[Zimmerman's-Cyclical-Model-of-Self-Regulation]]
