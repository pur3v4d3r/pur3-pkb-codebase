## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Clinical Education and Medical Reasoning
> Medical education has independently converged on many of the same principles identified in this report, providing a powerful domain for far transfer analysis. Clinical reasoning depends on the construction of illness scripts — specialized schemas that organize symptom patterns, diagnostic probabilities, and treatment protocols into retrievable units that expert clinicians deploy automatically. The progression from novice medical student to expert diagnostician follows the schema construction → automation trajectory described in Section 4, with [[deliberate-practice|deliberate practice]] in clinical settings providing the encoding opportunities and [[Desirable Difficulties (Robert Bjork, 1994)|retrieval practice]] through case-based assessment providing the consolidation mechanism. Medical education's adoption of spaced repetition (Anki is ubiquitous among medical students), problem-based learning (a form of generative learning), and clinical reasoning portfolios (a domain-specific form of PKB) represents independent convergence on the cognitive strategy principles articulated here. The structural principle enabling transfer: **organized knowledge schemas reduce cognitive load in complex diagnostic environments, just as organized PKB structures reduce cognitive load in complex knowledge management environments.**
