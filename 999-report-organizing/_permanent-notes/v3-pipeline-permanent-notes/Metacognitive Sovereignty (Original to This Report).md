# Metacognitive Sovereignty (Original to This Report)

> [!definition] Metacognitive Sovereignty (Original to This Report)
> The capacity to function as one's own epistemologist — to monitor, evaluate, and regulate one's learning processes and outcomes without dependence on external assessment. Encompasses three interacting dimensions: metacognitive knowledge (knowing about one's own cognition), metacognitive monitoring (real-time awareness of comprehension and performance), and metacognitive control (strategic adjustment based on monitoring outputs).
>
> **Boundary:** Metacognitive sovereignty does not imply infallibility. It describes the capacity for honest self-assessment, including the ability to recognize the limits of one's own knowledge, not the achievement of perfect self-calibration.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Proposed as the cognitive engine that drives the entire SDL process; weakness in metacognitive sovereignty is identified as the primary reason SDL attempts fail.
>
> **See also:** [[metacognitive-monitoring-and-control]], [[metacognition]], [[Self-Assessment]]
