# Encoding Depth (Craik & Lockhart, 1972)

> [!definition] Encoding Depth (Craik & Lockhart, 1972)
> [[levels-of-processing-theory|Levels-of-processing theory]], proposed by Fergus Craik and Robert Lockhart, holds that the durability of a memory trace is a function not of how long information resides in short-term memory but of the *depth* at which it is processed during encoding. [[shallow-processing|Shallow processing]] — attending to surface features such as font, color, or phonological form — produces fragile traces. [[deep-processing|Deep processing]] — attending to meaning, generating associations, evaluating implications — produces durable ones. The critical implication for PKM is that the *activity performed during note creation* matters more than the organizational system into which the note is placed.
>
> **Boundary:** Depth of processing is not a unitary continuum but a cluster of qualitatively different operations; semantic elaboration, relational processing, and self-referential processing each contribute to trace durability through partially distinct mechanisms.
>
> **See also:** [[deep-processing-elaborative-encoding]], [[levels-of-processing-theory]], [[encoding-specificity-principle]]

## Additional Material (Merged)

> [!definition] Levels of Processing (Craik & Lockhart, 1972)
> A framework proposing that the durability of a [[long-term-memory|memory trace]] is determined not by the duration of processing but by the *depth* of processing — the degree to which encoding involves semantic analysis, elaborative association, and meaningful interpretation rather than surface-level perceptual features.
>
> **Boundary:** "Depth" is descriptive rather than precisely operationalized; the framework identifies a continuum from shallow to deep processing but does not provide a metric for measuring processing depth independently of its effects.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Establishes the foundational principle of encoding quality over encoding quantity in PKM design (Section 2). Deep processing justifies elaborative note-taking practices over copy-paste capture.
>
> **See also:** [[levels-of-processing-theory]], [[Elaborative-Processing]], [[deep-processing]]
