## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] attributional-vocabulary — Attribution as the Medium of inner-speech
> One might extend the integration further by noticing that causal attributions are typically formulated in natural language — they are sentences people say to themselves, narratives constructed through [[inner-speech]]. When a student fails and thinks "I'm just not smart enough for this," the internal sentence encodes a specific dimensional profile (internal, stable, uncontrollable) that generates predictable emotional and motivational consequences. [[inner-speech-vocabulary-as-self-generated-motivational-architecture|The vocabulary available for inner speech]] thus constrains the range of attributional options the person can generate. A learner who has been exposed to attribution retraining possesses an expanded [[attributional-vocabulary]] — they have access to alternative internal sentences ("My strategy wasn't effective," "I haven't mastered this yet," "The timing was off") that encode different dimensional profiles and therefore produce different motivational outcomes. This suggests that one function of motivational education is literally linguistic: expanding the causal vocabulary available for self-explanation, thereby expanding the dimensional space within which the attributional mechanism can operate. The richer the vocabulary, the more dimensionally varied the attributions, and the more flexible the motivational response.
