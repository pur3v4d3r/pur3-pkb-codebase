# Extended Mind Thesis (Clark & Chalmers, 1998)

> [!definition] Extended Mind Thesis (Clark & Chalmers, 1998)
> The [[Extended Mind Thesis (Clark & Chalmers, 1998)|extended mind thesis]] proposes that cognitive processes can extend beyond the boundaries of the biological brain into the external environment when external resources are reliably available, habitually used, and their outputs are automatically endorsed. Under the parity principle, if an external process performs a function that would count as cognitive if performed internally, it should be considered a genuine component of the cognitive system.
>
> **Boundary:** The thesis is philosophically contested; critics argue that mere functional equivalence does not establish cognitive constitution, and that the distinction between cognitive processing and cognitive tools remains meaningful.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Provides philosophical grounding for the claim that a well-designed PKB constitutes a genuine cognitive extension rather than merely a tool (Section 7).
>
> **See also:** [[andy-clark]], [[Cognitive Offloading]], [[the-parity-principle]], [[distributed-cognition]]

## Additional Material (Merged)

> [!definition] Extended Mind Thesis
> A philosophical position, advanced by Clark and Chalmers (1998), holding that cognitive processes can extend beyond the biological brain to include external artefacts when those artefacts are reliably available, regularly consulted, and functionally integrated into cognitive workflows. The thesis challenges the assumption that cognition is skull-bound.
>
> **Boundary:** The extended mind thesis is a philosophical framework, not an empirical finding. It remains contested in philosophy of mind. Its application in this report is as a *justificatory framework* for treating PKB scaffolds as constitutive (not merely supportive) components of metacognitive architecture.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Provides the philosophical foundation for the report's central thesis that PKBs are not merely tools that *assist* metacognition but can become *part of* the metacognitive system.
>
> **See also:** [[externalized-metacognition]], [[extended-metacognition]], [[Cognitive Offloading]]
