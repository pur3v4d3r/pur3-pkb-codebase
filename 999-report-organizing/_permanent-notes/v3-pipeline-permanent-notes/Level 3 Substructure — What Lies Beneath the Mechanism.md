## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Level 3: Substructure — What Lies Beneath the Mechanism
> **Zoom progression:** Level 2 revealed the architecture of regulatory styles but left open the question of what makes the architecture dynamic — what allows a behavior to move from external to introjected, from introjected to identified, from identified to integrated. This level reveals the substrate: the three basic psychological needs whose satisfaction or frustration constitutes the conditions under which internalization either proceeds or stalls.
> **What you'll see at this level:** The substantive theory of [[autonomy-need|autonomy]], [[competence-need|competence]], and [[relatedness-need|relatedness]] as basic psychological needs; why these three rather than others; and how each contributes specifically to the work of internalization.
> **Specialist value:** The basic needs construct is often presented as a list — autonomy, competence, relatedness — without articulating why those three constitute a complete and minimal set, or how each does specifically the work that internalization requires. The complete answer requires opening the substrate carefully.
