# mastery-goal-orientation

> [!definition] mastery-goal-orientation
> - **Key-Term**: [[mastery-goal-orientation]]
> - **Definition**: Mastery-goal-orientation refers to an individual's focus on learning and improving their skills, rather than outperforming others. It is characterized by a desire to understand the material deeply and develop competence in a given domain.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Mastery-goal-orientation is rooted in achievement goal theory, which posits that individuals have different orientations towards goals related to performance or learning. Those with a mastery orientation are more concerned with mastering tasks and improving their abilities over time, rather than simply achieving high grades or outperforming others.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Individuals with a mastery goal orientation tend to engage in deep processing strategies such as elaboration, organization, and self-explanation, which help them understand the material better. They also persist through challenges and view feedback as an opportunity for growth, leading to more effective learning outcomes.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> This orientation can be seen across various domains including education, sports, and professional development.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, fostering a mastery goal orientation can lead to deeper learning and better retention of information. Teachers can promote this by providing formative assessments and encouraging students to focus on understanding rather than just getting the right answer.

> [!example] Application
> In sports training, coaches who emphasize skill development over winning can help athletes build resilience and improve their performance in the long term.
