# Scaffolded Fading

> [!definition] Scaffolded Fading
> The deliberate, progressive withdrawal of external scaffolding support as the learner develops internal competence in the scaffolded function. Derived from Vygotsky's zone of proximal development, fading follows a trajectory from full support through partial support to independent performance.
>
> **Boundary:** Fading is not abrupt removal. It is gradual, monitored, and reversible — if performance degrades upon fading, the scaffold is temporarily reinstated. Fading describes a *process*, not an *event*.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Scaffolded fading resolves the scaffold paradox — the tension between short-term scaffolding benefit and long-term developmental risk — and provides the mechanism for the Developmental Arc Model's stage transitions.
>
> **See also:** [[Scaffolded Fading]], [[scaffold-dependence]], [[zone-of-proximal-development]]

## Additional Material (Merged)

> [!definition] Scaffolding-Fading Progression (Vygotsky/Bruner/Wood)
> The deliberate, contingent process of providing structured support within the learner's [[zone-of-proximal-development|zone of proximal development]] and then systematically withdrawing that support as the learner develops the internal capacity to perform independently. The mechanism operates through the transfer of regulatory function from external scaffolding to internal self-regulation.
>
> **Boundary:** Scaffolding-fading is not a gradual reduction of difficulty but a reduction of external support in the face of increasing challenge. The expertise reversal effect demonstrates that maintaining scaffolding beyond the point of necessity actively impedes learning.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The primary operational mechanism through which curriculum structure transforms dependent learners into self-directed ones.
>
> **See also:** [[Scaffolded Fading]], [[zone-of-proximal-development]], [[Scaffolded Fading]], [[guidance-fading-principle]], [[Expertise Reversal Effect (Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler, Sweller, 2003)]]
