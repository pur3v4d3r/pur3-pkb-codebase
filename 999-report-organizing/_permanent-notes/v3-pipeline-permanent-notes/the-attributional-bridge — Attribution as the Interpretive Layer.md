## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] the-attributional-bridge — Attribution as the Interpretive Layer
> One can now see attribution theory's distinctive contribution to the motivational landscape with considerable clarity: it provides the *interpretive layer* through which the constructs of other motivational theories acquire their specific psychological content. [[self-efficacy]] is not built from raw mastery experiences; it is built from mastery experiences *as interpreted through causal attribution* — and the same experience can strengthen or weaken self-efficacy depending on whether success is attributed to internal or external causes, and whether failure is attributed to stable or unstable ones. Similarly, a [[growth-mindset]] does not directly produce motivation; it produces motivation *through the attributional patterns it generates* — specifically, by promoting unstable, controllable attributions for difficulty, which preserve expectancy and generate productive emotions. [[self-determination-theory]]'s concept of autonomy does not exist independently of how a person attributes the causes of their behaviour — autonomy is experienced when behaviour is attributed to internal, volitional causes. In each case, attribution theory names the *mechanism* through which the other theory's constructs operate on the person's lived motivational experience. It is, in this sense, the cognitive plumbing of the motivational house.
