# Elaborative Encoding (Craik & Tulving, 1975; Pressley et al., 1987)

> [!definition] Elaborative Encoding (Craik & Tulving, 1975; Pressley et al., 1987)
> [[elaboration|Elaborative encoding]] is the process of enriching incoming information with additional meaning, connections, and contextual associations during initial processing, producing memory traces that are more distinctive, more connected to existing knowledge, and more retrievable than those produced by surface-level processing. Elaboration operates through the creation of multiple retrieval paths and the integration of new information with established schemas.
>
> **Boundary:** Elaboration must be semantically relevant to the material to enhance retention. Irrelevant elaboration (e.g., attention to superficial features) does not produce the same mnemonic benefit.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** Grounds the claim that wiki-linking constitutes an elaborative encoding event (Section 2) — each link creation requires the identification of meaningful relationships, an act of semantic elaboration.
>
> **See also:** [[levels-of-processing-theory]], [[encoding-specificity-principle]], [[semantic-memory]], [[wiki-links]]
