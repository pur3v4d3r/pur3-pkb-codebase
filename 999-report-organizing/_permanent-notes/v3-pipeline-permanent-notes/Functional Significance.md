# Functional Significance

> [!definition] Functional Significance
> The interpretive register through which a particular event in the social environment is processed by the person experiencing it. SDT distinguishes three functional significances: **informational** (the event provides information that supports competence and choice), **controlling** (the event communicates pressure to think, feel, or behave in particular ways), and **[[amotivation|amotivating]]** (the event communicates that effective action is impossible). The same objective event can take on different functional significance for different people, in different contexts, or for the same person at different moments — and the functional significance, not the objective features of the event, determines its effect on need satisfaction and motivational regulation.

> [!evidence] Functional Significance
> The interpretive frame through which the person experiences a contextual event, classified in SDT as informational (supports autonomy and competence), controlling (pressures behavior), or amotivating (signals incompetence or helplessness). A single objective event can carry different functional significance for different people or under different conditions, and the regulatory consequences flow from the functional significance experienced rather than from the objective properties of the event.
