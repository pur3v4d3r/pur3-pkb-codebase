## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] Retraining as Dimensional Recalibration, Not Belief Replacement
> A subtle but important distinction separates effective attribution retraining from mere positive thinking. The intervention does not ask the person to believe something false ("You are actually very talented") or to deny the reality of failure ("That didn't really happen"). It asks the person to reclassify the *cause* of a real failure along different dimensions — to shift from a stable, uncontrollable attribution to an unstable, controllable one. This is a recalibration of the interpretive mechanism, not a replacement of one belief with another. The distinction matters because recalibration respects the person's intelligence and autonomy in a way that belief replacement does not, and because recalibrated attributions are more robust (they are continuously regenerated by the mechanism) while replaced beliefs are fragile (they require ongoing external reinforcement).
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
