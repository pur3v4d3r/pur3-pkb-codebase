# introduction-to-metacognition

> [!definition] introduction-to-metacognition
> - **Key-Term**: [[introduction-to-metacognition]]
> - **Definition**: Metacognition refers to the ability to think about and reflect on one's own cognitive processes, including planning, monitoring, and evaluating these processes during learning and problem-solving activities.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Metacognition involves self-awareness of one's thought processes and strategies used in various tasks. It includes recognizing when help is needed, setting goals for learning, and assessing the effectiveness of different approaches to solving problems.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, metacognitive skills are applied through techniques such as planning a study session, monitoring understanding during reading, and evaluating the success of one's approach after completing a task. These skills can be developed and improved with training and experience.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between procedural and declarative knowledge in metacognition, where procedural knowledge involves knowing how to do something, while declarative knowledge is about knowing what or why.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering metacognitive skills can lead to better academic performance by helping students become more effective learners.

> [!example] Application
> In clinical decision-making, metacognition aids in the diagnostic process by enabling healthcare professionals to monitor their own reasoning and avoid cognitive biases.
