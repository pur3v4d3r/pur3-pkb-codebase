## Open Threads

> [!tension-identified] Strategy Instruction vs. Knowledge Building as the Path to Skilled Reading
> *Position A:* Reading skill is built primarily through *strategy instruction* — direct teaching of monitoring and regulatory strategies, with extended practice that builds the metacognitive architecture. Strongest advocates: most reading comprehension intervention researchers; the strategic-reading tradition descended from Pressley.
> *Position B:* Reading skill is built primarily through *knowledge building* — accumulation of domain knowledge that gives the reader the schemas needed to integrate text content. Strongest advocates: E.D. Hirsch and the knowledge-rich curriculum tradition; researchers showing that strategy instruction yields modest effects while domain knowledge produces large ones.
> *Current state of evidence:* Both contribute substantially, and the question is increasingly framed as a false dichotomy. Strategy instruction without knowledge to apply strategies to produces formalism without comprehension; knowledge without strategies for monitoring its application produces fluent recognition without integration.
> *Why it matters:* The tension shapes curriculum decisions and teacher education priorities.
> *This report's stance:* Both are necessary; the architecture this report describes operates as a frame within which knowledge and strategies must both be built.
> *— [[metacognition-and-reading-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*
