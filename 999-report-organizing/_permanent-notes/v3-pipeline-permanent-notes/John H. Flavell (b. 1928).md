## Additional Material

> [!person] John H. Flavell (b. 1928)
> [[john-h.-flavell|John Flavell]], Professor Emeritus at Stanford University, is the originator of the term "metacognition" and the developer of the first comprehensive taxonomy of metacognitive phenomena. His 1979 framework — distinguishing metacognitive knowledge, metacognitive experience, goals/tasks, and actions/strategies — established the conceptual landscape that Nelson and Narens later formalized.
>
> **Core contribution:** Coined "metacognition"; developed the tripartite taxonomy of metacognitive knowledge (person, task, strategy variables); established developmental metacognition research.
> **Relationship to other figures:** Provided the taxonomic foundation that Nelson and Narens formalized architecturally; influenced the entire field of metacognition research.
> **Key works:** Flavell (1976, 1979).
> *— [[nelson-narens-metacognitive-control-framework-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!person] John H. Flavell (b. 1928)
> Stanford University. Originator of the concept of [[metacognition|metacognition]] (1976, 1979). His framework distinguishing metacognitive knowledge, monitoring, and experience provides the foundational taxonomy for Section 5's analysis of the regulation problem.
>
> **Key work in this report:** Flavell, J. H. (1979). Metacognition and cognitive monitoring.
> *— [[pkb-pkm-planning-and-cognitive-science-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!definition] Alan Baddeley (1934–)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] Alan Baddeley (1934–)
> Professor of Psychology, University of York, UK. Developer of the multi-component model of [[working-memory]] that provides CLT's architectural foundation. Baddeley's model — phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, central executive, episodic buffer — established the capacity constraints that CLT's prescriptions are designed to manage.
>
> **Core contributions:** Working memory model, capacity limitations, dual-channel architecture.
>
> **Relationship:** Foundational influence; CLT presupposes Baddeley's architectural claims while not committing to all specific model components.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!definition] Asher Koriat (b. 1942)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] Asher Koriat (b. 1942)
> [[Asher-Koriat|Asher Koriat]], Professor at the University of Haifa, developed the cue-utilization framework that provided the mechanistic detail underlying Nelson and Narens' monitoring channel. His accessibility model of FOK judgments and his taxonomy of monitoring cues (intrinsic, extrinsic, mnemonic) transformed the study of metacognitive monitoring from a descriptive enterprise into a process-level investigation.
>
> **Core contribution:** Developed the cue-utilization framework; proposed the accessibility model of FOK; identified intrinsic/extrinsic/mnemonic cue classes.
> **Relationship to other figures:** Extended and mechanized the monitoring channel that Nelson and Narens had specified architecturally; influenced Dunlosky, Metcalfe, and subsequent calibration researchers.
> **Key works:** Koriat (1993, 1997, 2007).
> *— [[nelson-narens-metacognitive-control-framework-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!definition] Edward L. Deci (b. 1942)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!evidence] Edward L. Deci (b. 1942)
> Co-founder of self-determination theory. Foundational empirical work in the early 1970s on the undermining effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation; co-developer with Richard Ryan of cognitive evaluation theory and the broader SDT framework. Co-author of *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior* (1985), the founding book-length treatment of the framework. Continued programmatic work into the 2010s and remains a central reference figure in the field.
> *— [[self-determination-theory-internalization-continuum-deep-dive-2026-04-21]]*

> [!definition] Fergus I. M. Craik (b. 1935)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] Fergus I. M. Craik (b. 1935)
> University of Toronto. Co-originator (with Robert Lockhart) of the [[levels-of-processing-theory|levels-of-processing]] framework (1972), establishing that encoding depth — not duration or repetition — determines memory durability. Extended by subsequent work on distinctiveness, elaboration, and transfer-appropriate processing. Foundational for Section 2's encoding analysis.
>
> **Key work in this report:** Craik, F. I. M., & Lockhart, R. S. (1972). Levels of processing: A framework for memory research.
> *— [[pkb-pkm-planning-and-cognitive-science-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!definition] Fred Paas (b. 1963)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] Fred Paas (b. 1963)
> *Erasmus University Rotterdam, Netherlands*
>
> **Core contribution:** Advanced the measurement methodology of cognitive load theory through dual-task methods, subjective rating scales, and physiological indicators. Co-developed with van Merriënboer the efficiency metrics that enable empirical evaluation of instructional designs.
>
> **Relationships:** Collaborated with both Sweller and van Merriënboer, bridging theoretical development and empirical methodology. His measurement innovations enabled the evidence base for 4C/ID effectiveness reviewed in Section 7.
>
> **Key works:** "Instructional Control of Cognitive Load" (1992 with van Merriënboer); "Cognitive Load Measurement" (2003)
> *— [[four-component-instructional-design-model-4cid-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!definition] John H. Flavell (1928–)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] John H. Flavell (1928–)
> Stanford University. Pioneer of metacognition research. Developed the foundational tripartite taxonomy of [[Metacognitive-Knowledge-—-Flavell's-Tripartite-Taxonomy|metacognitive knowledge]] (person, task, strategy knowledge) that provides the conceptual vocabulary used throughout this report. His framework establishes what metacognitive scaffolds must target. Key work: "Metacognition and Cognitive Monitoring" (1979).
> **Relationship to other figures:** Flavell's descriptive taxonomy was operationalised into an intervention framework by Zimmerman; Nelson and Narens provided the process model (monitoring-control) that Flavell's categories describe the content of.
> *— [[pkb-metacognitive-scaffolding-for-study-and-planning-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!definition] John Sweller (1946–)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] John Sweller (1946–)
> Professor Emeritus of Educational Psychology, University of New South Wales, Australia. Originator of Cognitive Load Theory (1988). Sweller's foundational contribution was recognizing that the information-processing constraints identified by cognitive psychology had direct and systematic implications for instructional design. His subsequent work elaborated the tripartite load taxonomy, identified the major CLT effects, and led the 2010 reconceptualization of germane load.
>
> **Core contributions to this report:** CLT's founding framework, element interactivity construct, effect principles, germane load reconceptualization.
>
> **Key works referenced:** Sweller (1988), Sweller & Chandler (1991, 1992), Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas (1998), Sweller (2010).
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!definition] John Sweller (b. 1946)
> *Definition pending — derived from 2 source report(s).*

> [!person] John Sweller (b. 1946)
> *University of New South Wales, Australia*
>
> **Core contribution:** Developed [[Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)|cognitive load theory]] (CLT), the primary theoretical foundation upon which the 4C/ID model rests. Established the empirical basis for [[Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)|intrinsic]], [[Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)|extraneous]], and [[Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)|germane]] cognitive load categories, the [[the-worked-example-effect|worked example effect]], the [[split-attention-effect|split-attention effect]], the [[redundancy-effect|redundancy effect]], the [[Modality Effect|modality effect]], and the [[Expertise Reversal Effect (Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler, Sweller, 2003)|expertise reversal effect]].
>
> **Relationships:** Sweller's CLT provides the cognitive-architectural constraints that the 4C/ID model translates into design prescriptions. Van Merriënboer's model is, in significant part, an application of Sweller's theory to the specific problem of complex learning.
>
> **Key works:** "Cognitive Load During Problem Solving" (1988); *Cognitive Load Theory* (2011)
> *— [[four-component-instructional-design-model-4cid-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!person] John Sweller (b. 1946)
> University of New South Wales. Developer of [[Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)|cognitive load theory]], which integrates working memory limitations with instructional design. His three-load taxonomy (intrinsic, extraneous, germane) provides the design constraint framework used throughout this report, particularly in Section 7.
>
> **Key work in this report:** Sweller, J. (1988). Cognitive load during problem solving: Effects on learning; Sweller, J., Ayres, P., & Kalyuga, S. (2011). *Cognitive Load Theory*.
> *— [[pkb-pkm-planning-and-cognitive-science-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!definition] Joseph T. Hart (1936–)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] Joseph T. Hart (1936–)
> Joseph Hart conducted the first systematic experimental investigation of the feeling of knowing in 1965, demonstrating that FOK judgments have above-chance predictive validity for subsequent recognition performance. His work established the empirical phenomenon that would later become central to the Nelson-Narens framework's analysis of monitoring.
>
> **Core contribution:** First experimental demonstration of FOK validity.
> **Relationship to other figures:** Provided the empirical foundation that Nelson and Narens formalized within the two-level architecture.
> **Key works:** Hart (1965, 1967).
> *— [[nelson-narens-metacognitive-control-framework-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!definition] Louis Narens (b. 1941)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] Louis Narens (b. 1941)
> [[louis-narens|Louis Narens]], Professor of Cognitive Sciences at UC Irvine, brought the formal mathematical background that allowed the meta-level/object-level distinction to be stated with theoretical precision. His contributions to measurement theory and formal modeling provided the conceptual tools that elevated the framework from a descriptive taxonomy to a formally specified architecture.
>
> **Core contribution:** Provided the formal foundations for the two-level architecture; ensured the framework's mathematical rigor.
> **Relationship to other figures:** Collaborated with Thomas Nelson on the formal framework.
> **Key works:** Nelson & Narens (1990, 1994).
> *— [[nelson-narens-metacognitive-control-framework-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*

> [!definition] Paul Kirschner (b. 1951)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] Paul Kirschner (b. 1951)
> *Open University of the Netherlands*
>
> **Core contribution:** Co-authored with van Merriënboer the practical implementation framework *Ten Steps to Complex Learning*, translating 4C/ID theory into systematic design procedures. Advocated for evidence-based instructional design against minimally-guided discovery approaches.
>
> **Relationships:** Primary collaborator with van Merriënboer on the design methodology codification. Co-authored the influential "Why Minimal Guidance During Instruction Does Not Work" (2006 with Sweller and Clark), positioning 4C/ID within the broader debate on instructional guidance.
>
> **Key works:** *Ten Steps to Complex Learning* (2007, 2018 with van Merriënboer); "Why Minimal Guidance Does Not Work" (2006)
> *— [[four-component-instructional-design-model-4cid-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!definition] Peter M. Gollwitzer (b. 1950, NYU / University of Konstanz)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] Peter M. Gollwitzer (b. 1950, NYU / University of Konstanz)
> The principal architect of implementation-intention theory, whose 1993 chapter in the *European Review of Social Psychology* and 1999 *American Psychologist* article established the construct in its mature form. Gollwitzer's collaboration with Heinz Heckhausen on the action phase model provided the theoretical home from which implementation intentions were derived. Gollwitzer's subsequent collaborations with Paschal Sheeran (meta-analytic synthesis), Gabriele Oettingen (MCII development), and others extended the construct across health, academic, and clinical domains.
> *— [[implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-foundational-report-2026-04-20]]*

> [!definition] Richard Mayer (1947–)
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] Richard Mayer (1947–)
> Professor of Psychology, University of California, Santa Barbara. Developer of the [[cognitive-theory-of-multimedia-learning]] and its associated design principles. Mayer's framework shares CLT's architectural foundations while emphasizing active processing over load management.
>
> **Core contributions:** Multimedia learning principles, dual-channel processing model, evidence-based multimedia design.
>
> **Relationship:** Parallel development with Sweller; convergent predictions from shared architecture; subtle differences in analytical emphasis.
> *— [[cognitive-load-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*

> [!definition] Robert A. Bjork (b. 1939) & Elizabeth L. Bjork
> *Definition pending — derived from 1 source report(s).*

> [!person] Robert A. Bjork (b. 1939) & Elizabeth L. Bjork
> UCLA. Developers of the [[Desirable Difficulties (Robert Bjork, 1994)|desirable difficulties]] framework and the new theory of disuse (storage strength vs. retrieval strength). Their work grounds the retrieval analysis in Section 4 and the productive friction concept in PKM design.
>
> **Key work in this report:** Bjork, R. A., & Bjork, E. L. (2011). Making things hard on yourself, but in a good way.
> *— [[pkb-pkm-planning-and-cognitive-science-foundational-report-2026-04-18]]*
