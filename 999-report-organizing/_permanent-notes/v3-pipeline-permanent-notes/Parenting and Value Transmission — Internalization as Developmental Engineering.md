## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Parenting and Value Transmission — Internalization as Developmental Engineering
> The OIT framework (Section 4) describes the process by which externally regulated behaviors become internally endorsed. Parenting, viewed through this lens, is fundamentally an exercise in internalization engineering — the deliberate creation of conditions under which the values, practices, and regulatory norms of the family and culture are transmitted to the child not through coercion or conditional regard but through the need-supportive conditions that facilitate genuine internalization. The structural principle is that durable value transmission requires the same conditions as effective teaching: rationale (why this value matters), perspective-taking (acknowledging the child's experience of the regulation), and choice within boundaries (allowing the child to enact the value in personally meaningful ways). The boundary condition is developmental: younger children require more external structure and scaffolding, and the balance between structure and autonomy support must shift as the child develops the cognitive and emotional resources for more self-directed regulation.
