## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Important Nuance: Behavior Maintenance Is Not Internalization
> Casual descriptions often treat internalization as synonymous with the disappearance of external prompting and the appearance of self-maintained behavior. At surface level this conflation is harmless because both phenomena typically co-occur. At specialist level the distinction is critical: behavior can be maintained without external prompting through entirely controlling internal mechanisms (introjection, ego-involvement, contingent self-worth) that are functionally similar to external regulation in everything except the location of the controlling agent. The transformation that defines true internalization is qualitative and concerns the person's relationship to the behavior, not just whether they continue performing it.
>
> **When the distinction matters:** Always at the specialist level. Predictions about persistence under stress, affective accompaniment, and behavioral spillover all depend on which kind of "self-maintained" regulation is actually operating.
>
> **When it doesn't:** In casual conversation about whether a person has "made the behavior their own," the surface conflation is usually acceptable.
