# Cognitive-Revolution

> [!definition] Cognitive-Revolution
> - **Key-Term**: [[Cognitive-Revolution]]
> - **Definition**: The Cognitive-Revolution refers to the shift from behaviorist theories of learning and cognition to cognitive theories that emphasize mental processes such as problem-solving, reasoning, and memory in the mid-20th century.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> During the Cognitive-Revolution, researchers began to focus on internal mental processes rather than just observable behaviors. This shift was driven by new methodologies like introspection and cognitive modeling.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Key figures such as Jean Piaget and Noam Chomsky contributed significantly to this movement through their work on child development and language acquisition respectively.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The revolution also introduced concepts like schema, working memory, and mental representations which have become fundamental in understanding human cognition.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, the Cognitive-Revolution led to a greater emphasis on teaching strategies that promote active learning and problem-solving skills.

> [!example] Application
> In artificial intelligence, it inspired the development of computational models of human thought processes.
