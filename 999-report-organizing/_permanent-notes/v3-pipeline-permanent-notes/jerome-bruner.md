# jerome-bruner

> [!definition] jerome-bruner
> - **Key-Term**: [[jerome-bruner]]
> - **Definition**: Jerome Bruner is an American psychologist and cognitive theorist known for his work on the process of human cognition, learning, and development, particularly emphasizing the role of scaffolding in education and the importance of discovery learning.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Bruner's theory posits that individuals learn best when they are actively engaged in the learning process. He introduced the concept of 'scaffolding,' where a teacher or more knowledgeable other provides support to help learners reach their goals, gradually reducing this support as learners become more competent.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> In practice, Bruner advocated for discovery learning, encouraging students to explore and discover knowledge on their own rather than simply receiving information from an instructor. This approach is particularly effective in subjects like mathematics and science where problem-solving skills are crucial.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the idea that learning should be meaningful and connected to real-world applications, and that learners need to construct their own understanding through active engagement.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, Bruner's theories have led to the development of methods such as inquiry-based learning and project-based learning, which emphasize student-centered approaches.

> [!example] Application
> His work has influenced assessment practices by promoting formative assessments that focus on ongoing feedback and support rather than just final grades.

## Open Threads
