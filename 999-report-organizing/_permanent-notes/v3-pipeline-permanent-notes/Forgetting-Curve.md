# Forgetting-Curve

> [!definition] Forgetting-Curve
> - **Key-Term**: [[Forgetting-Curve]]
> - **Definition**: The Forgetting-Curve is the phenomenon where information learned over time tends to be forgotten unless it is actively reviewed or practiced, often following an exponential decay pattern.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The Forgetting-Curve was first described by Hermann Ebbinghaus in the late 19th century through his experiments with memory. He found that people tend to forget information at a high rate immediately after learning it and then at a decreasing rate over time, unless they actively review or practice the material.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This curve is often visualized as an exponential decay function, where the rate of forgetting slows down over time but never completely stops. The curve highlights the importance of spaced repetition in education and training to maintain long-term memory retention.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> There are several sub-variants of the Forgetting-Curve, such as the spacing effect, which suggests that learning is more effective when study sessions are spread out over time rather than crammed into a single session.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education and training, implementing spaced repetition techniques can help learners retain information longer by reviewing material at increasing intervals.

> [!example] Application
> For clinical education and medical reasoning, understanding the Forgetting-Curve helps in designing effective study schedules for medical students to ensure they remember critical information over long periods.
