# Locus of Causality (Heider, 1958; Weiner, 1972)

> [!definition] Locus of Causality (Heider, 1958; Weiner, 1972)
> The first dimension of the causal taxonomy, classifying causes as internal to the person (ability, effort, mood) or external (task difficulty, luck, other people). Primary determinant of self-referent affects: internal attributions for success generate pride; internal attributions for failure generate shame.
>
> **Boundary:** Distinct from locus of control (Rotter, 1966), which is a generalised dispositional belief rather than a situation-specific causal classification.
>
> **See also:** [[Locus of Causality (Heider, 1958; Weiner, 1972)]], [[locus-of-control]], [[perceived-locus-of-causality]]

## Additional Material (Merged)

> [!definition] The Locus of Causality Dimension (Weiner, 1972; cf. Heider, 1958)
> The [[Locus of Causality (Heider, 1958; Weiner, 1972)]] dimension classifies causes as either internal to the person (ability, effort, mood, personality) or external to the person (task difficulty, luck, other people's actions, institutional factors). This dimension is the direct descendant of [[fritz-heider]]'s original internal/external distinction, though Weiner refined it by distinguishing locus of causality from locus of control — a distinction that matters because a cause can be internal (it resides within me) without being controllable (I cannot change it).
>
> **Boundary:** Locus of causality should not be confused with [[locus-of-control]] (Rotter, 1966), which concerns generalised beliefs about whether outcomes are determined by one's own actions. Locus of causality is situation-specific and causal; locus of control is dispositional and expectancy-based.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The locus dimension is the primary determinant of pride and shame: internal attributions for success produce pride, internal attributions for failure produce shame.
>
