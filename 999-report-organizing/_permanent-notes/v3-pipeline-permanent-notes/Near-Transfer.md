# near-transfer

> [!definition] near-transfer
> - **Key-Term**: [[near-transfer]]
> - **Definition**: Near-transfer refers to the application of learned skills, knowledge, or strategies from one context to another that is closely related and shares some common elements or principles.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Near-transfer occurs when previously acquired skills are applied in a similar but not identical situation. For example, if someone learns how to solve algebraic equations, they can apply this knowledge to solve more complex mathematical problems.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The mechanism behind near-transfer involves the activation of relevant prior knowledge and the ability to adapt it to new situations that share some common features with the original learning context.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the degree of similarity between the source and target tasks. The closer the resemblance, the higher the likelihood of successful transfer.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, near-transfer can enhance learning efficiency by leveraging existing knowledge to tackle new but related problems.

> [!example] Application
> In professional development, near-transfer allows individuals to apply their skills in different job roles or industries that share common tasks and processes.
