# SDT-Internalization-Continuum

> [!definition] SDT-Internalization-Continuum
> - **Key-Term**: [[SDT-Internalization-Continuum]]
> - **Definition**: The SDT-Internalization-Continuum refers to the process by which external motivations become more internally driven, as described within Self-Determination Theory (SDT). This continuum ranges from amotivation and external regulation to intrinsic motivation through various stages of internalization.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The SDT-Internalization-Continuum is a fundamental concept in Self-Determination Theory that explains how individuals can transform externally imposed motivations into personally embraced ones. It starts with behaviors driven by external rewards or pressures and progresses towards more autonomous forms of motivation, such as identified regulation (where one sees the value in an activity) and ultimately intrinsic motivation (where the behavior is engaged for its own sake).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This process involves several stages: amotivation (lack of motivation), external regulation (motivated by external rewards or punishments), introjected regulation (motivated by internalized rules or norms), identified regulation (motivated by personal values and beliefs), and finally, integrated regulation (where the behavior is fully aligned with one's self-concept).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> The continuum highlights that different types of motivation can coexist at various points in an individual’s development. For instance, a student might initially be motivated by grades but eventually come to value learning for its own sake.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, understanding the SDT-Internalization-Continuum helps educators design interventions that promote intrinsic motivation and long-term engagement in activities.

> [!example] Application
> In organizational contexts, managers can use this theory to foster a supportive environment that encourages employees to internalize company goals as their own personal objectives.
