## Methodology & Sources

> [!methodology-and-sources] Methodological Transparency
> **Traditions and disciplines synthesized:** This report draws primarily on motivation science, personality psychology, developmental psychology, humanistic/organismic psychology, educational psychology, health psychology, organizational behavior, and cross-cultural psychology. It synthesizes empirical research (experimental, longitudinal, cross-cultural), meta-analytic evidence, and theoretical exposition across SDT's 50+ year development.
>
> **Claim Type Taxonomy:**
>
> | Claim Type | Epistemic Status | Example |
> |------------|-----------------|---------|
> | SDT framework descriptions (need definitions, mini-theory structure) | Established (codified in Deci & Ryan, 1985, 2017) | "SDT identifies three basic psychological needs" |
> | Empirical findings (undermining effect, cross-cultural studies) | Established (peer-reviewed, meta-analyzed) | "Tangible expected rewards reduce intrinsic motivation" |
> | Cross-framework comparisons (SDT vs. AGT, SDT vs. flow theory) | Well-motivated (interpretive integration) | "Flow can be understood through SDT's competence + autonomy framework" |
> | Dynamic equilibrium reconceptualization | Speculative (original to this report) | "Need satisfaction is better modeled as dynamic equilibrium than static nutrient level" |
> | Motivational hydraulics model | Speculative (original to this report) | "Need frustration produces defensive compensatory dynamics" |
> | Far transfer applications (game design, PKM) | Well-motivated (structural analogy) | "PKB design maps onto SDT's autonomy-competence-relatedness framework" |
>
> **Established findings vs. original contributions:** The vast majority of this report describes, analyzes, and synthesizes established SDT research. Two contributions are original to this report: the "motivational hydraulics" metaphor for need frustration dynamics (Section 7) and the "dynamic equilibrium" reconceptualization of need satisfaction as temporally fluctuating (Section 9). These are clearly marked with `[!original-synthesis]` callouts and should be understood as interpretive proposals, not empirical findings.
>
> **Limitations:** This report is based on Claude's training data and does not incorporate literature published after the knowledge cutoff. The analysis privileges English-language publications, which introduces a potential Western-centric bias in a theory where cultural generalizability is a central question. Self-report dominance in SDT's empirical base (discussed in Section 9) should be kept in mind when evaluating claims about cross-cultural invariance.
