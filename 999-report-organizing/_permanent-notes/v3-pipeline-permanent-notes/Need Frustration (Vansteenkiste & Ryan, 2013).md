# Need Frustration (Vansteenkiste & Ryan, 2013)

> [!definition] Need Frustration (Vansteenkiste & Ryan, 2013)
> The active obstruction of basic psychological needs — distinct from need deprivation (mere absence of support). Autonomy frustration occurs through coercion, surveillance, and the removal of choice; competence frustration through undermining, excessive difficulty, and negative feedback; relatedness frustration through rejection, exclusion, and conditional regard.
>
> **Boundary:** Need frustration is not the opposite end of a need satisfaction continuum; satisfaction and frustration are partially independent dimensions with distinct antecedents and consequences. The absence of autonomy support (deprivation) produces motivational flatness; the presence of control (frustration) produces defensive compensation.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The satisfaction-frustration distinction is critical for the dark-side account (Section 7) — need frustration produces not merely the absence of growth but the active presence of defensive and potentially pathogenic processes.
>
> **See also:** [[Need Frustration]], [[need-thwarting]], [[need-satisfaction-vs.-need-frustration]]
