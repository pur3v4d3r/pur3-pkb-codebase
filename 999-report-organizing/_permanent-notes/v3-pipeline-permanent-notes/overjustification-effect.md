# overjustification-effect

> [!definition] overjustification-effect
> - **Key-Term**: [[overjustification-effect]]
> - **Definition**: The overjustification effect occurs when an external reward, such as money or praise, diminishes a person's intrinsic motivation to perform a task that they previously enjoyed doing for its own sake.
> - **Domain**: other
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> The core mechanism of the overjustification effect is rooted in self-determination theory (SDT), which posits that people have innate psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness. When these needs are satisfied through intrinsic motivation, individuals experience a sense of enjoyment and fulfillment from their activities.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> However, when external rewards are introduced to encourage behavior that was previously driven by internal motivations, the individual may start to rely on these rewards as a reason for engaging in the activity. This can lead to a decrease in intrinsic motivation, as the person no longer finds the task enjoyable or personally fulfilling without the external reward.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the fact that not all external rewards have the same effect; some forms of recognition and social validation may be less likely to diminish intrinsic motivation compared to tangible rewards like money.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In educational settings, providing students with grades or monetary incentives for completing tasks can reduce their interest in learning if these extrinsic motivators become the primary reason for engaging in academic activities.

> [!example] Application
> In workplace environments, offering bonuses or promotions as a means to increase productivity might initially boost performance but could eventually lead to decreased job satisfaction and intrinsic motivation among employees.
