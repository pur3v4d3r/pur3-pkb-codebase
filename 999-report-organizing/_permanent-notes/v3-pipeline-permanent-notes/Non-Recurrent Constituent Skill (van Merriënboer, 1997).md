# Non-Recurrent Constituent Skill (van Merriënboer, 1997)

> [!definition] Non-Recurrent Constituent Skill (van Merriënboer, 1997)
> A constituent skill component of a complex cognitive skill whose execution varies across problem situations. Non-recurrent skills require reasoning, problem-solving, or decision-making that adapts to contextual features. They are governed by schemas (cognitive frameworks) rather than by automated rules, and their performance improves through schema construction (elaboration, induction from varied examples) rather than through repetitive practice.
>
> **Boundary:** The recurrent/non-recurrent distinction is relative to the analysis level and the domain. A skill that appears non-recurrent at one level of analysis may contain recurrent sub-components at a finer-grained level.
>
> **Report-Specific Significance:** The recurrent/non-recurrent distinction is the analytic key to the model's dual-mechanism architecture — it determines which instructional strategies serve which skill aspects.
>
> **See also:** [[schema-formation]], [[Cognitive-Skill-Acquisition]], [[cognitive-task-analysis]]
