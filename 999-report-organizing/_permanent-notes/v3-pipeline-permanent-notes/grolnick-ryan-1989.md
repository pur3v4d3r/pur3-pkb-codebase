# grolnick-ryan-1989

> [!definition] grolnick-ryan-1989
> - **Key-Term**: [[grolnick-ryan-1989]]
> - **Definition**: The Grolnick and Ryan (1989) study introduced the concept of autonomy-supportive teaching, which refers to instructional practices that foster students' autonomous motivation by providing choice, rationale, and opportunities for self-regulation in educational settings.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> In their seminal work, Grolnick and Ryan (1989) proposed that autonomy-supportive teaching is a key factor in promoting students' intrinsic motivation. They argued that teachers who provide choices to students, explain the rationale behind tasks, and encourage self-regulation enhance students' sense of control over their learning processes.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> The study involved qualitative and quantitative methods to assess how different teaching styles impact students' psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness. The findings suggested that autonomy-supportive practices are more effective in fostering intrinsic motivation compared to controlling or neglectful approaches.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between autonomy-supportive and autonomy-invasive behaviors, where the former enhances self-determination while the latter undermines it.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In classrooms, teachers can implement autonomy-supportive practices by offering students choices in their learning activities, providing clear explanations for tasks, and encouraging them to set personal goals.

> [!example] Application
> School administrators can promote a culture of autonomy support by training teachers on effective motivational strategies and creating an environment that values student voice and choice.
