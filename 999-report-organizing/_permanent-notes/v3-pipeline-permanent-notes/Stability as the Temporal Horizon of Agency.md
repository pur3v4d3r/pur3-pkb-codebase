## Reflections

> [!claude-insight] Stability as the Temporal Horizon of Agency
> One might think of the stability dimension as setting the temporal horizon of perceived agency. When causes are perceived as unstable, agency extends forward indefinitely — what happened this time need not happen next time, because the causal landscape is fluid. When causes are perceived as stable, agency collapses into the present moment — the future is already determined by enduring factors that the person cannot alter. This framing clarifies why stability, rather than controllability, is the primary determinant of expectancy: controllability addresses whether one *can* change the cause, but stability addresses whether the cause *will* be there to change. A controllable but stable cause (such as chronic laziness, if perceived as a fixed character trait) still produces negative expectancy, because the perception of stability overrides the theoretical possibility of control.
> *— [[attribution-theory-foundational-report-2026-04-19]]*
