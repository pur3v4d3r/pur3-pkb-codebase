# situated-learning

> [!definition] situated-learning
> - **Key-Term**: [[situated-learning]]
> - **Definition**: Situated learning is an educational theory that posits that learning is deeply influenced by the context and culture of its occurrence, emphasizing the role of social interaction and practical engagement in knowledge acquisition.
> - **Domain**: educational-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Paragraph 1: Foundational context and core mechanism. Situated learning theory, developed by Jean Lave and Etienne Wenger, suggests that learning is not confined to formal educational settings but occurs naturally within everyday activities and social interactions. It emphasizes the importance of embedding learning in real-world contexts where learners can see the relevance and application of what they are learning.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Paragraph 2: How it works or is applied in practice. In practice, situated learning involves hands-on experiences, collaborative problem-solving, and the integration of theory with practical application. Learners engage in tasks that require them to use their knowledge in authentic situations, which helps them understand how to apply what they have learned.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Paragraph 3: Key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots. The concept has evolved into various forms such as communities of practice and situated cognition. It is closely related to the idea of apprenticeship learning, where learners are mentored by experts in a specific domain.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> Implication 1: Concrete application or real-world consequence. In educational settings, this theory supports the design of project-based and experiential learning activities that mimic real-world scenarios.

> [!example] Application
> Implication 2: A second distinct application. It also informs workplace training programs where employees learn through on-the-job tasks and interactions with colleagues.
