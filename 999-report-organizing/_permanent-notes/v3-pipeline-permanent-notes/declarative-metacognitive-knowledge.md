# declarative-metacognitive-knowledge

> [!definition] declarative-metacognitive-knowledge
> - **Key-Term**: [[declarative-metacognitive-knowledge]]
> - **Definition**: Declarative-metacognitive-knowledge refers to the explicit understanding of one's own cognitive processes and strategies, including knowledge about when and how to apply them effectively in different contexts.
> - **Domain**: cognitive-psychology
> - **Status**: 🌱 budding | Confidence: speculative

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Declarative-metacognitive-knowledge is a subset of metacognition that involves knowing about one’s own thought processes and learning strategies. It includes understanding the nature of tasks, recognizing one's strengths and weaknesses, and knowing how to use appropriate strategies for different situations.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> This knowledge enables individuals to plan their cognitive activities, monitor their progress, and adjust their strategies as needed. For example, a student might know that they need to review material multiple times before an exam (strategy) and recognize when they are struggling with a particular concept (self-monitoring).

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Key nuances include the distinction between declarative knowledge about tasks and processes versus procedural knowledge of how to perform them. Theoretical roots can be traced back to cognitive psychology, particularly in theories of self-regulated learning.

## Practical Implications

> [!example] Application
> In education, fostering declarative-metacognitive-knowledge helps students become more independent learners who can adapt their study strategies based on their understanding of their own learning processes.

> [!example] Application
> In coaching and skill development, coaches can use this knowledge to tailor interventions that help athletes or performers recognize and correct their mistakes in real-time.
