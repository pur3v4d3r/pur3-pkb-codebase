## Cross-Domain Connections

> [!cross-domain-connection] How This Report Integrates with the Broader Knowledge Graph
> ### Upstream Dependencies (concepts this report builds on)
> - **[[metacognition]]** — The general construct that supplies the conceptual frame for the entire report. Without the broader metacognitive framework from Flavell and subsequent research, the reading-specific architecture has no theoretical anchorage. This report assumes familiarity with metacognition as a general phenomenon and applies it specifically to reading.
> - **[[Flavell's-Metacognitive-Framework]]** — The tripartite organization of metacognition (knowledge, experiences, regulation) that organizes Section 4's treatment of knowledge of cognition. The framework is presupposed throughout and is the entry point for any deeper engagement with the report's claims.
> - **[[reading-comprehension]]** — The cognitive processes by which readers construct meaning from text, supplying the object-level architecture that the metacognitive layer monitors and regulates. Section 1 assumes basic familiarity with comprehension as a constructive process.
> - **[[information-processing-theory]]** — The broader cognitive-psychology paradigm within which the monitoring–control loop is intelligible. The architectural framing throughout the report assumes information-processing-style analysis as the appropriate level of cognitive description.
> - **[[schema-theory-and-knowledge-organization]]** — The framework for understanding how prior knowledge organizes incoming information, presupposed in Section 3's treatment of how external coherence standards interact with what the reader already knows.
>
> ### Downstream Applications (concepts this report enables)
> - **[[Metacognitive Scaffolding]]** — Educational design principles that build metacognitive skill through structured scaffolding; this report supplies the architectural target that effective scaffolding must hit. Sections 6 and 7 are particularly relevant.
> - **[[reciprocal-teaching]]** — The most empirically validated metacognitive reading intervention; this report provides the architectural justification for why it works and what its effective implementation requires. Section 7 applies directly.
> - **[[comprehension-monitoring-training-protocols]]** — Specific protocols for building monitoring sensitivity; this report supplies the architectural context that distinguishes effective from ineffective protocols. Sections 5 and 6 are foundational.
> - **[[fix-up-strategy]]** — The regulatory repertoire of skilled reading; this report locates fix-up strategies within the broader architecture and explains why some are more effective than others. Section 6 is the primary treatment.
> - **[[self-explanation-effect]]** — A specific high-effectiveness regulatory strategy whose architectural role this report explains in detail. Sections 6 and 7 develop the analysis.
>
> ### Lateral Connections (concepts that mutually enrich)
> - **[[transfer-of-learning]]** — The general literature on conditions under which learning in one domain transfers to others; this report's far transfer section both draws on and contributes to the transfer literature, suggesting that architectural transfer may be more tractable than strategy transfer.
> - **[[Active-Recall]]** — A general learning practice whose effectiveness operates through mechanisms structurally similar to those that make self-explanation work in reading; both substitute external evidence for internal inference about learning state.
> - **[[Spaced-Repetition-Algorithms-and-Memory]]** — A general retention practice whose effectiveness can be analyzed in terms of the calibration problem this report develops; spaced repetition systems function as externalized calibration mechanisms that supply the ground truth ordinary self-monitoring cannot.
> - **[[Algorithmic-Metacognition-—-When-Spaced-Repetition-Systems-Do-Metacognitive-Work]]** — A complementary investigation of how computational tools perform metacognitive work that overlaps with the PKB-as-externalized-metacognition argument in Section 8.
> - **[[cog-sci-pkm-the-role-of-reading-in-pkm]]** — A direct overlap with the PKB-frontier section; the two notes mutually enrich each other and should be read together.
>
> ### Strengthened Nodes (existing permanent notes this report enriches)
> - **[[comprehension-monitoring]]** — This report supplies the most comprehensive architectural treatment of comprehension monitoring in the PKB, locating it within the broader Nelson–Narens framework and connecting it to the standard-of-coherence and knowledge-of-cognition literatures.
> - **[[calibration]]** — This report develops the calibration problem with particular attention to its structural rather than informational character, supplying the framing in which the PKB's other calibration-related notes can be situated.
> - **[[Fluency Illusion]]** — This report provides the most extensive treatment of how the fluency illusion specifically operates in reading, with attention to its mechanism, conditions, and instructional implications.
> - **[[reading-strategies]]** — This report supplies the architectural context within which individual strategies are intelligible, enriching the existing notes on rereading, summarization, self-explanation, and other specific moves.
> - **[[metacognitive-knowledge]]** — This report develops the declarative/procedural/conditional distinction at length, particularly the centrality of conditional knowledge as the bottleneck for skilled metacognitive reading.
