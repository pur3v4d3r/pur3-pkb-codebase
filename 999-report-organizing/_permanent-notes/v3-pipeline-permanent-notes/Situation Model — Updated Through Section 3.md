## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Situation Model — Updated Through Section 3
> **Key Entities:** The three dimensions (locus, stability, controllability); the stability-expectancy mechanism (now elaborated); learned helplessness and explanatory style (Seligman); implicit theories (Dweck); self-efficacy (Bandura); mastery experience.
> **Causal Map:** Triggering conditions → causal search → causal explanation → classified on three dimensions → **stability dimension → expectancy change → motivational trajectory** (this pathway now fully elaborated). Upstream beliefs (implicit theories) constrain dimensional classification. Attribution mediates the impact of mastery experience on self-efficacy.
> **Structural Overview:** We now have the triggering architecture (Section 1), the dimensional structure (Section 2), and the first of the three major consequence pathways — the stability-expectancy mechanism (Section 3). Two consequence pathways remain: emotion (Section 4) and bias/distortion (Section 5).
> **Evolution This Section:** The model gains its most consequential causal pathway — stability → expectancy. The connection to learned helplessness, implicit theories, and self-efficacy integrates attribution with the broader motivational landscape.
> **Emerging Patterns:** Upstream beliefs (implicit theories) shape the dimensional classification of causes, which in turn shapes downstream consequences. The system is not mechanistically rigid but interpretively flexible — the same objective cause can be classified differently depending on the perceiver's belief system. This flexibility is what makes intervention possible.
> **Open Threads:** How do the dimensions generate specific emotions? What is the relationship between attribution-based emotion and attribution-based expectancy? Section 4 addresses the emotional architecture.
