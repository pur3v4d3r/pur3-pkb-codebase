## 🪞 Reflexion (5-Question Pass)

### 1. What worked well?

- **Sequential authorship within a single batch was efficient**: drafting four conceptually-related notes back-to-back produced significantly better cross-linking than the Session 2 single-note pass. Each subsequent note could *reference forward* to the previous, and `latticework.structural-analogs` declarations naturally formed reciprocal pairs.
- **Differentiating canonical examples per note** (Berkshire Hathaway → SpaceX → Jacobi/Wason → Korzybski's menu) prevented case-study collision and gave each note its own evidentiary anchor.
- **The hub note's `hallucination_check: true` finalization** was a clean, low-cost discharge of the Session 2 open question. No regression introduced.

### 2. What was harder than expected?

- **Maintaining structural-correspondence specificity**: the temptation to write generic "both involve X" descriptions was constant. Every analog was rewritten at least once to ensure the *cross-domain-problem-illuminated* field actually surfaced something the structural mapping made *visible* that single-domain framing did not. This is the crux of latticework value and cannot be skimped.
- **Avoiding the Session 2 case-study magnet (Three Mile Island)**: each Phase-2 note needed its own case study. `latticework-of-mental-models` got 2008 financial crisis (single-discipline framing); `first-principles-thinking` got Apollo Lunar Orbit Rendezvous; `inversion` got Boeing 737 MAX MCAS; `map-vs-territory` re-read the 2008 crisis through a *map-territory lens*. The 2008 cross-reference between notes 2 and 5 is intentional — same territory, two distinct maps — and is itself an instance of the principle each note teaches.

### 3. What were the cross-cutting design decisions?

- **`[[mental-model]]` declared as a structural-analog in every Session-3 note**: the hub-and-spoke connectivity is intentional. Each Phase-2 connector relates to the meta-concept hub by a *different* structural correspondence (composition, construction, reverse-deployment, representation-discipline). This populates the MOC's Bridges table densely without over-counting.
- **All four notes pre-flagged `hallucination_check: true`** since Q2 was pre-authorized in user response.
- **Personal Application / Personal Notes blocks left as italicized `*Placeholder*` text**: this preserves Q1's deferred status — drafting these from daily-note material is a future dedicated session, not Session 3 work.
- **Composite scores reflect honest assessment, not inflation**: `latticework-of-mental-models` and `first-principles-thinking` correctly score 4.33 (not 5.0) because each has a real weakest-dimension that would be dishonest to elide. Only `inversion` and `map-vs-territory` earned 5.0/5.0/5.0 — and both shifted their cultivation-targets from acquisition to *guarding against complacency*, which is the appropriate cultivation logic for already-mastered uniformly-strong models.

### 4. What did the latticework gain?

The Bridges table grew from empty to 14 declared edges. New structural categories surfaced:

- **Decomposition family**: first-principles ↔ reductionism ↔ axiomatization ↔ refactoring
- **Reversal family**: inversion ↔ contrapositive ↔ dual-problem ↔ adversarial-thinking ↔ premortem ↔ devils-advocate ↔ via-negativa
- **Representation-discipline family**: map-vs-territory ↔ abstraction ↔ representation-theory ↔ reification-fallacy ↔ bayesian-updating

These three families are the spine of Phase 2's cross-domain reach. Subsequent phase notes (3 — Cognitive Science, 4 — Decision & Behavior) will plug into these families primarily through the *first-principles* and *map-vs-territory* anchors.

### 5. What did Session 3 reveal about the master plan?

- **The 1-MOC-update + 1-handoff pattern is correctly weighted**: ~5% of session token budget. Sustainable for the remaining 11 sessions.
- **The 800–2000 word target is producing ~1900–2100 word notes** for Phase 2 connectors. This is the *high* end of the band. Phase 3+ notes (more specialized, more constrained scope) should aim for the 800–1400 range to compensate.
- **The Latticework Bridges table will outgrow its current single-table format by Phase 5**. Recommend Phase 8 densification audit refactor it into a per-family sub-table layout.

### Per-Note Suggested Structural Analogs

These are *suggestions* (matching the master plan's compositional logic), not constraints. The author should evaluate and substitute as the writing surfaces stronger correspondences.

#### `[[second-order-thinking]]`
- `[[mental-model]]` — second-order thinking is the *recursive deployment* of mental models (running the model on the model's own outputs)
- `[[feedback-loop]]` — first-order causes produce second-order effects through loop closure; the formal version of "what happens next?"
- `[[chess-lookahead]]` — game-tree search as the explicit operationalization
- `[[unintended-consequences]]` — the failure-mode (the territory beyond first-order analysis surfaces what was missed)

**Differentiation rule**: use Garrett Hardin's "and then what?" or a chess-engine ply-depth example as canonical. *Avoid* re-using Apollo, MAX, 2008 — those are taken.

#### `[[feedback-loop]]`
- `[[mental-model]]` — feedback structure is one of the most cross-transferable model classes (per Forrester, Meadows)
- `[[homeostasis-and-equilibrium]]` — biological version
- `[[predictive-coding]]` — neural version
- `[[control-theory]]` — engineering version (PID, Francis-Wonham)

**Differentiation rule**: this note overlaps heavily with `[[mental-model]]`'s Craik loop and `[[predictive-coding]]`. Differentiate by focusing on the *typology* (reinforcing vs. balancing, delays, oscillation) rather than the cognitive instantiation. Use Jay Forrester's *Industrial Dynamics* (1961) and Donella Meadows' *Thinking in Systems* (2008) as primary cites.

#### `[[opportunity-cost]]`
- `[[mental-model]]` — economic primitive routinely overlooked by non-economists
- `[[expected-value]]` — both quantify alternatives; opportunity-cost is the *foregone-alternative* component of EV reasoning
- `[[trade-off]]` — operationalized in everyday choice
- `[[counterfactual-reasoning]]` — what-would-have-been is the cognitive substrate of opportunity-cost reasoning

**Differentiation rule**: Frédéric Bastiat's *That Which Is Seen and That Which Is Not Seen* (1850) is the foundational essay; use it. Time-allocation rather than financial-capital examples for the canonical case.
