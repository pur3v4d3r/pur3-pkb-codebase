# PRACTICE PROBLEM (Hard) — Diagnosing "The Stuck Thinker"

> [!info] How to Use This Practice Problem
> The challenge: reflection on someone else's (fictional) three-month practice log to **diagnose** what is going wrong and prescribe **specific interventions**. This trains the reflection capability on **harder data** — patterns that contradict the practitioner's self-narrative, asymmetric bottlenecks, and second-order failures (i.e., failures of the reflection process itself, not just the practice).
>
> Budget **~35 minutes**. Use the [[_master-metacognitive-reflection-v1.0.0|reflection template]] adapted: instead of "what did *I* learn," answer "what does this practitioner *not yet see* about their own practice?"

---

## 🎯 The Object: A Three-Month Practice Log

A friend (call them "T") has been doing daily CT practice for 3 months. They report feeling "stuck" — sessions feel routine, no breakthroughs. They ask you to look at their data and tell them what's wrong.

**Their session data (aggregated):**

| Metric | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sessions completed | 22 | 24 | 26 |
| Mean rigor | 3.6 | 3.9 | 4.0 |
| **Standards distribution** | | | |
| Clarity | 4.4 | 4.6 | 4.8 |
| Accuracy | 4.0 | 4.1 | 4.2 |
| Precision | 3.8 | 4.0 | 4.1 |
| Relevance | 4.5 | 4.6 | 4.7 |
| **Depth** | **3.1** | **3.1** | **3.2** |
| **Breadth** | **3.0** | **3.0** | **3.1** |
| Logic | 4.2 | 4.4 | 4.5 |
| Significance | 3.8 | 3.9 | 4.0 |
| **Fairness** | **3.2** | **3.2** | **3.3** |
| Framework distribution | PE 75% / FRISCO 20% / SEE-I 5% | PE 70% / FRISCO 25% / SEE-I 5% | PE 70% / FRISCO 25% / SEE-I 5% |
| Avg session duration | 35 min | 38 min | 42 min |
| Pre→post confidence Δ | -0.5 avg | -0.3 avg | -0.2 avg |
| Object types | 80% personal decisions / 20% external arguments | 75% personal / 25% external | 75% personal / 25% external |
| Virtues most exercised | Perseverance, Clarity | Perseverance, Clarity | Perseverance, Clarity |
| Virtues most absent | Empathy, Breadth, Autonomy | Empathy, Breadth, Autonomy | Empathy, Breadth, Autonomy |

**T's self-narrative:** "I'm getting better — my mean rigor keeps going up. I just feel like I've plateaued and I don't know why."

---

## 📝 Your Workspace

Diagnose. For each finding, you must:
1. **Cite the data** that supports the finding.
2. **Name what T does not yet see** about their practice.
3. **Prescribe a specific intervention** (not a vague aspiration).

Suggested structure: 4-6 specific diagnostic findings, each ~80-150 words, each ending with one intervention.

---

## 🧭 Hints

> [!tip]- Hint 1 — Reading the trend, not the level
> T cites "rigor going up" as evidence of progress. But look at *which* standards rose: Clarity, Logic, Relevance — already T's strong standards. Look at which are flat: Depth, Breadth, Fairness. **T is not improving on the dimensions where they need to improve.** The rigor mean rises because the strong standards mask the bottleneck standards. T's self-narrative is wrong; the data shows training the strengths, not the weaknesses. This is the [[Local Optimum]] failure mode of deliberate practice.

> [!tip]- Hint 2 — Confidence delta is collapsing
> Month 1: pre→post confidence change averaged -0.5 (analysis was meaningfully changing T's mind).
> Month 3: averaged -0.2 (analysis is barely changing T's mind anymore).
> Either T has become very well-calibrated (good!) OR T has learned to perform the framework in ways that do not threaten their initial positions (bad). Given the flat Fairness rating, the second interpretation is far more likely. T is **going through the motions** of analysis without letting it bite.

> [!tip]- Hint 3 — Object distribution
> 75-80% of T's sessions are personal decisions. Combined with low Fairness (3.3) and absent Empathy virtue, this strongly suggests T is using CT practice as a way to *post-hoc justify decisions they have already made*, not to test their positions. The session pattern is a [[Confirmation Bias]] machine wearing a critical-thinking costume.

> [!tip]- Hint 4 — Framework monoculture
> SEE-I at 5% across all three months. T has essentially abandoned concept-elaboration practice. This means T is not training the **conceptual precision** that would feed back into Depth on Paul-Elder sessions. The framework choice and the Depth bottleneck are causally connected, not independent.

> [!tip]- Hint 5 — Duration creep
> 35 → 38 → 42 minutes per session, with rigor barely rising. T is spending more time per session for less marginal rigor. This is the signature of **rumination, not analysis** — re-doing the same kind of moves more thoroughly rather than doing different kinds of moves.

---

## ✅ Solution Sketch

> [!example]- Solution Sketch — Diagnosis
>
> **Finding 1 — Training the strengths, not the weaknesses (the [[Local Optimum]] trap).**
> Across three months, T's already-strong standards (Clarity +0.4, Logic +0.3, Relevance +0.2) improved noticeably; the bottleneck standards (Depth +0.1, Breadth +0.1, Fairness +0.1) barely moved. T's "rigor going up" narrative is true at the *mean*, false at the *bottlenecks*. T is doing more reps of what they're already good at. This is the boundary between *experienced* and *expert* practice in [[Deliberate Practice]] research — experts target weaknesses; non-experts get more reps.
> **Intervention:** for the next month, T should **score Depth, Breadth, and Fairness *first*** before mean-averaging, and refuse to log a session as "complete" if any of those three is below 3.5. Force the bottleneck to surface.
>
> **Finding 2 — Confidence delta is collapsing (the "going through the motions" pattern).**
> Pre→post confidence Δ has shrunk from -0.5 to -0.2 over three months. The optimistic reading: T has become well-calibrated. The pessimistic reading, far more likely given the flat Fairness rating: T has unconsciously learned to *perform* the framework in ways that don't disturb prior positions. The session has become a ritual, not a tool. T's "feel like I've plateaued" is the *correct* report of a practice that is no longer changing T's mind.
> **Intervention:** for the next 4 weeks, T should *only* run sessions on objects where they have initially-held positions they would prefer not to be wrong about. Explicit pre-statement: "I am writing this analysis hoping to be proven wrong." If Δ does not return to ≥-0.4 within a month, the practice has hardened into ritual and needs structural redesign.
>
> **Finding 3 — Object distribution is a [[Confirmation Bias]] machine.**
> 75-80% of sessions are personal decisions. Personal decisions are where motivated reasoning runs strongest and where Fairness is hardest. The combination — high-stakes personal-decision objects + low Fairness rating + absent Empathy virtue — is the empirical signature of using analytic frameworks to post-hoc rationalize already-made decisions. T is not analyzing; T is auditing for plausible deniability.
> **Intervention:** rebalance the object mix to ≤50% personal decisions. Force the other ≥50% to be external arguments (FRISCO) or concept elaboration (SEE-I). This restores object diversity and prevents the practice from being captured by self-justification.
>
> **Finding 4 — Framework monoculture compounds the Depth bottleneck.**
> SEE-I usage at 5% across all three months means T is not training conceptual precision. But Depth on Paul-Elder sessions *depends on* the precision of the concepts T can deploy in element 1.5. Without SEE-I practice, T's conceptual vocabulary stays at the surface level T entered the practice with. The Depth bottleneck and the SEE-I deficit are the same problem viewed from different angles.
> **Intervention:** require 1 SEE-I session per week (~4/month), on concepts that appeared in recent Paul-Elder sessions but were used without being defined. This creates a feedback loop: poorly-understood concepts in Paul-Elder become next week's SEE-I targets, which improves the following Paul-Elder.
>
> **Finding 5 — Duration creep without rigor gain (rumination, not analysis).**
> Session duration rose from 35 → 42 minutes while rigor rose negligibly. T is spending more time doing the *same kind* of work more thoroughly, not different kinds of work that would address the bottlenecks. This is rumination, and it is a leading indicator that the practice will become aversive within 2-3 more months.
> **Intervention:** strict 30-minute time-boxes on FRISCO sessions, 45 minutes on Paul-Elder, 15 minutes on SEE-I. When the time-box hits, *stop*. If a section is unfinished, that itself is data about which sections T avoids or rushes — log it.
>
> **Finding 6 — The reflection process itself has failed.**
> The most important finding is meta: T has been doing reflection sessions for three months and has not detected any of findings 1-5. The reflection process itself is operating with the same biases as the underlying practice. T sees "rigor up" and stops, because the rising mean confirms the narrative T wants. This is **second-order failure** — the practice meant to detect first-order failures has the same blind spot as the first-order practice.
> **Intervention:** T should have someone else (peer, coach, even an LLM with explicit "challenge me" instructions) review their next reflection. External review breaks the self-confirmation loop in a way that no internal procedure can.
>
> **Expected output:** 800-1200 words across 5-6 findings; the *hardest* part is finding #6 — the second-order failure — because it requires T to accept that the reflection capability itself is suspect. Most practitioners stop at findings 1-5.
