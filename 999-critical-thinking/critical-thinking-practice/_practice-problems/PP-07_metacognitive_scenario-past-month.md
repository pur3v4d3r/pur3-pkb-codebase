# PRACTICE PROBLEM (Easy) — Metacognitive Reflection on a fictional past month

> [!info] How to Use This Practice Problem
> Metacognitive reflection is **second-order** — you reflect on your own practice. Until you have a month of real practice to reflect on, use the **fictional dataset below** to train the reflection moves. Use the [[_master-metacognitive-reflection-v1.0.0|metacognitive reflection template]]. Budget **~25 minutes**.

---

## 🎯 The Object of Analysis: A Fictional Month of CT Practice

Imagine this is *your* past month. The session data:

| Date | Framework | Object | Rigor | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-04-19 | paul-elder | Decision: take a new job | 3.5 | rushed; afternoon |
| 2026-04-22 | frisco | News article: AI doom | 4.0 | morning; concise |
| 2026-04-24 | see-i | [[Working Memory]] | 4.3 | strong |
| 2026-04-26 | paul-elder | Argument: should I move cities | 3.2 | weak; emotional |
| 2026-04-29 | frisco | Op-ed on tipping | 4.1 | morning |
| 2026-05-02 | paul-elder | Decision: switch supplements | 3.0 | very weak; defensive |
| 2026-05-04 | see-i | [[Anchoring Bias]] | 4.2 | strong |
| 2026-05-06 | frisco | Twitter thread on EVs | 3.8 | morning |
| 2026-05-09 | paul-elder | Career pivot to ML | 4.1 | morning; strong |
| 2026-05-11 | see-i | [[Cognitive Load]] | 4.4 | morning; strong |
| 2026-05-13 | frisco | Op-ed on housing | 4.0 | strong |
| 2026-05-15 | paul-elder | Argument: vegetarianism | 3.5 | weak depth |

**Aggregate:** 12 sessions; mean rigor 3.84; mean by framework — paul-elder 3.46, frisco 3.98, see-i 4.30.

**Standards trend (4-week):** Clarity rose (4.5 → 4.8). **Depth** flat at 3.2-3.4. **Fairness** flat at 3.4-3.6. Logic and Relevance high.

**Virtues:** Humility ✅, Courage ✅, Perseverance ✅, Empathy ✅, Autonomy ❌ (3 sessions deferred to expert opinion).

---

## 📝 Your Workspace

Work through all 8 sections of the metacognitive reflection template:

1. **Sessions Under Review** — already given above.
2. **Person Knowledge** — what does this month reveal about *how I think*?
3. **Task Knowledge** — what does this month reveal about *the frameworks themselves*?
4. **Strategy Knowledge** — what strategies/prompts worked best?
5. **Monitoring** — where did I catch biases in-the-moment vs only post-hoc?
6. **Regulation** — what 2-3 changes for next month?
7. **Standards Trend** — pick a bottleneck, propose deliberate practice.
8. **Virtues Inventory** — most exercised, most missing.
9. **Commitments** — 3-5 dated, falsifiable items.
10. **Key Insight** — one paragraph compressing the whole month.

---

## 🧭 Hints

> [!tip]- Hint 1 — Read the data, don't react to it
> The first move in reflection is **descriptive**, not evaluative. Before you decide what's "good" or "bad" about the month, simply describe the patterns: time-of-day, framework distribution, standard distribution, emotional-charge correlation with rigor. The patterns *tell you* the insights; you don't have to manufacture them.

> [!tip]- Hint 2 — Find the strongest pattern
> The most obvious pattern in this dataset is that Paul-Elder sessions on **emotionally-charged personal decisions** scored ~1 point lower than Paul-Elder sessions on neutral arguments. That single pattern (a) names a [[Motivated Reasoning]] bottleneck, (b) suggests a concrete remediation (cooling-off period before personal-decision sessions), and (c) generates a falsifiable commitment.

> [!tip]- Hint 3 — Bottleneck standards
> Depth is the bottleneck standard. A good Regulation section would design a *specific drill* — e.g., "for every analytic claim, ask 'why?' three times before moving on" — not a vague aspiration like "be deeper."

## ✅ Solution Sketch

> [!example]- Solution Sketch — Key Analytical Moves
>
> **Person Knowledge:**
> - Paul-Elder rigor (3.46) is 0.84 below SEE-I rigor (4.30). The difference is not framework difficulty; it's that Paul-Elder is preferentially deployed on emotionally-charged personal decisions, which I handle worse than neutral conceptual material. The asymmetry is about *me*, not about Paul-Elder.
> - Morning sessions average 4.07; afternoon sessions average 3.35. Time-of-day is a meaningful intervention.
> - Three sessions (job, move, supplements) had explicit "defensive" / "rushed" notes. Together they account for the entire below-average tail.
>
> **Task Knowledge:**
> - FRISCO is reliably producing 3.8-4.1 across diverse objects — it is the most consistent tool for argument triage and should be the default for any single-claim object.
> - SEE-I is producing the highest mean rigor (4.30) — but I am only deploying it on concepts I already half-know. That inflates the mean; I should attempt SEE-I on at least one *unfamiliar* concept next month to get an unbiased read.
> - Paul-Elder is being mis-deployed. It is the right tool for **action-bound** objects (decisions I will execute), not for **emotionally-charged personal** objects per se — those need a 24-hour cooling-off period to manage motivated reasoning.
>
> **Strategy Knowledge:**
> - Worked: morning timing, disconfirming-evidence search subsection, the pre/post confidence delta (caught two confidence inflations).
> - Did not work: trying to run Paul-Elder on a personal decision the same day it was emotionally salient.
> - To adopt: 24-hour cooling-off rule for personal-decision Paul-Elder sessions; explicit Fairness self-check at the end of every session.
>
> **Monitoring:**
> - In-the-moment bias catches: ~3 across the month.
> - Post-hoc catches: ~6.
> - Ratio is moving in the right direction (3 months ago: 1 in-moment vs 8 post-hoc), but the practical move is to *trigger* in-moment monitoring by adding a checkpoint inside the template — e.g., "after each element, name one bias you might be running."
>
> **Regulation — next month:**
> 1. Schedule all CT sessions before 11 AM.
> 2. For any personal-decision object, wait 24 hours from initial trigger before starting the Paul-Elder session.
> 3. Add a "name one bias" prompt at the end of each element.
> 4. SEE-I one unfamiliar concept (pick a concept I rate ≤2/5 known).
>
> **Standards Trend — Depth bottleneck:**
> Depth has been flat at 3.2-3.4 for the full month while Clarity rose. The pattern says: I am getting better at *making the question crisp* and not at *digging deeper into the answer*. **Deliberate practice for Depth:** for every claim I make inside an analysis, ask "why?" three times. If I cannot answer the third "why" without inventing speculation, I have hit my depth limit and should mark it as a [[Knowledge Gap]] rather than papering over it.
>
> **Virtues:** Most exercised — Perseverance (completed 12/12 planned sessions). Most missing — **Autonomy** (3 sessions deferred to expert opinion). Next-month repair: at least 4 sessions where I write my analysis *before* reading anyone else's view.
>
> **Commitments (3-5, dated, falsifiable):**
> - [ ] By 2026-06-15: 12 sessions complete, all before 11 AM.
> - [ ] By 2026-06-15: 24-hour cooling-off applied to all personal-decision objects (track in frontmatter).
> - [ ] By 2026-06-15: Depth mean rigor ≥3.7 (currently 3.3).
> - [ ] By 2026-06-15: 1 SEE-I session on a concept I rate ≤2/5 known.
> - [ ] By 2026-06-15: 4 sessions analyzed before reading others' views.
>
> **Key Insight:**
> "This month's data says I am getting better at the *framing* of critical thinking and not yet at the *substance* — Clarity rises, Depth stalls — and the substance gap is concentrated in objects I am emotionally invested in. The bottleneck is therefore not skill but *self-regulation*: I need to apply the techniques *to objects I'd rather not examine carefully*. The remediation is structural (cooling-off period, morning timing, explicit Depth drill) rather than aspirational. The pattern would not be visible from any single session — it required four weeks of data and the [[Metacognitive Reflection Practice]] template to surface it."
>
> **Expected output:** ~600-900 words across the 8 sections; should feel less like analysis and more like *reading what the data already says*.
