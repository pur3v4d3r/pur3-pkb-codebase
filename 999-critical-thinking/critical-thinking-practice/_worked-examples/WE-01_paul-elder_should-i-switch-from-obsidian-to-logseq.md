# WORKED EXAMPLE — Paul-Elder: Should I switch from Obsidian to Logseq?

> [!abstract] How to Use This Worked Example
> This is a **completed reference session** showing the [[Paul-Elder Framework]] applied to a real personal decision. Read it slowly. Compare every section to the [[_master-critical-thinking-deliberate-practice-v1.0.0|template prompts]]. Notice **what counts as a good answer at each step** — the depth, the honesty, the willingness to write down uncomfortable things. The point is not to agree with the conclusion; the point is to internalize the *moves*.

## 0 · Pre-Analysis Snapshot — Calibration

> [!attention] Why this section matters
> Without recording your **starting position**, you cannot detect whether analysis actually moved you. The default human pattern is to perform analysis as a ritual that confirms what we already wanted. The pre-snapshot is the [[Confirmation Bias]] tripwire.

**Initial position / gut answer (in one sentence):**
> I'm 80% sure I should switch to [[Logseq]] because its outliner-first model and block references would fix my note-fragmentation problem.

**Confidence in initial position (1-5):** `4`

**Emotional charge around this topic (1-5):** `4` — I am genuinely excited by Logseq's design and frustrated with my current [[Obsidian]] friction. Excitement and frustration both bias analysis.

**What outcome am I motivated to reach?**
> I want to be told it's reasonable to switch, because I've already invested mental energy imagining the switch. Naming this is the antidote.

**Time-box for this session (minutes):** `60`

---

## 1 · The Eight Elements of Thought — [[Paul-Elder Framework]]

### 1.1 · Purpose — *what is the reasoning for?*

[**Stated-Purpose**:: Determine whether migrating my PKB from Obsidian to Logseq would meaningfully improve my knowledge-work outcomes, measured over a 6-month horizon.]

- **Stated purpose:** Decide whether to switch.
- **Real purpose (honest version):** Find a reason to switch that I can justify to myself. The stated purpose is decision-framed; the real purpose is permission-seeking.
- **Could the purpose be unrealistic?** Yes — "fix my note-fragmentation" assumes the *tool* is the bottleneck. My own discipline could be the bottleneck. If so, no tool switch resolves it.
- **Could the purpose be unethical?** Not in the moral sense, but it is *self-deceptive*, which is the intellectual analogue.

> The most important move in this section was distinguishing **stated** from **real** purpose. Critical thinking that does not surface motivated reasoning at the purpose stage will fail downstream.

---

### 1.2 · Question at Issue — *what precise question am I answering?*

[**Central-Question**:: Will switching from Obsidian to Logseq, given my actual workflows and the specific frictions I currently experience, produce a net improvement in knowledge-management outcomes over 6 months that justifies the migration cost?]

- **Is this the right question?** No — my gut question was *"Is Logseq better than Obsidian?"* That is the **wrong question**. Tool-A-vs-tool-B in the abstract has no answer. The right question is the one above: tool-fit for *my* specific workflows, with *my* migration cost.
- **Is the question loaded?** Slightly — "improvement" presupposes my current state is suboptimal. I should verify that first.
- **What would count as a satisfactory answer?** A claim of the form: *"Switching would [help / harm / be neutral] because [3 specific workflow predictions], at a migration cost of [X hours], justified by [Y measurable benefit]."*

---

### 1.3 · Information — *what data am I using?*

[**Key-Evidence**:: My direct experience with Obsidian over 18 months (≈800 notes, daily use); 4 hours of Logseq trial use; 6 YouTube comparison videos; 2 Reddit threads (r/ObsidianMD, r/logseq); zero peer-reviewed studies on PKM tool outcomes.]

**Supporting evidence (favoring switch):**
- Logseq's block-level addressing solves my "fragment a quote across 3 notes" problem.
- Logseq's outliner is closer to how I think when capturing.
- Logseq is open-source and file-format-stable.

**Disconfirming evidence I deliberately searched for:**
- Logseq's plugin ecosystem is ~5% the size of Obsidian's. I rely on Templater, Dataview, Meta Bind, QuickAdd — **none have first-class Logseq equivalents**.
- Logseq's mobile experience is reported as significantly weaker.
- Migration of my YAML frontmatter system and Dataview queries is **non-trivial** — likely 30-60 hours.
- Reddit threads showed several people switched *back* to Obsidian within 3 months.

**Information gaps I'm aware of:**
- I have not measured how often I actually *use* block-level addressing in my current workflow. I'm extrapolating from one frustrating session.
- I have no data on how many of my 800 notes would migrate cleanly.

> The disconfirming-evidence section is **the highest-leverage subsection in the entire template**. Notice it produced facts that my initial enthusiasm had not surfaced.

---

### 1.4 · Interpretation & Inference — *what conclusions am I drawing?*

[**Primary-Inference**:: The migration cost (30-60 hours plus loss of mature plugin workflows) plausibly exceeds the marginal benefit of Logseq's outliner model, because the specific friction I want to fix can be addressed with Obsidian plugins I have not fully configured.]

**My inference chain:**
1. The friction I attribute to Obsidian (fragmented quotes) is real.
2. Logseq's design solves it natively.
3. **But** Obsidian's [[Better Word Count]] + [[Note Composer]] + [[Embed Anchors]] plugins also address it, untried.
4. Migration cost is high (estimated 30-60 hours).
5. **Therefore** the question is not Obsidian vs Logseq but *configured-Obsidian vs Logseq*, and I haven't tested the former.

**Alternative inferences I rejected:**
- "Switching will be net-positive because Logseq feels better in trials." — Rejected: 4 hours of trial use is not predictive of 6-month use; honeymoon effects dominate short trials.
- "Switching will be net-negative because change is hard." — Rejected: change-aversion is a bias, not an argument.

---

### 1.5 · Concepts — *what ideas/theories frame my thinking?*

[**Key-Concept**:: "Tool-fit" — the dynamic match between a tool's affordances and a user's workflow, mediated by configuration depth and switching cost.]

**Concepts in play (with my working definitions):**
- **Tool-fit**: degree to which a tool's *configured* (not default) state supports a user's workflows.
- **Migration cost**: total hours + cognitive disruption to move from tool A to tool B at acceptable fidelity.
- **Sunk cost** (relevant bias): hours already invested in Obsidian should **not** influence the decision — they are gone either way. But [[Switching Cost]] (forward-looking) **does** count.
- **Honeymoon effect**: short trials systematically overstate new-tool benefits because friction takes weeks to surface.

**Where I was [[Equivocation|equivocating]]:** I was using "better" to mean both "feels nicer" and "produces better outcomes." Those are different.

---

### 1.6 · Assumptions — *what am I taking for granted?*

> [!warning] Highest-leverage section. Slow down.

[**Load-Bearing-Assumption**:: That the bottleneck in my knowledge work is my *tool* rather than my *practice*.]

**Assumptions I'm aware of:**
- That Logseq's open-source status will translate into long-term reliability.
- That my workflows are stable enough that today's tool-fit predicts the next 6 months.

**Assumptions that, if false, would collapse my conclusion:**
- **If** the bottleneck is practice, not tool → switching produces no benefit and incurs full migration cost.
- **If** Obsidian plugins I haven't configured already solve my pain points → switching is strictly inferior.
- **If** I will, like my Reddit-thread counterparts, switch back within 3 months → migration is **double cost**.

**Assumptions of those who disagree with me (people who would say "stay"):**
- That mature plugin ecosystems are load-bearing infrastructure, not optional polish.
- That my friction is solvable in-place with 5 hours of configuration vs 50 hours of migration.
- That my desire to switch is driven by [[Novelty Bias|novelty-seeking]] rather than principled assessment.

> The "assumptions of those who disagree" subsection forced me to **steelman** the opposing view. Notice it produced the strongest counter-arguments in the entire session.

---

### 1.7 · Implications & Consequences — *what follows from this?*

[**Key-Implication**:: If I switch, I am committing to rebuild 18 months of tooling investment; if I do not switch, I am committing to invest ≥5 hours configuring Obsidian to address the specific frictions I have identified.]

**Logical implications:**
- Either decision **requires action**. "Don't switch" is not "do nothing" — it is "configure Obsidian properly."
- Whichever I choose, I am committing to a 6-month evaluation window with measurable criteria.

**Practical consequences:**
- **Switch:** 30-60 hours migration, 2-3 weeks of reduced output, loss of plugin workflows, gain of block-references.
- **Stay + configure:** 5-10 hours configuration, ~0 output disruption, retention of plugin workflows, possible non-resolution of friction (in which case re-evaluate).
- **Switch then switch back (likely worst case):** 60+ hours lost, productivity disruption twice.

---

### 1.8 · Point of View — *from where am I reasoning?*

[**My-Standpoint**:: I am reasoning as a power-user PKM enthusiast who values novelty and tool craft, currently 18 months into Obsidian, with the cognitive bias of someone who enjoys the *idea* of optimized systems more than the discipline of using them.]

**My standpoint and what it makes visible:**
> I see plugin ecosystems clearly because I have built workflows in them. I see Logseq's outliner clearly because I am attracted to it. This is the [[Streetlight Effect]] — I see what I have a flashlight pointed at.

**Alternative standpoint #1 (the practitioner who uses default Obsidian, steelmanned):**
> "Most users get 90% of the value from default features. The marginal gain of switching is dwarfed by the cost of any non-default workflow. Stay."

**Alternative standpoint #2 (the researcher who has tried 5+ PKM tools, steelmanned):**
> "All PKM tools have the same productivity ceiling — what differs is what kind of friction they produce. There is no escape; choose the friction you tolerate best, then stop switching."

---

## 3 · Conclusion & Revised Position

**Initial position (pre-analysis):** "I'm 80% sure I should switch to Logseq." (confidence 4/5)
**Revised position (post-analysis):** "I should **not** switch yet. I should invest 5-10 hours configuring Obsidian (specifically: Note Composer + Embed Anchors + a workflow change for quote capture) and re-evaluate in 30 days. If the configured state still produces the same friction, then a switch is justified — but with a 2-week pilot, not a full migration." (confidence 2/5)

**Confidence dropped from 4 to 2.** That is a *good* outcome: the analysis correctly surfaced uncertainty I had been suppressing. False certainty is the failure mode the [[Paul-Elder Framework]] is designed to prevent.

---

## 4 · Metacognitive Debrief

- **What was the biggest move in this session?** Renaming the question from "Logseq vs Obsidian" to "configured-Obsidian vs Logseq, given my migration cost."
- **What bias did I most need to defeat?** [[Confirmation Bias]] and [[Novelty Bias]] — both flagged in section 0 and addressed in 1.3 (disconfirming evidence) and 1.6 (steelmanning).
- **What would I do differently next time?** Spend more time on section 1.6 (assumptions) earlier — the load-bearing assumption was the pivot of the whole analysis.
