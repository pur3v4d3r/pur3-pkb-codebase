# WORKED EXAMPLE — Metacognitive Reflection: Weekly Review (Week of 2026-05-09)

> [!abstract] How to Use This Worked Example
> This is a **completed reference session** showing the [[Metacognitive Reflection Practice|metacognitive reflection template]] applied as a weekly review. This is **second-order** practice: not critical thinking *about* an object, but critical thinking *about your critical thinking*. The sessions referenced are realistic but fictional; treat them as a model of *what to look for* when reviewing your own real week. Compare every section to the [[_master-metacognitive-reflection-v1.0.0|template prompts]].

## 1 · Person Knowledge

> *What did I learn this week about how **I** think?*

[**Person-Insight**:: I consistently inflate my pre-analysis confidence on decision-type objects (mean drop: 4 → 2.7) but stay flat or rise on argument-type objects. This suggests I am over-confident about my own decisions and appropriately calibrated on third-party arguments — exactly the asymmetry [[Motivated Reasoning]] predicts.]

- I run 4 of 7 sessions before 8 AM. Quality is meaningfully higher in those (mean rigor 4.2) than in afternoon sessions (mean rigor 3.5). My time-of-day matters more than I had assumed.
- When the object is one I have an emotional stake in (own decisions, ethical arguments), my **Fairness** standard scores ~0.6 points lower than when the object is neutral (op-eds, third-party claims). This is the [[Self-Serving Bias]] showing up in the rubric.
- I tend to over-deliver on **Clarity** and under-deliver on **Depth**. I make the question crisp, then stop digging. This is a 12-week trend, not just this week.

---

## 2 · Task Knowledge

> *What did I learn about the **frameworks/tools themselves**?*

[**Task-Insight**:: FRISCO is dramatically more efficient than Paul-Elder for argument objects (mean 8 min vs 35 min) with comparable rigor scores (3.9 vs 4.0); I should default to FRISCO for arguments and reserve Paul-Elder for decisions and concepts I plan to take action on.]

- **FRISCO** is the right tool when the object is an *external argument* I am evaluating, not acting on.
- **Paul-Elder** is the right tool when the object is a *decision I will execute*, because section 1.7 (Implications) and 1.8 (Standpoint) force the action-readiness check.
- **SEE-I** is the right tool when I notice I am using a concept without being able to define it. The mastery self-check is the diagnostic, not the elaboration.
- I have under-used **Toulmin** and **Delphi**. Plan: deliberately rotate them in next month, two sessions each.

---

## 3 · Strategy Knowledge

> *Which thinking strategies, prompts, or moves were **most useful** this week?*

[**Strategy-That-Worked**:: The "disconfirming evidence search" subsection of Paul-Elder section 1.3 was the single highest-leverage move across the week; it changed my conclusion in 2 of 3 Paul-Elder sessions.]

- **What worked**: Disconfirming-evidence search; steelmanning in section 1.8; the pre/post confidence delta as a calibration signal.
- **What didn't work**: My SEE-I on [[Bayesian Updating]] (Friday) failed — mastery stayed at 2/2 because I didn't generate a transfer-domain example. Lesson: SEE-I on a concept I don't yet understand is a *diagnostic*, not a way to *acquire* the concept. I need to read first, then SEE-I.
- **Strategy to adopt next week**: Time-box every session strictly. Two sessions ran 50% over and their extra time didn't produce proportionally more rigor.

## 4 · Monitoring

> *How well did I notice in-the-moment what was working/failing?*

- **Times I caught myself mid-bias this week:** 4 (logged in real time inside sessions — confirmation bias ×2, sunk-cost rationalization ×1, novelty bias ×1).
- **Times I noticed the bias only on review:** 3 (one steelman I performed was a strawman of the position; two cases where I rated my own Depth higher than evidence warranted).
- **Trend:** Real-time monitoring is improving (was ~1/week three months ago), but **post-hoc detection** is improving faster, which means my baseline standards are rising even though my in-the-moment catching hasn't kept up. This is fine — accurate post-hoc review is what *trains* in-the-moment monitoring over months.

## 5 · Regulation

> *What will I change in **next week's** practice based on this review?*

[**Next-Week-Change-1**:: Schedule all 7 planned sessions before 10 AM. Predicted effect: +0.3 mean rigor.]
[**Next-Week-Change-2**:: When using SEE-I on a concept I rate <3/5 in self-knowledge, do a 10-minute read first, then SEE-I. Predicted effect: SEE-I sessions stop being mastery diagnostics for concepts I don't know and become mastery confirmations for concepts I do.]
[**Next-Week-Change-3**:: Run one Toulmin and one Delphi session to break tool monoculture. Predicted effect: build comfort with secondary frameworks before I need them.]

## 9 · Key Insight

> *The single most important thing from this week, in one paragraph.*

[**Key-Insight**:: I have been training Clarity, Logic, and Relevance — my already-strong standards — and avoiding Depth and Fairness, my weak ones. The 12-week rigor curve is rising mainly because I am getting better at things I was already good at. To break the plateau I need to deliberately work on the weak standards, not let weekly practice accumulate on the strong ones. This is the same pattern [[Deliberate Practice]] research describes as the boundary between *experienced* and *expert* practitioners: experienced ones get more reps; experts target the weakest link. I am currently the former.]

## How This Example Demonstrates Metacognitive Reflection

- **Sessions Under Review** — In the real template, Dataview auto-pulls the last week's `type: critical-thinking-practice` notes. The table is the *evidence base* for everything that follows.
- **Person / Task / Strategy Knowledge** — The classic three-part [[Flavell's Metacognitive Knowledge|Flavellian]] schema. Person = self-as-thinker; Task = the frameworks themselves; Strategy = which moves work when.
- **Monitoring + Regulation** — The [[Self-Regulated Learning]] core loop. Monitoring is detection; regulation is response. The template forces both.
- **Standards Trend** — In the real template, a DataviewJS query produces this table from session frontmatter. The point is to surface bottleneck standards that single sessions cannot reveal.
- **Virtues Inventory** — Pulled from the [[Paul-Elder Framework|Paul-Elder]] intellectual-virtues taxonomy. Checking these monthly prevents the practice from becoming purely technical.
- **Commitments** — Dated, falsifiable, ≤5 items. More than 5 means the reflection produced fantasies, not commitments.
- **Key Insight** — One paragraph. The discipline of compressing a week into one paragraph is itself the highest-leverage metacognitive move.
