# Local vs. Global Coherence

> [!definition] **Local vs. Global Coherence** *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> **Local coherence**: the connectedness of adjacent text units — whether each sentence links to its immediate neighbors. A textbase-level property. **Global coherence**: the unity and consistency of the situation model as a whole — whether all representation elements integrate into a non-contradictory simulation. A situation-model-level property. A text can be locally coherent throughout while failing to produce global coherence.
> 
> **See also:** [[textbase]], [[situation-model]], [[schema-theory]]

## Core Explanation

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> The founding monograph of the situation model framework. Introduces the three-level representation architecture, the textbase-situation model distinction, macrostructure, and the local/global coherence distinction. Required primary reading for anyone working in discourse comprehension.
> 
> **Recommended Sections:** Chapters 2 (propositional analysis), 5 (situation models), 8 (comprehension strategies).

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> Kintsch's comprehensive synthesis of 30 years of research on text comprehension, integrating propositional analysis, the Construction-Integration model, and extensions to learning from text and problem solving. The definitive C-I model account.
> 
> **Recommended Sections:** Chapters 1 (overview), 4 (C-I model), 6 (situation models and knowledge), 10 (learning from text).

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> The original Event Indexing Model paper. Concise but theoretically dense; provides the structural specification of situation model content across five dimensions.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> Comprehensive review of the situation model literature through the late 1990s. The most accessible survey of the empirical evidence base for the situation model as a distinct level of representation.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> The original formal presentation of the C-I model. More accessible than the 1998 book; the clearest statement of the two-phase architecture and its psychological motivations.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> Presents the Structure Building Framework with empirical support, including the suppression mechanism and individual differences evidence. The most direct treatment of why comprehension skill is partly determined by inhibitory capacity.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> The definitive statement of Constructionist Theory. Specifies which inference types are generated automatically (causal antecedents, goal-motives) versus selectively (predictive, elaborative).

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> Foundational study establishing the correlation between reading span (WMC measure) and reading comprehension. First rigorous demonstration that WMC constraints affect situation model construction quality.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> The seminal self-explanation study. Demonstrates that prompting readers to explain content produces substantially better comprehension (including more inference questions answered correctly) than re-reading. > [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> Foundational study demonstrating that causal connectivity — the number of causal connections a text event has — is the primary predictor of recall probability, more predictive than serial position. The empirical basis for the primacy of causal structure in situation model construction.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> Counterintuitive finding: high-knowledge readers learn more from low-coherence texts (they must generate more inferences, producing deeper processing); low-knowledge readers learn more from high-coherence texts (explicit coherence compensates for absent prior knowledge). Demonstrates the interaction between domain knowledge and text coherence in situation model construction.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> Classic demonstration of prior knowledge dependence in comprehension. The "washing clothes" passage: incomprehensible without context; highly comprehensible with it. Among the most elegant demonstrations of the schema-dependence of situation model construction.

> [!evidence] Supporting Evidence *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> Introduces the propositional analysis methodology and the macrostructure model. Essential historical context for understanding how the situation model framework emerged from the propositional tradition.

> The situation model is the cognitive gold standard for reading comprehension. It is not what a text says — it is what the text is *about*. Constructing one requires generating information beyond what the text explicitly states, integrating new content with prior knowledge, and building a coherent mental simulation of the described domain. All effective reading strategies work, ultimately, by facilitating this construction.

> [!example] **Application** *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> **Causal Chain Annotations (C-dimension):** Use directional markers (→) to make explicit the causal relationships between described events, mechanisms, or claims. When a text implies causation without stating it, writing the causal annotation IS the bridging inference made explicit and externalized: "[Event/state A] → [Event/state B] because [your generated causal explanation]." Causal annotations directly target the dimension of the situation model that most strongly predicts comprehension…

> [!example] **Application** *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> **Dimensional Gap Annotations (T, S, I, E-dimensions):** When you notice that one of the Event Indexing dimensions is unclear — you have lost track of when, where, who, or why — annotate the gap explicitly rather than reading over it: "T?: unclear when this follows from [prior event]" or "I?: unclear why this agent is pursuing this goal at this point." Gap annotations make comprehension failures explicit and located, converting vague confusion into specific, addressable deficits.

> [!example] **Application** *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> **Integration Annotations:** When you recognize a connection between current content and earlier text, other texts, or prior knowledge, annotate the connection immediately with a linking label: "CONNECTS TO: [[relevant-prior-concept]]" or "CONTRADICTS: [prior claim]." > [!example] **Application** *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> **Question Annotations:** The most powerful single annotation type for situation model construction. Rather than noting that something is interesting, note the question it raises: "WHY does this follow from the previous argument?" or "WHAT WOULD FALSIFY this claim?" or "HOW does this mechanism account for [counterexample you are generating]?" Question annotations activate elaborative inference generation in a way that assertive annotations do not — they create an obligation to generate the…

> [!example] **Application** *(from [[situation-models-text-comprehension-foundational-report-2026-03-29]])*
> **Compression Annotations:** After annotating a section, attempt to compress your marginal notes into a single sentence capturing the causal structure of the section. If the compression cannot be produced, your annotations captured detail, not structure. The compression annotation at the section end is the highest-level summary of your situation model for that section.

## References

- **van Dijk, T. A., & Kintsch, W. (1983). *Strategies in Discourse Comprehension*. Academic Press.**: The founding monograph of the situation model framework. Introduces the three-level representation architecture, the textbase-situation model distinction, macrostructure, and the local/global coherence distinction. Required primary reading for anyone working in discourse comprehension.

**Recommended Sections:** Chapters 2 (propositional analysis), 5 (situation models), 8 (comprehension strategies).
- **Kintsch, W. (1998). *Comprehension: A Paradigm for Cognition*. Cambridge University Press.**: Kintsch's comprehensive synthesis of 30 years of research on text comprehension, integrating propositional analysis, the Construction-Integration model, and extensions to learning from text and problem solving. The definitive C-I model account.

**Recommended Sections:** Chapters 1 (overview), 4 (C-I model), 6 (situation models and knowledge), 10 (learning from text).
- **Zwaan, R. A., Langston, M. C., & Graesser, A. C. (1995). The construction of situation models in narrative comprehension: An event-indexing model. *Psychological Science, 6*(5), 292–297.**: The original Event Indexing Model paper. Concise but theoretically dense; provides the structural specification of situation model content across five dimensions.
- **Zwaan, R. A., & Radvansky, G. A. (1998). Situation models in language comprehension and memory. *Psychological Bulletin, 123*(2), 162–185.**: Comprehensive review of the situation model literature through the late 1990s. The most accessible survey of the empirical evidence base for the situation model as a distinct level of representation.
- **Kintsch, W. (1988). The role of knowledge in discourse comprehension: A construction-integration model. *Psychological Review, 95*(2), 163–182.**: The original formal presentation of the C-I model. More accessible than the 1998 book; the clearest statement of the two-phase architecture and its psychological motivations.
- **Gernsbacher, M. A. (1990). *Language Comprehension as Structure Building*. Erlbaum.**: Presents the Structure Building Framework with empirical support, including the suppression mechanism and individual differences evidence. The most direct treatment of why comprehension skill is partly determined by inhibitory capacity.
- **Graesser, A. C., Singer, M., & Trabasso, T. (1994). Constructing inferences during narrative text comprehension. *Psychological Review, 101*(3), 371–395.**: The definitive statement of Constructionist Theory. Specifies which inference types are generated automatically (causal antecedents, goal-motives) versus selectively (predictive, elaborative).
- **Daneman, M., & Carpenter, P. A. (1980). Individual differences in working memory and reading. *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 19*(4), 450–466.**: Foundational study establishing the correlation between reading span (WMC measure) and reading comprehension. First rigorous demonstration that WMC constraints affect situation model construction quality.
- **Chi, M. T. H., de Leeuw, N., Chiu, M.-H., & LaVancher, C. (1994). Eliciting self-explanations improves understanding. *Cognitive Science, 18*(3), 439–477.**: The seminal self-explanation study. Demonstrates that prompting readers to explain content produces substantially better comprehension (including more inference questions answered correctly) than re-reading. Primary empirical support for self-explanation as a situation model construction strategy.
- **van den Broek, P., Risden, K., & Husebye-Hartmann, E. (1995). The role of readers' standards for coherence in the generation of inferences during reading. In R. F. Lorch & E. J. O'Brien (Eds.), *Sources of coherence in reading* (pp. 353–373). Erlbaum.**: Presents the Landscape Model and the standards of coherence concept. Explains how individual differences in coherence standards produce different rates of inference generation and different situation model qualities.
- **Trabasso, T., & van den Broek, P. (1985). Causal thinking and the representation of narrative events. *Journal of Memory and Language, 24*(5), 612–630.**: Foundational study demonstrating that causal connectivity — the number of causal connections a text event has — is the primary predictor of recall probability, more predictive than serial position. The empirical basis for the primacy of causal structure in situation model construction.
- **McNamara, D. S., & Kintsch, W. (1996). Learning from texts: Effects of prior knowledge and text coherence. *Discourse Processes, 22*(3), 247–288.**: Counterintuitive finding: high-knowledge readers learn more from low-coherence texts (they must generate more inferences, producing deeper processing); low-knowledge readers learn more from high-coherence texts (explicit coherence compensates for absent prior knowledge). Demonstrates the interaction between domain knowledge and text coherence in situation model construction.
- **Bransford, J. D., & Johnson, M. K. (1972). Contextual prerequisites for understanding: Some investigations of comprehension and recall. *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 11*(6), 717–726.**: Classic demonstration of prior knowledge dependence in comprehension. The "washing clothes" passage: incomprehensible without context; highly comprehensible with it. Among the most elegant demonstrations of the schema-dependence of situation model construction.
- **Kintsch, W., & van Dijk, T. A. (1978). Toward a model of text comprehension and production. *Psychological Review, 85*(5), 363–394.**: Introduces the propositional analysis methodology and the macrostructure model. Essential historical context for understanding how the situation model framework emerged from the propositional tradition.
