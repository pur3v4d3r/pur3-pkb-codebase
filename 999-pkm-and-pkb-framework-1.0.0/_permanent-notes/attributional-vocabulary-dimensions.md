# Attributional Vocabulary Dimensions

> [!definition] **Attributional Vocabulary Dimensions** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> **Definition:** The three dimensions along which causal attributions vary and to which vocabulary items commit: *locus* (internal vs. external — whether the cause is within or outside the learner), *stability* (stable vs. unstable — whether the cause persists over time), and *controllability* (controllable vs. uncontrollable — whether the learner can modify the cause). Vocabulary pre-loads dimensional commitments: ability vocabulary encodes internal-stable-uncontrollable; strategy vocabulary encodes internal-unstable-controllable.
> 
> **Cross-References:** [[attribution-theory]], [[bernard-weiner]], [[attribution-style]], [[attribution-retraining]]

> **A:** **Q:** What is the Attributional Vocabulary Loop, and in which direction does it most commonly self-amplify?

It most commonly amplifies destructively because poor outcomes are more likely to produce ability attribution vocabulary (which forecloses the loop) than strategy attribution vocabulary (which keeps it open).

> **A:** **Q:** What does SDT predict will happen when identical learning activities are framed in controlled regulatory language versus autonomous regulatory language?

> [!flashcard] **Card 6** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 6
> **A:** **Q:** Why is positive ability vocabulary ("I'm naturally good at this") also motivationally problematic, according to the Self-Efficacy Calibration Mechanism?
**A:** Because it encodes efficacy as stable and uncontrollable — just like negative ability vocabulary, only in the positive direction. This produces efficacy fragility: when difficulty arrives (inevitable in academic learning), there is no strategy-and-effort vocabulary available to interpret the experience except as evidence that the original ability attribution was wrong. Positive ability vocabulary reduces strategic investment and creates brittle confidence.

> [!flashcard] **Card 7** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 7
> **A:** **Q:** What does "monitoring vocabulary sensitization" mean, and why does it matter for reading comprehension?
**A:** The process by which goal vocabulary in the forethought phase tunes the monitoring system to track specific comprehension cues during reading. Mastery-oriented goal vocabulary sensitizes monitoring to understanding cues (noticing non-comprehension, noticing inferential gaps); completion-oriented vocabulary sensitizes monitoring to completion cues (page progress, time). Only understanding-cue monitoring enables effective error detection and repair, so forethought goal vocabulary directly determines comprehension quality.
**Source:** Phase III, AGT Evidence / Phase IV
**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Type:** Process + Connection
**Tags:** #metacognitive-monitoring #agt #comprehension

> [!flashcard] **Card 8** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 8
> **A:** **Q:** What is the Motivational Lexicon, and how does it differ from domain vocabulary?
**A:** The Motivational Lexicon is the vocabulary repertoire available for narrating, evaluating, and directing one's own motivational and cognitive engagement during academic learning — specifically in the attributional, regulatory, and goal-construction registers. Domain vocabulary is for understanding content; the Motivational Lexicon is for understanding and directing your motivational experience of engaging with that content. Both are cognitive resources; the Motivational Lexicon enables motivational metacognition that is impossible without it.
**Source:** Phase VI, Original Synthesis
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Type:** Definition + Distinction

> [!flashcard] **Card 9** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 9
> **A:** **Q:** How does the vocabulary specificity of an intermediate reading goal affect comprehension?
**A:** Generic intermediate goal vocabulary ("I want to understand this section") fails to generate monitoring checkpoints because there is no moment when you know whether you "understand." Specific generative vocabulary ("I want to be able to explain the main argument's structure" or "I want to be able to critique the central assumption") generates precise checkpoints: you know exactly when you have or have not achieved them. Specificity in goal vocabulary therefore directly determines monitoring sensitivity and error-detection frequency.
**Source:** Phase V, Tool 4 / Phase IV Mechanism 3
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Type:** Application + Process
**Tags:** #goal-construction #monitoring #comprehension

> [!flashcard] **Card 10** *(from [[inner-speech-attribution-self-efficacy-focused-analysis-2026-03-25]])*
> **Q:** Seed 10
> **A:** **Q:** What structural analogue connects the vocabulary-modifiability principle from Attribution Theory to clinical therapeutic dialogue?
**A:** In both attribution research and clinical frameworks (narrative therapy, CBT), the key structural move is from trait vocabulary (what someone *is* — stable, uncontrollable) to process vocabulary (what someone *does* or *how they approached something* — unstable, controllable). In academic reading: "I'm not good at this" → "My strategy for this text type needs development." In therapy: "I am an anxious person" → "I experience anxiety in specific contexts." Same dimensional reassignment principle; different domain surface content.
**Source:** Phase VII, FAR Transfer, Abstract Principle 1

**Type:** Connection + Transfer
