#### 2. [DEFINITION] Judgment of Learning — JOL (Cognitive Psychology, Nelson & Narens 1990; Dunlosky & Metcalfe 2009) *(Line 163)*

> [!definition] Judgment of Learning — JOL (Cognitive Psychology, Nelson & Narens 1990; Dunlosky & Metcalfe 2009)
> A metacognitive monitoring process in which a learner predicts, at the time of or shortly after studying material, how likely they are to remember that material on a future test. JOLs are the primary input by which the metacognitive monitoring system guides study-time allocation. A critical finding: *immediate* JOLs (made right after study) are systematically inflated by perceptual fluency, while *delayed* JOLs (made 24+ hours after study) are significantly more accurate predictors of actual retention. This delay-dependency has direct implications for when PKB review workflows should solicit confidence judgments.

#### 3. [DEFINITION] Feeling of Knowing — FOK (Cognitive Psychology, Hart 1965; Nelson & Narens 1990) *(Line 166)*

> [!definition] Feeling of Knowing — FOK (Cognitive Psychology, Hart 1965; Nelson & Narens 1990)
> A metacognitive monitoring judgment about whether one would be able to recognize or recall something that one cannot currently retrieve. FOK provides the monitoring system with information about the accessibility of knowledge, distinct from its current availability. A high FOK on material that cannot currently be recalled signals "I know this, I just can't access it right now" — guiding the monitoring system to schedule retrieval practice rather than re-study. A low FOK signals genuine knowledge absence. PKB design should distinguish FOK signals from JOL signals in review workflows.

#### 4. [DEFINITION] Ease of Learning — EOL (Cognitive Psychology, Underwood 1966; Nelson & Narens 1990) *(Line 169)*

> [!definition] Ease of Learning — EOL (Cognitive Psychology, Underwood 1966; Nelson & Narens 1990)
> A metacognitive prediction, made before studying material, about how easy or difficult it will be to learn. EOL judgments guide the forethought phase of self-regulated learning — how much time to allocate, how to sequence study, what scaffold to build first. EOL accuracy is typically poor because it relies on prior knowledge about a domain rather than direct experience with the specific material, which is why learners consistently underestimate difficulty in domains where their prior knowledge is low.

#### 5. [DEFINITION] Retrospective Confidence Judgment — RCJ (Cognitive Psychology, Gigerenzer, Hoffrage & Kleinbölting 1991) *(Line 172)*

> [!definition] Retrospective Confidence Judgment — RCJ (Cognitive Psychology, Gigerenzer, Hoffrage & Kleinbölting 1991)
> A confidence rating assigned to an answer already given. Unlike JOLs (which predict future memory), RCJs assess confidence in a current response — the primary mechanism for detecting potential errors and triggering self-correction. RCJs are particularly useful in PKB active-recall workflows: when a learner attempts to recall a concept from a note, the RCJ they assign to their answer determines whether they flag the note for re-study or pass it through.

#### 6. [CROSS-DOMAIN-CONNECTION] Monitoring Processes ↔ Zimmerman's SRL Phases *(Line 177)*

> [!cross-domain-connection] Monitoring Processes ↔ Zimmerman's SRL Phases
> There is a structural correspondence between the four monitoring processes and Zimmerman's three-phase SRL cycle that illuminates how they should be distributed across PKB workflow design. EOL judgments belong in the **forethought phase** — they guide planning and resource allocation before study. JOLs and FOK judgments belong in the **performance phase** — they guide real-time and post-study decisions about what to review and how deeply. RCJs belong in the **self-reflection phase** — they enable evaluation of response quality and calibration of confidence. Distributing monitoring prompts according to this structural correspondence is more effective than clustering them all at a single point in the workflow.

#### 7. [DEFINITION] Zimmerman's Three-Phase SRL Cycle (Educational Psychology, Zimmerman 1989, 2000, 2002) *(Line 184)*

> [!definition] Zimmerman's Three-Phase SRL Cycle (Educational Psychology, Zimmerman 1989, 2000, 2002)
> A model of self-regulated learning in which effective learning proceeds through three recursive phases: (1) **Forethought Phase** — task analysis (goal-setting, strategic planning) and motivational beliefs (self-efficacy, goal orientation); (2) **Performance Phase** — self-control (self-instruction, imagery, attention focusing) and self-observation (self-monitoring, self-recording); (3) **Self-Reflection Phase** — self-judgment (self-evaluation, causal attribution) and self-reaction (self-satisfaction, adaptive responses). The cycle is recursive: the self-reflection phase feeds into revised forethought for the next learning episode. Critically, the phases must all be engaged — learners who excel at performance-phase execution but skip the self-reflection phase fail to update their strategies and plateau.

#### 8. [KEY-CLAIM] The Three-Phase Design Gap *(Line 189)*

> [!key-claim] The Three-Phase Design Gap
> The most common structural failure in PKB design is providing excellent support for content engagement (the performance phase) while providing little or no structural support for forethought (planning what to engage with, estimating difficulty, setting learning goals) and self-reflection (evaluating what was learned, attributing difficulties, revising strategies). This means that even disciplined PKB users are chronically operating with one-third of the SRL infrastructure they need for effective self-regulation.

#### 9. [DEFINITION] Implementation Intentions (Behavioral Science, Gollwitzer 1993, 1999; Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006) *(Line 196)*

> [!definition] Implementation Intentions (Behavioral Science, Gollwitzer 1993, 1999; Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)
> A self-regulatory strategy in which a person supplements a goal intention ("I intend to do X") with a situational specification ("I intend to do X when situation Y occurs"). Formulated as explicit if-then plans: "If situation Y arises, then I will initiate behavior X." Gollwitzer and Sheeran's (2006) meta-analysis across 94 studies found that implementation intentions increased goal achievement with an effect size of d = 0.65 — a large effect by behavioral science standards. The mechanism is the creation of a mental link between a situational cue and a behavioral response, such that the cue automatically activates the behavior without requiring deliberate retrieval of the goal intention. Implementation intentions do not strengthen motivation; they automate the detection of the appropriate moment to act.

#### 10. [ANALYTICAL-INSIGHT] The Cue-Specificity Requirement for Monitoring Automation *(Line 201)*

> [!analytical-insight] The Cue-Specificity Requirement for Monitoring Automation
> Implementation intentions research reveals a specificity principle that is directly actionable for PKB design: the narrower and more situation-specific the if-then trigger, the more reliable the behavioral automaticity. "When I finish reading a note" is too vague — it lacks a physical or digital marker that unambiguously signals the trigger moment. "When the cursor reaches the end of a note template and I encounter the `confidence::` metadata field" is a precise situational cue embedded in the PKB architecture itself. The PKB design task is not to persuade users to monitor, nor to remind them to monitor, but to engineer the specific situational cues that reliably activate monitoring at the moments when it is most valuable.

#### 11. [DEFINITION] Fluency Illusion (Cognitive Psychology, Bjork 1994; Jacoby, Kelley & Dywan 1989; Koriat & Bjork 2005) *(Line 204)*

> [!definition] Fluency Illusion (Cognitive Psychology, Bjork 1994; Jacoby, Kelley & Dywan 1989; Koriat & Bjork 2005)
> The systematic metacognitive error in which the perceptual ease with which information is processed — its fluency — is misattributed as evidence of knowing. When we re-read familiar text, processing fluency is high because pattern recognition is fast. This high fluency is experienced as familiarity, and familiarity is misread by the monitoring system as understanding or mastery. The crucial empirical finding is that fluency is determined by perceptual factors (repetition, clarity, prior exposure) rather than by depth of understanding — meaning high-fluency experiences reliably overestimate actual learning and lead to premature termination of study and under-allocation of review time.

#### 14. [EVIDENCE] The Re-Reading vs. Testing Effect (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Karpicke & Blunt 2011) *(Line 229)*

> [!evidence] The Re-Reading vs. Testing Effect (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Karpicke & Blunt 2011)
> In what became one of the most influential studies in educational psychology, Roediger and Karpicke (2006) compared four study conditions: (1) study once, (2) study four times, (3) study once then test three times, (4) study then create concept maps. On a recall test one week later, the retrieval practice condition (3) dramatically outperformed all others. Karpicke and Blunt (2011) further demonstrated that free-recall practice produced better learning than elaborate concept mapping. Critically for monitoring: subjects in the repeated-study condition *predicted* they would perform better than the retrieval-practice condition immediately after studying, demonstrating that the fluency illusion was actively distorting their monitoring accuracy.

#### 15. [WHAT-THE-EVIDENCE-SUGGESTS] The Re-Reading Default as a Structural Monitoring Failure *(Line 234)*

> [!what-the-evidence-suggests] The Re-Reading Default as a Structural Monitoring Failure
> The testing effect literature, taken together with the fluency illusion research, suggests that the majority of time spent in "review" by PKB users may not be producing the learning that monitoring is reporting. The tragedy of this situation is that it is not visible from inside the experience — the fluency illusion makes re-reading feel productive. This means that users without structural prompts to switch to retrieval-based review will not spontaneously do so, because their monitoring system is telling them the current approach is working. Structural embedding is needed precisely because motivation and awareness are insufficient when the monitoring system itself is compromised by fluency.

#### 16. [EVIDENCE] Implementation Intentions and Self-Regulatory Depletion (Sheeran et al. 2005; Muraven & Baumeister 2000) *(Line 243)*

> [!evidence] Implementation Intentions and Self-Regulatory Depletion (Sheeran et al. 2005; Muraven & Baumeister 2000)
> Sheeran and colleagues found that implementation intentions maintained their efficacy even when participants' self-regulatory resources were depleted through a prior demanding task. This finding is particularly relevant to PKB use: monitoring is most important precisely when cognitive resources are most taxed — during active learning, problem-solving, or note-taking that places high demands on working memory. Standard goal intentions ("I will monitor my understanding") require volitional activation under exactly these conditions of resource depletion. Implementation intentions embedded in PKB structure do not — the cue activates the behavior automatically, decoupled from the volitional resource pool.

#### 17. [TENSION-IDENTIFIED] The Automation Paradox: Can Metacognition Be Automated? *(Line 248)*

> [!tension-identified] The Automation Paradox: Can Metacognition Be Automated?
> There is a genuine intellectual tension in the application of implementation intentions and habit formation science to metacognitive monitoring. Metacognition, by definition, requires awareness and deliberate reflection — can it become automatic without losing its essence? The tension is not merely semantic. There is evidence (Schraw 2001; Veenman, Van Hout-Wolters & Afflerbach 2006) that highly automatized monitoring heuristics can become context-insensitive "pseudometacognition" — performance of monitoring behaviors without genuine reflective engagement. The implementation intentions mechanism may create monitoring behavior without monitoring substance. Resolving this tension requires distinguishing between the *initiation* of monitoring (which can and should be automated via situational cues) and the *quality* of monitoring (which requires genuine cognitive engagement that cannot be automated). PKB design should automate the *triggers* for monitoring while preserving the effortfulness of the monitoring response itself.

#### 18. [WHAT-THE-EVIDENCE-SUGGESTS] Specificity Is Not Optional — It Is the Active Ingredient *(Line 257)*

> [!what-the-evidence-suggests] Specificity Is Not Optional — It Is the Active Ingredient
> The self-questioning and self-explanation literatures converge on a finding that has direct implications for PKB reflection prompt design: the specificity and cognitive demand of a monitoring prompt determines whether it elicits genuine monitoring or fluency-based pseudo-monitoring. Generic prompts ("how well do you understand this?") are insufficient and may even be counterproductive by providing an easy fluency-based exit. Effective monitoring prompts must require cognitive operations that cannot be satisfied by fluency recognition — recall, explanation, application, or connection. This means that the specific wording of embedded reflection prompts in note templates is a consequential design decision, not an afterthought.

#### 21. [ANALYTICAL-INSIGHT] The Comfort Gradient: How PKB Design Creates Monitoring Resistance *(Line 284)*

> [!analytical-insight] The Comfort Gradient: How PKB Design Creates Monitoring Resistance
> What we see here is a structural comfort gradient in which the behaviors that feel productive (reading, capturing, linking) are systematically easier and more immediately rewarding than the behaviors that are genuinely productive (active recall, confidence assessment, error detection, strategy revision). This gradient is not random — it emerges from the interaction between the natural properties of fluency and the typical PKB design's optimization for capture throughput. Designing for metacognitive monitoring means deliberately inverting this gradient at key moments: making the monitoring action the structurally easier choice, not the structurally harder one.

#### 22. [CROSS-DOMAIN-CONNECTION] Gollwitzer's Implementation Intentions ↔ Dewey's Reflective Triggers *(Line 293)*

> [!cross-domain-connection] Gollwitzer's Implementation Intentions ↔ Dewey's Reflective Triggers
> There is a structural parallel between Gollwitzer's mechanism and Dewey's account of what initiates reflective inquiry. Dewey argued that reflection is triggered not by a deliberate decision to reflect but by a "felt difficulty" — a moment of cognitive resistance where the current flow of experience is interrupted. Dewey's felt difficulty is, functionally, a situational cue that triggers the metacognitive monitoring process. This parallel suggests that implementation intentions and Deweyan reflective triggers operate through the same underlying mechanism: an environmental signal interrupts automatic processing and redirects attention to the quality of ongoing cognitive activity. PKB design can engineer Deweyan felt difficulties by structurally placing monitoring prompts at moments of natural cognitive transition — between reading and writing, between capture and filing, between study and retrieval.

#### 23. [ANALYTICAL-INSIGHT] The Double-Assessment Design Pattern *(Line 302)*

> [!analytical-insight] The Double-Assessment Design Pattern
> The delayed JOL effect implies a specific PKB design pattern that goes beyond simple confidence tracking. A monitoring-aware PKB should maintain two confidence signals for each note or concept: a **capture confidence** (assessed at note creation or immediately after first study — expected to be inflated by fluency) and a **retrieval confidence** (assessed at the first retrieval practice attempt after a delay — expected to be more accurate). The *discrepancy* between these two assessments is diagnostic information: large discrepancies signal nodes where the fluency illusion is operating most powerfully and where additional retrieval practice is most needed. This discrepancy signal is more actionable than either confidence estimate alone.

#### 24. [CROSS-DOMAIN-CONNECTION] Zimmerman's Forethought Phase ↔ Dewey's Problem Formulation *(Line 311)*

> [!cross-domain-connection] Zimmerman's Forethought Phase ↔ Dewey's Problem Formulation
> Dewey's theory of reflective inquiry begins with *problem formulation* — the precise articulation of what one does not yet know or understand that has created the felt difficulty. This maps directly onto Zimmerman's forethought phase: both require the learner to explicitly define what they are trying to accomplish before engaging with material. The parallel reveals a design principle: the PKB's review session initiation should require a brief problem formulation moment — not a general note about "reviewing material," but a specific statement of the learning question being pursued. This forethought record provides the evaluative standard that makes genuine self-reflection possible and transforms the review session from passive scrolling into directed inquiry.

#### 25. [ANALYTICAL-INSIGHT] Structural Prompts as False-Signal Interrupters *(Line 322)*

> [!analytical-insight] Structural Prompts as False-Signal Interrupters
> Implementation-intention-based monitoring prompts embedded in PKB structure work not by improving the quality of the monitoring system itself but by routing the monitoring process around the fluency illusion. When a note template requires the learner to answer "Explain this concept's mechanism in your own words before rating your confidence," the prompt forces a cognitive operation (generation) that cannot be satisfied by fluency recognition. The monitoring response that results — after the learner discovers what they can and cannot explain — is based on genuine knowledge access rather than perceptual familiarity. The structural prompt is not replacing metacognitive effort with automation; it is ensuring that metacognitive effort is directed at the right cognitive operations.

#### 27. [BEST-PRACTICE] Template Architecture for Structural Priority *(Line 344)*

> [!best-practice] Template Architecture for Structural Priority

#### 32. [ORIGINAL-SYNTHESIS] The Structural Metacognition Principle *(Line 401)*

> [!original-synthesis] The Structural Metacognition Principle
> The fundamental insight of this cross-domain synthesis is that metacognitive monitoring is a default-dependent behavior: what monitoring users perform is determined primarily by what the system makes easiest to do, not by what users know about monitoring's importance. This is the **Structural Metacognition Principle**: *the monitoring behaviors that will occur in PKB practice are those that have been made structurally prior, not those that have been made motivationally salient.*
> 
> Three corollaries follow. First, any monitoring prompt that requires deliberate effort to initiate will be performed inconsistently and will degrade under cognitive load, regardless of the user's metacognitive awareness. Second, monitoring prompts that interrupt the primary workflow at natural transition points — leveraging Gollwitzer's situational specificity mechanism and Dewey's felt-difficulty trigger structure — will be performed far more reliably than those appended to optional workflow sections. Third, the most powerful lever for improving monitoring quality is not education about monitoring's importance (which most PKB users already have in abundance) but architectural change to the PKB itself — specifically, redesigning the note template, review workflow, and session initialization structure to place monitoring actions at structurally unavoidable positions.
> 
> This synthesis cannot be derived from any single contributing discipline. Cognitive psychology provides the monitoring processes and the fluency illusion mechanism but not the structural design response. Educational psychology provides the SRL framework but not the implementation science for closing the intention-behavior gap. Behavioral science provides implementation intentions and habit formation theory but not the metacognitive content they should be applied to. Educational philosophy provides the reflective inquiry framework but not the mechanism for making reflection a daily structural discipline rather than an occasional practice. The Structural Metacognition Principle emerges specifically from the intersection of all four — it is what becomes visible when you look across the disciplines simultaneously.

#### 35. [DEFINITION] Metacognitive Monitoring (Cognitive Psychology, Flavell 1979; Nelson & Narens 1990) *(Line 475)*

> [!definition] Metacognitive Monitoring (Cognitive Psychology, Flavell 1979; Nelson & Narens 1990)
> The ongoing process by which the metacognitive system assesses the current state of cognitive activity, knowledge, and understanding, producing signals (JOLs, FOK, RCJs) that guide the control system's allocation of resources and selection of strategies. Distinct from metacognitive knowledge (knowing about cognition in general) and metacognitive control (taking action based on monitoring outputs). Monitoring is the *detection* function; control is the *response* function.

#### 36. [DEFINITION] Fluency Illusion (Cognitive Psychology, Bjork 1994; Jacoby et al. 1989; Koriat & Bjork 2005) *(Line 478)*

> [!definition] Fluency Illusion (Cognitive Psychology, Bjork 1994; Jacoby et al. 1989; Koriat & Bjork 2005)
> The systematic metacognitive error in which perceptual processing fluency — the ease with which information is recognized — is misattributed as evidence of understanding or mastery. High fluency arises from familiarity and prior exposure rather than from depth of comprehension, causing the monitoring system to systematically overestimate actual learning when learners engage in re-reading. The central mechanism by which passive review fails as a learning strategy.

#### 37. [DEFINITION] Delayed JOL Effect (Cognitive Psychology, Dunlosky & Nelson 1992; Kimball & Metcalfe 2003) *(Line 481)*

> [!definition] Delayed JOL Effect (Cognitive Psychology, Dunlosky & Nelson 1992; Kimball & Metcalfe 2003)
> The empirical finding that Judgments of Learning made 24+ hours after studying a given item are significantly more accurate predictors of subsequent recall than JOLs made immediately after studying. The mechanism: immediate JOLs are contaminated by the recency and activation of the study material, producing inflated confidence estimates; delayed JOLs are based on genuine memory accessibility once recency activation has decayed.

#### 38. [DEFINITION] Implementation Intentions (Behavioral Science, Gollwitzer 1993, 1999) *(Line 484)*

> [!definition] Implementation Intentions (Behavioral Science, Gollwitzer 1993, 1999)
> Self-regulatory plans formulated as explicit if-then propositions: "If situation Y occurs, then I will perform behavior X." Distinct from goal intentions ("I intend to achieve X"), which require deliberate volitional activation at each opportunity. Implementation intentions create automatic situation-behavior links through mental simulation of the if-then scenario, producing large effects on goal attainment (d = 0.65 across 94 studies, Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006) that are robust to self-regulatory resource depletion.

#### 39. [DEFINITION] Forethought Phase (Educational Psychology, Zimmerman 2000, 2002) *(Line 487)*

> [!definition] Forethought Phase (Educational Psychology, Zimmerman 2000, 2002)
> The first phase of Zimmerman's Self-Regulated Learning cycle, encompassing task analysis (goal-setting and strategic planning) and motivational belief activation (self-efficacy assessment, goal orientation). The forethought phase determines the evaluative standard against which performance will be assessed in the self-reflection phase; without forethought activation, the self-reflection phase lacks a standard and defaults to fluency-based assessment.

#### 40. [DEFINITION] Structural Metacognition Principle (This Report — Original Synthesis) *(Line 490)*

> [!definition] Structural Metacognition Principle (This Report — Original Synthesis)
> The finding that metacognitive monitoring behaviors in PKB practice are determined primarily by structural defaults — what the system makes easiest to do — rather than by users' knowledge of monitoring's importance or their motivation to monitor. Corollaries: (1) monitoring prompts requiring deliberate effortful initiation will be performed inconsistently; (2) monitoring prompts at structurally unavoidable positions will be performed reliably; (3) the primary lever for improving monitoring practice is architectural redesign, not metacognitive education.

#### 41. [DEFINITION] Embedded Monitoring Architecture (This Report — Original Framework) *(Line 493)*

> [!definition] Embedded Monitoring Architecture (This Report — Original Framework)
> A PKB design framework implementing the Structural Metacognition Principle across four temporal scales: note-level (monitoring gateway in template, double-assessment confidence tracking), session-level (forethought initialization template), daily-level (structured monitoring log), and weekly/monthly-level (SRL self-reflection reviews). Creates a complete structural environment for metacognitive monitoring by making monitoring the structurally prior action at each scale.

#### 42. [DEFINITION] Calibration Discrepancy (This Report — Design Pattern) *(Line 496)*

> [!definition] Calibration Discrepancy (This Report — Design Pattern)
> The computed difference between a note's capture-confidence rating (assigned at creation, expected to be fluency-contaminated) and its retrieval-confidence rating (assigned at first retrieval practice attempt after 24+ hour delay, expected to be more accurate). High calibration discrepancy indicates a fluency illusion hot-spot: a concept where the user's monitoring system is significantly overestimating actual knowledge. Calibration discrepancy sorted by magnitude provides a structurally generated review priority queue.

#### 43. [DEFINITION] Monitoring Gap (This Report — Diagnostic Construct) *(Line 499)*

> [!definition] Monitoring Gap (This Report — Diagnostic Construct)
> The systematic divergence between (1) a PKB user's conceptual knowledge about the importance and mechanisms of metacognitive monitoring and (2) their actual monitoring behaviors in daily PKB practice. The monitoring gap is the primary phenomenon this report addresses; its cause is structural (PKB designs that make monitoring effortful) rather than motivational.

#### 44. [DEFINITION] Pseudometacognition (Metacognition Research, Schraw 2001; Veenman et al. 2006) *(Line 502)*

> [!definition] Pseudometacognition (Metacognition Research, Schraw 2001; Veenman et al. 2006)
> The performance of metacognitive monitoring behaviors — answering reflection prompts, assigning confidence ratings, reviewing learning logs — without genuine cognitive engagement in the monitoring process. Pseudometacognition is possible when monitoring prompts are sufficiently generic to be satisfied by fluency-based responses rather than requiring active knowledge access and explanation. Represents a failure mode of structural monitoring embedding when prompt specificity is insufficient.

#### 45. [DEFINITION] Forethought-Reflection Closure (This Report — Design Principle) *(Line 505)*

> [!definition] Forethought-Reflection Closure (This Report — Design Principle)
> The structural pairing of a session forethought record (stating the learning goal, estimating difficulty, specifying a success criterion) with a session self-reflection record (evaluating whether the goal was met, assessing actual vs. estimated difficulty, revising strategy). The closure of this loop is what makes the SRL self-reflection phase genuinely evaluative rather than impressionistic — the forethought record provides the standard; the reflection record provides the assessment against it.

#### 57. [METHODOLOGY-AND-SOURCES] Research Grounding for This Report *(Line 549)*

> [!methodology-and-sources] Research Grounding for This Report
> This report draws on four distinct empirical and theoretical traditions. **Cognitive psychology** contributes the monitoring process taxonomy (JOL, FOK, EOL, RCJ) from Nelson & Narens' formally specified two-level model, the fluency illusion from Bjork's desirable difficulties research and Jacoby et al.'s perceptual fluency work, and the delayed-JOL effect from Dunlosky & Nelson's experimental studies. These findings are empirically well-established with strong replication records.
> 
> **Educational psychology** contributes Zimmerman's self-regulated learning model, which has been validated across decades and multiple learning contexts with consistent findings. King's self-questioning research and Chi et al.'s self-explanation work are empirically established in educational contexts.
> 
> **Behavioral science** contributes Gollwitzer and Sheeran's implementation intentions research (meta-analytic effect size d = 0.65 across 94 studies) and Lally et al.'s habit formation empirical research. These represent strong empirical findings directly applicable to behavior change in PKB contexts, though the transfer to PKB-specific monitoring behaviors has not been directly tested — this is Claude's analytical extension.
> 
> **Educational philosophy** contributes Dewey's reflective inquiry framework, which is theoretical rather than empirical but has been influential and broadly consistent with the cognitive and behavioral science findings.
> 
> Three types of claims are present in this report and should be distinguished: (1) **Empirically established**: the fluency illusion mechanism, delayed JOL accuracy, testing effect superiority, implementation intentions effect size — all directly supported by experimental research; (2) **Theoretically well-grounded**: Zimmerman's SRL three-phase structure, Nelson & Narens' monitoring-control loop architecture — supported by converging evidence across studies; (3) **Claude's original analytical synthesis**: the Structural Metacognition Principle, the Embedded Monitoring Architecture design framework, the monitoring gateway template concept, the double-assessment design pattern, and the application of implementation intentions to PKB structural design — these are this report's genuine cross-domain contributions, grounded in the evidence above but not directly tested as PKB design interventions.
