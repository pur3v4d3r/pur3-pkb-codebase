# 🧭 Map of Content — Self-Determination Theory & Motivation

> [!abstract] Domain Overview
> Self-Determination Theory (SDT) is the most empirically supported macro-theory of human motivation, organizing decades of research around basic psychological needs, the quality (not just quantity) of motivation, and the conditions that support or thwart flourishing. This MOC also covers broader motivation frameworks that interact with or extend SDT.

[**SDT-Core-Architecture**:: A macro-theory composed of six mini-theories (CET, OIT, BPNT, COT, GCT, RMT) organized around three fundamental claims: (1) humans have innate needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness; (2) motivation varies in quality along an internalization continuum; (3) social contexts support or thwart needs, producing flourishing or ill-being.]
