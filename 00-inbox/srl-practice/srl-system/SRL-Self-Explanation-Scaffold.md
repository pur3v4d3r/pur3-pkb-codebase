# 🔬 Self-Explanation Scaffold

> [!abstract] Purpose
> This scaffold guides the [[Elaborative Interrogation|self-explanation]] process for passages requiring the deepest processing — dense theoretical arguments, multi-step mechanisms, complex distinctions. Use it during the [[performance-phase]] when your Forethought process goals specify self-explanation at section boundaries. Based on [[Chi-et-al.|Chi et al.'s (1994)]] self-explanation research: self-explanation forces integration of new information with [[prior-knowledge|prior knowledge]], which is the mechanism that produces genuine understanding rather than surface familiarity. See [[the-fluency-illusion]].

> [!tip] When to Use
> - Dense theoretical sections where you notice comprehension slipping
> - Multi-step mechanism descriptions where causal chains are complex
> - Sections containing key distinctions that require precise understanding
> - Any passage flagged in your Forethought Protocol as requiring deep processing
> - When you catch yourself re-reading without generating new understanding

### 3. Evidence & Argument Assessment

**What evidence or argument does the author use?** *(Summarized, not transcribed)*
> 

**What type of evidence is this?**

- [ ] Theoretical argument (logical derivation from premises)
- [ ] Case study / exemplification

- [ ] Authority appeal / consensus claim
- [ ] Analogical reasoning

**How strong is this evidence for the claim?** *(1–10):* ___

### 4. Assumption Detection

**What is assumed but not stated?** *(What must be true for this argument to hold?)*

**Are any of these assumptions questionable?**

### 7. Compression Test *(The Two-Sentence Explanation)*

> [!warning] This IS the comprehension test.
> If you cannot explain this section to a skeptical colleague in two sentences, you have not yet understood it. The inability to compress is diagnostic of incomplete processing. See [[the-generativity-diagnostic]].

**If I explained this section to a skeptical colleague in two sentences:**
> Sentence 1: 
> Sentence 2: 

**Did I struggle with this?** Yes / No
*(If yes — flag for Cold Reconstruction in Self-Reflection and attribute to a specific comprehension gap)*

---

> [!tip] After Completing This Scaffold
> - If you completed it fluently → the section is well-understood; move on
> - If you struggled at Steps 2 or 7 → flag this section for Cold Reconstruction in Self-Reflection
> - If you couldn't complete Step 4 → your understanding may be surface-level; re-read with attention to the author's reasoning, not just their conclusions
> - If Step 5 produced rich connections → this is the material for permanent note creation
