- Quote from Report
> A learner with low self-efficacy for understanding dense theoretical texts will set more superficial goals (because they expect to fail at deeper ones) and will select less demanding strategies. This means that motivational deficits in Subsystem 2 corrupt the outputs of Subsystem 1 in ways that are often invisible to the learner themselves.


Focus on more than just the Strategic Planning and Goal setting [the-visible-traits]. Don't neglect the motivational aspect of the [[forethought-phase|Forethought Phase]].

#### Subsystem 1a: Goal-Setting in the Forethought Phase

**Goal-setting**
Goals should be specific in nature, MODERATELY CHALLENGING, and supported by a feedback mechanism.

**[[Proximal Goals]]** achievable in a single session.
**[[Distal Goals]]** achievable across multiple sessions, over longer time frames.

During the Forethought phase prioritize [[Proximal-Goals]] over distal ones.
*Cross-References*: [[self-efficacy]], [[mastery-goal]], [[goal-setting-theory]]


**Goal Type** 
[[mastery-goal]] -> Developing competence, understanding, and learning. -> Better Comprehension and persistence on difficult tasks.
[[performance-goal]] -> focuses on demonstrating competence and superiority over competition, showing how smart you are. -> Undermines [[Intrinsic Motivation|Intrinsic-motivation]]

**Outcome-referenced mastery goals** -> ("By the end of this session I will have learned X")
**process-referenced mastery goals** -> ("During this session I will practice strategy Y, apply strategy Z, and monitor my comprehension at intervals")

#### Subsystem 1b: Strategic Planning in the Forethought Phase

Specifically choosing and selecting strategies to be used during the [[performance-phase]], with specification on how they will be monitored and adjusted.
