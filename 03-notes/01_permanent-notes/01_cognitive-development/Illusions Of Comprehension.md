> - **Key-Term**: [[Illusions of Comprehension]]
> - **Definition**: Illusions of Comprehension refer to the phenomenon where individuals mistakenly believe they understand information, even when their understanding is superficial or incorrect.

## Core Explanation

> [!analytical-insight] Core Explanation
> Paragraph 1: Foundational context and core mechanism. Illusions of Comprehension often occur due to cognitive biases such as overconfidence in one's own knowledge or the illusion of explanatory depth, where people believe they understand a concept more deeply than they actually do.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 2
> Paragraph 2: How it works or is applied in practice. This can be seen in various contexts, from academic settings where students think they grasp complex material but fail on exams, to everyday situations like believing one understands a technical topic without truly comprehending its nuances.

> [!analytical-insight] Explanation 3
> Paragraph 3: Key nuances, sub-variants, or theoretical roots. These illusions are closely related to metacognition and can be exacerbated by factors such as the ease of retrieving information from memory, which may give a false sense of understanding.
