## 🧭 Hints

> [!tip]- Hint 1 — Source vs Content
> Sperber et al. distinguish vigilance toward the **source** (who is telling me this, and what are their interests?) from vigilance toward the **content** (does what they say cohere with what I already know?). Mature epistemic vigilance integrates both; a State sentence covering only one is incomplete.

> [!tip]- Hint 2 — Why it's evolved, not learned
> Children as young as **3 years** show differential trust based on prior speaker reliability (Koenig & Harris experiments). Cross-cultural evidence supports this developmental timeline. This is one of the strongest arguments for vigilance as an evolved cognitive adaptation responding to selection pressure from deceptive communication.

> [!tip]- Hint 3 — Counter-example direction
> A conspiracy theorist who distrusts all mainstream sources exhibits *miscalibrated* vigilance, not heightened vigilance. True vigilance is *selective and accurate*; uniform distrust is failure of calibration. This is the counter-example axis.

## ✅ Solution Sketch

> [!example]- Solution Sketch — Key Analytical Moves
>
> **State:** Epistemic vigilance is the suite of evolved cognitive mechanisms that calibrate trust in communicated information by jointly evaluating the reliability of the **source** and the coherence of the **content** with prior knowledge, operating largely automatically and developing from early childhood as a defense against the adaptive costs of deceptive communication.
>
> **Elaborate:**
> Epistemic vigilance, introduced by [[Sperber|Sperber, Clément, Heintz, Mascaro, Mercier, Origgi, and Wilson]] (2010), names the *cognitive* (not merely deliberative) capacity to filter the information we accept from others. Communicated information is enormously valuable — it dwarfs what any individual could learn alone — but it is exploitable: speakers can mislead. Without vigilance, humans would be cognitive parasites' easy prey; with universal distrust, humans would lose the benefits of cooperation. Vigilance is the calibration between these failure modes.
>
> The concept includes two parallel mechanisms: **source-tracking** (we monitor cues to speaker reliability — track record, competence, benevolence, in-group status) and **content-checking** (we monitor whether claims cohere with what we already know — internal consistency, plausibility, fit with established beliefs).
>
> What it excludes: deliberate critical thinking (vigilance is *automatic*); skepticism (an attitude or temperament, not a mechanism); fact-checking (a deliberate practice). Vigilance produces the *defaults* on which deliberative reasoning operates.
>
> The concept matters because it predicts that humans are neither gullible (as cultural-transmission theorists once assumed) nor systematically rational; we are *selectively trusting* in ways shaped by evolutionary pressure.
>
> **Exemplify 1 (familiar — adult social reasoning):**
> A friend you have known for ten years tells you a surprising fact. A stranger on the street tells you the same fact. You give them different default credences — not because you deliberated, but because source-tracking ran automatically. If the friend has a known track record of accuracy, you accept it tentatively; if they have a track record of exaggeration, you discount even claims you'd believe from strangers. This is source-vigilance operating without deliberation.
>
> **Exemplify 2 (developmental/transfer — Koenig & Harris paradigm):**
> In experiments by [[Koenig and Harris]], 3- and 4-year-olds watch two adults label common objects. One adult labels correctly ("that's a shoe"); the other labels incorrectly ("that's a ball"). When the adults then offer competing labels for novel objects, children preferentially adopt the previously-accurate adult's labels. This is **automatic source-tracking at age 3**, before any explicit instruction in critical thinking — evidence that vigilance is a developmental capacity, not a learned skill.
>
> **Counter-example:**
> A conspiracy theorist who distrusts every claim from "mainstream" sources and accepts every claim from a fringe blogger is *not* exhibiting heightened epistemic vigilance — they are exhibiting **miscalibrated** vigilance. True vigilance is *selective and accurate*: it tracks actual reliability cues (track record, expertise, conflict of interest). Uniform distrust of a category, paired with uncritical trust of another, is the **inverse** of vigilance; it has the *form* of skepticism but the *function* of credulity. Distinguishing miscalibration from heightened vigilance is the practical core of media literacy.
>
> Similarly: a paranoid patient who suspects all caregivers is not displaying heightened vigilance but a pathological **decoupling** of vigilance from the cue-tracking that calibrates it.
>
> **Illustrate (analogy):**
> Epistemic vigilance is like the **immune system**, not like the **conscious decision to wash your hands**. It is (a) **evolved and automatic** — it runs without deliberation; (b) **calibrated by experience** — it learns which sources are reliable the way the immune system learns which antigens are threats; (c) **fails by miscalibration in both directions** — too sensitive produces autoimmunity (conspiracy theorizing), too tolerant produces infection (gullibility); (d) **interacts with deliberate practices** — conscious fact-checking is to vigilance what hygiene is to immunity: a *supplement*, not a replacement.
>
> ```mermaid
> flowchart LR
>   I[Incoming Claim] --> S[Source Track]
>   I --> C[Content Check]
>   S --> X{Trust Calibration}
>   C --> X
>   X -->|high source + high coherence| A[Accept default]
>   X -->|low source OR low coherence| D[Discount / Investigate]
>   X -->|low source AND low coherence| R[Reject default]
> ```
>
> **Mastery self-check:**
> - Teach to a 12-year-old? ✅ via the immune system analogy.
> - Distinguish from: [[Critical Thinking]] (deliberate, learned); [[Skepticism]] (attitude); [[Generalized Distrust]] (miscalibration in one direction); [[Gullibility]] (miscalibration in the other).
> - Transfer? ✅ developmental psychology, public health (vaccine messaging), media literacy, AI alignment (LLM source-reliability calibration).
> - Paul-Elder element? Primarily **Information (1.3)** — vigilance is the *mechanism by which we filter information sources* — and **Point of View (1.8)** — vigilance includes evaluating the source's standpoint, not just their content.
>
> **Expected mastery rating:** 3/5 if you covered only the deliberate-fact-checking layer; 4/5 if you got the source-vs-content distinction; 5/5 if you also captured automaticity + calibration *and* generated the immune-system analogy unaided.
